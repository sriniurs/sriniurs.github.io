A KARNATAKA HISTORY INVESTIGATION

A Philosopher
Beneath the Bricks

Zappy opens a history detective agency. Prof. Bolt insists on evidence before printing the visiting cards.

PART 1 · THE CASE OF THE QUIET PHILOSOPHER

A find, a date and a very important difference.

EP 1

The bricks have a lead

ZappyA philosopher beneath the bricks? Even my missing socks have less dramatic hiding places.

Prof. BoltOpen a case file. Leave the shovel out of it.

CLUE!

The reported discovery

A sculpture was found at Hampapura, in K. R. Nagar taluk, Mysuru district. A 6 November 2026 report says ASI specialists identified it as Adi Shankaracharya. [1]

ZappySo our first entry is a reported identification.

Prof. BoltCorrect. A newspaper reporting an expert’s finding is our starting source.

ZappyMy agency shall have a source column. And a snack column.

Keep the discovery, its reported identification and your own guesses separate.
EP 2

A birthday for the bricks?

ZappyNinth century! Shall I put the same birthday on the sculpture?

Prof. BoltOnly if you share a birthday with your house.

HOLD IT!

One date, careful scope

The report assigns the ninth-century date to the brick structure. That statement alone does not establish the sculpture’s carving date. [1]

ZappyThe address has a date. The resident needs a separate file.

Prof. BoltExactly. Context is a clue, not an automatic date stamp for every object.

The evidence cited here does not establish an exact age for the sculpture or proof of a personal visit.
Ask what was dated, how it was dated and what the evidence actually covers.
PART 2 · THE DEEP DIVE BEGINS

How do you question a witness made of stone?

EP 3

History has paperwork

ZappyCan we ask the statue whether the philosopher visited?

Prof. BoltTry the written evidence. Stone faces are terrible interviewees.

READ ON!

Meet epigraphy

Epigraphy means studying inscriptions—writing on materials such as stone or metal. ASI’s classification includes image inscriptions, sale records and commemorative records. [2]

ZappySo an old wall can turn out to be a filing cabinet.

Prof. BoltYes. Read what a record says before deciding which question it answers.

ZappyNew agency rule: no travel map until the paperwork passes inspection.

An inscription about a teaching, gift or person must be read in its own context. A connection is not automatically a travel diary.
EP 4

The philosopher in the file

ZappyBefore I open another folder, who exactly is our philosopher?

Prof. BoltAdi Shankaracharya. The ideas matter as much as the statue.

A small doorway into a big philosophy

Shankara is associated with Advaita Vedanta: a tradition teaching that ultimate reality, Brahman, is non-dual—without a second. This is a philosophical teaching, not an archaeological finding. [5]

ZappySo this case has two desks: philosophy and physical evidence.

Prof. BoltGood filing. Understanding an idea and tracing its history ask different questions.

A sculpture can prompt questions about a thinker. It cannot explain or prove an entire philosophy by itself.
EP 5

A very large inbox

ZappyThe video says 84,000-plus. Has every record mentioned Shankara?

Prof. BoltThat would be a remarkably specialised filing cabinet.

Search field ≠ search result

The reported ASI collection exceeds 84,000 inscriptions; researchers are looking within it for relevant references. [1]

ZappyA library full of books is not a library full of my biography.

Prof. BoltExactly. Always ask what a large number counts before being impressed by it.

Collection size, relevant records and authenticated conclusions are different totals. Never quietly substitute one for another.
EP 6

The map is getting ahead of us

ZappyMore than 86 places! I shall order a heroic travel map.

Prof. BoltOrder a pencil. We may need the eraser.

Leads, not verified stops

The report describes more than 86 traditionally associated places for investigation, not a proven itinerary. [1]

ZappySo a pin means “check here”, not “he definitely stood here”.

Prof. BoltPrecisely. A research question deserves a question mark, even on a colourful map.

Tradition can guide a search. Evidence must establish the particular historical link being claimed.
EP 7

A teacher gets a land grant

ZappyAt last, paperwork! Was ancient homework also submitted late?

Prof. BoltThis record is more interested in supporting a teacher.

A concrete example: Cholamadevi

A reported 1065 CE inscription at Cholamadevi records land supporting a teacher of a commentary on Shankara’s work. [1]

ZappyThat tells us the work had a teaching life there.

Prof. BoltYes. Evidence of teaching is valuable without upgrading it into a personal visit.

Ask the smallest solid question first: what activity does this particular record document?
EP 8

A photocopy without the machine

ZappyDo we carry the temple wall back to the office?

Prof. BoltThe office landlord would have questions.

Meet the estampage

An estampage is a paper impression of an inscription. ASI describes using wet paper and gentle tamping to capture its details for preservation and study away from the site. [3]

ZappyThe writing travels. The wall keeps its address.

Prof. BoltThat is the idea. Leave physical documentation to trained specialists; our detective kit can stay digital.

An impression preserves a record of the surface. Reading and interpreting that record remain separate work.
PART 3 · THE EVIDENCE COMES HOME

Mysuru, digital files and the limits of a good clue.

EP 9

Old letters, new screen

ZappyIf we scan everything, does the computer finish the history?

Prof. BoltIt also scans my shopping list. It has yet to cook dinner.

What BharatSHRI adds

BharatSHRI means Bharat Shared Repository of Inscriptions. Its official project page describes digitising ASI’s preserved impressions in Mysuru for online access, alongside transcripts and metadata—details describing the records. [4]

ZappySearchable files would make comparing clues much easier.

Prof. BoltYes. Better access helps research. A scan still needs a careful reading, attribution and explanation.

Digitisation shares evidence. It does not automatically settle a disputed reading or interpretation.
EP 10

Mysuru has another kind of treasury

ZappyI usually associate Mysuru treasure with palaces. And sweets.

Prof. BoltSome treasure comes with accession numbers instead of sugar.

The local connection is bigger than one find

ASI’s account says its Epigraphy Branch moved to Mysore in 1966. Its inscription archive connects local research with records from much farther afield. [2]

ZappySo the city is part of the investigation, not just its backdrop.

Prof. BoltExactly. The quiet work of collecting, editing and preserving records makes later discoveries useful.

Heritage is also an archive, a specialist’s reading and a record someone bothered to preserve.
EP 11

Three clues walk into an office

ZappyMy file contains a statue, an inscription and a tradition. Same drawer?

Prof. BoltSame investigation. Give each its own label.

Each clue answers a different question

ImageWho or what might be represented? Check the identification and context.
InscriptionWhat does this written record actually say? Check its reading and date.
TraditionWhat association has been remembered? Investigate the historical connection.

ZappyThat stops my favourite theory from swallowing every clue.

Prof. BoltA useful habit. Explain why pieces fit, rather than merely placing them next to each other.

This comparison is our reading framework, not a claim that any one kind of evidence is always sufficient.
EP 12

The blank space stays blank

ZappyThe exact sculpture date is still blank. My page looks unfinished.

Prof. BoltA blank is more useful than a confident invention.

What this case file cannot yet settle

The cited reporting does not supply a full excavation report or a demonstrated carving date for the Hampapura sculpture. This comic therefore leaves those questions open.

ZappyCan I at least plan a visit with tickets and opening hours?

Prof. BoltOnly after checking current official access. A news story is not a visitor-information desk.

An unknown is a research task. Do not fill it with a guess, a borrowed date or an invented visitor plan.
PART 4 · THE INSPECTOR’S VERDICT

Keep the curiosity. Tighten the claims.

EP 13

OPINION · Prof. Bolt’s verdict

Bright side

ZappyIs our agency allowed to be excited yet?

Prof. BoltAbsolutely. A local discovery can open a much larger conversation about teaching, memory and ideas.

Hard part

ZappyAnd the part that refuses to fit on a dramatic poster?

Prof. BoltDates need scope. Identifications need reasons. Uncertainty needs space, even when the headline is impatient.

MY TAKE

Prof. BoltCelebrate the clue. Respect the scholarship. A story grows stronger when its claims stay within its evidence.

ZappyOur agency shall sell excitement with the source attached.

EP 14

So what should Zappy do?

ZappyI am ready for fieldwork. I have bought an enormous hat.

Prof. BoltExcellent shade. Now equip the notebook.

Before sharing

Open the source. Note the date. Write down exactly what was found and who identified it.

Before drawing conclusions

Separate the structure’s date from the object’s date. Ask whether a record supports teaching, influence or a personal visit.

ZappyThree questions. Much cheaper than my hat.

Prof. BoltExplore the official archive. Keep the record’s reference beside your notes.

Before any visit

Check official access information. Leave objects and surfaces undisturbed; report a suspected find to the relevant heritage authority.
Curiosity works best with a source, a clear question and respect for the site.
EP 15

Fridge-door cheat sheet

Remember

A dated structure does not automatically date every object inside it. A teaching link does not automatically prove a personal visit.

Do this

Check the source, date and exact claim before forwarding. Keep reported findings separate from your own interpretation.

Deep dive

Try BharatSHRI. Learn the difference between the image, transcription and interpretation of a record.

ZappyCase file updated. We have a real clue, open questions and no unauthorised digging.

Prof. BoltA promising start. Now investigate those missing socks.

ZappyFirst I must establish the age of the laundry basket.
