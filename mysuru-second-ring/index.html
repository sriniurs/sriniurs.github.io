Why Is Mysuru Building a Second Ring Around Itself?

The Outer Ring Road took about 12 years to build, and traffic and houses filled it anyway. Now a road two and a half times longer is planned outside it. Here’s why, and why the land beside it is the real story.

ABOUT 13 MINUTES

01

A bangle that filled up

Ask anyone in Mysuru how to get from Hebbal to Bogadi without crawling through the city, and they’ll say: take the Ring Road.

The Outer Ring Road (ORR) is a 42.5 km loop around the city. It links the roads that fan out from the centre like spokes: the Bengaluru highway, KRS Road, Hunsur Road, Bogadi Road, H.D. Kote Road, Nanjangud Road, T. Narasipur Road, Bannur Road and Mahadevapura Road. [1] A ring road is a bypass shaped like a bangle. A truck coming down one spoke can swing around the edge to another without ever entering the city centre.

It was slow to build. Work began around 2003 and went ahead in five phases. In 2015 the last stretch was still unfinished, and the estimate had reached ₹252 crore. [2] By 2017 the Mysuru Urban Development Authority (MUDA, the body that plans the city’s growth) said it had spent ₹332.20 crore building and widening the road. Keeping it up would cost more than ₹30 crore a year, so MUDA handed its maintenance to the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI). [3] Today the loop is numbered as a national highway, NH-275K. [4]

Then something predictable happened. Houses followed the road. New layouts came up all along the loop, and traffic on it grew so heavy that planners now say a second ring cannot wait. [5]

ZappySo we built a road to keep the traffic out… and then moved in next to it?

Prof. BoltEvery city does it. A ring road is a magnet. Build one, and the city grows out to meet it.

The Ring Road worked. That’s exactly why it filled up.
02

Ring number two

The answer is a bigger bangle. The Mysuru Development Authority (MDA, as MUDA is now called) plans a Peripheral Ring Road (PRR): a second loop, further out, that goes all the way around the first one. [6]

On paper it is 105.31 km long and 45 metres wide, with six lanes (three each way) plus service roads on both sides. [7] That is about two and a half times the length of the ORR (our arithmetic).

In October 2025 the MDA invited bids from consultants worldwide to write the Detailed Project Report (DPR). That is the master plan for a road: the exact route, every plot of land needed, the bridges and the cost. It set aside ₹7.5 crore for the report and gave the consultant six months. The MDA Commissioner said the foundation stone could be laid by late 2026, “if all goes according to plan”. [6]

In January 2026 the MDA board changed who builds it. The earlier plan was a public-private partnership (PPP), where a private company builds the road and earns its money back over years. Now it will be an EPC contract (engineering, procurement and construction): the MDA pays a contractor to build it and keeps control itself. [8]

03

The 100-foot idea

Here is the part most people miss.

When MUDA built the first ring road, it bought only the land the road itself needed: the carriageway and the service roads. [7] The fields right next to the road stayed with their owners. Once the road opened, those fields became prime plots. Layouts and real-estate firms gained from the price rise. MUDA, which had built the road, got none of it. [7]

This time the MDA wants to do it differently. Along with the road, it plans to take an extra strip of at least 100 feet on each side, for the whole 105 km, and keep it as public land. [7] Star of Mysore puts that at about 6.8 crore sq ft. [7] By our arithmetic, that’s roughly 1,600 acres, or close to 900 football pitches, before counting the road itself.

RING ROAD 1 (ORR)

Bought just the road. The land next to it rose in value, and the gain went to private owners.

RING ROAD 2 (PRR)

Plans to buy the road plus a 100-ft strip on each side, and use or lease that land itself.

What would go in the strip? The list includes truck terminals, warehouses, logistics hubs, service centres, water pipelines, power substations, waste-handling sites and green zones. [7] Officials told the paper that selling or leasing parts of it could earn “lakhs of crores”, enough to pay back the road quickly. That figure comes from unnamed officials, and we couldn’t check it. [7]

ZappySo the road pays for itself, by owning the land next to it?

Prof. BoltThat’s the plan. But someone owns that land today. Ask the farmer whose field is in the strip.

The first ring road made private landowners rich. The second is designed so the city keeps some of that money.
04

The road that keeps changing size

This is not a new idea. Mysuru has been drawing a second ring on maps for about ten years, and the ring keeps changing size.

The city’s Master Plan for 2031 included it. In 2016 MUDA proposed a road of roughly 120 km and set aside ₹750 crore. By 2019 the project was being called a non-starter: not even a basic road survey had been done. Some town planners argued then that the ring road was not yet full, and that the land around it was still empty. [9]

By 2022 the plan had shrunk to 73.25 km, with ₹1,971 crore estimated for land and road, and a project report was sent to the government. That year a Union minister asked for the report so the road could be considered under the Centre’s highway programme, Bharatmala Phase-2. [1] In 2023 the same ₹1,971 crore estimate was reported for a 104 km road: ₹1,236 crore for land and ₹735 crore for building. [10] Today the number is 105.31 km. [6]

ZappyWait, it shrank by 47 km and then grew back, and the price didn’t move?

Prof. BoltThat’s what happens when the route is still a line on a map. The real cost arrives with the final report.

Until the project report and land notice are public, every length and price is a draft.
05

So what changes for you?

If you drive the Ring Road every day: nothing changes soon. Even on the official timetable, only a foundation stone was due this year. A 105 km road with land to buy takes years. Smaller fixes, like the railway underpasses being built along the ORR, will matter to you first. [4]

If you are buying a site on the outskirts: “ring road coming” will be in every advertisement. But the exact route is not public yet, and the plan is to take a strip of land on each side. [7] A plot that sounds “next to the ring road” could end up inside the strip the MDA buys, and in that case you’d get compensation, not a road-facing plot.

If you own farmland out there: you’ll want to know your rights before a notice arrives. That’s episode 6.

If you live in one of the villages beyond the ring: the bigger city limits and planning area mean that around 40 villages are expected to come under the city. [8] That brings city rules, city taxes and, slowly, city services.

ZappySo right now, the ring road is mostly a rumour with a number on it?

Prof. BoltA serious plan with a number on it. But yes, don’t pay extra for a plot because of a line nobody has published.

For most Mysureans, the PRR is a 2030s road. The land decisions being made now are what matter today.

06

The land maths

A road is mostly a land problem. Here are the three sizes of land that have been on the table:

Piece of landSizeWhat it’s for
The road itselfAbout 824 acres (2022–23 estimate)Six lanes plus service roads [1] [10]
The two 100-ft stripsAbout 1,600 acres (our arithmetic)Public land the MDA keeps [7]
A 2022 ‘town planning’ beltAbout 27,500 acres750 m each side, proposed in 2022 [1]

That last idea was huge. 27,500 acres is about 111 sq km (our arithmetic), more than the whole of the old city corporation’s 86 sq km. [8] The 2025 plan talks about the narrower 100-ft strips instead. [7]

What does the law pay? Land taken for a public project is paid for under a central law of 2013 (the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act). In rural areas, the market value is multiplied by a factor of 1 to 2, depending on how far the land is from a town. [11] The value of buildings, trees and crops is added. Then comes a solatium: an extra 100% on top, as a payment for being forced to sell. [12]

Market value · ₹10 lakh→× rural factor up to 2 · ₹20 lakh→+ 100% solatium · ₹40 lakh

Our arithmetic, for a plot far enough from town to get the full factor of 2, with nothing built on it. The factor actually applied is set by state rules.

There is another way, and Mysuru is already trying it. For the new Bommenahalli layout, the MDA is offering a 50:50 deal: owners hand over raw land and get back half of the developed sites, whatever the difference in market value. [13] Farmers there had agreed to give about 500 of the nearly 1,000 acres needed by March 2026. [13]

Cash under the 2013 law, or developed plots in a pooling deal: the PRR will need one of these, and possibly both.
07

Bengaluru’s 18-year warning

Bengaluru drew its own peripheral ring road years before Mysuru. In June 2007, the Bangalore Development Authority (BDA) issued its final notice to acquire 1,810 acres. [14] The road was not built, and the notice did not go away. In 2025 it was reported that, 18 years on, the BDA had acquired just 3.21 acres. [14]

Meanwhile the price climbed. The project was costed at ₹5,800 crore in 2007 and about ₹27,000 crore by 2025. Deccan Herald put the jump down to land acquisition not being finished on time. The road has since been renamed the Bengaluru Business Corridor. [15] For families across the 67 villages named in the 2007 notice, that has meant years in limbo. [16] [15]

It is finally moving. In March 2026, global tenders went out for the first 20 km at ₹3,348 crore, and officials said 83% of the land for that phase had been acquired. [17]

ZappyEighteen years to buy three acres?

Prof. BoltNotifying land is easy. Paying for it is the hard part. Until the money arrives, everyone along the line is stuck.

The lesson for Mysuru: a notice without the money to pay for it doesn’t freeze a road. It freezes people.
08

Two cities that finished

Hyderabad built a 158 km, eight-lane ring that cost ₹6,696 crore. Work began in the mid-2000s, and the last stretch was finished in 2018. [18] In 2023, the state leased the right to collect tolls on it to a private company for 30 years, for an upfront ₹7,380 crore. [19] On paper, that one lease brought in more than the road had cost to build (our comparison, not adjusted for inflation).

Ahmedabad opened its 76 km, 60-metre-wide Sardar Patel Ring Road in 2004. [20] [21] Its development authority, AUDA, has long used Gujarat’s town planning schemes: owners pool their land, the authority lays out roads and services, and everyone gets back a smaller but serviced plot. [22] [21]

OPINION: the strip is the smart part

This section is our opinion, not reporting.

WHAT’S RIGHT

Keeping a strip of land beside the road is the right lesson from the first ring road. The value a public road creates should pay for the next one, rather than all going to whoever happens to own the fields beside it.

WHAT’S RISKY

A wider strip means more land to buy, and more owners to pay. Bengaluru shows what a notice without the money behind it does: it freezes people’s land for years.

WHAT WOULD HELP

Publish the route early. Fund the land before notifying it. Offer owners a real choice between cash under the 2013 law and a share of the developed land, as Bommenahalli is trying.

✓

So what should Zappy do?

Buying a site? Don’t pay extra for “near the ring road” until the MDA publishes the route. Then check that your survey number isn’t inside the road or its 100-ft strips.

Own land on the outskirts? Watch for an MDA notice that names your survey number. Notices come with a window for objections. Use it, and learn the 2013 law’s formula (market value, rural factor, 100% solatium) before anyone makes you an offer.

Commuting on the ORR? Plan your life around the current road. The new one is years away.

Just curious? Ask your local councillor or MLA when the project report will be made public. Everything else waits on it.

Cheat sheet

📍 ORR: Mysuru’s 42.5 km first ring, built from around 2003 for ₹332 crore.

⭕ PRR: a planned 105.31 km, six-lane second ring outside it.

📏 The twist: the MDA wants an extra 100-ft strip on each side, about 1,600 acres, as public land.

📝 Status: the project report is being prepared. No foundation stone found yet.

🔄 Size history: about 120 km, then 73 km, then 104 km, now 105.31 km.

⚖️ The law: market value × up to 2 in rural areas, plus 100% solatium.

⏳ Bengaluru: 1,810 acres notified in 2007, just 3.21 acres acquired by 2025.

🏆 Hyderabad: a 158 km ring, finished in 2018, whose 30-year toll lease earned ₹7,380 crore upfront.

So, how do you get from Hebbal to Bogadi without crawling through the city? For now, the answer is still: take the Ring Road.

One day the answer may be: which one? Whether that day comes in five years or twenty-five depends less on asphalt than on who owns a few thousand acres of fields today, and how fairly they are paid.
