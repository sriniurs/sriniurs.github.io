Why Do Indian Houses Keep Getting Smaller?

The brochure says 2BHK. The old teak sofa won’t fit. A Mysuru house hunt shows how your budget can buy less space even when average flat sizes rise.

Carpet areaPrice per sq ftThe sofa test

7–8 MINUTE READ · 9 EPISODES

01

The sofa that didn’t fit

On a Saturday morning in Mysuru, Zappy carried one thing to his cousin Meera’s flat viewing: the measurements of her mother’s old teak sofa. The brochure promised a generous 2BHK. The sofa, measured arm to arm, needed seven feet of wall. In the show flat, the first wall ended at the balcony door and the second had a switchboard behind it. The salesperson pointed to the sample sofa. It was barely five feet wide.

Meera had grown up in an independent house with a verandah. Now she wanted a place within a manageable commute of work, with a second bedroom for her mother to stay over. On paper both homes had two bedrooms. In practice, the older house had somewhere to leave wet shoes, dry clothes and seat six people for lunch. Those jobs had to go somewhere in the new flat too.

The viewing is fictional, but it raises three different questions. Are new homes actually getting smaller? How much space can one buyer afford in a particular location? And how much of that space works for daily life? A national headline, a property budget and a tape measure each answer a different one.

02

The numbers tell a different story

Zappy expected the city data to confirm what he’d seen. Instead, ANAROCK’s market tracking, as reported in February 2026, showed that average flat sizes across seven major markets rose by about 17% from 2023 to 2025. In the Mumbai Metropolitan Region, the reported figure went from 810 to 904 sq ft. In the National Capital Region, it went from 1,890 to 2,466 sq ft. Two markets can move in the same direction and still offer very different amounts of space. [1]

What the market tracker covers matters. It describes the apartments in major markets it tracks and reflects the mix of new supply. It cannot tell Meera what happened to entry-level 2BHKs in her part of Mysuru. A growing share of larger or premium projects can lift an average even when homes in a buyer’s price band remain tight. ANAROCK’s own 2025 annual report documents a shift in new supply towards higher-priced stock. [2]

The city figures are quoted on the source’s area basis. The published summary doesn’t establish that they are comparable to RERA carpet areas. Use them to understand the reported direction and contrast between those markets.

03

Whose house are we counting?

At lunch, Meera’s mother said the old house had felt spacious even with five people around. Zappy asked whether independent houses were rare in Indian cities. The National Sample Survey gives a broader snapshot. In July–December 2018, about 56.4% of urban households living in houses lived in an independent house. The survey estimated average dwelling floor area at 46.1 m² urban and 46.6 m² rural, about 496 and 502 sq ft respectively. [3]

Those figures describe occupied dwellings across the country at one point in time. They include housing that would never appear in a launch report for seven big markets. A survey floor-area measure also has its own definition. Putting the 2018 urban number beside a 2025 market-launch number would tell a misleading story about growth.

Then there’s the household itself. A 750 sq ft home shared by two people has 375 sq ft per person on paper. The same home shared by five has 150. A family gathering also puts pressure on particular rooms. What matters at lunchtime is whether people can sit together and still reach the kitchen.

The Numbers That MatterWhat a fixed budget buys as rates rise
04

What ₹75 lakh buys when the rate rises

Meera writes ₹75 lakh on the back of the brochure and draws a circle around it. That’s her fictional base-price ceiling. At ₹7,500 per sq ft of carpet area, it buys 1,000 sq ft. At ₹10,000 per sq ft on the same basis, it buys 750 sq ft. The unit price has risen by one-third, and the area her budget buys falls by one-quarter.

The numbers illustrate a mechanism. They are neither Mysuru quotes nor a forecast. At a fixed budget, area equals price budget divided by rate per square foot. This is how a buyer can lose a quarter of the space without changing the budget or dropping the second bedroom. If she wants to hold on to 1,000 sq ft at the higher rate, the base price becomes ₹1 crore.

In a real purchase, the ceiling has to make room for more than the quoted base price. Ask for an itemised cost sheet and budget for applicable taxes, stamp duty, registration and any relevant project charges. Also work out how much the household can comfortably pay each month. Two homes with a similar quote can leave very different amounts for maintenance, transport and everything else.

05

Why a builder might draw a smaller flat

Imagine a builder has 12,000 sq ft of total carpet area to divide after the building and common areas have been planned. One pencil sketch makes 12 homes of 1,000 sq ft each. Another makes 16 homes of 750 sq ft each. At a hypothetical ₹10,000 per carpet sq ft, the smaller home’s base price is ₹75 lakh instead of ₹1 crore. In this model, gross base-price revenue is ₹12 crore in either case.

The drawings are deliberately simple. A real redesign may need more kitchens, bathrooms, service runs and doors. More homes can also change the circulation, parking and approval requirements. Local planning controls affect the amount and form of building a site can accommodate. A developer still has to consider construction cost, what buyers will pay and how many units can sell.

That explains two decisions you might see on the same road. A builder targeting families with firm budget limits might use smaller units to reach a lower price per home. Another builder might sell fewer, larger homes to buyers who can pay more. The same piece of city can produce both. ANAROCK’s 2025 report shows how the mix of supply can shift towards premium stock in the markets it tracks. [2]

06

Three areas can describe one flat

The brochure says 1,200 sq ft. The price sheet says 900 sq ft carpet. There’s also an exclusive balcony. Zappy wants to subtract one number from the other and call the difference “the missing 300”. Prof. Bolt asks him to get the breakdown first. A brochure number may include a share of common areas and other components, depending on how it’s presented.

India’s Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act defines carpet area in Section 2(k). It counts the floor area covered by internal partition walls. It excludes external walls, service shafts, exclusive balconies or verandahs and exclusive open terraces. Ask for the carpet area of the specific unit and for exclusive outdoor areas separately. [4]

Here’s the practical difference. At the same hypothetical ₹90 lakh base price, dividing by the brochure’s 1,200 sq ft gives ₹7,500 per sq ft. Dividing by the flat’s 900 sq ft carpet area gives ₹10,000 per carpet sq ft. The flat and its price stayed put. Changing the denominator changed the quoted rate by a third.

For a registered project, match the particular unit and the stated carpet area to the project documents and agreement. Then put both homes on the same comparison basis. You can still give the balcony its own value. [5]

The Deep DiveWhere floor area turns into everyday space
07

When 750 square feet lives like more

In the second flat, Meera finds a short entry, a dining table tucked beside the kitchen and a long wall for the teak sofa. In the first, the entrance corridor turns twice, and doors break up the living room walls. Suppose both have 750 sq ft of carpet area. If one uses 90 sq ft for circulation and the other 45, the second has 45 sq ft more elsewhere in the plan, with the rest of the allocation held constant.

Corridors can provide privacy and separate noisy spaces. Their value depends on what they do. The same is true of an open kitchen. It can make the living room feel larger and also let cooking noise and smells reach the sofa. A wide window can change how the room feels, while the direction it faces and nearby buildings affect the light and view you actually get.

Bring the dimensions of your own furniture. Draw the bed and check the path around it. Open the wardrobe and imagine carrying a bucket through the utility area. Locate the washing machine, where clothes will dry and where someone can take a call during dinner. These tests show what floor area alone can hide.

Measure continuous wall lengths and door swings. Mark beds and wardrobes on the plan before you judge how the rooms work.

08

The space outside your front door

Meera has one more pair of fictional options. A compact flat is close to work and her mother’s clinic. A larger one is farther away. If the nearer flat saves 25 minutes each way on 20 commuting days a month, that is 1,000 minutes, or 16 hours and 40 minutes. The calculation assumes both routes keep their usual times. She still has to test the commute when she would actually make it.

A park, a shared play area or a walkable street can take some pressure off a small flat. Each has its limits. A clubhouse can host a birthday party if residents can book it. It cannot give an older relative a quiet bedroom on an ordinary Tuesday. A lift can make upper floors accessible, so think about who uses it, the route from the gate and how the building works during maintenance.

Compare location and layout with the household that will live there. A couple who both work from home may value two doors that close. A family helping an elderly parent may put access and nearby services ahead of a bigger living room. Add maintenance and travel costs to the comparison so that the gain in space or time has a visible price.

The VerdictBack to the sofa, with a tape measure
09

Back to the teak sofa

At the end of the viewing, Meera returns to the first flat with a tape measure. The quoted area had looked generous. The sitting wall still ended too soon. In the second flat, she marks the sofa’s actual length on the floor with her heel. There’s room for someone to walk past without turning sideways. She asks for the unit plan and the itemised quote before deciding.

Are Indian houses shrinking? The seven-market launch figures show that average sizes can rise. Meera’s search shows why a buyer can still face smaller choices at a given price and location. Once you find two genuine options, the useful comparison is what they cost, how much carpet area they provide and how well the rooms serve your household.

Put those measures together before paying a booking amount. Check the project record where applicable. Compare the written costs on the same basis, then do a walk-through with a tape measure and the people who will live there. If the sofa fits and the wardrobe opens, keep going. Try the morning routine and test the commute too.
