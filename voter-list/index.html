ABOUT 9 MINUTES

01

One in five names, gone

On 27 October 2026, Karnataka published its new voter list. Nearly one crore names that were on the old list are not on the new one.

Before the revision, the state had 5,54,32,314 voters. The draft list in August cut that to 4,46,35,975. After claims and objections, the final list settled at 4,55,00,011, a net drop of about 99 lakh, roughly 18% of the electorate. [1] [3]

The cut was not even. Bengaluru fell from about 1.03 crore voters to 59.08 lakh, a drop of 43%. [2] Mysuru district went from 27.99 lakh to 22.80 lakh, about one in every five names. [2]

The final list has about 2.29 crore women and 2.25 crore men, 6.83 lakh first-time voters aged 18–19, and 3.92 lakh voters above 85. [3]

02

How a voter list is rebuilt

Normally the voter list is updated a little at a time: new names added, deaths removed, people shifted. A Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is different. Every person already on the list must confirm they still belong on it. Booth-level officers (BLOs) went house to house handing out an enumeration form, and no documents were needed at that stage. [6]

The form asked one unusual question: were you, or a parent or grandparent, on the 2002 voter list? That year is the reference point because it is treated as the last comparable revision in Karnataka, although some experts dispute that it was the same kind of exercise. [10] Linking yourself is called self-mapping; linking through a relative, who can be from any state, is progeny mapping. [6]

About 1.56 crore voters were matched to 2002 directly and 1.93 crore through a relative. Around 17.19 lakh forms came back with no link at all. [7] People who couldn’t be linked, or whose details didn’t match, got a notice and had to show at least one of the listed documents. Officials could ask for up to three, including Aadhaar. [8]

03

Why names fell off: the ASDD-O list

Names that could not be confirmed were marked under five headings, known by their initials, ASDD-O: Absent, Shifted, Dead, Duplicate, and Others. [5] At the draft stage, they broke down like this: [4]

SHIFTED OR UNTRACEABLE

80.62 lakh — by far the biggest group. People not found at the address on the list.

DEAD

16.39 lakh names of people who had died but were still on the rolls.

DUPLICATES

More than 7 lakh names registered in more than one place.

OTHERS

3.83 lakh, including people who, the CEO’s office said, refused to sign.

Some of this is plain housekeeping. In Mysuru, officials cited a booth-level officer whose own name was on the rolls in his home district, at his workplace and in Mysuru. [11] Dead voters lingering on lists for years is a known problem everywhere.

But the biggest group wasn’t the dead or duplicates. It was people the list couldn’t find at their old address. Against those 80.62 lakh, only 7,87,534 applications to be included came in during the claims period, counting brand-new voters too. [4] The Election Commission has said that being marked ASDD-O was not meant to mean automatic deletion. [5]

A name marked “shifted” usually belongs to a real person who still lives somewhere in Karnataka. The question is whether they ever got the message.

04

Why cities lost the most

Bengaluru holds about one in five of Karnataka’s voters but produced about 44% of the draft deletions. Every assembly seat in Bengaluru Urban lost at least 36% of its voters. [12] One seat, Bommanahalli, fell from 4.84 lakh to 2.19 lakh at the draft stage. [13]

In the final count, the reasons for Bengaluru’s deletions were: 32.74 lakh permanently shifted, 7.27 lakh absent, 3.19 lakh dead and 63,549 duplicates. [2] In other words, the city’s problem is mostly movement, not death.

Think of how a city lives. A family rents a flat in Koramangala, moves to Whitefield three years later, then to Electronic City. Each move leaves an old address on the list. Students and workers from Mysuru, Hubballi or other states keep their names at home while living in the city, or end up on both. A BLO knocking on an old door finds strangers.

It isn’t just Bengaluru. Lucknow lost about 23% of its voters, Surat 25% and Ahmedabad 21.5%. Chennai’s draft list dropped from 40.04 lakh to 25.79 lakh. [14] Critics argue that tenants and internal migrants were often treated as “permanently shifted” instead of being helped to move their registration to the new address. [22]

05

Who fell off

A newspaper analysis of the draft data looked only at deletions for reasons other than death, and found a pattern. Women made up 51.1% of them. Among people aged 18–29, 8.1 lakh women were marked for deletion against 5.3 lakh men. [12]

The data doesn’t say why. One plausible explanation is ordinary life: women in India commonly move to their husband’s home after marriage, often to a different town, and their registration may stay at their parents’ address. Civil-society groups raised exactly this risk, listing people who shifted for work or marriage, tenants, elderly voters, and those with minor errors on their forms. [15]

The final list still has more women than men, 2.29 crore to 2.25 crore. [3] But for a young married woman who moved district, the safest assumption is: check.

The pattern repeats: big cities and states with heavy migration lose the most names.

07

The debate, both sides

This revision has become political. Here are the main arguments, stated as fairly as we can. You can decide for yourself.

THE CASE FOR

Rolls full of the dead, duplicates and people long gone make elections easier to manipulate and harder to run. Karnataka’s Chief Electoral Officer has said no eligible voter was arbitrarily deleted, and that fewer than 1,000 foreigners were identified. [2] The Supreme Court has upheld the Bihar revision as constitutionally valid and proportionate, and within the Election Commission’s powers. [19]

THE CASE AGAINST

The scale is unprecedented, and most deletions are “shifted” or “absent”, not dead or duplicate. Tens of thousands of deletion objections were reportedly “auto-generated” by the system. [4] Women and young people appear over-represented. [12] Critics say the burden fell on ordinary voters to prove they belonged.

WHAT THE COURTS SAID

In the Bihar case, the Supreme Court ordered that Aadhaar be accepted as a 12th document, for identity though not as proof of citizenship. [20] It has also asked the Commission why deleted Delhi voters were told to re-apply on Form 6. [21]

What both sides agree on: the list should include every eligible voter and no one else. The Election Commission has ordered a special drive to re-enrol eligible people who were left out, and has dropped the extra SIR declaration from Form 6 in states where the revision is over. [24]

08

Check yours in two minutes

Don’t assume. Renters who moved, students in another city, women who moved after marriage, and anyone who was away during the home visits are the most likely to be affected.

1 · Search

Go to voters.eci.gov.in or open the Voter Helpline app. Search with your EPIC (voter ID) number, or by name, age and district. [23]

2 · Found? You’re fine

If your name shows on the final roll, your registration is active. Check that the address and polling booth are right.

3 · Missing? File Form 6

Apply online on the same portal or app, or through your BLO. Deleted voters can now use the regular Form 6, without the extra SIR declaration. [24]

4 · Wrong details? That’s Form 8

Form 8 corrects or shifts an existing entry. It cannot add a name that is missing. [23]

Late applications are added through supplementary lists published every quarter, and the Chief Electoral Officer’s office says an enrolment drive will follow. [4] Anyone turning 18 on or before 1 October 2026 can apply at any time. [25] Zilla and taluk panchayat seat reservations were notified on 7 October [27], so local elections may not be far away. Checking now costs two minutes.
