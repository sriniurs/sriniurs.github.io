The Night the Ones Walked Out

Four ones escape from a sale banner. Zappy follows them back to the story of Singles’ Day, and finds out how one date became a festival that stretched across weeks.

ABOUT 6 MINUTES

01

Four ones go missing

11 November · 11:11 p.m.

The first clue was the dot. It was still on the screen. The four ones were halfway across the kitchen counter.

Zappy had opened the tablet to see what the 11.11 sale was about. Now there was a hole where the date had been. One digit slipped down the charging cable. The others followed. From the floor came the sound of eight tiny feet.

Prof. Bolt picked up the kitchen calendar. November 11 was circled there, too. The date had meant something long before anyone put a shopping cart beside it. [1]

ZappyQuick. They might know where the good deals are.

Prof. BoltTake the calendar. I think we’re following the date.

02

The date before the sale

11 / 11Four ones, each standing on its own.

Look at the date as four separate marks. Each 1 can stand for one person. Put them side by side and you get the visual joke behind China’s Singles’ Day, an occasion to celebrate being single.

The tradition is commonly traced to Chinese university students in the 1990s. Several versions of the founding story circulate, and the exact details are uncertain. The symbol is easier to explain. Anyone who saw 11/11 could remember the date and pass it on. [1]

At the bottom of the stairs, the fourth one waited while the others caught up. Zappy counted all four again. They seemed to know exactly where they were going.

ZappyIf they’re singles, why are they going out together?

Prof. BoltYou can be single and still have plans for dinner.

03

The first 11.11 sale

27 merchants

Alibaba’s first 11.11 sale · 2009

In 2009, Alibaba used Singles’ Day to try a different kind of gift sale. The person choosing the gift could also be the person receiving it. Twenty-seven merchants took part in that first event. [2] [3]

Think about what a shared date does for a small shop. On an ordinary day it has to give people a reason to visit. During a sale that several shops run together, customers can remember one date and browse several sellers in the same visit. The four ones already gave Alibaba a date people could recognise.

Zappy looked up from his tablet. The digits had stopped outside a café. Through the window he could see four cups and one plate of samosas. He checked the door for a sale sign.

ZappyTwenty-seven shops? What happened to that little sale?

Prof. BoltBy 2017, the festival named after one day stretched across twenty-four.

04

How one date became 24 days

The date · 11.11

A date shoppers and merchants could remember.

The 2017 festival · 24 days

Alibaba’s description included shopping and entertainment beyond November 11.

Once shoppers knew when to look, a merchant could start getting their attention before November 11. Alibaba’s 2017 factsheet describes shoppers browsing and filling carts ahead of the main day. It also describes an entertainment programme alongside the shopping. The date still gave people a shared point on the calendar, even as the event spread around it. [3]

Zappy looked at the clock above the café door. Only a few minutes had passed since the digits escaped. Somewhere, a marketing team had managed to stretch the same date across weeks.

ZappyDo the four ones get overtime?

Prof. BoltI’d ask before you order for the table.

05

Your cart gives the warehouse a clue

An item in a cart can tell a shop something before there’s a sale.

Say a shop offers a blue backpack and a red one. In the run-up to 11.11, it notices that far more shoppers have put blue backpacks in their carts. Some will change their minds. Others will leave the cart sitting there. Even so, the shop now has a clue about which colour might run out first.

Alibaba’s 2017 factsheet describes early cart activity helping brands plan inventory. A retailer could use an early signal alongside past sales to decide how much stock to have ready. If the clue is wrong, the retailer may still end up with a pile of unsold backpacks. [3]

ZappyI put three backpacks in my cart to compare them.

Prof. BoltWhich is exactly why three in the cart can turn into one at the till.

06

India tried a date you could remember

China · 11/11

Singles’ Day already gave the date a meaning. Alibaba built a sale around it.

India · 12/12/12

Google India and retail partners used a memorable date for a 24-hour online sale.

On 12 December 2012, Google India and its partners ran the Great Online Shopping Festival. One of its aims was to get first-time online buyers to try shopping on the web. A single date gave participating shops a way to invite people together. Google ended the event in 2015, pointing to the growth of retailers’ own sale days. [4] [5]

Zappy wrote 12/12/12 beside the four ones on the calendar. The two events had different beginnings. They shared a trick that fits on one line of a calendar.

07

A fast website still has to pack a parcel

A shopper can place an order in seconds. Someone still has to find the right backpack, pack it, stick on a label, sort it by destination and get it to the doorstep. When lots of shoppers check out together, all that physical work arrives together too.

In its account of preparing for its 2015 Big Billion Day sale, Flipkart describes two separate challenges. Its website had to handle the rush, and its teams had to match delivery promises to what they could pack and move. That’s where knowing which items people might want becomes useful. It buys time to get the stock ready before the orders land. [6]

08

Four tables for one?

The waiter looked up from the reservation book. “Four tables for one?”

Each digit grabbed a corner of a small table. They dragged it into the middle of the room and pulled up four stools. “One table for four,” said the smallest, brushing a crumb off its seat.

Then the first one handed Zappy a phone. He took the picture just as the waiter put down the samosas. For the second time that night, all four ones fit on one screen.

ZappyWell, I found out where they go on 11.11.

Prof. BoltCheck your phone. I think they’ve found out where to find you.
