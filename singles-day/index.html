11.11: Four Singles,
Two Countries,
One Very Full Cart

How China’s celebration of single life became a shopping festival—and why the shopping scene feels familiar in India.

Money TalksChina + IndiaCelebrations & shopping

ABOUT 6 MINUTES

01

Why four ones?

11 / 11Four ones, each standing on its own.

November 11 gives China’s Singles’ Day its visual joke: four separate ones representing single people. The celebration is widely traced to Chinese university students in the 1990s; the precise founding story is less certain. [1]

Its appeal was straightforward. Couples had their occasions. Here was a day to enjoy being single, with friends, gatherings or a gift to yourself.

The four ones came before the overflowing shopping cart.
02

Then shopping joined the party

In 2009, Alibaba turned Singles’ Day into an online shopping festival, encouraging people to buy themselves presents. The company’s own historical factsheet records that shift. [2]

The invitation was clever: you need not wait for a romantic partner to give you something nice. A celebration of single life had gained a commercial engine.

Prof. BoltWait until you meet the recommendations.

03

India had a memorable date too: 12/12/12

India’s connection is more interesting than simply enjoying a bargain. On 12 December 2012, Google organised the Great Online Shopping Festival with ecommerce partners: a 24-hour sale built around another memorable date. [3]

Its goal included encouraging first-time online buyers. In November 2015, Google announced the festival’s retirement, pointing to the growth of retailers’ own deal days. [4]

CHINA · 11/11

A celebration of single life became a commercial shopping occasion.

INDIA · 12/12/12

A coordinated online sale aimed to introduce more people to shopping on the web.

Flipkart’s first Big Billion Day followed on 6 October 2014, according to its own retrospective. [5] These are different histories, rather than one shared national festival.

The comparison reveals two jobs a shopping event can do: create an occasion for existing shoppers and give newcomers a reason to try. It can also turn an ordinary purchase into something people anticipate together.

Our fictional Zappy needs no introduction to online shopping. He opens a festive sale looking for headphones. A speaker soon joins his cart.

05

The day that outgrew its date

Here is the surprising part: a festival named after one date need not last one day.

Alibaba’s 2017 factsheet already described a 24-day shopping and entertainment festival. That is a historical description, not the timetable for this year. [2]

11.11

A memorable symbol and annual focal point.

THE CAMPAIGN

24days in Alibaba’s 2017 description

The activity spread beyond the date itself.

There was a business reason to get people involved early. The same document says encouraging shoppers to fill their carts before November 11 helped brands plan inventory—the goods they expected to need. [2]

Adding something to a cart is not a completed purchase. But it can give a seller an early clue about demand. The shopper sees a wish list; the retailer sees information that may help it prepare.

That gives our China–India comparison another layer. A sale is a visible event on the phone, supported by less visible decisions about which products to stock and how much to have ready.

ZappySo my cart can be useful before I pay?

Prof. BoltA wish list to you. A clue to the warehouse.

06

When the shop becomes a show

Another part of the Chinese festival story is livestream shopping: a host demonstrates products on live video while viewers can ask questions and buy through the platform.

Alibaba’s official 2023 festival results describe merchant-run livestream channels as part of its sales activity. That means the seller itself can be the broadcaster; it is not only about celebrity hosts. [6]

Imagine watching someone open a suitcase, roll it across the floor and show its compartments. A photograph shows its appearance. A demonstration can show how it works. Questions, product explanation and the purchase button now share one screen.

ZappyIs this a programme or a shop?

Prof. BoltBoth. The counter has acquired a camera.

07

A fast website cannot pack a parcel

Checkout is only the beginning of the physical job. The correct item must be found, packed, labelled, sorted, transported and handed over. A sudden rush of orders tests every step.

Flipkart’s account of preparing for its 2015 sale makes a telling distinction: handling heavy website traffic was not enough without the capacity to pack and deliver. Its teams also had to match delivery promises to what the fulfilment network could actually handle. [5]

This connects back to those early shopping carts. Hints about demand can help a retailer prepare stock; enough people, equipment and transport are then needed to move it. An online shopping festival is also a large exercise in coordinating physical work.

08

Four ones—and plenty of company

1   1   1   1A simple date with a surprisingly busy second life.

The four ones put single people at the centre of the day. Shopping gave the occasion a wider invitation: a gift to yourself did not require a romantic partner.

That is the curious journey behind 11.11. A symbol of standing alone became the centre of a bustling shopping festival, complete with shows, stock planning and delivery networks. Along the way, the shopping cart became both a place to collect purchases and a source of clues about demand.
