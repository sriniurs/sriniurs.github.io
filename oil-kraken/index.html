The Oil Kraken: Hormuz and Your Wallet

A comic about a 33-km strip of sea that decides what you pay for petrol, vegetables and your EMI. Oil just hit its highest level since April, as tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz had their worst month yet. A curious young learner asks, and a wise mentor who has lived through four oil shocks answers.

EP 2

Shocks that shaped India

1973 Arab oil embargo. Oil prices roughly quadruple in months.

1990–91 Gulf War. Oil spikes, India's foreign reserves nearly run out, and gold is shipped abroad to borrow money. That crisis forces the 1991 reforms.

2008 Oil touches about $147 a barrel, then crashes with the global meltdown.

DÉJÀ VU!

Prof. BoltIn 1991 I saw India almost go bankrupt because of an oil bill. Every time, the lesson was the same: don't depend on one road for your fuel. Every time, we half-forgot it.

2022 Russia–Ukraine war. Europe stops buying Russian oil; India buys it at a discount.

<1%→~38%

EP 3

The price freeze

From April 2022 Government oil companies stop changing petrol and diesel prices daily.

Oil went up and down; your pump price stayed almost the same for four years. The oil companies quietly absorbed the losses and gains.

ZappySo petrol never got costlier? Nice!

Prof. BoltNothing is free. When the companies lose money, you pay later through taxes, dividends the government doesn't get, or a sudden big hike. That bill arrived in 2026.

THE PRESENT2026: the biggest oil shock in history

EP 4

28 February 2026: the gate slams shut

The trigger US–Israeli strikes on Iran bring tanker traffic through Hormuz to a near halt.

$72→~$100

Brent crude, within days. It went well above $120 at the peak in February–March.

A roller-coaster year

Feb–Mar: $120+→June ceasefire: below $100→Late Aug: fighting resumes→Oct: worst month for ship attacks
BOOM!
$116.54

Brent on 30 October, after another tanker was hit with explosives. Oil was heading for a fourth straight monthly gain.

EP 6

The domino in your wallet

Oil up→More dollars needed→Rupee falls (record ~₹95.6/$)→Imports costlier→Trucks, flights, fertiliser, plastics costlier→Prices up→RBI raises rates → EMIs up

Wholesale inflation hit 9.68% in May, with fuel and power up about 30%. On 8 October the RBI raised its repo rate by 0.25% to 5.5%.

Rule of thumb (MUFG estimate)

Every $10 rise in oil cuts India's GDP growth by about 0.1–0.2% and adds about 0.2% to inflation.

Karnataka's role

Two of India's three emergency oil caverns are on the Karnataka coast: Mangaluru (1.5 MMT) and Padur near Udupi (2.5 MMT). The third is Visakhapatnam.

In July, ONGC said it will build another 1.75 MMT reserve at Mangaluru.

India's emergency cushion is barely one-ninth of the global benchmark. Economists, including Montek Singh Ahluwalia, call this the biggest lesson of 2026.

EP 8

How India survived (so far)

Buy from everywhere

27→40+

Countries India buys crude from, a decade ago vs 2026. Oil not passing through Hormuz rose from 55% to about 70% of supply.

The world chipped in

  • The IEA released a record ~400 million barrels from reserves.
  • Saudi Arabia and the UAE piped oil around Hormuz.
  • The US exported record amounts.

Prof. BoltIndia bent, but it didn't break. The RBI spent about $46 billion of reserves smoothing the rupee. That's a cushion we can't use twice.

THE FUTUREWhat changes, and Prof. Bolt's guesses

EP 9

Lessons India is (finally) learning

1. Bigger tanks

More strategic reserves under Indian control, like the new Mangaluru cavern. The gap to 90 days is huge and will take years.

2. More roads

Keep spreading purchases across the US, Africa, Latin America and Russia, so no single strait can choke us.

3. Less oil, full stop

Every electric scooter, solar rooftop, metro line and ethanol blend is one less barrel to import. Energy independence starts at home. (Prof. Bolt's view)

EP 10

Who wins, who loses

KA-CHING!

Winners when oil stays high: oil producers like ONGC, EV and solar makers, railways and metros, anyone selling energy-saving tech.

OUCH!

Losers: airlines, paint and tyre makers (oil is their raw material), fuel retailers stuck with frozen prices, and every household on a tight budget.

This isn't a tip sheet. It just shows why some stocks rise and others fall every time a tanker is hit.

EP 11

2027: three endings

PHEW!

The good ending. A lasting US–Iran deal, ships return, Brent drops toward $80–90. Inflation eases, the RBI cuts rates, and India keeps building reserves anyway.

HMM…

The likely ending. On-and-off attacks, oil stuck around $100–120. Fuel prices creep up in small steps, EMIs stay high, and growth slows a little but holds.

CRASH!

The bad ending. Full closure for months, oil far above $130, real fuel shortages, the rupee much weaker, and India's growth sharply hit.

Prof. BoltThese are my guesses, not forecasts. Nobody, not even oil traders, knows how a war ends. Plan for the middle, prepare for the worst.

EP 12

So what should Zappy do?

At home

  • Budget for higher fuel and grocery bills for at least a year.
  • Next vehicle: seriously price an EV.
  • Floating-rate loan? Expect EMIs to stay high; prepay if you can.

With money

  • Keep a bigger emergency fund. Shocks travel fast.
  • Some gold and some overseas exposure help when the rupee falls.
  • Don't trade on tanker headlines. They flip overnight.

ZappyAny career angle in this?

Prof. BoltEnergy is hiring: EV charging, solar, smart grids, shipping and logistics. Every crisis creates a new industry.
