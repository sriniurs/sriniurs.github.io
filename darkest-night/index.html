Why the Festival of Lights Falls on the Darkest Night

Zappy has lamps, oil and a box of crackers ready, and one question: why does India pick the one night with no moon at all? Prof. Bolt points at the empty sky.

TODAY IS SPECIAL!It's Deepavali! This year the South's dawn oil bath and the North's Lakshmi Puja at dusk both land on the same Sunday 🪔 Today we find out why that doesn't always happen.

PART 1: HOW THE MOON PICKS THE DATENo fixed date, no mistake

EP 1

Why Deepavali keeps moving

ZappyChristmas is always 25 December. Why can't Deepavali just pick a date and stay there?

Prof. BoltBecause Deepavali doesn't follow the sun's calendar. It follows the moon. And the moon keeps its own time.

The moon's clock

From one new moon to the next takes about 29.5 days. Twelve of those make a "moon year" of about 354 days.

The sun's year is about 365 days. So the moon falls behind by about 11 days every year.

EP 2

Amavasya: the night the moon hides

How it works

The moon has no light of its own. It shines by reflecting sunlight.

On Amavasya (new moon), the moon sits on the same side of the Earth as the sun. Its lit half faces away from us, and it rises and sets with the sun. So it's up in the daytime, lost in the glare, and the night sky has no moon at all.

The lunar "day"

The Hindu calendar splits the moon's month into 30 tithis (lunar days): 15 as the moon grows, 15 as it shrinks. Amavasya is the 30th, the last day of the dark half.

A tithi isn't a sunrise-to-sunrise day. It can start at any hour, even mid-morning.

WAIT, REALLY?

This year Amavasya begins around 11 am on Sunday and ends around 11:30 am on Monday (Indian time).

The moment the moon is truly "new" comes at that end point, Monday morning, in broad daylight. Sunday night is the dark night closest to it.

Tithi timings: Hindu Blog panchanga for 8 Nov 2026; other panchangas differ by under an hour.

Lux measures how much light falls on a surface. A living room is about 50 lux. Figures for a clear sky: Wikipedia's Daylight table. Bars not to scale; the real gap is far bigger.

100 TIMES DARKER

A full moon lights the ground about 125 times more than a moonless, starlit sky (0.25 ÷ 0.002, our arithmetic).

Before electricity, that was the difference between walking home and getting lost. A moonless night was the scariest night of the month.

MYTH / FACT

MYTH: Deepavali is the darkest night of the whole year.

FACT: Every Amavasya is equally moonless. What makes this one special is the season: the monsoon has gone, skies are clear, and nights are growing longer as winter comes.

Verdict: it's the darkest night of the festival season, not of the year.

EP 4

Dawn or dusk? The rule that splits India

Two clocks, two rules

Most of India celebrates when Amavasya is running just after sunset (called pradosha). That's when Lakshmi Puja is done and lamps are lit.

Tamil Nadu and Karnataka celebrate when the day before, Chaturdashi (the 14th tithi), is running just before sunrise (Brahma muhurta). That's the oil bath, new clothes and dawn crackers.

Usually: South goes a day early

Because the South's moment comes in the morning and the North's in the evening, they usually fall on different days, with the South a day ahead.

So in many years, Chennai has finished its crackers by the time Delhi starts.

This year: both on Sunday

🌅 Sun dawn: still Chaturdashi → oil bath→☀️ ~11 am: Amavasya begins→🪔 Sun dusk: Amavasya → Lakshmi Puja

Chaturdashi lasts till late Sunday morning, and Amavasya covers Sunday evening. So one Sunday holds both.

EP 5

Same night, two month names

ZappyMy friend in Delhi says Deepavali is in Kartik. Ajji says it's in Ashvayuja. Who's wrong?

Prof. BoltNeither. They're looking at the same night and just disagree on where the month ends.

Where does a month end?

  • Amanta (Karnataka, Andhra, Telangana, Maharashtra, Gujarat): the month ends on the new moon. Deepavali is the last night of Ashvayuja.
  • Purnimanta (most of North India): the month ends on the full moon. The same night is already in Kartik.
SO THAT'S WHY

In Karnataka, Karthika masa, the month of lamps, starts the day after Deepavali (about 9 Nov this year) and runs to about 8 Dec.

In the North, Kartik ran from about 27 Oct and ends on 24 Nov, Kartik Purnima.

EP 7

Lamps for the ones who came before

Bengal's 14 lamps

On the night before Kali Puja, called Bhoot Chaturdashi, Bengali homes light 14 small lamps in the dark corners of the house.

The belief: 14 generations of ancestors visit that night, and the lamps guide them home and keep unwanted spirits away. Families also eat a dish of 14 leafy greens.

INDIA'S HALLOWEEN?

People sometimes call it Bengal's Halloween. But where Halloween is about scaring, this is about welcoming family back for one night.

The month of ancestors

The new moon before this one, Mahalaya Amavasya (10 Oct this year), ends Pitru Paksha, the fortnight of offerings to ancestors. Some families believe the Deepavali lamps light the ancestors' path back.

A belief that varies by family and region, not one single rule.

EP 8

The farmer's reason

The granary is full

Deepavali comes just after the kharif (monsoon-season) harvest: rice, maize, cotton and pulses are in. Many historians think it began as a harvest festival.

Full stores, cash in hand, new grain: no wonder it's the night to thank Lakshmi, goddess of wealth.

A new year, for some

In Gujarat, the new year begins right after Deepavali. In the North, Deepavali marks the start of a new Vikram Samvat year for many traders, who open fresh account books.

The cow's turn

The day after the new moon is Balipadyami in Karnataka. Farm families bathe their cows, decorate them with turmeric, kumkum and flowers, and feed them rice and jaggery (Gopuja).

THE DEEP DIVEThe research behind the festival

EP 9

The maths: how India stops Deepavali drifting into summer

The 11-day problem

  • One moon month: 29.53 days
  • 12 moon months: 354.37 days
  • One sun year: 365.25 days
  • Gap: about 10.9 days a year

Left alone, after 9 years Deepavali would be about 99 days early, around the monsoon. After about 33 years it would go right round the calendar.

The fix: an extra month

About every 32–33 months, the gap adds up to one whole moon month. So the calendar adds a 13th month, called Adhika masa (the extra month).

That's the "leap forward" you saw in EP 1: 17 Oct in 2028, then 5 Nov in 2029.

WAIT, REALLY?

2026 is one of those years. An extra Jyeshtha month ran in June, so this year has 13 new moons on the panchanga instead of 12.

Adhika Jyeshtha Amavasya: 15–16 June 2026 (SmartPuja Amavasya list). Islamic months add no extra month, which is why Ramzan moves through every season.

COUSINS

Chinese New Year also falls on a new moon, and also moves by weeks each year, for the same reason. Two of Asia's biggest festivals are timed by the dark moon.

Why the moon at all?

Before printed calendars, the moon was the one clock everyone could read for free. A farmer anywhere could look up and know how many nights were left to Amavasya.

EP 12

What NASA says about "Diwali from space"

MYTH

Every year a glowing, colourful map of India goes viral as "NASA's photo of India on Diwali night".

FACT

NASA's Earth Observatory says that picture does not show Diwali. It's a 2003 image made by a US government (NOAA) scientist, with colours marking which city lights appeared in 1992, 1998 and 2003: a map of population growth.

NASA's own satellite view on Diwali 2012 looks like any other night. It says the extra light is so subtle it's likely impossible to see from space.

BUT...

In 2020, a UK satellite-data firm compared the same sensor's images of Ayodhya a week apart. On the night of the record diya-lighting at the river ghats, the brightness did show up.

One city, one record-breaking night, not the whole country. Source: Pixalytics blog (Nov 2020), NOAA data.

EP 13

The Karnataka angle: Deepavali, our way

How we do it

💧 Neeru tumbuva habba: scrub and fill the water pots→🛁 Naraka Chaturdashi: oil bath before dawn→🪔 Amavasya: Lakshmi Puje→👑 Balipadyami: King Bali visits

Morning greeting on the big day: "Ganga snana aytha?" (Had your Ganga bath?). The belief: before sunrise, all water counts as Ganga water, even a bucket.

The king who comes back

On Balipadyami, the generous demon-king Bali Chakravarthi is believed to rise from the underworld for one day, a boon from Vishnu as Vamana. Kerala welcomes the same king at Onam.

The story was carved in stone at Badami, in Cave 2 and Cave 3, back in the 6th–7th century.

ONLY IN KARNATAKA
  • Many homes call it the festival of sons-in-law: the aliya is invited, gifted and fed royally (Star of Mysore).
  • In Gummatapura, Deepavali ends with Gorehabba, a village cow-dung fight.
  • In the Malnad, herb water is filled for the oil bath, and singing groups go house to house (antige pintige).
EP 14

OPINION: Prof. Bolt's verdict

GENIUS

The date isn't random. It's an ancient piece of astronomy. A calendar that tracks the moon, corrects itself against the sun every 2–3 years, and has kept a festival in the same season for over a thousand years.

WE'RE MISSING THE POINT

The festival is about light beating darkness, but most of us never see the darkness any more. City lights drown the moonless sky. We light lamps against a sky that's already orange.

MY TAKE

Notice the dark before you light the lamp. The festival works best when you first see how dark a no-moon night really is. That's the night our ancestors saw.

Sunday dawn
