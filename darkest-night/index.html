Why the Festival of Lights Falls on the Darkest Night

Zappy has lamps, oil and a box of crackers ready, and one question: why does India pick the one night with no moon at all? Prof. Bolt points at the empty sky.

TODAY IS SPECIAL!It's Deepavali! This year the South's dawn oil bath and the North's Lakshmi Puja at dusk both land on the same Sunday 🪔 Today we find out why that doesn't always happen.

PART 1: HOW THE MOON PICKS THE DATENo fixed date, no mistake

EP 1

Why Deepavali keeps moving

ZappyChristmas is always 25 December. Why can't Deepavali just pick a date and stay there?

Prof. BoltBecause Deepavali follows the moon, not the sun. And the moon has never once checked a wall calendar.

The moon's clock

New moon to new moon: about 29.5 days. Twelve of those make a "moon year" of about 354 days.

The sun's year is about 365 days. So the moon falls behind by about 11 days every year.

EP 2

Amavasya: the night the moon hides

How it works

The moon has no light of its own; it reflects sunlight. On Amavasya (new moon) it sits on the same side of the Earth as the sun, lit side facing away. It's up in the daytime, lost in the glare, and the night sky gets no moon at all.

125 TIMES DARKER

A full moon lights the ground about 125 times more than a moonless, starlit sky (0.25 vs 0.002 lux, our arithmetic).

ZappySo before electricity…

Prof. BoltA moonless night meant walking into a buffalo. The scariest night of the month, every month.

MYTH / FACT

MYTH: Deepavali is the darkest night of the year.

FACT: Every Amavasya is equally moonless. This one is special because of the season: monsoon gone, skies clear, nights getting longer.

Fun twist: this year the moon is truly "new" around 11:30 am on Monday, in broad daylight. Sunday night is just the dark night closest to it.

EP 3

Dawn or dusk? The rule that splits India

Two clocks, two rules

Most of India celebrates when Amavasya is running just after sunset: Lakshmi Puja and lamps.

Karnataka and Tamil Nadu celebrate when the day before, Chaturdashi, is running just before sunrise: oil bath, new clothes, dawn crackers.

So in most years the South goes a day early. Chennai has finished its crackers before Delhi lights its first one.

This year: both on Sunday

🌅 Sun dawn: still Chaturdashi → oil bath→☀️ ~11 am: Amavasya begins→🪔 Sun dusk: Amavasya → Lakshmi Puja

One Sunday holds both. North and South, same day, for once.

ZappyMy Delhi friend says Deepavali is in Kartik. Ajji says Ashvayuja. Who's wrong?

Prof. BoltNeither. In Karnataka the month ends on the new moon; in the North it ends on the full moon. Same night, two month names. Don't tell Ajji I said she's only half right.

The night before Kali Puja, Bengali homes light 14 lamps in dark corners, one for each of 14 generations of ancestors believed to visit. Halloween scares the dead away; Bengal sets the table for them.

EP 5

The maths that stops Deepavali drifting into summer

The 11-day problem

Moon year 354.37 days, sun year 365.25: a gap of about 10.9 days a year.

Left alone, in about 9 years Deepavali would land in the monsoon. In about 33 years it would go right round the calendar.

The fix: a bonus month

About every 32–33 months the calendar adds a 13th month, Adhika masa. That's the 2029 leap from EP 1.

WAIT, REALLY?

2026 is one of those years. An extra Jyeshtha ran in June, so this year has 13 new moons.

ZappyA whole extra month? Do we get an extra salary?

Prof. BoltAsk your boss. The calendar has done its part.

The Islamic calendar adds no extra month, which is why Ramzan moves through every season.

EP 6

That "NASA Diwali photo" in your family group

MYTH

Every year a glowing, colourful map of India goes viral as "NASA's photo of India on Diwali night".

FACT

NASA's Earth Observatory says it does not show Diwali. It's a 2003 map by a US government (NOAA) scientist, coloured to show city lights in 1992, 1998 and 2003: a map of population growth.

NASA says Diwali's extra light is likely impossible to see from space.

BUT...

One exception: in 2020 satellite images of Ayodhya did pick up its record diya-lighting night. One city, not the whole country.

ZappyShould I tell Mama in the family group?

Prof. BoltGently. After he's had his sweets.

EP 7

The Karnataka angle: Deepavali, our way

How we do it

💧 Neeru tumbuva habba: fill the water pots→🛁 Naraka Chaturdashi: oil bath before dawn→🪔 Amavasya: Lakshmi Puje→👑 Balipadyami: King Bali visits

The morning greeting: "Ganga snana aytha?" Before sunrise, the belief goes, all water is Ganga water. Even the bucket.

The king who comes back

On Balipadyami, the generous king Bali is believed to visit for one day, a boon from Vishnu as Vamana. Kerala welcomes the same king at Onam. The story is carved at Badami caves, 6th–7th century.

ONLY IN KARNATAKA
  • Many homes call it the festival of sons-in-law: the aliya is gifted and fed royally.
  • In Gummatapura, it ends with Gorehabba, a village cow-dung fight.

ZappyCan I be an aliya next year?

Prof. BoltFirst become a son-in-law. Then dodge the cow dung.

EP 8

OPINION: Prof. Bolt's verdict, and what Zappy should do

GENIUS

The date is ancient astronomy, not chance. A calendar that tracks the moon, corrects itself against the sun every 2–3 years, and has kept one festival in the same season for over a thousand years.

MY TAKE

We celebrate light beating darkness, but we never see the darkness any more. City lights have turned the moonless sky orange. Notice the dark first; then light the lamp.

So what should Zappy do?

  • 🛁 Oil bath before sunrise: it's still Chaturdashi.
  • 🌑 Before the lamps, switch off the lights for 5 minutes. Look up. No moon.
  • 🚫 Don't forward the "NASA Diwali photo".

ZappySo the darkness is part of the festival?

Prof. BoltIt's the whole reason for it. Happy Deepavali.
