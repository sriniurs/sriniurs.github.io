THE LAST WORD: MAṄGALAM

So far, the Suprabhātam woke the Lord, the Stotram praised Him, and the Prapatti surrendered at His feet.

Part 5 is the fourth and last section, the Maṅgalāśāsanam: fourteen short verses in which the devotee blesses the Lord. Almost every verse ends with one word: maṅgalam, "may all be well with You".

It is the natural end of love. Having woken Him, praised Him and given ourselves to Him, we can only wish Him well.

Govindā Govindā,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: We bless God? Isn't it the other way round?

PROF. BOLT: Usually! But love wishes the beloved well, even when He lacks nothing. That's maṅgalāśāsanam.

THE SHAPE OF THE MAṄGALĀŚĀSANAM

Blessing the Lord in four movements
Verses 1–4: His beauty. Beloved of Śrī, eyes for Lakṣmī and the world, feet that crown the hill, beauty that enchants every heart.
Verses 5–8: His nature. Eternal, the inner Self of all; all-knowing yet easy to reach; the Supreme Brahman; a nectar no one tires of.
Verses 9–12: His grace. His hand points to His feet; His glance cools the world; He adorns His ornaments; He chose Tirumala over Vaikuṇṭha.
Verses 13–14: the teachers. He lives in my teacher's heart; I bless Him as all our teachers have.

🪔 Tiruppallāṇḍu

The model is Periyāḻvār's Tamil hymn wishing the Lord "many, many years": the first Śrīvaiṣṇava maṅgalāśāsanam.

🎵 The sound

All 14 verses in simple anuṣṭubh, easy to sing together, like a closing chorus.

ZAPPY: Why end with a blessing and not a request?

PROF. BOLT: The Prapatti already asked for everything. What's left is pure love: may all be well with You.

VERSE 1 · GOING DEEPER

MAṄGALAM TO ŚRĪNIVĀSA

Blessing the Lord. The last section turns everything around: instead of asking the Lord to bless us, the devotee blesses the Lord. Maṅgalāśāsana means "wishing auspiciousness" on Him.

Out of love. The Lord needs nothing; but love always wishes the beloved well. The model is Periyāḻvār's Tamil Tiruppallāṇḍu, "many years to You!", sung at the start of Śrīvaiṣṇava worship.

Short verses. All 14 verses are in the simple anuṣṭubh metre, and nearly all end maṅgalam: easy to sing together, like a closing chorus.

💡 Maṅgalāśāsana

Wishing auspiciousness to the Lord, out of love.

💡 Tiruppallāṇḍu

Periyāḻvār's Tamil hymn wishing the Lord "many, many years".

Love does not only ask; it blesses.

ZAPPY: We bless God?

PROF. BOLT: Out of love, yes! Like a mother saying "may you be well" to a grown child. He needs nothing; we wish it anyway.

"Maṅgalam": the devotee blesses the Lord.

VERSE 2 · GOING DEEPER

EYES FOR LAKṢMĪ, EYES FOR THE WORLD

Two kinds of seeing. The same eyes that exchange loving glances with Lakṣmī watch over every world. Private love and universal care are one.

"Eye of the worlds". Cakṣuḥ is a name for the sun, which lets all beings see. The Lord is the light by which the worlds see, and the eye that sees them.

Wordplay. Cakṣuṣe … cakṣuṣe: the word ending line 2 opens line 3, linking the verse like a chain.

💡 Vibhrama

Playful grace, especially of a lover's glance.

💡 Loka-cakṣuḥ

"Eye of the world": a name of the sun, used here for the Lord.

The love we give one can be the love we give all.

ZAPPY: He's looking at Lakṣmī and the world?

PROF. BOLT: Both! The same eyes that love Her watch over every one of us.

Eyes for Lakṣmī, an eye for the whole world.

VERSE 3 · GOING DEEPER

HOME OF ALL AUSPICIOUSNESS

Blessing the source of blessing. A charming paradox: we wish maṅgalam to the very one who is the home of all maṅgala.

Two "nivāsas". Maṅgalānāṃ nivāsāya śrīnivāsāya: "to the dwelling of auspiciousness, to the dwelling of Śrī". Lakṣmī and auspiciousness are one.

Echoes. The feet as the hill's ornament recall Prapatti verse 13.

💡 Maṅgala

Auspiciousness, well-being, blessing.

💡 Aṅghri

A foot: another word for the Lord's feet.

Wish well even to the one who has everything. Love gives anyway.

ZAPPY: Blessing the source of all blessings?

PROF. BOLT: Funny, isn't it? But that's love: we wish Him well even though all good things live in Him.

Maṅgalam to the home of all auspiciousness.

VERSE 4 · GOING DEEPER

BEAUTY THAT ENCHANTS EVERY HEART

Head to foot. The Stotram and Prapatti described Him part by part: eyes, chest, feet. This verse sums up: every limb is beautiful.

Enchanting. Sammohana: He captivates every mind, as Kṛṣṇa captivated the gopīs (Stotram verse 5).

Always. Sadā: not once, not sometimes; His beauty never fades (Prapatti verse 3, "ever new").

💡 Saundarya

Beauty: one of the Lord's bodily glories.

💡 Sammohana

Enchanting, captivating the mind.

True beauty draws the heart toward goodness.

ZAPPY: Every heart?

PROF. BOLT: Every one! His beauty draws people from everywhere, again and again.

Beautiful in every limb, enchanting every heart.

VERSE 5 · GOING DEEPER

THE ETERNAL, INNER SELF OF ALL

The philosophy. Verses 5–8 turn from beauty to theology. This one gives the Upaniṣadic definition of Brahman: truth, consciousness, bliss.

Antaryāmin. Sarva-antarātman, "inner Self of all": in Rāmānuja's Viśiṣṭādvaita, the Lord dwells in every soul and every thing as its inner controller, and the world is like His body.

On the hill and in the heart. The Lord standing on Veṅkaṭa is the same Lord within each of us.

💡 Antaryāmin

"The inner controller": the Lord dwelling within all.

💡 Viśiṣṭādvaita

Rāmānuja's philosophy: one Reality, with souls and world as its body.

The Lord you visit on the hill is already within you.

ZAPPY: He's inside everyone?

PROF. BOLT: As the inner Self of all, says Rāmānuja's philosophy. On the hill and in every heart.

Truth, bliss, consciousness: the inner Self of all.

VERSE 6 · GOING DEEPER

GREAT, AND YET EASY TO REACH

Greatness and nearness. The first three qualities show how great He is; the last two show how near. Śrīvaiṣṇavas cherish this pairing: paratva (supremacy) with saulabhya (accessibility).

Echo of Prapatti verse 2. The same qualities answered our doubts before surrender; now they are celebrated.

Suśīla. One who, though great, mixes freely with the lowly, as Rāma embraced Guha the boatman.

💡 Paratva · Saulabhya

Supremacy and accessibility: the two sides of the Lord.

💡 Guha

The boatman-chief whom Rāma embraced as a friend.

The greatest are those who stay within reach.

ZAPPY: All-powerful and easy to reach?

PROF. BOLT: That's the wonder. Too great to describe, yet as close as a friend.

Knowing all, master of all, and still easy to reach.

VERSE 7 · GOING DEEPER

TO THE SUPREME BRAHMAN

"I offer". Here, for once, the poet speaks in the first person: prayuñje, "I offer". He makes his wish personal.

He lacks nothing. Pūrṇa-kāma: all His desires are fulfilled. So why wish Him well? Because love does, regardless. That is the whole spirit of maṅgalāśāsana.

The Brahman on a hill. The Upaniṣads' Supreme Brahman is the same Lord who stands, in a form we can see, on Veṅkaṭa.

💡 Brahman

The Supreme Reality of the Upaniṣads.

💡 Arcā

The Lord's form in a temple image: His most accessible form.

Give to the one who lacks nothing. That is pure love.

ZAPPY: Why bless someone who has everything?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly the point! He lacks nothing; we wish Him well purely out of love.

"Prayuñje": I offer my blessing to the Supreme.

VERSE 8 · GOING DEEPER

NECTAR THAT NEVER SATISFIES

Never enough. Most pleasures fade with repetition. This one grows: the more you look, the more you want to look. Atṛpti, "no satiety", is here the highest praise.

The liberated souls. Śrīvaiṣṇava teaching says the freed souls in Vaikuṇṭha enjoy the Lord endlessly. This verse brings that bliss to Tirumala.

Echo. Prapatti verse 3: "ever enjoyed, yet always new."

💡 Anubhava

Experiencing, enjoying the Lord: a key Śrīvaiṣṇava idea.

💡 Nitya-sūri

The eternally free souls who serve the Lord in Vaikuṇṭha.

Some joys grow the more you have them. Seek those.

ZAPPY: Doesn't anyone get tired of looking?

PROF. BOLT: Never! It's nectar you can never have enough of. That's why devotees return again and again.

A nectar you can never have enough of.

VERSE 9 · GOING DEEPER

HIS HAND POINTS TO HIS FEET

The famous gesture. As in Prapatti verses 10–11: the lower right hand of the image at Tirumala points down to the feet. Tradition reads it as the Lord's silent teaching: "surrender here".

Out of mercy. Kṛpayā: He shows the way not because we deserve it, but because He is kind.

For everyone. Puṃsām, "to people": all people, without conditions.

💡 Varada-hasta

A hand gesture of granting; at Tirumala it points to the feet.

💡 Kaṭi-hasta

The left hand on the hip, read as "saṃsāra is only waist-deep".

The answer is shown to everyone. We only need to look where He points.

ZAPPY: That hand again!

PROF. BOLT: It's the heart of the whole hymn: He points to His feet and says "come here". The Prapatti, the Gītā, and now the blessing.

His hand points to His feet, our refuge.

VERSE 10 · GOING DEEPER

A GLANCE LIKE COOL WAVES

Cooling. In a hot land, coolness is the deepest comfort. The Lord's glance cools the heat of worldly sorrow.

A sidelong look. Apāṅga, the corner of the eye. Even a passing glance from Him is a wave of mercy: compare the teacher's glance in Prapatti verse 15.

The whole world. Viśvam: no one is left dry.

💡 Kaṭākṣa

The Lord's sidelong glance of grace.

💡 Dayā

Compassion: Vedānta Deśika wrote a hundred verses on Veṅkaṭeśa's dayā (Dayā-śatakam).

A kind look is a gift. Give it freely.

ZAPPY: Just a glance?

PROF. BOLT: Even a glance from Him is a cool wave of mercy, and it reaches the whole world.

His glance: cool waves of mercy over the world.

VERSE 11 · GOING DEEPER

HE ADORNS HIS ORNAMENTS

The reverse of usual. We wear ornaments to look better. The Lord makes the ornaments look better. His beauty flows into them.

Weapons too. Conch and discus are beautiful on Him: power in His hands is never ugly, because it protects.

Calming sorrow. Sarvārti-śamana: the sight of Him soothes every pain. Pilgrims often speak of this.

💡 Alaṅkāra

Adornment of the deity, a major part of temple worship.

💡 Āyudha

The Lord's weapons: conch, discus, mace, bow, sword.

A good person makes their surroundings better, not the other way round.

ZAPPY: He makes jewels look good?

PROF. BOLT: Normally jewels make us look good. With Him, it's the other way around!

He adorns His ornaments, and calms every sorrow.

VERSE 12 · GOING DEEPER

HE PREFERRED TIRUMALA TO VAIKUṆṬHA

Earth over heaven. In Suprabhātam verse 20, people preferred earth after seeing His tower. Now the Lord Himself prefers Tirumala to Vaikuṇṭha!

The legend. The Purāṇic story says Viṣṇu came down to the Veṅkaṭa hill and settled by the Svāmi Puṣkariṇī. Tirumala is called Bhū-Vaikuṇṭha, "Vaikuṇṭha on earth".

Ramayā ramamāṇa. A lovely sound-play: "delighting (ramamāṇa) with Ramā". He is never without Her, here as in heaven.

💡 Bhū-Vaikuṇṭha

"Vaikuṇṭha on earth": a name for Tirumala.

💡 Svāmi Puṣkariṇī

The sacred tank beside the temple (Suprabhātam verse 14).

The Lord chose to come close. Meet Him where He came.

ZAPPY: He preferred earth to heaven?

PROF. BOLT: So the verse says! That's why Tirumala is called Vaikuṇṭha on earth.

He left Vaikuṇṭha to delight with Lakṣmī on the Seven Hills.

VERSE 13 · GOING DEEPER

WHO LIVES IN MY TEACHER'S HEART

The teacher again. As in Prapatti verse 15, the poet names his ācārya, here as Sundara Jāmātṛ Muni, another Sanskrit form of Maṇavāḷa Māmuni ("beautiful bridegroom sage").

Two dwellings. The whole world dwells in Him; and He dwells in the heart of a devotee. The infinite fits inside a loving heart.

Three "nivāsas". Śrī-nivāsa, sarva-loka-nivāsa, and the one who dwells in the muni's mind: the Lord is a home, and has a home.

💡 Jāmātṛ

"Son-in-law, bridegroom": Sanskrit for Tamil maṇavāḷan.

💡 Ācārya-bhakti

Devotion to one's teacher, central in Śrīvaiṣṇava practice.

Make your heart a home where the Lord would want to live.

ZAPPY: God lives in the teacher's heart?

PROF. BOLT: The one in whom all worlds live chooses to live in a devotee's heart. What an honour!

All worlds live in Him; He lives in a devotee's heart.

VERSE 14 · GOING DEEPER

AS ALL OUR TEACHERS HAVE BLESSED HIM

Joining a long line. The poet does not bless the Lord alone: he joins a chain of teachers, from Rāmānuja and the Āḻvārs down to his own guru, who all blessed the Lord in the same way.

Guru-paramparā. The lineage of teachers is central to Śrīvaiṣṇava life; every recitation is done "in their footsteps".

The last word. The whole 70-verse hymn ends with maṅgalam: "may all be well." From "kausalyā suprajā rāma" to this blessing, a single morning has become a whole life of devotion.

💡 Guru-paramparā

The unbroken line of teachers handing on the tradition.

💡 Pūrvācāryas

"The earlier teachers": the Āḻvārs, Rāmānuja and their successors.

We pray in the footsteps of all who prayed before us.

ZAPPY: Is that the end of the Suprabhātam?

PROF. BOLT: The very last verse of all seventy! It ends where love always ends: "May all be well with You."

As all our teachers did: maṅgalam to the Lord.

THE WHOLE SUPRABHĀTAM ON ONE PAGE

Four sections, 70 verses, one morning
Part 1 · Suprabhātam 1–15. Viśvāmitra's call to Rāma; good morning to Lakṣmī; sages, gods, parrots, bees and cowherds wake the Lord.
Part 2 · Suprabhātam 16–29. The court gathers at His door: guardians, planets, avatāras, scholars with scented water; the sun rises.
Part 3 · Stotram (11). Praise and plea: protect me; none higher; Kṛṣṇa and Rāma; forgive my faults.
Part 4 · Prapatti (16). Surrender through the Mother at His feet: "śaraṇaṃ prapadye".
Part 5 · Maṅgalāśāsanam (14). Having surrendered, we bless Him: "maṅgalam".

ZAPPY: So it starts with 'wake up' and ends with 'be well'?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly. Wake Him, praise Him, surrender to Him, bless Him: the arc of a devotee's whole life, sung every morning.
