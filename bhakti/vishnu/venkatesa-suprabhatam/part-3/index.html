HOW THIS WORKS: First Part 1 for chanting in one go. Then the story of how the hymn came to be and its background, and two pages for each verse: the chant, a picture, what it says and the key words; then a deeper look, background facts, the inner teaching and the one thing to remember.

NOW THAT HE IS AWAKE

In Parts 1 and 2, the 29 verses of the Suprabhātam woke the Lord: Lakṣmī first, then the sages, the birds, the bees, the gods, the planets, the whole court at the golden door, until the sun was up.

Part 3 is the second section of the hymn, the Stotram: eleven verses of praise. The poet now speaks to the Lord directly. He asks for protection, confesses his faults, sees the Lord as Kṛṣṇa and Rāma, and ends by asking forgiveness.

Govindā Govindā,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is the Stotram part of the Suprabhātam?

PROF. BOLT: It's the second of the four sections, always recited right after. Shorter verses, a quicker beat, and much more personal.

THE SHAPE OF THE STOTRAM

One Lord, seen three ways
VersesWhom he seesWhat he says

4Veṅkaṭeśa, the generousNone higher than Lakṣmī's beloved
5Kṛṣṇa with the gopīsNone higher than Vasudeva's son
6–8Rāma, Sītā's husbandGrant blessings; I worship no other
9–10Veṅkaṭeśa againNo lord but You; grant eternal service

🎵 Three rhythms

Verses 1–8 in toṭaka (da-da-DUM ×4); 9–10 in bhujaṅga-prayāta (da-DUM-DUM ×4); 11 in anuṣṭubh.

🙏 From praise to plea

The Suprabhātam asked nothing; the Stotram asks: protect, save, bless, forgive.

ZAPPY: Why Kṛṣṇa and Rāma in a hymn to Veṅkaṭeśa?

PROF. BOLT: Because they're one Lord. The poet sees the flute-player and the archer standing on the Seven Hills.

VERSE 1 · GOING DEEPER

VICTORY TO THE LORD OF VEṄKAṬA

Now He is awake. The Suprabhātam woke the Lord; the Stotram ("hymn of praise") greets Him awake. The first word is Kamalā, Lakṣmī, just as the Suprabhātam began with the Mother in verse 3.

Blue and red. The Lord's body is dark blue like a rain cloud; the red of kuṅkuma from Lakṣmī's embrace marks His chest. The colour picture says: He is never without Her.

A new rhythm. Verses 1–8 are in toṭaka metre: short, quick lines of four beats, da-da-DUM ×4, like a march.

💡 Toṭaka

A brisk 12-syllable metre; Śaṅkara's disciple Toṭaka is named after it.

💡 Kuṅkuma

Red powder worn by married women, a sign of auspiciousness.

The Lord is never without Lakṣmī; grace is never far from Him.

ZAPPY: Why is the blue Lord red?

PROF. BOLT: Lakṣmī's kuṅkuma! She's always on His chest, so its red always marks Him. Never apart.

"Vijayī bhava": victory to the Lord of Veṅkaṭa.

VERSE 2

O crest-jewel of all the gods, led by four-faced Brahmā, six-faced Kārttikeya and five-faced Śiva; O One who loves those who surrender to You, treasure-house of all that is best: protect me, O Lord of Vṛṣa hill.
You are the jewel on the crowns of all the gods, Brahmā, Kārttikeya and Śiva included; You love all who come to You: please protect me, Lord of the hills.

VERSE 2 · GOING DEEPER

CROWN-JEWEL OF ALL GODS

The first request. In the Suprabhātam the poet asked nothing; here, in verse 2, he asks: paripālaya mām, "protect me". Praise turns personal.

Vatsala. The love of a cow for her calf, which licks it clean without minding the dirt. The Lord loves those who surrender in the same way, faults and all. Vātsalya is one of the qualities Śrīvaiṣṇavas most cherish in the Lord.

Crown-jewel. The other gods bow to Him, so He is the jewel at the top of their crowns.

💡 Vātsalya

A mother cow's love for her calf: love that overlooks faults.

💡 Śaraṇāgata

"One who has come for refuge": the surrendered devotee.

You don't need to be clean to come to Him. A mother cow licks the calf clean.

ZAPPY: What does vatsala mean?

PROF. BOLT: The love of a cow for her calf. She doesn't mind the mud; she licks it off. That's how He loves those who come to Him.

Crown-jewel of the gods, loving to all who seek refuge.

I am weighed down by hundreds of offences, committed again and again, beyond all limits, unbearable to You. Quickly, O Lord of Vṛṣa hill, protect me with Your supreme compassion, O Hari!
I have done wrong hundreds of times, over and over, more than You could bear. Lord of the hills, please save me quickly, out of Your great mercy.

VERSE 3 · GOING DEEPER

SAVE ME, IN SPITE OF MY FAULTS

An honest confession. The poet does not plead innocence. He admits hundreds of offences, again and again. His only argument is the Lord's kṛpā, mercy.

Quickly! Tvaritam: the devotee does not want to wait. Like a child calling its mother, he asks for help now.

Not merit but mercy. This is the Śrīvaiṣṇava view of salvation: we are saved by His grace, not earned by our record. Part 4, the Prapatti, will make this a formal surrender.

💡 Aparādha

An offence, especially against the Lord or His devotees.

💡 Kṛpā

Compassion, mercy: the Lord's grace freely given.

Don't hide your faults from the Lord. Name them, and ask for mercy.

ZAPPY: He admits hundreds of mistakes?

PROF. BOLT: Honestly, yes. He doesn't argue his case; he just asks for mercy, quickly!

Not my merit but His mercy.

VERSE 4 · GOING DEEPER

NONE HIGHER THAN LAKṢMĪ'S BELOVED

A refrain of faith. Verses 4 and 5 both end na paraṃ kalaye, "I see none higher". The poet states his choice of God plainly.

More than asked. The Lord of Tirumala is famous for granting wishes; the verse says He gives more than people want. That generosity is His nature (udāra).

Kamalā-dayita. "Beloved of Lakṣmī": once again He is named through the Goddess.

💡 Nigama

A name for the Vedas: "that which has come down".

💡 Kaliyuga Varada

A title of Veṅkaṭeśvara: the boon-giver of the Kali age.

Ask the Lord, but don't limit Him: He gives more than we know to ask.

ZAPPY: "None higher": isn't that strong?

PROF. BOLT: It's the poet's own choice of God, said with love. And the reason he gives: the Lord gives more than anyone asks.

He gives more than we ask: none higher than Lakṣmī's beloved.

VERSE 5 · GOING DEEPER

NONE HIGHER THAN KṚṢṆA

Kṛṣṇa of Vṛndāvana. The poet now sees the Lord of the hills as Kṛṣṇa, playing His flute among the gopīs. The scene is the rāsa dance of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, where Kṛṣṇa seems present to every gopī at once.

Each one his own. Prati-vallavikā-abhimata: "dear to each cowherd girl". Every devotee feels the Lord is especially theirs.

Same refrain. Like verse 4: "I see none higher". The Lord of Veṅkaṭa and Kṛṣṇa are one.

💡 Gopī

The cowherd women of Vraja, models of selfless love for Kṛṣṇa.

💡 Rāsa-līlā

Kṛṣṇa's moonlit circle dance with the gopīs, told in Bhāgavatam book 10.

To each devotee the Lord is wholly their own, and wholly everyone's.

ZAPPY: Now it's Kṛṣṇa?

PROF. BOLT: The same Lord! The poet sees the flute-player of Vṛndāvana standing on the Seven Hills.

The flute-player of Vṛndāvana: none higher than Kṛṣṇa.
Some texts read prati-pallavikā for prati-vallavikā.

VERSE 6 · GOING DEEPER

O RĀMA, OCEAN OF KINDNESS

Back to Rāma. The Suprabhātam began with Rāma (verse 1). The Stotram now gives three verses (6–8) to Him.

Rameśa. "Lord of Ramā": Ramā is Lakṣmī. Rāma's Sītā is Lakṣmī Herself; so Rāma is again the beloved of Lakṣmī.

Dhīra. Rāma's calm in exile and war is the steadiness the devotee prays for.

💡 Dāśarathi

"Son of Daśaratha": a name of Rāma.

💡 Raghu-vaṃśa

The solar dynasty of Raghu, into which Rāma was born.

Steadiness of mind is a gift worth asking for.

ZAPPY: Why so much Rāma now?

PROF. BOLT: The hymn began with Rāma's morning. Now three verses praise Him: the archer, the steady one, the ocean of kindness.

O Rāma, archer of the world: be my giver of blessings.

VERSE 7 · GOING DEEPER

RĀMA, THE SUN AGAINST RĀVAṆA'S DARKNESS

Sun and moon. A playful pair: Rāma's face is lovely as the moon, yet He is the sun that drives off Rāvaṇa, the king of "night-walkers" (rākṣasas).

Sītā, Earth's daughter. Sītā was found in a furrow by King Janaka; so she is avanī-tanayā, daughter of the Earth.

Holding her hand. The image is of Rāma's wedding, pāṇi-grahaṇa, "taking the hand", and again of Lord and Goddess together.

💡 Pāṇi-grahaṇa

"Taking the hand": the key rite of a Hindu wedding.

💡 Rākṣasa

Night-walking demons of the epics; Rāvaṇa was their king.

Light does not argue with darkness. It simply rises.

ZAPPY: Moon and sun at once?

PROF. BOLT: His face is gentle as the moon for Sītā, and fierce as the sun for Rāvaṇa's darkness. Both are Rāma.

Moon-faced for Sītā, a sun to Rāvaṇa's darkness.

VERSE 8 · GOING DEEPER

NO ONE BUT RĀMA

Eight "su" words. The verse strings together su- ("good") words: good face, good heart, easy to reach, good joy, good brother, good body. A pleasing sound pattern.

Sulabha. "Easy to reach": for Śrīvaiṣṇavas, the Lord's saulabhya, His accessibility, matters as much as His greatness. Rāma befriended a boatman, a vulture, monkeys.

Ekānta bhakti. "I will worship no other": single-minded devotion to one Lord.

💡 Saulabhya

Accessibility: the great Lord is easy to approach.

💡 Lakṣmaṇa

Rāma's devoted younger brother: the "good brother" of this verse.

The greatest is also the easiest to reach. That is His greatness.

ZAPPY: Why "easy to reach"?

PROF. BOLT: Rāma was friends with a boatman, a vulture, a monkey army. Great but never distant: that's what devotees love.

Kind, a friend, easy to reach: no one but Rāma.

VERSE 9 · GOING DEEPER

NO LORD BUT VEṄKAṬEŚA

Back to Veṅkaṭeśa. After Kṛṣṇa and Rāma, the hymn returns to the Lord of the hills, by name, four times in one verse.

Twice over. Every key word is doubled: na nātho na nāthaḥ, smarāmi smarāmi, prasīda prasīda, prayaccha prayaccha. It is the speech of someone pleading with all their heart.

A new metre. Verses 9–10 are in bhujaṅga-prayāta, "the serpent's glide", with a rolling da-DUM-DUM rhythm. This is the best-known verse of the Stotram.

💡 Bhujaṅgaprayāta

"Serpent's movement": a 12-syllable metre of four da-DUM-DUM beats.

💡 Smaraṇa

Remembering the Lord: one of the nine forms of bhakti.

Say His name twice when once is not enough.

ZAPPY: Why say everything twice?

PROF. BOLT: That's how we plead when it matters: please, please! The doubling makes it heartfelt.

"Vinā veṅkaṭeśaṃ na nātho": no lord but Veṅkaṭeśa.

VERSE 10 · GOING DEEPER

ONE VISIT, ETERNAL SERVICE

The pilgrim's prayer. Every devotee who climbs the Seven Hills comes "from afar". This verse is their voice: I came, I bowed once; let that once become forever.

What is asked. Not wealth or health but nitya-sevā, eternal service. The same wish as the devotees of Suprabhātam verse 19.

One for all. One sincere moment of service can carry the fruit of a lifetime: that is the Lord's generosity of verse 4 again.

💡 Sevā

Service to the Lord; also each ritual of worship in the temple.

💡 Yātrā

Pilgrimage: a journey to a holy place.

Make one sincere visit, and ask that it never end.

ZAPPY: He came from far away?

PROF. BOLT: Every pilgrim does! He asks: for this one visit, let me serve You forever.

One visit, one bow: grant me eternal service.

VERSE 11 · GOING DEEPER

FORGIVE ME

Ending with forgiveness. Most Hindu hymns end with a kṣamā-prārthanā, a prayer for forgiveness for any mistakes in worship. The Stotram does the same, in a short anuṣṭubh verse.

Aśeṣa and Śeṣa. A gentle wordplay: forgive my faults aśeṣān, "without remainder", O jewel of the Śeṣa hill.

Said twice. "Kṣamasva tvaṃ, kṣamasva tvaṃ": like verse 9, the plea is doubled.

💡 Kṣamā-prārthanā

The closing prayer of forgiveness for mistakes in worship.

💡 Anuṣṭubh

The 8-syllable-line metre of the epics; also used for verses 1–2 of the Suprabhātam.

End every prayer by asking pardon. We never get it entirely right.

ZAPPY: Why ask forgiveness at the end?

PROF. BOLT: For every mistake, in life and in the prayer itself. It's a humble way to close.

"Kṣamasva": forgive all my faults, O Lord.

PART 3 ON ONE PAGE

The Stotram in five lines
Verses 1–3. Victory to the Lord reddened by Lakṣmī's kuṅkuma; protect me; save me despite my faults.
Verse 4. He gives more than we ask: none higher than Lakṣmī's beloved.
Verse 5. Kṛṣṇa with His flute among the gopīs: none higher than Him.
Verses 6–8. Rāma: archer, Sītā's husband, sun to Rāvaṇa's darkness, easy to reach. I worship no other.
Verses 9–11. No lord but Veṅkaṭeśa; one visit, eternal service; forgive all my faults.

PROF. BOLT: Part 4: the Prapatti, sixteen verses of formal surrender at the Lord's feet. The heart of the Śrīvaiṣṇava way.
