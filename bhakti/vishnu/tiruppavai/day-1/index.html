TIRUPPĀVAI

ĀṆḌĀḶ’S SONGS FOR DHANURMĀSA · PĀSURAM 1
“Mārgaḻit tiṅgaḷ” · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: One song a day, for the 30 days of Dhanurmāsa. Each day: the pāsuram to sing, a picture, what it says, the key words, and one insight to carry through the day. Day 1 also tells you who Āṇḍāḷ was.

FOREWORD

“Mārgaḻit tiṅgaḷ…” In the cold dark before dawn, through the month of Mārgaḻi, these words ring out from temples and homes across the South. Girls draw kōlams at the door; families wake to a song written more than a thousand years ago by a young woman.

She is Āṇḍāḷ, the only woman among the twelve Āḻvārs, the poet-saints of Viṣṇu. Her Tiruppāvai is thirty songs, one for each day of the month. In them she becomes a cowherd girl of Kṛṣṇa’s village, waking her friends to bathe in the river and go to Him together.

This series takes one song a day, from the first day of Dhanurmāsa to Saṅkrānti.

ZAPPY: Is this in Sanskrit?

PROF. BOLT: No: Tamil! The Tiruppāvai is part of the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham, the 4,000 Tamil verses of the Āḻvārs. Tradition calls it “the seed of all the Vedas”.

THE STORY · THE GIRL WHO WORE THE GARLAND

Āṇḍāḷ of Śrīvilliputtūr
The story in 5 frames
Found in the garden. Tradition says Periyāḻvār, a temple gardener-saint of Śrīvilliputtūr, found a baby girl under a tulasī plant and named her Kōdai.
The borrowed garland. Each day she secretly wore the garland made for the Lord, to see if she was fit for Him. When her father found out, the Lord said He wanted only the garlands she had worn.
A new name. So she is called śūḍikkoḍutta śuḍarkkoḍi, “the shining creeper who wore it and then gave it”, and Āṇḍāḷ, “she who rules” the Lord’s heart.
The Mārgaḻi vow. She imagined herself a gopī of Kṛṣṇa’s village keeping the girls’ month-long vow, the pāvai nōṉbu, and sang the 30 songs of the Tiruppāvai.
Union. Tradition holds that she was wedded to Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam and merged into Him.

ZAPPY: She wore God's garland first? Wasn't that wrong?

PROF. BOLT: Her father thought so! But the Lord wanted it that way. Love that holds nothing back was the point.

PĀSURAM 1

THE FULL-MOON MORNING
mārgaḻit tiṅgaḷ madi niṟainda nannāḷālnīrāḍap pōduvīr pōdumiṉō nēriḻaiyīrśīr malgum āyppāḍic celvac ciṟumīrgāḷkūrvēl koḍuntoḻilaṉ nandagōpaṉ kumaraṉērārnda kaṇṇi yaśōdai iḷañciṅgamkārmēṉic ceṅgaṇ kadirmadiyam pōl mugattāṉnārāyaṇaṉē namakkē paṟai taruvāṉpārōr pugaḻap paḍindēlōr empāvāy ‖1‖
It is the month of Mārgaḻi, on the good day of the full moon. Girls in fine jewels, you who wish to bathe, come! Rich young girls of the prosperous cowherd village: Nārāyaṇa, the son of fierce Nandagopa with his sharp spear, the young lion of lovely-eyed Yaśodā, with a body dark as a rain cloud, eyes red as lotus, and a face like the sun and the moon, He alone will give us the drum. Come, let us bathe, so the whole world praises us.
It’s the full-moon morning of Mārgaḻi. Come, friends, let’s go bathe! Kṛṣṇa, Nanda’s son and Yaśodā’s lion cub, who is Nārāyaṇa Himself, will give us what we ask.

🥁 The paṟai

Literally a drum used in the girls’ vow. The teachers read it as the real boon: to be with the Lord and serve Him.

🎵 How it’s sung

In rāga Nāṭṭai, Ādi tāḷa. It is verse 474 of the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham.

GOING DEEPER

Why the first song is about Nārāyaṇa

The best month. Kṛṣṇa says in the Gītā (10.35), “Among months I am Mārgaśīrṣa.” Āṇḍāḷ begins on its full moon: the right time, the right day.

Who gives the drum? The verse builds up a picture of little Kṛṣṇa, Nanda’s boy and Yaśodā’s lion cub, and then turns: nārāyaṇaṉē, “Nārāyaṇa alone”. The cowherd child is the Supreme. Note the double -ē: Nārāyaṇa alone will give, and to us alone. Only He gives; we need only ask Him.

Five forms in five songs. Śrīvaiṣṇava teachers see the first five songs as the Lord’s five forms: here the Supreme (para); then the Lord on the milk ocean, Trivikrama’s strides, the inner Lord as rain, and Kṛṣṇa of Mathurā.

Morning, bath, friends, the Lord: Āṇḍāḷ turns an ordinary dawn into worship.

ZAPPY: What's the one thing to remember today?

PROF. BOLT: Don't go alone! Āṇḍāḷ wakes her friends first. The way to Him is together.
