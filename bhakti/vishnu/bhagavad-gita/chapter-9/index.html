WHERE WE ARE

In Chapter 8 Kṛṣṇa taught that what we remember at death we become, and that the supreme abode is reached by undivided devotion.

Chapter 9 is the most intimate chapter of devotion. Kṛṣṇa calls it the royal secret: easy, direct, and open to all. He pervades everything; he is father, mother and friend; he carries his devotees’ needs; he accepts a leaf or a flower offered with love; and his devotee never perishes.

ŚLOKA 9.1 · DEEP DIVE

THE MOST SECRET KNOWLEDGE

🔎 In context

Chapter 9 opens with Kṛṣṇa calling this the most secret knowledge. Chapter 9 is named Rāja-Vidyā Rāja-Guhya Yoga, the yoga of the royal knowledge and royal secret. Many devotees consider it the heart of the Gītā’s teaching on bhakti; it ends with the verse that returns as 18.65.

📜 Looking deeper

Arjuna is called anasūyu, one without envy or fault-finding: the right listener. Commentators say the “secret” is the intimacy of devotion, revealed only to the open-hearted. Commentators note the steps of secrecy in the Gītā: guhya, secret (18.63); guhyatara, more secret; guhyatama, most secret (9.1, 15.20); sarva-guhyatama, most secret of all (18.64). Śaṅkara says the secret here is knowledge of Brahman. Rāmānuja says it is bhakti, devotion, in which the Lord gives himself. Anasūya, one who does not find fault, is the right listener. Commentators note vijñāna-sahitam, “with realisation”: the teaching is not only to be understood but lived. Madhusūdana says freedom from evil, aśubha, comes by knowing it. Many devotees call chapter 9 the chapter of grace.

🌱 For today

Openness is the key that opens deep teaching. Come without suspicion. Deep things are shared with those who listen openly. Come to teachings without suspicion, and more will open.

ŚLOKA 9.2 · DEEP DIVE

THE ROYAL SECRET

🔎 In context

Kṛṣṇa praises this teaching with seven qualities.

📜 Looking deeper

Note pratyakṣa, directly experienced, and susukha, easy to practise: devotion is not remote theory. The chapter’s name, Rāja Vidyā Rāja Guhya Yoga, comes from this verse. Seven qualities in one verse. Commentators linger on two. Pratyakṣāvagama, directly experienced: unlike heaven promised after death, the fruits of devotion are felt now, as peace and joy. Su-sukha, very easy: one need not be learned, rich or ascetic to love. Śaṅkara says the knowledge is easy because it is the knowledge of one’s own Self. Rāmānuja says bhakti is easy because the Lord himself helps. Some read rāja also as “for kings”: a teaching fit for those in the world. Commentators note dharmyam, “in keeping with dharma”: devotion does not oppose right living. Madhusūdana says the knowledge is avyaya, imperishable, because its fruit never fades. Rājavidyā has given its name to schools and institutions across India.

🌱 For today

The highest teaching is also simple to practise. Don’t assume depth means difficulty. The deepest practices are often the simplest. Love, gratitude and offering are open to anyone today.

ŚLOKA 9.3 · DEEP DIVE

THOSE WITHOUT FAITH

🔎 In context

Without faith, one stays in the cycle.

📜 Looking deeper

Faith (śraddhā) opens the door; its absence keeps it shut. Commentators say faith here means willingness to try. Mṛtyu-saṃsāra-vartman, “the road of death and wandering”, is a stark phrase for the cycle of rebirth. Commentators explain śraddhā here not as blind belief but as willingness to trust and try. Śaṅkara says those without faith remain in the cycle because they do not practise the means. Rāmānuja says they turn away from the easy path offered. Chapter 17 (17.3) will say a person is made of their faith: yo yac-chraddhaḥ sa eva saḥ. Commentators note parantapa, “scorcher of foes”: Kṛṣṇa reminds Arjuna of his strength even when warning. Madhusūdana says lack of faith keeps people circling in rebirth. Faith here is like a door: it must be opened from inside.

🌱 For today

A little trust opens many doors. A little trust is needed to begin anything worthwhile. Give a good path a real try before deciding.

↑ 9.3 · Without faith, the cycle continues.

ŚLOKA 9.4

“By me, in my unmanifest form, this whole world is pervaded. All beings dwell in me, but I do not dwell in them.”
WordMeans

jagat avyakta-mūrtināthe world, by my unmanifest form
mat-sthāni sarva-bhūtāniall beings dwell in me

ŚLOKA 9.4 · DEEP DIVE

PERVADING ALL, YET UNSEEN

🔎 In context

A paradox: God pervades all, yet is not contained by anything.

📜 Looking deeper

Commentators compare it to space: everything is in space, yet space is not limited by the things in it. The next verse deepens the paradox. Verses 4–6 form one of the Gītā’s great paradoxes. Commentators of every school treat them carefully. Śaṅkara: beings exist in the Lord as appearances, but the Lord, being the reality, is not contained in them. Rāmānuja: beings depend on the Lord for existence, but the Lord does not depend on them. He supports them as the soul supports the body. Madhva: the Lord is independent; all else depends on him. The image of space is common: everything exists in space, but space is not bounded by things. Compare 7.12: “they are in me, I am not in them”. Commentators note avyakta-mūrtinā, “by my unmanifest form”: the Lord pervades all without being seen. Madhusūdana says beings exist in the Lord as pots exist in clay. A simple reflection: everything you see rests on something you cannot see.

🌱 For today

The Divine supports everything and is limited by nothing. Everything depends on something larger. Remembering that can ease the burden of feeling we hold it all.

ŚLOKA 9.5 · DEEP DIVE

MY DIVINE YOGA

🔎 In context

The paradox goes further: beings are not even in him.

📜 Looking deeper

Śaṅkara reads it as: beings only appear in God, as a snake appears in a rope; ultimately only God is. Rāmānuja reads: beings depend on him without touching his nature. Kṛṣṇa calls it his divine mystery. Kṛṣṇa now seems to contradict 9.4: beings are not in him either. Commentators delight in this. Paśya me yogam aiśvaram, “behold my divine power”: Kṛṣṇa calls it a mystery to be seen, not merely reasoned. Śaṅkara’s famous image is the rope and snake: in dim light a rope looks like a snake; the snake is “in” the rope only as appearance. Rāmānuja: beings are not in the Lord in the sense of limiting or touching him; he holds them by his will. The phrase aiśvara yoga, divine power, returns in 11.8, when Arjuna is given divine sight. Commentators note bhūta-bhāvana, “source of beings”: the Lord brings beings into existence. Madhusūdana says the mystery is called divine because reason alone cannot solve it. Some teachers say a mind that can hold a paradox calmly has begun to grow wise.

🌱 For today

Some truths are mysteries to be wondered at, not solved. Some truths are better wondered at than solved. Let a little mystery remain.

↑ 9.5 · See my divine yoga!

ŚLOKA 9.6 · DEEP DIVE

LIKE WIND IN SPACE

🔎 In context

An image for the paradox: wind in space.

📜 Looking deeper

Wind is in space, moves freely, yet does not touch or change space. So beings move in God without affecting him. The wind-and-space image helps with the paradox. Wind is always in space, moves freely, can be violent, yet never touches or changes space. Commentators note how apt the image is: we never see space directly; we see what is in it. Śaṅkara says beings exist in the Lord without attachment, as wind in space. Rāmānuja says the Lord holds beings by his will, untouched. Many find this image calming: however stormy life is, it moves in a vast stillness that is never disturbed. Commentators note mahān, “mighty”: the wind is strong, yet space is untouched. Madhusūdana says beings, however active, do not disturb the Lord. Many find this image helpful in times of turmoil: the vast stillness remains.

🌱 For today

However stormy life is, it moves in a vast stillness. However stormy today is, it moves within a vast, quiet space. Notice the space as well as the storm.

ŚLOKA 9.7 · DEEP DIVE

THE KALPA ENDS AND BEGINS

🔎 In context

Cosmic cycles of dissolution and creation.

📜 Looking deeper

This continues 8.18–19. Commentators: creation is the Lord’s ongoing activity, through his nature. Kalpa, a day of Brahmā (8.17), is the unit of cosmic creation. Commentators note that this continues 8.18–19 but now speaks of the Lord’s own nature, prakṛti māmikā, as the womb into which beings return. Śaṅkara calls it the Lord’s lower nature, made of the guṇas. Rāmānuja calls it subtle matter, part of the Lord’s body. The cycle is not chaos but rhythm, held in the Lord. The Purāṇas describe these cycles in great detail. Commentators note māmikām prakṛtim, “my nature”: beings return to the Lord’s own nature at dissolution. Madhusūdana says they rest there like seeds in winter. The image is of a cosmic sleep and waking.

🌱 For today

Endings and beginnings are part of one great rhythm. Endings and beginnings belong to one rhythm. What closes now prepares what opens next.

ŚLOKA 9.8 · DEEP DIVE

THROUGH MY NATURE

🔎 In context

God creates through nature; beings are carried by their nature.

📜 Looking deeper

Commentators: beings are helpless because bound by karma and guṇas; God acts freely through prakṛti. Two kinds of power are contrasted. The Lord is sva-vaśa, free, acting through his own nature. Beings are avaśa, helpless, carried by nature and karma. Commentators note the repeated punaḥ punaḥ, “again and again”: creation is ongoing. Śaṅkara says beings are helpless because of ignorance and its products. Rāmānuja says they are helpless because of karma, which the Lord honours. The verse does not blame beings; it describes their situation, and the rest of the chapter offers the way out: devotion. Commentators note avaṣṭabhya, “resting on” or “taking hold of”: the Lord governs nature. Madhusūdana says the Lord creates according to each being’s karma. The verse holds together divine sovereignty and human responsibility.

🌱 For today

We are carried by habits until we find freedom. Much of what we do is habit. Freedom grows as we notice our habits and choose consciously.

ŚLOKA 9.9 · DEEP DIVE

UNBOUND BY CREATION

🔎 In context

Even creating worlds does not bind God.

📜 Looking deeper

Udāsīna, sitting above, like a neutral witness. Commentators say this models detached action for us too. Udāsīna, literally “sitting above”, means neutral or uninvolved, like a judge. Commentators stress that it does not mean uncaring; the Lord is the friend of all beings (5.29). It means unattached: he acts without needing anything from the act. Śaṅkara says the Lord is not bound because he has no sense of doership. Rāmānuja says he is not bound because his actions are for the good of beings, according to their karma. The Lord models the detached action asked of Arjuna in 3.22–25. Commentators note dhanañjaya, “winner of wealth”: Kṛṣṇa calls Arjuna by a name earned in action as he speaks of unattached action. Madhusūdana says the Lord sits above his works like a neutral judge. The verse models how one can act fully without being bound.

🌱 For today

Act fully; stay unattached. Even God works this way. Act fully, but don’t let the action own you. Even the Creator, in this view, works this way.
