WHERE WE ARE

In Chapter 9 Kṛṣṇa revealed the royal secret of devotion: offer a leaf with love, and he carries the rest.

In chapter 10 Arjuna asks: where should I think of you? Kṛṣṇa names his glories, the best in every class, from the sun and Mount Meru to the letter A and the month of Mārgaśīrṣa. The lesson: wherever there is excellence, see a spark of the Divine.

ŚLOKA 10.1

HEAR AGAIN
śrībhagavān uvācabhūya eva mahābāho śṛṇu me paramaṃ vacaḥyatte’haṃ prīyamāṇāya vakṣyāmi hitakāmyayā ‖1‖
The Lord said: Hear again, mighty-armed one, my supreme word, which I will speak to you, who delight in it, wishing your good.
WordMeans
bhūyaḥ eva mahā-bāhoagain, mighty-armed one
śṛṇu me paramam vacaḥhear my supreme word
yat te aham prīyamāṇāyawhich to you, who delight
vakṣyāmi hita-kāmyayāI will speak, wishing your good
Spoken for your good.

ŚLOKA 10.1 · DEEP DIVE

HEAR AGAIN

🔎 In context

Kṛṣṇa continues, encouraged by Arjuna’s delight. Chapter 10 is named Vibhūti Yoga, the yoga of divine glories. Arjuna asked nothing at the end of chapter 9; Kṛṣṇa simply goes on, as a friend keeps talking to one who loves to listen.

📜 Looking deeper

The teacher speaks more because the student enjoys listening. Commentators note hita-kāmyayā: everything Kṛṣṇa says is for Arjuna’s good. Commentators note prīyamāṇa, “delighting”: Arjuna’s joy in hearing draws out more teaching. Śaṅkara says the Lord teaches out of concern for Arjuna’s welfare. Rāmānuja says the Lord, seeing Arjuna’s love, longs to reveal more of his glory. Chapter 10 is in a sense the Lord speaking about himself to a friend, the way one shares what one loves. It prepares the ground for the vision of chapter 11. Commentators note paramaṃ vacaḥ, “supreme word”: Kṛṣṇa continues because Arjuna listens with joy. Madhusūdana says the Lord’s motive is Arjuna’s good. Many teachers say a student’s delight is the best encouragement a teacher can receive.

🌱 For today

A willing listener draws out the best from a teacher. A willing listener draws out the best in a teacher. Bring delight, not just duty, to what you learn.

↑ 10.1 · Spoken for your good.

ŚLOKA 10.2

NOT EVEN THE GODS KNOW
na me viduḥ suragaṇāḥ prabhavaṃ na maharṣayaḥahamādirhi devānāṃ maharṣīṇāṃ ca sarvaśaḥ ‖2‖
“Neither the hosts of gods nor the great sages know my origin; for I am in every way the source of the gods and the great sages.”
WordMeans
na me viduḥ sura-gaṇāḥthe hosts of gods do not know
prabhavam na mahā-ṛṣayaḥmy origin, nor great sages
aham ādiḥ hi devānāmfor I am the source of the gods
maharṣīṇām ca sarvaśaḥand of the great sages, in every way
The source is beyond its creations.

ŚLOKA 10.2 · DEEP DIVE

NOT EVEN THE GODS KNOW

🔎 In context

Even gods and sages cannot know God’s origin, since he is their origin.

📜 Looking deeper

A child cannot remember its parents’ birth. Commentators say the source is beyond its products. Commentators read prabhava as “origin” or “coming forth”: how the Lord manifests. The Ṛg Veda’s Nāsadīya Sūkta (10.129) asks who knows how creation came about; even the gods came later, so who can tell? Commentators link the two passages. Śaṅkara says what is the source cannot be fully known by its products. Rāmānuja says even the gods cannot grasp the Lord’s greatness, only aspects of it. The verse sets a tone of humility for the whole chapter: what follows are glimpses, not a complete account. Commentators note sarvaśaḥ, “in every way”: the Lord is the source of gods and sages in every respect. Madhusūdana says a source is always prior to and greater than what comes from it. The Kena Upaniṣad tells how even the gods did not recognise Brahman when it appeared before them as a mysterious being.

🌱 For today

The deepest source is beyond full understanding. Humility is fitting. The deepest source is always larger than our grasp. Humility is not weakness; it is accuracy.

↑ 10.2 · The source is beyond its creations.

ŚLOKA 10.3

THE UNBORN, BEGINNINGLESS
yo māmajamanādiṃ ca vetti lokamaheśvaramasammūḍhaḥ sa martyeṣu sarvapāpaiḥ pramucyate ‖3‖
“One who knows me as unborn, beginningless, the great Lord of the worlds, is undeluded among mortals and freed from all sins.”
WordMeans
yaḥ mām ajam anādim cawho me as unborn, beginningless
vetti loka-maheśvaramknows, great Lord of the worlds
asammūḍhaḥ saḥ martyeṣuhe is undeluded among mortals
sarva-pāpaiḥ pramucyateis freed from all sins

ŚLOKA 10.3 · DEEP DIVE

THE UNBORN, BEGINNINGLESS

🔎 In context

Knowing God as unborn and Lord frees one.

📜 Looking deeper

Commentators: this knowledge removes the deepest delusion, that we and the world are self-made. Aja, unborn, and anādi, beginningless, recall 4.6: the Lord takes birth but is unborn. Commentators note that knowing this frees one from sin. Why? Śaṅkara: because knowing the Lord as the Self removes the ignorance that causes sinful action. Rāmānuja: because such knowledge produces devotion, and devotion purifies. Asammūḍha, undeluded, is a strong word: among people confused about who they are and where the world comes from, such a person sees clearly. Commentators note martyeṣu, “among mortals”: such knowledge is rare among humans. Madhusūdana says it removes the delusion that we are self-made. The verse invites gratitude toward the source of all.

🌱 For today

Knowing there is a source beyond us brings both humility and freedom. Knowing there is a source beyond us brings both humility and a quiet freedom.

↑ 10.3 · Know me as unborn, and be freed.

ŚLOKA 10.4

ALL QUALITIES FROM ME · I
buddhirjñānamasaṃmohaḥ kṣamā satyaṃ damaḥ śamaḥsukhaṃ duḥkhaṃ bhavo’bhāvo bhayaṃ cābhayameva ca ‖4‖
“Intelligence, knowledge, freedom from delusion, forbearance, truth, self-control, calm, pleasure and pain, birth and death, fear and fearlessness,”
WordMeans
buddhiḥ jñānam asammohaḥintelligence, knowledge, clarity
kṣamā satyam damaḥ śamaḥforbearance, truth, restraint, calm
sukham duḥkham bhavaḥ abhāvaḥpleasure, pain, birth, death
bhayam ca abhayam eva cafear and fearlessness
All states arise from me.

ŚLOKA 10.4 · DEEP DIVE

ALL QUALITIES FROM ME · I

🔎 In context

A list of human states, all arising from God.

📜 Looking deeper

The list includes opposites: pleasure and pain, fear and fearlessness. Commentators: the source of all states is one; even hard ones serve a purpose. The sentence continues. Verses 4–5 list twenty states of beings. Commentators note that virtues and their opposites appear side by side: pleasure and pain, birth and death, fear and fearlessness, fame and infamy. Śaṅkara says all arise from the Lord according to the karma of beings. Rāmānuja says the Lord gives each state as fits each being’s past actions. Kṣamā, forbearance or forgiveness, and satya, truth, are two of the most praised virtues in Indian ethics. Dama and śama, control of senses and of mind, are the first steps in Vedānta practice. Commentators note that bhava and abhāva, birth and death, are both from the Lord. Madhusūdana says even fear has its purpose: it protects us from danger. Seeing every state as coming from one source makes it easier to bear the hard ones.

🌱 For today

Even your difficult feelings arise from the same source as your best ones. Even difficult feelings arise from the same source as joyful ones. Each has something to teach.

↑ 10.4 · All states arise from me.

ŚLOKA 10.5

ALL QUALITIES FROM ME · II
ahiṃsā samatā tuṣṭistapo dānaṃ yaśo’yaśaḥbhavanti bhāvā bhūtānāṃ matta eva pṛthagvidhāḥ ‖5‖
“…non-violence, equanimity, contentment, austerity, charity, fame and infamy: these various states of beings arise from me alone.”
WordMeans
ahiṃsā samatā tuṣṭiḥnon-violence, equanimity, contentment
tapaḥ dānam yaśaḥ ayaśaḥausterity, charity, fame, infamy
bhavanti bhāvāḥ bhūtānāmarise, the states of beings

ŚLOKA 10.5 · DEEP DIVE

ALL QUALITIES FROM ME · II

🔎 In context

The list completes: every quality comes from the Lord.

📜 Looking deeper

Ahiṃsā heads the list, as in many Indian lists of virtues. Commentators note fame and infamy too come from him. Ahiṃsā, non-violence, heads the second line. Patañjali places it first among the yamas (Yoga Sūtra 2.30), and Jain and Buddhist ethics give it central place. Gandhi made it the core of his politics. Commentators note that the Gītā names it here as a divine quality, even while urging Arjuna to fight a just war. They reconcile the two: ahiṃsā is the inner absence of hatred, which a righteous warrior can keep. Samatā, equanimity, and tuṣṭi, contentment, are key words of earlier chapters. The list ends with fame and infamy, both from the Lord. Commentators note pṛthag-vidhāḥ, “of various kinds”: the states are many, the source one. Madhusūdana says each arises according to karma. The list of virtues here has been used in Indian schools as a guide to character.

🌱 For today

Cultivate the good qualities; know their source. Cultivate the good qualities on this list, and remember where they come from.

↑ 10.5 · Every quality has one source.

ŚLOKA 10.6

THE SEVEN SAGES AND THE MANUS
maharṣayaḥ sapta pūrve catvāro manavastathāmadbhāvā mānasā jātā yeṣāṃ loka imāḥ prajāḥ ‖6‖
“The seven great sages and the four before them, and the Manus, were born of my mind, filled with my nature; from them come these creatures of the world.”
WordMeans
mahā-ṛṣayaḥ sapta pūrvethe seven great sages, of old
catvāraḥ manavaḥ tathāthe four, and the Manus
mat-bhāvāḥ mānasāḥ jātāḥof my nature, born of my mind

ŚLOKA 10.6 · DEEP DIVE

THE SEVEN SAGES AND THE MANUS

🔎 In context

The ancestors of humanity were born of God’s mind.

📜 Looking deeper

The phrase can be read as “the seven great sages, the four earlier ones (the Sanakas), and the Manus”, or “the seven sages of old, and the four Manus”. Commentators differ. The point: all lineages trace back to God. The seven great sages, saptarṣi, are often listed as Marīci, Atri, Aṅgiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu and Vasiṣṭha; lists vary. The four earlier ones may be the Kumāras: Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana and Sanatkumāra, eternal child-sages. The Manus are the progenitors of humanity, fourteen in each kalpa. Commentators differ on how to divide the phrase. Śaṅkara takes “four Manus”. Rāmānuja and others take “the four (Kumāras) and the Manus”. All agree the point: every lineage traces back to the Lord’s mind. The seven stars of the Great Bear are named after the seven sages. Commentators note mad-bhāvāḥ, “filled with my nature”: the ancestors of humanity carry the Lord’s nature. Madhusūdana says they were born of the Lord’s mind, not by ordinary birth. Many Hindu families trace their lineage, gotra, to one of the seven sages.

🌱 For today

Every family tree goes back to one source. Every family tree, however long, goes back to one source. In that sense, all people are kin.

↑ 10.6 · All lineages from my mind.

ŚLOKA 10.7

“One who truly knows this glory and power of mine is united with unwavering yoga; of this there is no doubt.”
WordMeans
etām vibhūtim yogam cathis glory and power
mama yaḥ vetti tattvataḥof mine, who knows truly
saḥ avikampena yogenahe, with unwavering yoga
yujyate na atra saṃśayaḥis united, no doubt

ŚLOKA 10.7 · DEEP DIVE

KNOWING MY GLORY

🔎 In context

Knowing God’s glory steadies devotion.

📜 Looking deeper

Vibhūti, divine glory, names this chapter. Commentators say seeing God’s glory everywhere keeps the mind from wavering. Vibhūti, divine glory or manifestation, names the chapter. Yoga here means the Lord’s power, as in 9.5 (yogam aiśvaram). Commentators explain the link: when one sees the Divine in all that is excellent, devotion becomes steady, avikampa, unshaking. Śaṅkara says knowing the Lord’s glory leads to firm knowledge. Rāmānuja says it leads to firm bhakti. The word vibhūti also means sacred ash in Śaiva practice, worn as a sign of the divine; the shared meaning is “divine presence made visible”. Commentators note avikampena, “unshaking”: knowing the Lord’s glory makes devotion steady. Madhusūdana says wavering comes from seeing the world apart from God. Seeing God in the world removes the cause of wavering.

🌱 For today

Look for the Divine in what is excellent around you. Look for the Divine in what is excellent around you: a skilled hand, a kind word, a great tree. It steadies the heart.

↑ 10.7 · Know my glory, be steady.

THE ORIGIN OF ALL
