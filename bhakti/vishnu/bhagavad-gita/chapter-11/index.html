WHERE WE ARE

In Chapter 10 Kṛṣṇa named his glories and ended: the whole universe is one fragment of me.

Now Arjuna asks to see it. Kṛṣṇa gives him a divine eye, and the universal form fills the sky: countless faces, the sun and moon as eyes, all gods within. Then the vision turns fierce: the warriors rushing into fiery mouths. “I am Time,” says Kṛṣṇa; “be merely my instrument.” Arjuna sings a great hymn, begs forgiveness, and asks for the gentle form again.

MY DELUSION IS GONE

ŚLOKA 11.1 · DEEP DIVE

MY DELUSION IS GONE

🔎 In context

After hearing chapters 7–10, Arjuna feels his confusion lifting. He opens chapter 11 by thanking Kṛṣṇa. Chapter 11 is named Viśvarūpa-Darśana Yoga, the yoga of the vision of the universal form. It has 55 verses, many in the long triṣṭubh metre, and is often called the dramatic climax of the Gītā.

📜 Looking deeper

Arjuna says “out of grace to me”: he now sees the teaching as a gift, not an argument he must win. Commentators note his delusion about death (chapter 2) and about God (chapters 7–10) has eased. Yet one thing remains: he has heard of the Lord’s glory, but not seen it. That longing drives this chapter. Śaṅkara says the delusion removed is the idea that Arjuna would be the killer and his elders the killed. Rāmānuja says it is the delusion about the Lord’s nature. Jñāneśvar, the thirteenth-century Marathi saint, wrote the Jñāneśvarī, a poetic commentary of some nine thousand verses, gives chapter 11 some of its finest passages. Abhinavagupta, the Kashmiri philosopher, wrote a commentary on the Gītā in the tenth century, reading it in the light of Kashmir Śaivism.

🌱 For today

Gratitude is the right response to good teaching. Before asking for more, Arjuna thanks his teacher. It is a good habit for any student. Name what has become clearer for you lately, and give thanks for it, before reaching for the next thing.

ŚLOKA 11.2

YOUR GREATNESS
bhavāpyayau hi bhūtānāṃ śrutau vistaraśo mayātvattaḥ kamalapatrākṣa māhātmyamapi cāvyayam ‖2‖
“I have heard from you in detail of the origin and dissolution of beings, lotus-eyed one, and also of your imperishable greatness.”
WordMeans

ŚLOKA 11.2 · DEEP DIVE

YOUR GREATNESS

🔎 In context

Arjuna summarises what he has learned.

📜 Looking deeper

Arjuna calls Kṛṣṇa kamala-patra-akṣa, “lotus-petal-eyed”, a loving epithet. He has heard; now he wants to see. Commentators note the classic sequence of learning: hearing (śravaṇa), then reflecting and seeing. The epithet kamala-patrākṣa, lotus-petal-eyed, is a classic description of Viṣṇu and Kṛṣṇa in hymns and temple images. Arjuna summarises chapters 7–10: the Lord as origin and end (7.6, 9.7–8, 10.8) and his greatness (10.19–42). Many temple images of Viṣṇu, such as the reclining Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam or Padmanābha at Thiruvananthapuram, are praised in hymns for their lotus eyes. The Ālvārs, the Tamil poet-saints, wrote whole poems on those eyes alone. Arjuna’s epithet here belongs to a very long devotional tradition. Hearing first and seeing later is also the order of the Upaniṣadic method: śravaṇa, then manana and nididhyāsana (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.4.5).

🌱 For today

Hearing about something is the first step. A longing to see for oneself is a good sign. Summarise what you have learned before asking for more. It shows you where you really stand.

ŚLOKA 11.3

I WISH TO SEE IT
evametadyathāttha tvamātmānaṃ parameśvaradraṣṭumicchāmi te rūpamaiśvaraṃ puruṣottama ‖3‖
“It is as you have described yourself, supreme Lord. Yet I wish to see your divine form, O Supreme Person.”
WordMeans

ātmānam parama-īśvaraof yourself, supreme Lord
draṣṭum icchāmi te rūpamI wish to see your form

ŚLOKA 11.3 · DEEP DIVE

I WISH TO SEE IT

🔎 In context

Arjuna makes his request: show me your divine form.

📜 Looking deeper

He first affirms faith (“it is as you say”), then asks to see. Commentators note the order: not doubt seeking proof, but faith seeking sight. The word aiśvara means sovereign, lordly: Arjuna wants to see God as Lord of all. In the Udyoga Parva, Kṛṣṇa had already shown a cosmic form in the Kaurava court when Duryodhana tried to seize him; the blind Dhṛtarāṣṭra was briefly given sight to see it. Arjuna asks for a fuller vision here. Rāmānuja notes that Arjuna’s wish comes from love, the longing to see the beloved in full glory. Commentators contrast Arjuna with Duryodhana, who saw a cosmic form in the Kaurava court and dismissed it as a conjuror’s trick. Seeing is not the same as recognising. Arjuna asks with faith, so the vision transforms him. Śrī Vaiṣṇava teachers say that the desire to see the Lord is itself a gift of the Lord. Many devotees pray simply for darśan, the sight of God, before any other request.

🌱 For today

It is natural to want to see what we believe. Faith and the wish for experience can go together. Let your longing to experience the truth grow from trust, not from suspicion.

ŚLOKA 11.4

IF YOU THINK I CAN
manyase yadi tacchakyaṃ mayā draṣṭumiti prabhoyogeśvara tato me tvaṃ darśayā’tmānamavyayam ‖4‖
“If you think it possible for me to see it, Lord, master of yoga, then show me your imperishable Self.”
WordMeans

mayā draṣṭum iti prabhofor me to see, Lord

ŚLOKA 11.4 · DEEP DIVE

IF YOU THINK I CAN

🔎 In context

Arjuna asks humbly, leaving the decision to Kṛṣṇa.

📜 Looking deeper

Commentators admire the humility: “if you think I am fit”. Arjuna does not demand. He calls Kṛṣṇa yogeśvara, master of yoga, a title used again at the very end (18.78). Yogeśvara, Lord of yoga, returns in Sañjaya’s last verse: yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇaḥ (18.78). Commentators say Arjuna’s request is a model of prayer: sincere wish, humble manner, final word left to the Lord. Śaṅkara notes that the vision depends on the Lord’s grace, not Arjuna’s power. Madhusūdana Sarasvatī observes that Arjuna does not doubt the Lord’s power, only his own fitness. That humility is what makes him fit. The Kaṭha Upaniṣad (1.2.23) says the Self reveals itself to the one it chooses. Some teachers compare the moment to a student asking a master musician to play a difficult rāga, knowing that he may not be able to follow it. The answer, in the next verse, is an eager yes.

🌱 For today

Ask humbly and leave the timing to the wise. Not every gift is ready when we want it. Ask sincerely, and let go of the timing. What is meant to come will come when you are ready.

↑ 11.4 · “If you think I am able.”
