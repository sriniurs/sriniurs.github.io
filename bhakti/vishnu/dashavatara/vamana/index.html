VĀMANA

DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Three small steps that measured the worlds

FOREWORD

The fifth avatāra is a little boy with an umbrella and a water-pot. Bali, the good and generous demon king, grandson of Prahlāda, had conquered heaven itself. At his great sacrifice he promised to give anyone anything they asked. The boy, Vāmana, asked for just three steps of land.

Bali laughed and agreed, though his guru warned him. Then the boy grew. With one step he covered the earth, with the second the heavens, and there was nowhere left for the third, so Bali offered his own head. Viṣṇu sent him to rule the underworld, and promised to stand guard at his door.

In Kerala, Bali comes home every year at Onam; in Karnataka he is remembered on Bali Pāḍyami. This guide tells the story and its long life, from the Ṛgveda’s three strides to the festivals of today.

BALI, THE GENEROUS DEMON

Grandson of Prahlāda
FamilySon of Virocana, grandson of Prahlāda
VirtueJust, devoted and so generous that no one left his court empty-handed
PowerWith his guru Śukrācārya’s rites he defeated Indra and ruled the three worlds
The problemEven a good ruler of heaven upset the order: the gods were homeless
NameMahābali, “great Bali”, as Kerala calls him

ZAPPY: A demon who was a good king?

PROF. BOLT: Asura doesn't always mean wicked. Bali is one of the most loved figures in these stories.

ADITI’S PRAYER

The mother of the gods
GriefAditi, mother of Indra and the gods, grieved for her exiled sons
The vowHer husband, the sage Kaśyapa, taught her the payovrata, a twelve-day vow of milk
The promiseViṣṇu appeared and promised to be born as her son
The birthHe was born as a dwarf Brahmin boy, and the gods gave him his thread, staff, umbrella, deerskin and water-pot

THREE STEPS OF LAND

The request
The sacrifice. Bali was holding a great horse sacrifice at Bhṛgukaccha on the Narmadā, giving gifts to all.
The boy. Vāmana arrived, glowing. Bali offered him villages, gold, elephants. The boy asked only for three steps of land, measured by his own feet.
The warning. Śukrācārya saw who the boy was and begged Bali to refuse. Bali would not break his word.
The water. A gift was sealed by pouring water. Folk tellings say Śukra hid in the spout of the pot to block it, and Vāmana cleared it with a blade of grass, costing the guru an eye.

TRIVIKRAMA

The three strides
GrowthThe boy grew until his head passed the stars
First stepThe whole earth
Second stepThe heavens, up to the world of Brahmā
Third stepThere was nothing left. Bali bowed and said: “Place it on my head.”
The GaṅgāBrahmā washed the foot that reached his world; that water became the heavenly Gaṅgā, which flows from Viṣṇu’s foot (Viṣṇupadī)

ZAPPY: The Gaṅgā came from his foot?

PROF. BOLT: In this story, yes. That is why Gaṅgā is called Viṣṇupadī.

BALI IN SUTALA

The guard at his door
SutalaViṣṇu sent Bali to Sutala, a realm below the earth more splendid than heaven
The boonViṣṇu himself would stand at Bali’s door, mace in hand, as his gatekeeper
The futureBali will be Indra in a coming age (the Sāvarṇi Manvantara)
PrahlādaHis grandfather came to rejoice, for Bali had won the Lord himself
ReturnKerala and Karnataka traditions say he may visit his people once a year

ZAPPY: God became his doorman?

PROF. BOLT: That's how the Bhāgavata honours Bali. He gave everything; he got God.

THE THREE STRIDES OF THE ṚGVEDA

The oldest root
Ṛgveda 1.154Viṣṇu “who measured out the earthly realms in three strides”, in whose highest step there is a well of honey
UrukramaHe is praised as wide-striding (urukrama, urugāya)
BrāhmaṇasThe Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa tells how the gods, as Viṣṇu the dwarf, won from the demons as much land as he could lie upon
MeaningThe three steps were read as earth, sky and heaven, or as the sun’s rising, zenith and setting

chalayasi vikramaṇe balim adbhuta-vāmana, pada-nakha-nīra-janita-jana-pāvana; keśava dhṛta-vāmana-rūpa jaya jagadīśa hare

Jayadeva, Gītagovinda: “Wondrous dwarf, by your strides you outwit Bali; the water from your toenail purifies the people.”

ONAM

Kerala welcomes Mahābali
WhenThe Malayalam month of Chingam (Aug–Sep), ten days from Atham to Tiruvōṇam
BeliefMahābali returns to see his people, and they show him they are happy
CustomsFlower carpets (pookkalam), the great feast (sadya), boat races, new clothes
Vāmana tooAt Tṛkkākara, Vāmana is honoured as well
Old recordTṛkkākara temple inscriptions hold the earliest known mention of Onam, from 861 CE

ZAPPY: Kerala celebrates the defeated king?

PROF. BOLT: Lovingly. Onam remembers a golden age when 'all men were equal'.

TṚKKĀKARA

The Vāmana temple
WhereTṛkkākara, near Kochi, Kerala, said to be Mahābali’s capital
DeityVāmana, shown about to place his foot on Bali; also called Tṛkkākarayappan
Divya DeśamOne of the 108 Viṣṇu temples sung by the Āḻvārs
Two templesA Śiva temple stands beside it, said to be the one Bali worshipped at
Ten avatārasDuring Onam, the deity is dressed each day as a different avatāra
See: All ten avatāras

BALI PĀḌYAMI

Karnataka and Mahārāṣṭra
WhenKārttika Śukla Pratipadā, the day after Dīpāvalī’s new moon
KarnatakaBali Pāḍyami: homes welcome Bali, with lamps and images made of cow dung or rice flour
MahārāṣṭraBali Pratipadā; the saying “Iḍā pīḍā javo, Balīcē rājya yevo”: may troubles go and Bali’s rule return
MeaningThe good king’s yearly visit, at the brightest festival of the year

ZAPPY: So Karnataka has its own Onam?

PROF. BOLT: In spirit, yes. Bali's day sits right in Dīpāvalī.
