NARASIṂHA

DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
He came out of the pillar for a child

FOREWORD

The fourth avatāra keeps a promise to a little boy. The demon king Hiraṇyakaśipu had won a boon that seemed to make him unkillable: no man or beast, no weapon, not by day or night, not inside or outside, not on the ground or in the sky could kill him. His son Prahlāda, though, would not stop praying to Viṣṇu.

“Is your Viṣṇu in this pillar?” the king roared, and struck it. Out burst Narasiṃha, half man and half lion, at twilight. He carried the king to the threshold, laid him on his lap, and tore him apart with his claws: every condition of the boon kept, and broken.

This guide tells the story, explains the many forms of the man-lion, and visits his temples, from Hampi’s giant to the hills of Ahobilam.

Keśava dhṛta-narahari-rūpa,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Wasn’t that too violent?

PROF. BOLT: The anger was for the tyrant who tortured his own child. To Prahlāda the man-lion was gentle.

THE PERFECT BOON

Hiraṇyakaśipu’s plan
RevengeHiraṇyakaśipu hated Viṣṇu, who as Varāha had killed his twin, Hiraṇyākṣa
TapasHe did fierce penance until Brahmā granted a boon
The boonNo being made by Brahmā could kill him: not man or beast, not by day or night, not inside or outside, not on earth or in the sky, by no weapon
TyrannyBelieving himself immortal, he conquered the three worlds and forbade the worship of Viṣṇu
The loopholeEvery rule had a gap: twilight, a threshold, a lap, claws, and a being both man and beast
The twin brother: Varāha and Hiraṇyākṣa

PRAHLĀDA

The child who would not stop praying
In the wombPrahlāda heard the sage Nārada’s teaching while still in his mother Kayādhū’s womb
At schoolAsked what he had learned, he praised Viṣṇu
TrialsHis father had him attacked by soldiers, trampled by elephants, bitten by snakes, poisoned and thrown from a cliff; each time he was saved
HolikāHis aunt Holikā, immune to fire, sat in a fire with him on her lap; she burned and he walked out unharmed
HoliThe bonfire of Holikā Dahan, the night before Holi, remembers it

ZAPPY: So Holi comes from this story?

PROF. BOLT: The bonfire on Holi eve does. Good survives the fire; Holikā doesn't.

THE PILLAR

“Is he in this?”
The question. “Where is your Viṣṇu?” the king demanded. “Everywhere,” said Prahlāda.
The challenge. “Is he in this pillar?” “He is.”
The blow. The king struck the pillar with his fist (or his mace).
The roar. The pillar split with a terrible sound, and the man-lion stepped out.

ZAPPY: Was God hiding in the pillar?

PROF. BOLT: Not hiding. Prahlāda said he was everywhere; the pillar just proved it.

THE RAGE AND THE CHILD

Who could calm him?
FuryEven after the demon died, Narasiṃha’s anger shook the worlds
The godsBrahmā, Śiva and the gods were afraid to approach; even Lakṣmī hesitated
PrahlādaThe child went forward and sang his praise; the Lord grew calm and placed his hand on the boy’s head
His wishOffered any boon, Prahlāda asked only for freedom from desire, and forgiveness for his father
ŚarabhaA Śaiva tradition says Śiva came as the Śarabha to calm him; Vaiṣṇava texts tell it differently

ZAPPY: Even Lakṣmī was scared?

PROF. BOLT: The story says so. Only innocence could walk up to him.

THE GIANT OF HAMPI

Lakṣmī Narasiṃha monolith
WhereHampi, Vijayanagara, Karnataka
WhatA seated man-lion carved from a single granite boulder, about 6.7 m high, with a seven-headed serpent hood above
When1528, in the time of Kṛṣṇadevarāya
LakṣmīA Lakṣmī figure once sat on his lap; it was broken in the sack of 1565, so he is often called Ugra Narasiṃha
RestoredThe yoga band and parts were restored by the Archaeological Survey of India

ZAPPY: Why was Lakṣmī removed?

PROF. BOLT: She was smashed in the destruction of the city. Only her hand on his back remains.

OTHER GREAT TEMPLES

Andhra and Telangana

WHAT NARASIṂHA TEACHES

The wisdom of the man-lion
God is everywhereEven in a pillar of stone
No loophole for evilEvery clever plan has a gap
Faith is protectionThe child was safe in fire and before elephants
Fierce loveGentle to the devotee, terrible to the tyrant

ZAPPY: What should I take from Narasiṃha?

PROF. BOLT: Trust like Prahlāda. Help comes from where you least expect, even from a pillar.
