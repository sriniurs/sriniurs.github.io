KALKI

DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The rider on the white horse

FOREWORD

The last avatāra has not yet come. When the dark age, the Kali Yuga, reaches its lowest point, the Purāṇas say, Viṣṇu will be born as Kalki in the village of Śambhala, to a Brahmin named Viṣṇuyaśas. He will ride a white horse and carry a blazing sword, end the rule of the wicked, and open the door to a new golden age.

His story is told in the Mahābhārata, the Viṣṇu and Bhāgavata Purāṇas, and in a whole later text, the Kalki Purāṇa, where Paraśurāma is his teacher. Buddhist texts too speak of Kalki kings of a hidden land called Shambhala.

This guide explains what the texts say, how the yuga clock works, and why the series ends not with a full stop but with hope.

Keśava dhṛta-kalki-śarīra,

ZAPPY: When will he come?

PROF. BOLT: By the traditional count, the Kali Yuga has hundreds of thousands of years left. No one can give a date.

THE DARK AGE

Signs of the Kali Yuga
DharmaDharma stands on one leg out of four
RulersKings plunder rather than protect
WealthMoney becomes the measure of worth, birth and virtue
LifeLifespans shrink; truth and kindness become rare
FaithReligion becomes show; scriptures are sold
TextLists of these signs fill Bhāgavata 12.2 and the Mahābhārata’s Vana Parva

THE YUGA CLOCK

How long is an age?
Four agesSatya, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali, in the ratio 4:3:2:1
Kali432,000 years by the Purāṇic count
StartTraditionally 3102 BCE, when Kṛṣṇa left the world; about 5,100 years have passed
MahāyugaThe four together, 4.32 million years, then the cycle repeats
Other viewsSome modern teachers propose much shorter cycles; the Purāṇic numbers are given here

THE END OF THE DARK AGE

Sword and white horse
The rideOn his white horse, sword blazing like a comet, he sweeps across the earth
The wickedHe ends the rule of the cruel and false, and the demon Kali himself
JayadevaPraises him wielding a sword “like a comet” to destroy the wicked
Our noteOlder texts use words for foreign or impure rulers that reflect their times; we follow the general sense, the end of tyranny

THE NEW GOLDEN AGE

Satya Yuga returns
RenewalAfter Kalki, people’s minds become pure as crystal, says the Bhāgavata
DharmaDharma stands again on all four legs
CycleThe wheel of ages turns: Satya, Tretā, Dvāpara, Kali, and Satya again
Back to MatsyaThe cycle that began with a fish in Manu’s hands turns back to its start

ZAPPY: So it all starts again?

PROF. BOLT: That's the Hindu idea of time: a wheel, not a line. Every ending is a beginning.

WHERE THE STORY COMES FROM

Older and later texts
MahābhārataVana Parva 188–191: the earliest full account, told by Mārkaṇḍeya
Viṣṇu Purāṇa4.24: Kalki’s coming at the end of the Kali Yuga
Bhāgavata1.3.25 lists him; 12.2 tells the signs of Kali and his coming
Kalki PurāṇaA later Upapurāṇa, a whole book of his life and battles; scholars date it to the last few centuries
DevelopmentScholars see the Kalki idea growing over many centuries, partly in response to invasions and hard times

SHAMBHALA

Hindu and Buddhist
Where?Some identify Śambhala with Sambhal in Uttar Pradesh; others see it as a hidden or symbolic land
KālacakraThe Buddhist Kālacakra Tantra (11th c.) tells of the kings of Shambhala called Kalkis; the 25th, Raudra Cakrin, will defeat the forces of darkness
Shared ideaA hidden kingdom, a future righteous king, a final battle: Hindu and Buddhist forms grew side by side
ModernShambhala became a byword in the West for a hidden paradise

KALKI TEMPLES

Waiting for him
JaipurA Kalki temple in Jaipur, built under Sawai Jai Singh II in the 18th century; a white horse stands before it, said to have a hoof wound that will heal when Kalki comes
SambhalThe Śrī Kalki Dhām project in Sambhal, Uttar Pradesh, had its foundation stone laid in 2024
In artKalki closes every Daśāvatāra panel, as at Tṛkkākara during Onam
Our noteKalki is mostly worshipped as part of the ten rather than in his own temples

CLAIMS AND CAUTION

Many have said “I am Kalki”
ClaimantsOver the centuries many people have claimed to be Kalki
TraditionThe texts describe a world-changing figure at the end of a 432,000-year age; no claimant has matched it
WisdomTeachers warn against those who use the name to gather followers or money
Inner readingSome read Kalki as the inner power that cuts through ignorance, in any age

ZAPPY: What if someone says they're Kalki?

PROF. BOLT: Be polite and keep your wallet. The texts describe someone the whole world couldn't miss.

THE TEN TOGETHER

The whole series
1. Matsya: saves Manu, the seeds of life and the Vedas from the flood.
2. Kūrma: bears Mount Mandara as the ocean of milk is churned.
3. Varāha: lifts the Earth from the cosmic ocean and slays Hiraṇyākṣa.
4. Narasiṃha: bursts from a pillar to save Prahlāda from Hiraṇyakaśipu.
5. Vāmana: asks Bali for three steps and spans the worlds.
6. Paraśurāma: the Brahmin warrior who humbles proud kings.
7. Rāma: the ideal king who defeats Rāvaṇa.
8. Balarāma · Buddha: the disputed eighth place: Kṛṣṇa’s elder brother, or the Buddha.
9. Kṛṣṇa: the cowherd, the charioteer, the teacher of the Gītā.
10. Kalki: will end the dark age and begin a new one.

ZAPPY: Fish to horseman: done?

PROF. BOLT: All ten. Yuge yuge: he comes again and again.
