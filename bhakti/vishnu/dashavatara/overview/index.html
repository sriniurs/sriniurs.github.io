DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Fish to horseman: the ten avatāras

FOREWORD

Whenever the world loses its balance, says the Bhagavad Gītā, the Lord comes down to set it right. Those “descents” are the avatāras, and ten of them have been sung, painted and carved for over fifteen centuries: the Daśāvatāra.

A fish that saves the seeds of life from the flood. A tortoise that carries a mountain. A boar who lifts the Earth. A man-lion who keeps a promise to a child. A dwarf who spans the universe in three steps. Then three Rāmas, the dark cowherd Kṛṣṇa, and a rider still to come.

This guide explains what an avatāra is, why the lists differ, where the stories come from and how they are worshipped. Ten detailed guides follow, one for each avatāra.

WHAT IS AN AVATĀRA?

The one who comes down
Wordava “down” + tṛ “to cross”: a descent, a crossing over
WhoAbove all Viṣṇu, the preserver, who keeps the world in order
WhyTo protect the good, remove evil and re-establish dharma
HowBy taking a form suited to the danger: fish for a flood, boar for the sunken Earth, man-lion for a riddle
Not lesserIn the tradition each avatāra is fully God, not a part

paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṃ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām, dharma-saṃsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge

“To protect the good, to destroy the wicked, to establish dharma, I come into being age after age.” Bhagavad Gītā 4.8

WHICH TEN?

The lists differ
Shared coreAlmost all lists agree on the first seven: Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Rāma, and on Kalki last
Common listEighth Kṛṣṇa, ninth Buddha: found in several Purāṇas and in North Indian tradition
South IndianŚrī Vaiṣṇava and Smārta lists give Balarāma eighth and Kṛṣṇa ninth, without the Buddha
JayadevaThe Gītagovinda (12th c.) names Balarāma and Buddha, for Kṛṣṇa is the source of all
RegionalSome lists place Jagannātha or Viṭhobā in the ninth place
This seriesArticle 8 covers Balarāma and the Buddha together; Kṛṣṇa is article 9

ZAPPY: So which list is right?

PROF. BOLT: Each tradition is right for itself. We show all of them and let you see why they differ.

MORE THAN TEN

The Bhāgavata’s longer lists
22 or moreThe Bhāgavata Purāṇa (1.3) lists over twenty avatāras, including the Kumāras, Nārada, Kapila, Dattātreya, Ṛṣabha, Pṛthu, Dhanvantari, Mohinī and Vyāsa
InnumerableIt adds that the avatāras are as countless as streams from an endless lake: avatārā hy asaṅkhyeyāḥ
KṛṣṇaIt ends with the famous line that Kṛṣṇa is the Lord himself: kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam
PāñcarātraSome Pāñcarātra texts count 39
Why ten?Ten became the classic set in poetry, temple art and festivals

OLDER THAN THE PURĀṆAS

Vedic roots
FishThe Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (1.8.1) tells of Manu, a fish and the flood
TortoisePrajāpati takes the form of a tortoise (kūrma) in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa
BoarIn the Taittirīya texts Prajāpati as a boar raises the earth from the waters
Three stepsThe Ṛgveda (1.154) praises Viṣṇu for measuring the worlds in three strides, the seed of the Vāmana story
LaterIn the Purāṇas these deeds are given to Viṣṇu and arranged in order

ZAPPY: The fish story is older than Viṣṇu's role in it?

PROF. BOLT: The Brāhmaṇa tells the flood without naming Viṣṇu. The Purāṇas made it his.

THROUGH THE FOUR AGES

The avatāras in time
Satya YugaMatsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha
Tretā YugaVāmana, Paraśurāma, Rāma
Dvāpara YugaBalarāma, Kṛṣṇa
Kali YugaThe Buddha (in lists that include him), and Kalki at its end
Our ageTradition says the Kali Yuga began when Kṛṣṇa left the world

AN EVOLUTION STORY?

A modern reading
The ideaFish (water), tortoise (water and land), boar (land), man-lion (half animal), dwarf (small man), then full humans with axe, bow and plough
Who says itA 19th–20th-century reading, popularised by writers comparing the list with Darwin
Not scripturalNo Purāṇa presents the avatāras as evolution; the order follows the yugas and the stories
Our viewAn interesting pattern people notice, not a claim of the texts

ZAPPY: So did the sages know about evolution?

PROF. BOLT: The pattern is striking, but the texts don't say so. Enjoy it as a reading, not a proof.

JAYADEVA’S DAŚĀVATĀRA

Gītagovinda, 12th century
PoetJayadeva of Odisha and Bengal, 12th century
HymnThe opening song of the Gītagovinda praises all ten, each verse ending keśava dhṛta … śarīra, jaya jagadīśa hare
Examplepralaya-payodhi-jale dhṛtavān asi vedam: “in the waters of dissolution you held the Veda” (Matsya)
His listBalarāma eighth and Buddha ninth; Kṛṣṇa is the Lord who takes all ten
SungIn Odissi dance, at Purī, and across India today

Onam at Tṛkkākara
WhereThe Vāmanamūrti temple at Tṛkkākara, near Kochi, Kerala
Ten daysDuring the Onam festival the deity is dressed each day as one of the avatāras
The setMatsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Rāma, Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa and Kalki, with Trivikrama
Why hereTradition says this was Mahābali’s capital, where Vāmana came

THE TEN AND THE TEN

Avatāras and Mahāvidyās
The ideaSome Tantras pair each avatāra with one of the ten Mahāvidyās, the Goddess’s ten forms
ExamplesKṛṣṇa with Kālī, Rāma with Tārā, Narasiṃha with Chinnamastā in one list
Lists varyThe Guhyātiguhya and Muṇḍamālā Tantras do not agree on every pair
MeaningThe Goddess is the power behind each of Viṣṇu’s descents

WHAT THEY TEACH

The wisdom of the ten
God takes our sideWhatever the danger, a form comes to meet it
Every shape is holyFish, tortoise, boar: God is not ashamed of any body
Promises keptTo Manu, Prahlāda and Bali, he keeps his word
HopeThe tenth is still to come: no age is forever dark
