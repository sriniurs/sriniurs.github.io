KṚṢṆA

DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Cowherd, charioteer, teacher of the Gītā

FOREWORD

The ninth avatāra is the one India knows most intimately. Kṛṣṇa was born at midnight in a prison, smuggled across a flooded river, and raised among cowherds as a butter-stealing child. He danced on a serpent’s heads, lifted a mountain like an umbrella, and played a flute that made the cowherd girls leave everything to dance with him.

He grew up to kill the tyrant Kaṃsa, built the island city of Dvārakā, and became the charioteer of Arjuna at Kurukṣetra, where he spoke the Bhagavad Gītā. For many traditions he is not just one avatāra but the Lord himself, the source of all the others.

This guide follows his life from Mathurā to Prabhāsa, and the saints and temples who keep him close, including Udupi in Karnataka.

Kṛṣṇāya Vāsudevāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: One avatāra, so many stories?

MIDNIGHT IN THE PRISON

Birth and the Yamunā
The prophecy. A voice warned Kaṃsa that his sister Devakī’s eighth child would kill him. He imprisoned Devakī and Vasudeva and killed six of their children.
The birth. At midnight on the eighth day of the dark fortnight, Kṛṣṇa was born. The guards slept, the chains fell, the doors opened.
The crossing. Vasudeva carried him through the storm across the flooded Yamunā, the serpent Śeṣa spreading his hoods as an umbrella, to Nanda and Yaśodā in Gokula.
The swap. He brought back Yaśodā’s newborn daughter, who rose from Kaṃsa’s hands as the goddess Yogamāyā and warned him his killer lived.

THE BUTTER THIEF

Childhood in Gokula
Makhan corHe stole butter from every house in the village and fed it to his friends and the monkeys
YaśodāWhen she looked into his mouth, she saw the whole universe in it
DāmodaraShe tied him to a mortar with a rope; he dragged it between two trees and freed two cursed beings
PūtanāThe demoness who came to poison him with her milk; he drank her life away
MeaningGod as a naughty child: love without fear

ZAPPY: God stealing butter?

PROF. BOLT: Devotees love it. A God you can scold is a God you can love.

RĀDHĀ AND THE RĀSA

The flute and the dance
The fluteHis flute called the gopīs, cowherd women, from their homes on autumn nights
Rāsa LīlāHe danced with all of them at once, a Kṛṣṇa for each gopī: the soul’s longing for God
RādhāThe beloved above all; not named in the Bhāgavata, she is central in the Gītagovinda and later devotion
MeaningBhakti saints read the love of the gopīs as the highest love of the soul for God
HoliRādhā and Kṛṣṇa’s play with colours is remembered at Holi in Vraja

MATHURĀ AND DVĀRAKĀ

Prince and king
KaṃsaAt sixteen Kṛṣṇa returned to Mathurā, broke the great bow, beat the king’s wrestlers and killed Kaṃsa
UgrasenaHe freed his parents and restored Kaṃsa’s father to the throne
SāndīpaniHe studied with the sage Sāndīpani at Ujjain, with his friend Sudāmā
DvārakāFacing endless attacks, he moved the Yādavas to Dvārakā, a city on the western sea
RukmiṇīHe carried off Rukmiṇī, who loved him, from her own wedding; she is Lakṣmī
SudāmāHis poor friend came with a handful of rice and went home rich
Read: Dvārakā

DraupadīWhen she was humiliated in the dice hall, he made her sari endless

THE BHAGAVAD GĪTĀ

The song of the Lord
SettingOn the field of Kurukṣetra, Arjuna saw his kin in the enemy army and laid down his bow
The answerKṛṣṇa’s teaching: 700 verses in 18 chapters, within the Mahābhārata’s Bhīṣma Parva
Karma yogaAct without attachment to the fruits of action
Jñāna and bhaktiKnow the undying Self; love and surrender to the Lord
PromiseWhenever dharma declines, I come (4.7–8)

karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana

“Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits.” Bhagavad Gītā 2.47

THE COSMIC FORM

Viśvarūpa

THE END AT PRABHĀSA

Departure of the Lord

UDUPI

Kṛṣṇa of Karnataka
MadhvācāryaThe 13th-century philosopher of Dvaita installed the image of Bālakṛṣṇa, said to have come in a ship’s ballast
Aṣṭa MaṭhasEight monasteries take turns to worship, changing every two years at the Paryāya festival
KanakadāsaRefused entry because of his caste, the poet sang outside; the wall cracked and the image turned to face him. The window is Kanakana Kiṇḍi
PurandaradāsaHis thousands of Kannada songs shaped Carnatic music
Facing westSo the Lord here looks through the window, westward

Where the facts come from

Texts: Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10–11 (birth, childhood, rāsa, Mathurā, Dvārakā, Prabhāsa); Mahābhārata (Sabhā, Udyoga, Bhīṣma and Mausala Parvas); Bhagavad Gītā 2.47, 4.7–8, 11, 18.66; Harivaṃśa; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 5; Jayadeva, Gītagovinda.

Udupi: Udupi Śrī Kṛṣṇa Maṭha traditions (Madhvācārya, Aṣṭa Maṭhas, Paryāya, Kanakana Kiṇḍi); Purandaradāsa and Kanakadāsa from Karnataka literary histories.

Other: Oppenheimer’s recollection of Gītā 11.32 (1965 interview); the 3102 BCE Kali Yuga date is traditional.

Sources differ / unverified: Kṛṣṇa’s historical dates; whether he is the eighth or ninth avatāra or the source; the Udupi image’s arrival story; Rādhā’s absence from the Bhāgavata is noted by scholars and explained differently by devotees. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
