The disputed eighth place

FOREWORD

The lists of the ten avatāras agree on the first seven and on the last. In between they differ. In the South and in Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition the eighth is Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa’s strong elder brother with the plough, and the ninth Kṛṣṇa. In many North Indian lists and several Purāṇas the eighth is Kṛṣṇa and the ninth is the Buddha. Jayadeva’s Gītagovinda praises both Balarāma and the Buddha, with Kṛṣṇa as the Lord of all ten.

This guide gives both their due. Balarāma: born from a womb moved by divine power, dragging the Yamunā with his plough, standing beside Jagannātha at Purī, and already on coins in the 2nd century BCE. The Buddha: honoured in the Purāṇas and by Jayadeva for his compassion, while Buddhists themselves do not accept him as an avatāra of Viṣṇu.

We set out the views fairly and let the reader see why the lists differ.

Keśava dhṛta-haladhara-rūpa,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: So who is really number eight?

PROF. BOLT: It depends on whose list you hold. Both are honoured, and neither tradition is “wrong” by its own texts.

BALARĀMA’S BIRTH

The moved womb
The threatKing Kaṃsa was killing every child born to his sister Devakī
The rescueBy Viṣṇu’s will, the goddess Yogamāyā moved Devakī’s seventh child into the womb of Rohiṇī, Vasudeva’s other wife, in Gokula
SaṅkarṣaṇaSo he is called Saṅkarṣaṇa, “drawn out”
ŚeṣaMany texts say he is Ādiśeṣa, the serpent on whom Viṣṇu rests, come down with him
NameBalarāma, “Rāma of strength”, or Balabhadra

ZAPPY: A womb transfer in an ancient story?

PROF. BOLT: Told as divine power. Readers today find it remarkable.

THE STRONG ONE

Balarāma’s deeds
ChildhoodWith Kṛṣṇa he grew up among the cowherds of Vraja; he killed the donkey-demon Dhenuka and the demon Pralamba
MathurāWith Kṛṣṇa he killed Kaṃsa’s wrestlers
The YamunāWhen the river would not come to him, he dragged it with his plough; Jayadeva sings of it
RevatīHis wife Revatī came from an earlier age and was very tall; he shortened her with a tap of his plough
HastināpuraAngered, he began to drag the whole city towards the Gaṅgā with his plough until the Kauravas begged him to stop

vahasi vapuṣi viśade vasanaṃ jaladābhaṃ, hala-hati-bhīti-milita-yamunābham; keśava dhṛta-haladhara-rūpa jaya jagadīśa hare

Jayadeva: “On your fair body you wear cloth dark as a rain cloud, dark as the Yamunā that fled in fear of your plough.”

NEUTRAL AT KURUKṢETRA

The teacher who walked away
TeacherHe taught the mace to both Duryodhana and Bhīma
NeutralLoving both sides, he would not fight in the Mahābhārata war; he went on pilgrimage along the Sarasvatī
The last duelHe returned for the mace fight of Bhīma and Duryodhana, and was angry at Bhīma’s blow below the waist
DepartureAt the end of the Yādavas, he sat in meditation by the sea at Prabhāsa, and a white serpent left his mouth: Śeṣa returning

LORD OF THE PLOUGH

Balarāma and farming
Haladhara“Plough-bearer”: the god of farmers and fertile fields
Hal ṢaṣṭhīIn North India, women keep the Hal Ṣaṣṭhī fast for their children’s welfare on his day
Wine and strengthHe is fond of wine (vāruṇī) and known for his temper and his kindness
Ancient coinsCoins of the Indo-Greek king Agathocles, about 180 BCE, show Saṅkarṣaṇa with a plough and Vāsudeva-Kṛṣṇa with a wheel: among the oldest images of Hindu gods
MathurāEarly stone images of Saṅkarṣaṇa come from the Mathurā region

THE BUDDHA IN THE PURĀṆAS

How the avatāra list grew
Bhāgavata 1.3.24At the start of the Kali age, the Lord will appear as Buddha, son of Añjanā, among the Kīkaṭas (Magadha), to bewilder the enemies of the gods
Other PurāṇasThe Agni, Garuḍa, Varāha and other Purāṇas list the Buddha as an avatāra
WhenScholars date the Buddha’s inclusion to roughly the Gupta age and after, as the list took shape
Two portraitsSome Purāṇas show him as misleading the wicked; Jayadeva shows him as compassion itself
DebateSome Hindu writers say the Purāṇic Buddha is a different person from Gautama

JAYADEVA’S BUDDHA

The compassionate one
VerseJayadeva praises the Buddha for condemning the Vedic rule of animal sacrifice, “with a heart full of compassion”
AhiṃsāIn this reading the Buddha is honoured for non-violence and kindness to animals
OdishaJayadeva’s list (with Balarāma and Buddha) shaped worship and dance at Purī

nindasi yajña-vidher ahaha śruti-jātaṃ, sadaya-hṛdaya darśita-paśu-ghātam; keśava dhṛta-buddha-śarīra jaya jagadīśa hare

Jayadeva, Gītagovinda: “Ah, with a compassionate heart you condemned the Vedic rules that showed the killing of animals. Keśava, in the body of the Buddha, victory!”

THE BUDDHIST VIEW

Not an avatāra of Viṣṇu
BuddhismBuddhist traditions see the Buddha as a fully awakened human teacher, not as an avatāra of any god
AmbedkarIn 1956 B. R. Ambedkar and his followers took 22 vows on converting to Buddhism; one was not to believe the Buddha is an incarnation of Viṣṇu
RespectMany Hindus honour the Buddha deeply in either case
Our viewBoth views are reported here; this guide does not decide between them

ZAPPY: So Hindus count him and Buddhists don't?

PROF. BOLT: Some Hindus count him, some lists don't, and Buddhists don't. All three are held sincerely.

GayāA few km away: the Viṣṇupada temple with Viṣṇu’s footprint, and the place for śrāddha rites to ancestors
Meeting pointThe two traditions have lived side by side here for over two thousand years
