PARAŚURĀMA

DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The Brahmin warrior

FOREWORD

The sixth avatāra carries an axe. Paraśurāma, “Rāma with the axe”, was born a Brahmin, son of the sage Jamadagni and Reṇukā, but lived as a warrior. He won his axe from Śiva himself.

When the thousand-armed king Kārtavīrya Arjuna stole his father’s wish-granting cow, and the king’s sons later killed his father, Paraśurāma swore to rid the earth of arrogant rulers, and did so twenty-one times. Then he gave the whole earth away and went to the mountains to meditate. On the west coast he is the hero who threw his axe into the sea and raised new land: the coast from Gokarṇa to Kerala, including Udupi’s Tuḷunāḍu.

He is also the great teacher of the Mahābhārata, and the only avatāra said to be still alive.

THE AXE FROM ŚIVA

How Rāma got his paraśu
The studentThe young Rāma went to the Himālaya and did penance to Śiva
The giftPleased, Śiva taught him the arts of war and gave him the axe and divine weapons
The nameFrom then on he was Paraśurāma
GaṇeśaA later story says he struck Gaṇeśa with the axe when he blocked his way to Śiva, breaking one of Gaṇeśa’s tusks
MeaningEven Viṣṇu’s avatāra learns from Śiva: the gods help each other

THE HARD COMMAND

Reṇukā and the sons
The lapseReṇukā, fetching water, was lost for a moment in admiring a passing celestial king; Jamadagni saw it and was enraged
The orderHe told his sons to kill her. The elder four refused; Paraśurāma obeyed
The boonPleased, Jamadagni offered a boon. Paraśurāma asked that his mother and brothers be restored, with no memory of it. All came back to life
ReṇukāShe is worshipped as a goddess, as Yellammā at Saundatti in Karnataka and at Māhūr in Mahārāṣṭra
Our noteA hard story, told in the Mahābhārata and Purāṇas, about obedience and its limits
See: Ekavīrikā, Māhūr

THE THOUSAND-ARMED KING

Kārtavīrya Arjuna and the cow
WhoKārtavīrya Arjuna (Sahasrārjuna), king of the Haihayas, who had a thousand arms by the boon of Dattātreya
The visitJamadagni fed the king and his whole army with the help of his wish-granting cow, Kāmadhenu
The theftThe king wanted the cow and took her by force
The answerParaśurāma pursued him, cut off his thousand arms and killed him, and brought the cow home

TWENTY-ONE TIMES

The great vow
RevengeThe king’s sons came while Paraśurāma was away and killed Jamadagni
The griefReṇukā beat her breast twenty-one times
The vowParaśurāma swore to clear the earth of arrogant kṣatriya rulers twenty-one times
SamantapañcakaHe filled five lakes with their blood at Kurukṣetra and offered the waters to his ancestors
The endHis ancestors asked him to stop; he gave the whole earth to the sage Kaśyapa and left for Mount Mahendra

ZAPPY: Twenty-one times! Isn't that terrible?

PROF. BOLT: The texts don't hide it. He stands for justice that has gone too far, and he himself stops.

THE TWO RĀMAS MEET

After the broken bow
The bowAt Sītā’s svayaṃvara, Rāma of Ayodhyā broke Śiva’s great bow
The challengeParaśurāma arrived in fury and challenged him to string Viṣṇu’s bow
The answerRāma strung it easily
The handoverParaśurāma recognised Viṣṇu in Rāma, yielded his power and went back to his penance
MeaningOne avatāra steps aside for the next
Next avatāra: Rāma

TEACHER OF THE MAHĀBHĀRATA

Bhīṣma, Droṇa and Karṇa
BhīṣmaLearned arms from him; later they fought to a standstill over Ambā
DroṇaReceived weapons and knowledge from him
KarṇaCame to him pretending to be a Brahmin. When a stinging insect bit Karṇa’s thigh and he bore it without moving, his guru knew he was a kṣatriya and cursed him to forget his weapons at his hour of need
MeaningThe teacher who shaped the greatest warriors of the epic

ZAPPY: He cursed his own student?

PROF. BOLT: For the lie. Karṇa's curse came true on the field of Kurukṣetra.

LAND FROM THE SEA

Gokarṇa to Kerala
The storyHaving given away the earth, Paraśurāma had no land of his own; he asked Varuṇa, god of the sea, for some
The throwHe threw his axe from the Sahyādri hills, and the sea drew back as far as it flew
The landThe coast from Gokarṇa to Kanyākumārī rose: Paraśurāma Kṣetra
TuḷunāḍuCoastal Karnataka, with Udupi, is part of this land; seven Paraśurāma kṣetras are listed there
KeralaThe Kēraḷōlpatti tradition says he founded Kerala and its 64 villages
GeologyScientists point out that this coast did rise from the sea over long ages

ZAPPY: A myth that matches geology?

PROF. BOLT: The coast really was under the sea once. The legend remembers it as an axe throw.

Temples and tīrthas

Cirañjīvin

WhereOn Mahendra mountain, in meditation
FutureThe Kalki Purāṇa says he will teach Kalki the arts of war

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Texts: Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 116–117 and Śānti Parva 49 (Reṇukā, Kārtavīrya, 21 campaigns, Samantapañcaka, gift to Kaśyapa); Bhāgavata Purāṇa 9.15–16; Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 74–76 (meeting with Rāma); Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva 3 (Karṇa’s curse); Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa (Gaṇeśa’s tusk); Kēraḷōlpatti; Kalki Purāṇa.

Places: Saundatti Yellammā, Udupi and Tuḷunāḍu traditions; Paraśurām Kuṇḍ (Arunachal Pradesh); Chiplūṇ; Thrikkakara temple tradition (Wikipedia).

Sources differ / unverified: the identity of Reṇukā with Yellammā is a regional tradition; the number and list of the seven Paraśurāma kṣetras; the Gaṇeśa tusk story is late; geological links are general. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
