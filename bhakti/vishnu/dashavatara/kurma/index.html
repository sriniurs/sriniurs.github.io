KŪRMA

DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The tortoise who held up the churning

FOREWORD

The second avatāra is a great tortoise at the bottom of the ocean, with a mountain on his back. The gods had lost their strength and their good fortune, and the only cure was the nectar of immortality hidden in the ocean of milk.

To churn it, gods and demons used Mount Mandara as the churning rod and the serpent Vāsuki as the rope. But the mountain began to sink, until Viṣṇu came as Kūrma and took its weight. Out of the churning came poison, which Śiva drank, then fourteen treasures, Lakṣmī herself, and at last the nectar.

This guide tells the whole story of the churning, and the tortoise in yoga, temples and the Gītā.

Keśava dhṛta-kacchapa-rūpa,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Why a tortoise?

PROF. BOLT: Its shell is the strongest base there is. Something steady had to hold the world while it was stirred.

The essential facts on one page

DeedBears Mount Mandara on his back during the churning of the ocean of milk (Samudra Manthana)
TextsBhāgavata Purāṇa 8.5–8.11; Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.9; Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva
Vedic seedPrajāpati takes the form of a tortoise in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa
ResultsLakṣmī returns; Dhanvantari brings the nectar; Śiva drinks the poison
TemplesŚrīkūrmam (Andhra), Gavirangapura (Karnataka), Kurmai (Andhra)

ZAPPY: The tortoise doesn't do much, does he?

PROF. BOLT: He holds still while everything else whirls. Without him, nothing works.

WHY THE GODS LOST EVERYTHING

Durvāsa’s garland
The giftThe sage Durvāsa gave Indra a garland of divine flowers
The insultIndra placed it on his elephant Airāvata, who threw it to the ground
The curseDurvāsa cursed Indra and the gods to lose their Śrī: their fortune, strength and glory
The resultLakṣmī left them; the demons (asuras) defeated the weakened gods
The planViṣṇu told the gods to make peace with the demons and churn the ocean together for the nectar of immortality (amṛta)

ZAPPY: All that from dropping a garland?

PROF. BOLT: Disrespect to a gift costs dearly in these stories. Fortune leaves where it is not honoured.

THE CHURNING

Mountain, serpent and tortoise
The rod. Mount Mandara was uprooted and carried to the ocean of milk.
The rope. The great serpent Vāsuki was wound around it. The demons took the head end; the gods the tail.
The sinking. As they began to churn, the mountain sank into the ocean floor.
Kūrma. Viṣṇu became a vast tortoise, dived beneath the mountain and held it on his back. The churning went on for ages; in some tellings Viṣṇu also sat on top of the mountain to steady it.

THE POISON

Śiva becomes Nīlakaṇṭha
HālāhalaThe first thing churned up was a deadly poison that began to burn the worlds
ŚivaAt the gods’ plea, Śiva gathered it in his palm and drank it
PārvatīShe pressed his throat so it would not go down; it stayed there, turning his throat blue
NameHe is Nīlakaṇṭha, “blue-throated”
LessonBefore any treasure comes up, someone must swallow the poison

THE FOURTEEN TREASURES

What the ocean gave
AnimalsKāmadhenu the wish-cow; Uccaiḥśravas the white horse; Airāvata the white elephant
ThingsThe Kaustubha jewel; the Pārijāta tree; the conch Pāñcajanya; the moon
BeingsThe apsaras; Vāruṇī, goddess of wine
LakṣmīRose on a lotus and chose Viṣṇu as her lord
DhanvantariLast came the physician of the gods, holding the pot of amṛta
Our noteThe list of fourteen varies from text to text

MOHINĪ AND THE NECTAR

How the gods got immortality
The grabThe demons seized the pot of nectar
MohinīViṣṇu appeared as Mohinī, an enchanting woman, and offered to share it fairly
The trickShe served the gods first; the demons, charmed, waited
RāhuOne demon slipped into the gods’ line; the Sun and Moon pointed him out, and Viṣṇu’s discus cut off his head just as he drank
Rāhu and KetuHis immortal head became Rāhu, his body Ketu; they chase the Sun and Moon, causing eclipses

ZAPPY: So eclipses are a revenge story?

PROF. BOLT: In the myth, yes. Rāhu never forgave the Sun and Moon.

DROPS OF NECTAR

The Kumbha Melā
The dropsAs the nectar pot was carried away, drops fell at four places on earth
Four citiesPrayāga, Haridvāra, Ujjain and Nāsik
Kumbha MelāEvery twelve years the Kumbha Melā is held at each, in turn, when the planets are in a set position
LargestThe Prayāga Mahā Kumbha draws tens of millions of pilgrims
Kumbha“Pot”: the pot of nectar
See: Prayāga

THE COSMIC TORTOISE

Older ideas
Śatapatha BrāhmaṇaPrajāpati takes the form of a tortoise to create living beings; kūrma is linked with kaśyapa, the sage-father of creatures
ĀkūpāraThe great tortoise that supports the world
In ritualA living tortoise was placed in the fire altar in some Vedic rites
FoundationTemple builders lay a kūrma-śilā, a tortoise stone, in foundations and below flag-staffs

THE TORTOISE IN THE GĪTĀ

Withdrawing the senses
Gītā 2.58The wise one draws the senses back from their objects as a tortoise draws in its limbs
PratyāhāraThis withdrawal is a stage of yoga in Patañjali’s system
Kūrma-nāḍīYoga Sūtra 3.31: concentration on the “tortoise channel” brings steadiness
KūrmāsanaThe tortoise pose in haṭha yoga

yadā saṃharate cāyaṃ kūrmo ’ṅgānīva sarvaśaḥ, indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyas tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā

“When, like a tortoise drawing in its limbs, one draws the senses back from their objects, one’s wisdom is steady.” Bhagavad Gītā 2.58

ŚRĪKŪRMAM

The great Kūrma temple
WhereŚrīkūrmam village, Śrīkākuḷam district, Andhra Pradesh, near the Bay of Bengal
UniqueThe only known pre-14th-century temple dedicated to the Kūrma avatāra
SanctumBoth a tortoise image and Viṣṇu with Lakṣmī; the deity faces west
HistoryInscriptions begin in the 11th–12th centuries; re-established under the Eastern Gaṅga king Anantavarman Coḍagaṅga; Rāmānuja is linked to it
Living tortoisesKept in a dedicated part of the temple
MuralsOld Odisha-style paintings line the circumambulatory hall

WHAT KŪRMA TEACHES

The wisdom of the tortoise
Be the baseGreat work needs someone steady underneath
WithdrawLike the tortoise, draw in the senses to find calm
Poison firstEvery churning brings up poison before treasure
Fortune needs respectLakṣmī leaves when gifts are despised

ZAPPY: What should I take from Kūrma?

PROF. BOLT: When life is churning, be the tortoise: still, strong and underneath it all.
