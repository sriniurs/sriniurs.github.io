MATSYA

DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The fish who saved the world

FOREWORD

The first avatāra begins with a tiny fish in a man’s cupped hands. “Protect me,” it says, “and I will protect you.” The man is Manu, father of humankind; the fish is Matsya, Viṣṇu himself.

It grows too big for a pot, a pond, a lake and a river, and then it warns of a flood that will drown the world. Manu builds a ship and fills it with the seeds of life and the seven sages; the great horned fish tows it through the storm to safety. In later tellings Matsya also rescues the Vedas from a demon who stole them.

This guide follows the story from the oldest Vedic prose to the Purāṇas, and to the temple where the sun touches the fish-god’s feet.

THE OLDEST TELLING

Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 1.8.1
Morning water. As Manu washed his hands, a little fish came into them. “Rear me,” it said, “and I will save you.”
Pot, trench, sea. Manu kept it in a jar; when it outgrew that, in a trench; when it was a great fish, he took it to the sea.
The warning. The fish told him the year of a coming flood, and told him to build a ship.
The horn. When the flood rose, Manu tied the ship to the fish’s horn, and it drew him over the northern mountain. The place where the ship came down is called Manor avasarpaṇam, “Manu’s descent”.

ZAPPY: No Viṣṇu in this version?

PROF. BOLT: Not by name. Later the Purāṇas reveal that the fish was Viṣṇu.

THE PURĀṆA VERSION

Satyavrata and the growing fish
The kingSatyavrata, a royal sage, later the Manu of our age (Vaivasvata Manu)
The riverHe found the fish while offering water in the Kṛtamālā river in the south
Ever largerCup, pot, well, lake, river, ocean: each time the fish grew to fill it
The recognitionThe king knew no ordinary fish could do this, and the Lord revealed himself
The floodIn seven days the three worlds would sink; a ship would come for him

THE SHIP OF SEEDS

Riding out the flood
AboardManu, the seven sages (saptarṣi), and seeds of every plant and creature
The ropeIn the Bhāgavata, the serpent Vāsuki served as the rope tied to the fish’s horn
The teachingThrough the flood, Matsya taught Manu: this is said to be the Matsya Purāṇa
AfterWhen the waters fell, Manu began the new human race; we are “mānava”, children of Manu

ZAPPY: So humans are named after Manu?

PROF. BOLT: Mānava, manuṣya: yes, the words for human come from his name.

THE STOLEN VEDAS

Hayagrīva
The theftAt the end of an age, as Brahmā slept, the demon Hayagrīva stole the Vedas from his mouth and hid in the ocean
The rescueMatsya killed the demon and returned the Vedas to Brahmā
Another nameSome versions call the demon Śaṅkhāsura, who hid in a conch
Two HayagrīvasNot to be confused with Hayagrīva, the horse-headed form of Viṣṇu who is himself a god of learning
MeaningKnowledge is the most precious thing to save from a flood

pralaya-payodhi-jale dhṛtavān asi vedam, vihita-vahitra-caritram akhedam; keśava dhṛta-mīna-śarīra jaya jagadīśa hare

Jayadeva, Gītagovinda 1.5: “In the waters of dissolution you held the Veda like a ship, untiring. Keśava, in the body of a fish, victory, Lord of the world!”

THE MATSYA PURĀṆA

A whole Purāṇa named for him
One of 18The Matsya Purāṇa is one of the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas
FrameTold by the fish to Manu during the flood
ContentsCreation, genealogies of kings, vows and pilgrimages
ArtIt has chapters on temple building and image-making, studied by art historians
DatesParts are thought to be among the older Purāṇic layers

VEDANĀRĀYAṆA, NĀGALĀPURAM

The fish-god’s temple
WhereNāgalāpuram, Tirupati district, Andhra Pradesh, about 70 km from Tirupati
DeityVedanārāyaṇa: Viṣṇu with a fish’s tail, with Śrīdevī and Bhūdevī
BuilderTradition credits Kṛṣṇadevarāya, who named the town after his mother Nāgāmbā
Sūrya PūjāFor a few days each year the setting sun’s rays travel down the temple and touch the deity, from the feet upwards
Managed byTirumala Tirupati Devasthanams
