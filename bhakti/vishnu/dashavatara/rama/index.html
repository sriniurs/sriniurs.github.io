RĀMA

DAŚĀVATĀRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Prince of Ayodhyā, the ideal man

FOREWORD

The seventh avatāra is the most loved hero of India. Rāma, eldest son of King Daśaratha of Ayodhyā, gave up his throne to keep his father’s word, went into the forest for fourteen years with his wife Sītā and brother Lakṣmaṇa, lost Sītā to the demon king Rāvaṇa, and won her back with an army of monkeys and bears led by Hanumān.

The story is the Rāmāyaṇa, first told by the poet Vālmīki and retold in every Indian language and across Asia. Rāma is maryādā puruṣottama, the best of men who kept every bound of duty. Kiṣkindhā, where he met Hanumān, is the boulder country of Hampi in Karnataka.

This guide tells the epic in its seven books, and follows Rāma through poems, places and festivals.

ZAPPY: Why is Rāma so loved?

1. BĀLA KĀṆḌA

Childhood and the bow
BirthChildless Daśaratha performed a sacrifice; four sons were born: Rāma, Bharata, Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna
ViśvāmitraThe sage took the boys to protect his rites; Rāma killed the demoness Tāṭakā and freed Ahalyā from a curse
SītāAt Mithilā, King Janaka’s daughter, found in a furrow, was to marry whoever could string Śiva’s bow
The bowRāma lifted, strung and broke it
ParaśurāmaThe axe-bearing avatāra came in anger and yielded to Rāma
The meeting: Paraśurāma

2. AYODHYĀ KĀṆḌA

The exile
The promiseQueen Kaikeyī claimed two boons Daśaratha once gave her: her son Bharata to be king, Rāma to go to the forest for fourteen years
Rāma’s choiceHe went calmly to keep his father’s word; Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa insisted on going with him
DaśarathaThe king died of grief
BharataRefused the throne, placed Rāma’s sandals on it and ruled as his deputy

ZAPPY: He just accepted it?

PROF. BOLT: That's Rāma. His father's word mattered more than his own crown.

3. ARAṆYA KĀṆḌA

The forest and the golden deer
The forestThe three lived among sages at Citrakūṭa and Pañcavaṭī
ŚūrpaṇakhāRāvaṇa’s sister tried to seduce Rāma and attacked Sītā; Lakṣmaṇa cut off her nose
The golden deerA demon in disguise lured Rāma away; Lakṣmaṇa followed at Sītā’s urging
The abductionRāvaṇa, disguised as a holy man, carried Sītā off to Laṅkā
JaṭāyuThe old vulture fought Rāvaṇa and fell; he lived only to tell Rāma
ŚabarīA tribal woman devotee fed Rāma berries she had tasted first, and he ate them gladly

4. KIṢKINDHĀ KĀṆḌA

Hanumān and the monkey kingdom
HanumānAt Ṛṣyamūka, Rāma met Hanumān, minister of the exiled monkey prince Sugrīva
The pactRāma killed Sugrīva’s brother Vālī and made Sugrīva king; Sugrīva promised to find Sītā
HampiTradition places Kiṣkindhā around Hampi on the Tuṅgabhadrā; Añjanādri hill nearby is called Hanumān’s birthplace
Places at HampiṚṣyamūka, Mālyavanta hill (where Rāma waited out the rains), Paṃpā Sarovar (Śabarī’s lake)

ZAPPY: The Rāmāyaṇa happened in Karnataka?

PROF. BOLT: This part, by tradition. Hampi's boulders are Kiṣkindhā.

5. SUNDARA KĀṆḌA

Hanumān’s leap
The leapHanumān leaped across the ocean to Laṅkā
Sītā foundHe found her in the Aśoka grove, refusing Rāvaṇa, and gave her Rāma’s ring
Laṅkā burnsCaptured, his tail set on fire, he burned the city and leapt back
The signHe brought Rāma Sītā’s hair jewel (cūḍāmaṇi)
Sundara“The beautiful book”: much loved and recited on its own

ZAPPY: Why is it called beautiful?

PROF. BOLT: Because hope returns in it. Sītā is found, and Hanumān shows what devotion can do.

6. YUDDHA KĀṆḌA

The bridge and the war
VibhīṣaṇaRāvaṇa’s good brother came over to Rāma
RāmeśvaramRāma worshipped Śiva on the shore before the crossing
Rāma SetuThe monkeys built a bridge of floating stones across the sea
The warKumbhakarṇa and Indrajit fell; Lakṣmaṇa was revived by the herb Hanumān fetched on a whole mountain
RāvaṇaRāma killed him with the brahmāstra
SītāHer purity was proved by fire (agni parīkṣā)
See: Rāmeśvaram

7. UTTARA KĀṆḌA

Rāma-rājya and after
ReturnRāma came home to Ayodhyā; the city lit lamps, remembered in Dīpāvalī in North India
Rāma-rājyaHis reign became the model of just and happy rule
Sītā’s exileHearing gossip, Rāma sent Sītā away; in Vālmīki’s hermitage she bore the twins Lava and Kuśa
The endSītā returned to the Earth, her mother; Rāma later entered the Sarayū and returned to Viṣṇu
DebateThe Uttara Kāṇḍa is thought by many scholars to be a later addition, and its story of Sītā is much debated

ZAPPY: He sent Sītā away? That's not fair!

PROF. BOLT: Many readers, and many later poets, felt the same. The Rāmāyaṇa leaves us to argue about it.

MARYĀDĀ PURUṢOTTAMA

The ideal man
SonHe gave up a kingdom for his father’s word
BrotherHe and his brothers are models of loyalty
HusbandOne wife, Sītā, in an age of many
FriendTo Sugrīva, Vibhīṣaṇa, Guha the boatman and Śabarī alike
KingRāma-rājya, rule for the people
RāvaṇaEven his enemy was a great scholar and devotee of Śiva; tradition says Rāma honoured him in death
Rāvaṇa’s past life: Jaya and Vijaya

WHAT RĀMA TEACHES

The wisdom of the bow
Keep your wordA promise is worth a kingdom
Bear hardship calmlyExile without bitterness
Friendship across linesMonkeys, a boatman, a tribal woman, a demon’s brother
Hard questionsEven the ideal king faces choices we still debate

ZAPPY: What should I take from Rāma?

PROF. BOLT: Do your duty even when it hurts. And ask hard questions about it, as readers always have.

The whole guide in five lines
