STORIES BEHIND THE NAMES

Four stories hidden in verses 15–27
The four vyūhas (v15, v20). In the Pāñcarātra tradition the Lord appears as Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha: hence "of four emanations" (138) and Aniruddha (185).
Vāmana (v17). The demon-king Bali ruled the three worlds. Viṣṇu came as a dwarf boy (152) and asked for three steps of land; then grew into a giant (153) and measured earth and sky.
The mountain (v19). Mahādridhṛk (180): as Kūrma He bore Mount Mandara at the churning of the ocean; as Kṛṣṇa He lifted Govardhana to shelter Vraja from Indra's storm.
Nara and Nārāyaṇa (v26). Twin sages who meditated at Badarī in the Himalayas; the Mahābhārata says they were reborn as Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa.
The Puruṣa (v24). The Ṛg Veda's Cosmic Person, "thousand-headed, thousand-eyed, thousand-footed": names 224–227.

ZAPPY: Why tell stories in a list of names?

PROF. BOLT: Because each name is a doorway into a story, and stories are how the names stay in the heart.

VERSE 15 · NAMES 133–140

THE FOURFOLD LORD
lokādhyakṣaḥ surādhyakṣodharmādhyakṣaḥ kṛtākṛtaḥ |caturātmā caturvyūhaścaturdaṃṣṭraś caturbhujaḥ ‖15‖
He oversees the worlds, the gods and dharma; He is cause and effect; He has four forms, four emanations, four tusks (or teeth) and four arms.
No.NameMeans
133loka-adhyakṣaḥoverseer of the worlds
134sura-adhyakṣaḥoverseer of the gods
135dharma-adhyakṣaḥoverseer of dharma
136kṛta-akṛtaḥcause and effect
137catur-ātmāof fourfold form
138catur-vyūhaḥof four emanations
139catur-daṃṣṭraḥfour-tusked
140catur-bhujaḥfour-armed
The vyūhas. In the Pāñcarātra tradition, the Lord shows Himself in four vyūhas: Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha (name 185 comes later). Four arms hold conch, discus, mace and lotus.
The Lord in four forms and four arms.

Food and eater. Names 142–143 echo the Upaniṣads: "I am food, I am the eater of food". He is both the offering and the one who receives it. And Sahiṣṇu (144): the Lord who patiently bears our faults.
He is the offering and the one who receives it.

The dwarf who became the giant of three strides.

He is to be known, the physician, ever in yoga, the slayer of the strong (demons), the Lord of knowledge, sweetness itself; beyond the senses, of great power of illusion, great zeal, great strength.

Two mountains. Mahādridhṛk (180) is read as Kūrma, the tortoise who bore Mount Mandara at the churning of the ocean, and as Kṛṣṇa, who lifted Govardhana to shelter Vraja from Indra's storm.
The Lord who holds up the mountain over us.

ŚRĪNIVĀSA, GOVINDA

He is the great archer, the supporter of the earth, the dwelling of Śrī, the goal of the good; the unobstructed, the joy of the gods, Govinda, the master of the wise.

Two great Tirumala names. Śrīnivāsa (183) and Govinda (187) are the two names pilgrims shout at the Seven Hills: "Govindā! Govindā!". Aniruddha (185) is the fourth vyūha (see verse 15).
Śrīnivāsa and Govinda: the names of the Seven Hills.

SWAN, EAGLE AND SERPENT
marīcir damano haṃsaḥsuparṇo bhujagottamaḥ |hiraṇyanābhaḥ sutapāḥpadmanābhaḥ prajāpatiḥ ‖21‖
He is a ray of light, the subduer, the swan, the fair-winged eagle, the best of serpents; golden-navelled, of great austerity, lotus-navelled, lord of creatures.
No.NameMeans
189marīciḥa ray of light

191haṃsaḥthe swan
192suparṇaḥthe fair-winged (Garuḍa)

Haṃsa. The swan is said to separate milk from water: the discriminating soul. The Lord is also Suparṇa (Garuḍa) and the best of serpents (Śeṣa): both His mount and His bed are His own forms.
Swan, eagle and serpent: all His forms.

VERSE 23 · NAMES 209–217

THE TEACHER OF TEACHERS
gurur gurutamo dhāmasatyaḥ satyaparākramaḥ |nimiṣo'nimiṣaḥ sragvīvācaspatir udāradhīḥ ‖23‖
He is the teacher, the greatest teacher, the abode of light; the Truth, of true valour; the one with closed eyes (in yoga), the ever-watchful, wearing a garland, the lord of speech, of generous mind.

Closed and open eyes. Names 214–215 are a pair: nimiṣa, eyes closed in yoga-nidrā, and animiṣa, never blinking, always watching over His devotees. Both at once.
Eyes closed in yoga, yet always watching over us.

The Cosmic Person: a thousand heads, eyes and feet.

He turns the wheel of saṃsāra, yet His nature is detached; He is veiled (by māyā), the crusher (of evil); the day that brings dissolution, the fire, the wind, the bearer of the earth.

He turns the wheel, yet stays still at its centre.

VERSE 26 · NAMES 236–246

NĀRĀYAṆA, THE GRACIOUS
suprasādaḥ prasannātmāviśvadhṛg viśvabhug vibhuḥ |satkartā satkṛtaḥ sādhurjahnur nārāyaṇo naraḥ ‖26‖
He is most gracious, of serene nature, bearer and enjoyer of the universe, the all-pervading; He honours the good, is honoured by the good, the righteous, the one who withdraws all, Nārāyaṇa, the eternal guide.

Nārāyaṇa: the refuge of all beings.

VERSE 27 · NAMES 247–255

WHOSE WILL IS ALWAYS DONE
asaṅkhyeyo'prameyātmāviśiṣṭaḥ śiṣṭakṛc chuciḥ |siddhārthaḥ siddhasaṅkalpaḥsiddhidaḥ siddhisādhanaḥ ‖27‖
He is beyond counting, of immeasurable nature, the most excellent, who makes the good, the pure; whose aims are fulfilled, whose will is always accomplished, the giver of success, and the means to it.
No.NameMeans
247asaṅkhyeyaḥbeyond counting
248aprameya-ātmāof immeasurable nature
249viśiṣṭaḥmost excellent
250śiṣṭa-kṛtwho makes and guards the good

The siddha names. Four names in a row on siddhi, accomplishment: He has nothing left to gain (252), His will never fails (253), He gives success (254), and He is Himself the way to it (255).
His will is always done: He gives success, and is the way to it.
