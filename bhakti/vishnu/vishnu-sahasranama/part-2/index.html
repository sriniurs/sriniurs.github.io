"Viśvaṃ viṣṇur vaṣaṭkāraḥ" · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: First verses 1–14 for chanting in one go. Then one page for each verse: the chant, a picture, every name with its number and meaning, one insight and the one thing to remember.

THE NAMES BEGIN

In Part 1, Yudhiṣṭhira asked his six questions and Bhīṣma answered: praise the Lord with His thousand names. We prepared with the opening prayers, the nyāsa and the meditation verses.

Part 2 begins the names themselves: verses 1–14, names 1 to 132. They start with the widest view possible, "Viśvam", the universe, and move through the Self within, the creator, the refuge, time itself, and the Veda.

ZAPPY: How do you count names inside a verse?

PROF. BOLT: Each verse packs eight to twelve names. Most are single words; a few, like 'muktānāṃ paramā gatiḥ', are phrases counted as one name.

HOW TO READ A NAME

Every name is a teaching
Names from qualities. Bhīṣma called them gauṇa: each name describes a quality or deed of the Lord (Part 1, pūrvabhāga 13).
More than one meaning. Sanskrit packs meanings: Āditya is "the sun" and "Aditi's son, Vāmana". The pages give the main sense and note others.
Two great guides. Ādi Śaṅkara (Advaita) and Parāśara Bhaṭṭar (Viśiṣṭādvaita) explained every name. Meanings here follow the commonly given ones, mostly Śaṅkara's, and say where the two differ.
Counting. Some names are phrases, and lists differ slightly in where they split them. Numbers here follow shlokam.org.
Repeats. A few names appear twice, like Veda-vit in verse 14. Each time, the commentators find a different sense.

ZAPPY: So I don't have to memorise a thousand meanings?

PROF. BOLT: No! Chant the names; read one page at a time. Even one name understood changes how you chant all the others.

VERSE 1 · NAMES 1–9

THE UNIVERSE ITSELF
viśvaṃ viṣṇur vaṣaṭkārobhūtabhavyabhavatprabhuḥ |bhūtakṛd bhūtabhṛd bhāvobhūtātmā bhūtabhāvanaḥ ‖1‖
The first nine names: He is the universe, the all-pervading, the one invoked in sacrifice, Lord of past, future and present, maker, sustainer and being of all creatures, their Self and their nourisher.
No.NameMeans
1viśvamthe universe: all that is
2viṣṇuḥthe all-pervading
3vaṣaṭkāraḥinvoked with "vaṣaṭ" in Vedic sacrifice
4bhūta-bhavya-bhavat-prabhuḥLord of past, future and present
5bhūta-kṛtmaker of beings
6bhūta-bhṛtsustainer of beings
7bhāvaḥpure being, existence
8bhūta-ātmāthe Self of all beings
9bhūta-bhāvanaḥnourisher of beings
The first word is "universe". The thousand names open not with a god in a temple but with viśvam: everything. Then viṣṇuḥ, "the one who pervades it". The rest of this verse traces His relation to every being: He makes, holds, nourishes and is the Self of each.
The first name: Viśvam, the whole universe.

VERSE 2 · NAMES 10–17

THE WITNESS WITHIN
pūtātmā paramātmā camuktānāṃ paramā gatiḥ |avyayaḥ puruṣaḥ sākṣīkṣetrajño'kṣara eva ca ‖2‖
He is the pure Self, the Supreme Self, the final goal of the freed; the imperishable, the Person, the Witness, the knower of the field, and the indestructible.
No.NameMeans
10pūta-ātmāthe pure Self
11param-ātmāthe Supreme Self
12muktānāṃ paramā gatiḥhighest goal of the liberated
13avyayaḥimperishable
14puruṣaḥthe Person, dwelling in the body
15sākṣīthe Witness
16kṣetra-jñaḥknower of the field (body)
17akṣaraḥthe indestructible
Gītā words. "Kṣetra" and "kṣetrajña", the field and its knower, come straight from Bhagavad Gītā chapter 13. The Lord is the silent Witness (sākṣī) in every heart, seeing all and untouched by it.
The Lord is the silent Witness in every heart.

VERSE 3 · NAMES 18–24

YOGA, NARASIṂHA, KEŚAVA
yogo yogavidāṃ netāpradhānapuruṣeśvaraḥ |nārasiṃhavapuḥ śrīmānkeśavaḥ puruṣottamaḥ ‖3‖
He is Yoga itself, the leader of those who know yoga, Lord of nature and souls; in the form of the man-lion, with Śrī, with beautiful hair, the Supreme Person.
No.NameMeans
18yogaḥYoga: the goal reached by yoga
19yoga-vidāṃ netāleader of the yogis
20pradhāna-puruṣa-īśvaraḥLord of nature and souls
21nārasiṃha-vapuḥin the form of the man-lion
22śrīmānwith Śrī, glorious
23keśavaḥwith beautiful hair; slayer of Keśī
24puruṣa-uttamaḥthe Supreme Person
The first avatāra named. Name 21 is Narasiṃha, the man-lion who saved young Prahlāda. Some see it as a reminder early on: the Lord protects His devotee instantly. Puruṣottama (24) is the title of Gītā chapter 15.
Yoga's goal, the man-lion, the Supreme Person.

VERSE 4 · NAMES 25–36

ŚIVA, THE AUSPICIOUS
sarvaḥ śarvaḥ śivaḥ sthāṇurbhūtādir nidhir avyayaḥ |sambhavo bhāvano bhartāprabhavaḥ prabhur īśvaraḥ ‖4‖
He is everything, the destroyer, the auspicious, the steady, the origin of the elements, the imperishable treasure; He who comes forth, the giver, the supporter, the source, the master, the Lord.
No.NameMeans
25sarvaḥthe All
26śarvaḥthe destroyer (at dissolution)
27śivaḥthe auspicious, pure
28sthāṇuḥthe steady, unmoving
29bhūta-ādiḥorigin of the elements
30nidhiḥ avyayaḥimperishable treasure
31sambhavaḥwho comes forth (in avatāras)
32bhāvanaḥgiver of fruits
33bhartāsupporter
34prabhavaḥsource
35prabhuḥmaster
36īśvaraḥthe Lord
Śiva in the names of Viṣṇu. Śarva, Śiva and Sthāṇu are also names of Śiva. Śaṅkara reads them to show the oneness of the divine; Parāśara Bhaṭṭar reads them as qualities of Viṣṇu ("the auspicious", "the steady"). Both readings are traditional.
"Śivaḥ": the auspicious one, read in two ways.

VERSE 5 · NAMES 37–45

SELF-BORN, WITHOUT BEGINNING
svayambhūḥ śambhur ādityaḥpuṣkarākṣo mahāsvanaḥ |anādinidhano dhātāvidhātā dhāturuttamaḥ ‖5‖
He is self-existent, the giver of happiness, the sun, lotus-eyed, of mighty sound; without beginning or end, the creator, the arranger, and the best of supports.
No.NameMeans
37svayambhūḥself-existent
38śambhuḥgiver of happiness
39ādityaḥthe sun; son of Aditi (Vāmana)
40puṣkara-akṣaḥlotus-eyed
41mahā-svanaḥof mighty sound: the Veda
42anādi-nidhanaḥwithout beginning or end
43dhātāthe creator, supporter
44vidhātāthe arranger, giver of results
45dhātuḥ uttamaḥthe best of supports
"Mighty sound". Mahāsvana is read as the Veda, His great voice. Āditya can mean the sun, or Vāmana, born to Aditi. Many names in the Sahasranāma carry two or more such meanings at once.
Self-born, without beginning or end.

VERSE 6 · NAMES 46–54

LORD OF THE SENSES
aprameyo hṛṣīkeśaḥpadmanābho'maraprabhuḥ |viśvakarmā manus tvaṣṭāsthaviṣṭhaḥ sthaviro dhruvaḥ ‖6‖
He is immeasurable, master of the senses, lotus-navelled, Lord of the immortals; maker of the universe, the thinker, the shaper, the vast, the ancient, the unmoving.
No.NameMeans
46aprameyaḥimmeasurable
47hṛṣīka-īśaḥmaster of the senses
48padma-nābhaḥlotus-navelled
49amara-prabhuḥLord of the gods
50viśva-karmāmaker of the universe
51manuḥthe thinker
52tvaṣṭāthe shaper
53sthaviṣṭhaḥthe most vast
54sthaviraḥ dhruvaḥthe ancient, the unmoving
Hṛṣīkeśa. "Lord of the senses" (hṛṣīka). In the Gītā, Arjuna calls Kṛṣṇa Hṛṣīkeśa as He drives the chariot: the horses are the senses, the reins the mind, and He holds them.
Hṛṣīkeśa: He holds the reins of the senses.
Here, as on shlokam.org, "sthaviraḥ dhruvaḥ" is counted as one name (54); some lists split it.

VERSE 7 · NAMES 55–63

KṚṢṆA, THE DARK ONE
agrāhyaḥ śāśvataḥ kṛṣṇolohitākṣaḥ pratardanaḥ |prabhūtas trikakubdhāmapavitraṃ maṅgalaṃ param ‖7‖
He cannot be grasped; He is eternal, Kṛṣṇa, red-eyed, the destroyer; full in glory, the support of the three regions, the purifier, the supreme auspiciousness.
No.NameMeans
55agrāhyaḥwho cannot be grasped by the senses
56śāśvataḥeternal
57kṛṣṇaḥthe dark one; "existence-bliss"
58lohita-akṣaḥred-eyed
59pratardanaḥdestroyer (at dissolution)
60prabhūtaḥfull of glory
61trikakub-dhāmasupport of the three regions
62pavitramthe purifier
63maṅgalaṃ paramsupreme auspiciousness
Kṛṣṇa (57). Literally "dark", but Śaṅkara quotes a derivation from kṛṣ (existence) + ṇa (bliss): "existence-bliss". Like many names, it is both a description and a teaching.
Kṛṣṇa: dark, and existence-bliss.

VERSE 8 · NAMES 64–73

GIVER OF LIFE
īśānaḥ prāṇadaḥ prāṇojyeṣṭhaḥ śreṣṭhaḥ prajāpatiḥ |hiraṇyagarbho bhūgarbhomādhavo madhusūdanaḥ ‖8‖
He is the ruler, the giver of life, life itself, the eldest, the best, the lord of creatures; the golden womb, the womb of the earth, the husband of Mā, the slayer of Madhu.
No.NameMeans
64īśānaḥthe ruler
65prāṇa-daḥgiver of life
66prāṇaḥlife itself, breath
67jyeṣṭhaḥthe eldest
68śreṣṭhaḥthe best
69prajā-patiḥlord of creatures
70hiraṇya-garbhaḥthe golden womb of creation
71bhū-garbhaḥwho holds the earth within
72mādhavaḥhusband of Mā (Lakṣmī)
73madhu-sūdanaḥslayer of the demon Madhu
Hiraṇyagarbha. The "golden womb" of the Ṛg Veda: the first seed from which the cosmos unfolds. And then, in the same line, the tender name Mādhava, Lakṣmī's husband. Cosmic and intimate side by side.
Life itself, the golden seed of creation.

VERSE 9 · NAMES 74–84

THE GRATEFUL LORD
īśvaro vikramī dhanvīmedhāvī vikramaḥ kramaḥ |anuttamo durādharṣaḥkṛtajñaḥ kṛtir ātmavān ‖9‖
He is the Lord, the valiant, the bowman, the wise, the great stride, the pervader; unsurpassed, unconquerable, grateful, the act itself, the Self-possessed.
No.NameMeans
74īśvaraḥthe Lord, all-powerful
75vikramīvaliant
76dhanvīthe bowman
77medhāvīthe wise
78vikramaḥthe great stride (Vāmana)
79kramaḥthe pervader
80anuttamaḥunsurpassed
81durādharṣaḥunconquerable
82kṛta-jñaḥgrateful: remembers every offering
83kṛtiḥthe act, effort
84ātmavānSelf-possessed
Kṛtajña: the grateful God. Name 82 is a favourite: the Lord "knows what has been done". He remembers even a leaf or flower offered with love (Gītā 9.26), and forgets our failings. A God who says thank you!
Kṛtajña: the Lord who remembers every offering.

VERSE 10 · NAMES 85–94

OUR REFUGE, OUR JOY
sureśaḥ śaraṇaṃ śarmaviśvaretāḥ prajābhavaḥ |ahaḥ saṃvatsaro vyālaḥpratyayaḥ sarvadarśanaḥ ‖10‖
He is Lord of the gods, the refuge, bliss, the seed of the universe, the source of beings; the day, the year, the elusive one, the knowledge, the all-seeing.
No.NameMeans
85sura-īśaḥLord of the gods
86śaraṇamthe refuge
87śarmabliss, shelter
88viśva-retāḥseed of the universe
89prajā-bhavaḥsource of all beings
90ahaḥthe day
91saṃvatsaraḥthe year
92vyālaḥelusive, like a serpent
93pratyayaḥknowledge, conviction
94sarva-darśanaḥthe all-seeing
Śaraṇam. Name 86 is simply "refuge": the heart of the Śrīvaiṣṇava way (see the Suprabhātam's Prapatti). He is also time itself, the day and the year: every moment belongs to Him.
"Śaraṇam": He is our refuge.

VERSE 11 · NAMES 95–103

ACYUTA, THE UNFAILING
ajaḥ sarveśvaraḥ siddhaḥsiddhiḥ sarvādir acyutaḥ |vṛṣākapir ameyātmāsarvayogavinissṛtaḥ ‖11‖
He is unborn, Lord of all, the perfect, the goal, the beginning of all, the unfailing; Dharma and Varāha in one, of immeasurable nature, revealed by all the paths of yoga.
No.NameMeans
95ajaḥunborn
96sarva-īśvaraḥLord of all
97siddhaḥthe perfect, ever-accomplished
98siddhiḥthe goal itself
99sarva-ādiḥthe beginning of all
100acyutaḥthe unfailing
101vṛṣākapiḥDharma and Varāha
102ameya-ātmāof immeasurable nature
103sarva-yoga-viniḥsṛtaḥknown through every path
Name 100: Acyuta. "He who never falls", and never lets His devotees fall (as in the Acyutāṣṭakam). Vṛṣākapi (101) is read as vṛṣa (dharma) + kapi (boar, Varāha): the one who lifted dharma and the earth.
Name 100: Acyuta, the One who never lets us fall.

VERSE 12 · NAMES 104–113

THE TRUE, THE EQUAL
vasur vasumanāḥ satyaḥsamātmā sammitaḥ samaḥ |amoghaḥ puṇḍarīkākṣovṛṣakarmā vṛṣākṛtiḥ ‖12‖
He is the dwelling of all, the generous-hearted, the Truth; the same in all, measured by none, impartial; whose acts never fail, lotus-eyed, whose deeds and form are dharma.
No.NameMeans
104vasuḥdwelling of all; wealth
105vasu-manāḥgenerous-hearted
106satyaḥthe Truth
107sama-ātmāthe same Self in all
108sammitaḥmeasured (known) by scripture
109samaḥimpartial, equal to all
110amoghaḥwhose acts never fail
111puṇḍarīka-akṣaḥlotus-eyed
112vṛṣa-karmāwhose deeds are dharma
113vṛṣa-ākṛtiḥwhose form is dharma
Samaḥ: the impartial. The Lord is the same to all (Gītā 9.29), loving every being equally. Name 108, sammita, is variously read; Śaṅkara takes it as "measured by all things", Bhaṭṭar as "who measures Himself to His devotees".
The Truth, equal to every being.
Readings of sammitaḥ (108) differ between commentators.

VERSE 13 · NAMES 114–122

THE THOUSAND-HEADED
rudro bahuśirā babhrurviśvayoniḥ śuciśravāḥ |amṛtaḥ śāśvatasthāṇurvarāroho mahātapāḥ ‖13‖
He is the one who makes others weep (at dissolution), many-headed, the supporter, the womb of the universe, of pure fame; the immortal, the eternal and steady, the excellent goal, of great austerity.
No.NameMeans
114rudraḥwho makes the world weep at dissolution
115bahu-śirāḥmany-headed
116babhruḥthe supporter
117viśva-yoniḥwomb of the universe
118śuci-śravāḥof pure fame, whose names are pure
119amṛtaḥthe immortal
120śāśvata-sthāṇuḥeternal and steady
121vara-ārohaḥthe excellent goal
122mahā-tapāḥof great austerity
Many-headed. Bahuśirāḥ recalls the Puruṣa Sūkta: "the Person has a thousand heads, a thousand eyes, a thousand feet". The Lord sees, thinks and acts through every being. Rudra again carries two readings, as Śiva did in verse 4.
A thousand heads: He sees through every being.

VERSE 14 · NAMES 123–132

THE VEDA AND ITS KNOWER
sarvagaḥ sarvavid bhānurviṣvakseno janārdanaḥ |vedo vedavid avyaṅgovedāṅgo vedavit kaviḥ ‖14‖
He is all-pervading, all-knowing, the radiant; whose army scatters everywhere, who troubles the wicked; He is the Veda, the knower of the Veda, the flawless, whose limbs are the Vedic sciences, the teacher of the Veda, the seer-poet.
No.NameMeans
123sarva-gaḥall-pervading
124sarva-vid bhānuḥall-knowing and radiant
125viṣvaksenaḥwhose army scatters the foe
126janārdanaḥwho afflicts the wicked; sought by people
127vedaḥthe Veda itself
128veda-vitknower of the Veda
129avyaṅgaḥflawless, complete
130veda-aṅgaḥwhose limbs are the Vedāṅgas
131veda-vitwho makes the Veda known
132kaviḥthe seer, the poet
Veda-vit, twice. The name appears at 128 and 131. Śaṅkara explains: first "He who knows the Veda", then "He who reflects on it" or "makes it known". The Sahasranāma never repeats a name without a different sense.
He is the Veda, and the seer who knows it.

PART 2 ON ONE PAGE

Names 1–132 in five lines
Verses 1–2. The universe, the all-pervading, Lord of all time; the Supreme Self, the silent Witness within.
Verses 3–5. Yoga’s goal, the man-lion, the Supreme Person; Śiva the auspicious; self-born, without beginning or end.
Verses 6–8. Hṛṣīkeśa, master of the senses; Kṛṣṇa, existence-bliss; life itself, the golden seed, Mādhava.
Verses 9–11. The grateful Lord who remembers every offering; our refuge; Acyuta, name 100, who never lets us fall.
Verses 12–14. The Truth, equal to all; the many-headed Puruṣa; the Veda and its seer.
