PART 10 · THE UTTARABHĀGA · THE FRUITS
The thousand names of Viṣṇu · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: First all of Part 10 for chanting in one go. Then the story and background, and one page for each verse: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

THE LAST WORD

In Parts 1–9, Yudhiṣṭhira asked his questions, Bhīṣma answered with a thousand names, and we went through every one, from "Viśvam", the universe, to "Sarvapraharaṇāyudha", whose weapons are all things.

Part 10 is the uttarabhāga, the closing section: 33 verses. First Bhīṣma tells what chanting the names brings (verses 1–22). Then others speak: Arjuna prays, Kṛṣṇa promises that one verse is enough, Vyāsa bows, Pārvatī asks for a shortcut and Śiva gives "Śrī Rāma Rāma Rāma", Brahmā praises, Sañjaya speaks the Gītā's last verse, and it ends with prayers for all who suffer, and an offering of everything we do.

Hari Oṃ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is there really a shortcut for all thousand names?

PROF. BOLT: Śiva himself gives it in verse 27: 'Śrī Rāma Rāma Rāma'. And Kṛṣṇa says even one sincere verse is enough!

ZAPPY: Śiva, Brahmā, Vyāsa, Arjuna: all together?

PROF. BOLT: The hymn ends like a festival: gods, sages and devotees all join in praising the same Lord.

THE PROMISES, IN SHORT

What Bhīṣma says the names bring
For everyone. Hearing alone is enough (2); every calling is blessed (3); whatever you seek, you find (4).
How. Rise early, be clean, keep your mind on Him, chant with devotion (5).
Outer gifts. Good name, steady fortune, health, freedom from illness, bondage, fear and danger (6–9).
Inner gifts. Joy, patience, steadiness, memory; no anger, envy or greed (12–13).
The highest gift. Purified, the devotee reaches the eternal, and is never defeated (10, 22).

ZAPPY: Is it just about getting things?

PROF. BOLT: The list starts with worldly goods but rises to inner peace and liberation. Bhīṣma meets everyone where they are, and leads them higher.

PHALAŚRUTI 1

THE THOUSAND, COMPLETE
itīdaṃ kīrtanīyasyakeśavasya mahātmanaḥ |nāmnāṃ sahasraṃ divyānāmaśeṣeṇa prakīrtitam ‖1‖
Thus have the thousand divine names of the great Keśava, who is worthy of praise, been declared in full.
So, says Bhīṣma, I have told you all thousand divine names of the great Lord Keśava.
WordMeans
iti idamthus this
kīrtanīyasyaof the one worthy of praise
keśavasyaof Keśava
divyānāmdivine
aśeṣeṇain full, without remainder
prakīrtitamhas been declared
Bhīṣma speaks again. The names are done. Now he tells Yudhiṣṭhira what they bring: the phalaśruti, "the hearing of the fruits". Verses 1–22 are his; then Arjuna, Kṛṣṇa, Vyāsa, Śiva, Brahmā and Sañjaya each add a verse.
The thousand names, given in full.

PHALAŚRUTI 2

HEARING IS ENOUGH
ya idaṃ śṛṇuyān nityaṃyaś cāpi parikīrtayet |nāśubhaṃ prāpnuyāt kiñcitso'mutreha ca mānavaḥ ‖2‖
Whoever hears this every day, or recites it, meets with no harm at all, here or hereafter.
Whether you chant it or simply listen to it daily, nothing bad will come to you, in this life or the next.
WordMeans
śṛṇuyātwould hear
nityamdaily
parikīrtayetwould recite
na aśubhamno harm
kiñcitat all
amutra ihahereafter and here
Listening counts. The first promise is generous: you need not chant; even listening every day is enough. That is why the Sahasranāma is so often played in homes in the morning.
Even listening daily brings its blessing.

PHALAŚRUTI 3

A BLESSING FOR EVERY CALLING
vedāntago brāhmaṇaḥ syātkṣatriyo vijayī bhavet |vaiśyo dhanasamṛddhaḥ syācchūdraḥ sukham avāpnuyāt ‖3‖
The brāhmaṇa would master the Vedānta, the kṣatriya would be victorious, the vaiśya would grow rich, and the śūdra would find happiness.
Whatever your work, says Bhīṣma, the names bring success in it: the scholar in learning, the leader in victory, the trader in wealth, the worker in happiness.
WordMeans
vedānta-gaḥmaster of the Vedānta
vijayīvictorious
dhana-samṛddhaḥrich in wealth
sukhamhappiness
avāpnuyātwould attain
bhavet · syātwould be
For everyone. Bhīṣma speaks in the social terms of his age, but the point is universal: the hymn is for all, and helps each person in their own calling. No one is excluded from chanting the names.
Whatever your calling, the names bless it.

PHALAŚRUTI 4

WHATEVER YOU SEEK
dharmārthī prāpnuyād dharmamarthārthī cārtham āpnuyāt |kāmān avāpnuyāt kāmīprajārthī cāpnuyāt prajām ‖4‖
One who seeks dharma gains dharma; one who seeks wealth gains wealth; one who desires pleasures gains them; one who longs for children gains children.
Whatever you are seeking, goodness, prosperity, happiness or a family, the names help you find it.
WordMeans
dharma-arthīseeker of dharma
artha-arthīseeker of wealth
kāmīone with desires
prajā-arthīone who wants children
prāpnuyātwould gain
āpnuyātwould obtain
The aims of life. Hindu thought names four aims: dharma, artha, kāma and mokṣa. This verse promises the first three; the following verses lead to the fourth, liberation. Bhīṣma meets people where they are.
Whatever you seek, He gives.

PHALAŚRUTI 5

HOW TO CHANT
bhaktimān yaḥ sadotthāyaśucis tadgatamānasaḥ |sahasraṃ vāsudevasyanāmnām etat prakīrtayet ‖5‖
The devoted person who, rising early, pure, with mind fixed on Him, recites these thousand names of Vāsudeva…
Rise early, wash, sit with your mind on the Lord and chant these thousand names with devotion…
WordMeans
bhaktimāndevoted
sadā utthāyarising early, always
śuciḥpure, clean
tat-gata-mānasaḥwith mind fixed on Him
vāsudevasyaof Vāsudeva
prakīrtayetwould recite
Bhīṣma's method. Three simple conditions: devotion, an early start, and a clean body with the mind on the Lord. Not perfection, not learning: just sincerity and regularity.
Rise early, be clean, keep your mind on Him.

PHALAŚRUTI 6

LASTING GOOD FORTUNE
yaśaḥ prāpnoti vipulaṃjñātiprādhānyam eva ca |acalāṃ śriyam āpnotiśreyaḥ prāpnoty anuttamam ‖6‖
…gains wide fame and the first place among their kin, gains steady fortune, and reaches the highest good.
…they become well known, respected in their family, steadily prosperous, and reach the highest good of all.
WordMeans
yaśaḥ vipulamwide fame
jñāti-prādhānyamfirst place among kin
acalām śriyamsteady fortune
śreyaḥthe highest good
anuttamamunsurpassed
āpnotigains
Steady, not fleeting. Acalā śrī, "fortune that does not move": not a lucky windfall but lasting well-being. And beyond all fortune, śreyas: the true good of the soul.
Fortune that lasts, and the highest good.
shlokam.org reads yātiprādhānyam; we follow the usual jñātiprādhānyam.

PHALAŚRUTI 7

NO FEAR, GOOD HEALTH
na bhayaṃ kvacid āpnotivīryaṃ tejaś ca vindati |bhavaty arogo dyutimānbalarūpaguṇānvitaḥ ‖7‖
They never meet with fear; they gain vigour and radiance; they become free of illness, bright, endowed with strength, beauty and virtue.
They become fearless, energetic, healthy and radiant, strong in body and good in character.
WordMeans
na bhayamno fear
vīryamvigour
tejaḥradiance
arogaḥfree of illness
dyutimānbright
bala-rūpa-guṇastrength, beauty, virtue
Body and mind. The fruits include health and courage. Many find that regular chanting calms the mind and steadies the breath. Fearlessness (na bhayam) answers Yudhiṣṭhira's worry in Part 1.
Fearless, healthy, radiant.

PHALAŚRUTI 8

FREED FROM EVERY TROUBLE
rogārto mucyate rogādbaddho mucyeta bandhanāt |bhayān mucyeta bhītas tumucyetāpanna āpadaḥ ‖8‖
The sick is freed from sickness, the bound from bondage, the frightened from fear, the one in trouble from trouble.
The ill recover, the trapped are freed, the afraid find courage, those in danger are rescued.
WordMeans
roga-ārtaḥone suffering illness
baddhaḥone who is bound
bhītaḥone who is afraid
āpannaḥone in trouble
mucyateis freed
āpadaḥfrom danger
Four freedoms. Illness, bondage, fear, danger: the four great troubles of life, each answered with "is freed" (mucyate). The names have already called the Lord Vaidya, the physician, and Bheṣaja, the medicine.
From illness, bonds, fear and danger: freedom.

PHALAŚRUTI 9

CROSSING EVERY HARDSHIP
durgāṇy atitaraty āśupuruṣaḥ puruṣottamam |stuvan nāmasahasreṇanityaṃ bhaktisamanvitaḥ ‖9‖
A person who praises the Supreme Person daily with the thousand names, full of devotion, quickly crosses all difficulties.
Praise the Lord daily with love through these names, and you will soon get past every hardship.
WordMeans
durgāṇidifficulties, hard places
atitaraticrosses over
āśuquickly
puruṣa-uttamamthe Supreme Person
nityamdaily
bhakti-samanvitaḥfull of devotion
Durga: a fortress, a hard place. Life has its steep passes. The verse does not promise none will come, but that we will cross them, and quickly. The key words again: nityam (daily) and bhakti (love).
Daily, with love: every hard pass is crossed.

PHALAŚRUTI 10

TO THE ETERNAL
vāsudevāśrayo martyovāsudevaparāyaṇaḥ |sarvapāpaviśuddhātmāyāti brahma sanātanam ‖10‖
The mortal who takes refuge in Vāsudeva, devoted to Him as the supreme goal, purified of all sins, reaches the eternal Brahman.
Whoever takes shelter in the Lord and makes Him their goal is washed clean of all wrongs and reaches the eternal.
WordMeans
vāsudeva-āśrayaḥwho takes refuge in Vāsudeva
martyaḥa mortal
parāyaṇaḥdevoted, as the goal
sarva-pāpa-viśuddhapurified of all sins
yātireaches
brahma sanātanamthe eternal Brahman
The fourth aim. After dharma, wealth and desire (verse 4), here is the last: mokṣa, reaching the eternal. And it comes through āśraya, refuge: the same surrender as the Prapatti.
Refuge in Him leads to the eternal.

PHALAŚRUTI 11

NO HARM FOR HIS DEVOTEES
na vāsudevabhaktānāmaśubhaṃ vidyate kvacit |janmamṛtyujarāvyādhi-bhayaṃ naivopajāyate ‖11‖
For the devotees of Vāsudeva there is no harm anywhere; the fear of birth, death, old age and disease never arises.
Nothing truly bad can touch the Lord's devotees; they lose the fear of birth, death, ageing and illness.
WordMeans
vāsudeva-bhaktānāmfor Vāsudeva's devotees
aśubhamharm
kvacitanywhere
janma · mṛtyubirth · death
jarā · vyādhiold age · disease
na eva upajāyatenever arises
The fear, not only the fact. Devotees still age and fall ill; what goes is the fear (bhaya). The four sights that sent the Buddha seeking (birth, age, sickness, death) lose their terror for one who rests in the Lord.
Age and death remain, but the fear goes.

PHALAŚRUTI 12

SEVEN GIFTS
imaṃ stavam adhīyānaḥśraddhābhaktisamanvitaḥ |yujyetātmasukhakṣānti-śrīdhṛtismṛtikīrtibhiḥ ‖12‖
One who studies this hymn with faith and devotion is blessed with inner happiness, patience, prosperity, steadiness, memory and good name.
Chant it with faith and love, and you gain inner joy, patience, prosperity, steadiness, a good memory and a good name.
WordMeans
adhīyānaḥstudying, reciting
śraddhā · bhaktifaith · devotion
ātma-sukhainner happiness
kṣāntipatience
dhṛti · smṛtisteadiness · memory
kīrtigood name
Gifts of character. Most of these are inner: joy, patience, steadiness, memory. Śraddhā (faith) and bhakti (love) are the two conditions. For students, smṛti, a good memory, is a much-loved promise.
Faith and love bring joy, patience and a steady mind.

PHALAŚRUTI 13

FREE FROM ANGER AND ENVY
na krodho na ca mātsaryaṃna lobho nāśubhā matiḥ |bhavanti kṛtapuṇyānāṃbhaktānāṃ puruṣottame ‖13‖
Anger, envy, greed and evil thoughts do not arise in the virtuous devotees of the Supreme Person.
Those who love the Lord lose their anger, jealousy, greed and bad thoughts.
WordMeans
krodhaḥanger
mātsaryamenvy
lobhaḥgreed
aśubhā matiḥevil thoughts
kṛta-puṇyānāmof the virtuous
puruṣa-uttametoward the Supreme Person
Inner change. The names change not just our fortune but our nature. Recall Krodhahā (314) and Jitamanyu (934): the Lord who has no anger takes ours away too.
The names wash away anger, envy and greed.

PHALAŚRUTI 14

HE HOLDS IT ALL
dyauḥ sacandrārkanakṣatrākhaṃ diśo bhūr mahodadhiḥ |vāsudevasya vīryeṇavidhṛtāni mahātmanaḥ ‖14‖
The heavens with the sun, moon and stars, the sky, the directions, the earth and the great ocean are held up by the power of the great Vāsudeva.
Sun, moon and stars, sky and earth and ocean: all are held in place by the Lord's power.
WordMeans
dyauḥthe heavens
candra-arka-nakṣatrāmoon, sun and stars
kham · diśaḥsky · directions
bhūḥ · mahodadhiḥearth · the great ocean
vīryeṇaby the power
vidhṛtāniare held up
Why the names work. Verses 14–20 explain: the One whose names we chant holds the whole cosmos. No wonder His names can hold up one human life.
He who holds the stars can hold us too.

PHALAŚRUTI 15

ALL BEINGS IN HIS CARE
sasurāsuragandharvaṃsayakṣoragarākṣasam |jagad vaśe vartatedaṃkṛṣṇasya sacarācaram ‖15‖
This whole world, moving and unmoving, with gods, demons, gandharvas, yakṣas, serpents and rākṣasas, is under the control of Kṛṣṇa.
Every being in the world, gods and demons, spirits and serpents, things that move and things that stand still, is in Kṛṣṇa's hands.
WordMeans
sura · asuragods · demons
gandharva · yakṣacelestial musicians · nature spirits
uraga · rākṣasaserpents · demons
jagatthe world
vaśe vartateis under the control
sa-cara-acarammoving and unmoving
Even the demons. The list includes asuras and rākṣasas: nothing is outside His rule. Good and bad, friendly and hostile: all are within His care and control.
Every being, friendly or not, is in His hands.

PHALAŚRUTI 16

BODY AND MIND ARE HIS
indriyāṇi mano buddhiḥsattvaṃ tejo balaṃ dhṛtiḥ |vāsudevātmakāny āhuḥkṣetraṃ kṣetrajña eva ca ‖16‖
The senses, mind, intellect, goodness, energy, strength and steadiness, the field and the knower of the field: all, they say, have Vāsudeva as their Self.
Our senses, mind and intelligence, our energy and strength, the body and the one who knows it: all are the Lord's.
WordMeans
indriyāṇithe senses
manaḥ · buddhiḥmind · intellect
tejaḥ · balamenergy · strength
dhṛtiḥsteadiness
vāsudeva-ātmakānihaving Vāsudeva as their Self
kṣetram · kṣetrajñaḥthe field · its knower
From cosmos to self. Verse 14 was the stars; here it is our own body and mind. The Gītā's "field" and "knower of the field" (see name 16) are both His.
Even our own mind and body belong to Him.

PHALAŚRUTI 17

GOOD CONDUCT COMES FIRST
sarvāgamānām ācāraḥprathamaṃ parikalpyate |ācāraprabhavo dharmodharmasya prabhur acyutaḥ ‖17‖
Of all the scriptures, good conduct is placed first; dharma springs from good conduct; and the Lord of dharma is Acyuta.
All scriptures put good conduct first; right living grows from it; and the Lord is the master of right living.
WordMeans
sarva-āgamānāmof all scriptures
ācāraḥgood conduct
prathamamfirst
ācāra-prabhavaḥborn of good conduct
dharmaḥdharma
prabhuḥ acyutaḥAcyuta is its Lord
Practice before theory. Bhīṣma, the old teacher of dharma, ends on conduct: what we do comes first. Chanting is not a substitute for living well; it supports it.
Conduct first; dharma follows; the Lord crowns it.
shlokam.org reads parikalpate; we follow the usual parikalpyate.

PHALAŚRUTI 18

ALL FROM NĀRĀYAṆA
ṛṣayaḥ pitaro devāmahābhūtāni dhātavaḥ |jaṅgamājaṅgamaṃ cedaṃjagan nārāyaṇodbhavam ‖18‖
The sages, ancestors, gods, the great elements and minerals, and this whole world of moving and unmoving things, have their origin in Nārāyaṇa.
Sages and ancestors, gods and elements, everything that moves or stands still, all come from Nārāyaṇa.
WordMeans
ṛṣayaḥsages
pitaraḥancestors
devāḥgods
mahā-bhūtānithe great elements
jaṅgama-ajaṅgamammoving and unmoving
nārāyaṇa-udbhavamborn of Nārāyaṇa
One source. Every being we honour, sages, ancestors, gods, comes from Him. Honouring Him honours all of them.
Everything comes from Nārāyaṇa.

PHALAŚRUTI 19

ALL KNOWLEDGE AND SKILL
yogo jñānaṃ tathā sāṅkhyaṃvidyāḥ śilpādi karma ca |vedāḥ śāstrāṇi vijñānametat sarvaṃ janārdanāt ‖19‖
Yoga, knowledge, Sāṅkhya, the sciences, the arts and crafts, work, the Vedas, the scriptures and practical wisdom: all this comes from Janārdana.
Yoga and philosophy, every science and art and craft, all work, the Vedas and all learning, come from the Lord.
WordMeans
yogaḥ · jñānamyoga · knowledge
sāṅkhyamSāṅkhya philosophy
vidyāḥthe sciences
śilpa-ādi karmaarts, crafts and work
vijñānampractical wisdom
janārdanātfrom Janārdana
Every skill is sacred. Not only scripture: arts, crafts, sciences and work all come from the Lord. A surgeon's skill, a potter's, a programmer's: each can be an offering.
Every science and skill comes from Him.

PHALAŚRUTI 20

ONE, IN MANY
eko viṣṇur mahad bhūtaṃpṛthagbhūtāny anekaśaḥ |trīṃl lokān vyāpya bhūtātmābhuṅkte viśvabhug avyayaḥ ‖20‖
The one Viṣṇu, the great Being, appears as many separate beings; pervading the three worlds as their Self, the imperishable enjoyer of all enjoys them.
There is one Viṣṇu, yet He appears as countless beings; living in all three worlds as their Self, He experiences everything.
WordMeans
ekaḥ viṣṇuḥthe one Viṣṇu
mahat bhūtamthe great Being
pṛthak bhūtāniseparate beings
anekaśaḥin many ways
vyāpyapervading
viśva-bhuk avyayaḥthe imperishable enjoyer of all
One sun, many reflections. This verse sums up the theology of the whole hymn: one Lord, many beings, He in all of them. That is why a thousand names can all point to one God.
One Viṣṇu, shining in every being.

PHALAŚRUTI 21

VYĀSA'S HYMN
imaṃ stavaṃ bhagavatoviṣṇor vyāsena kīrtitam |paṭhed ya icchet puruṣaḥśreyaḥ prāptuṃ sukhāni ca ‖21‖
Whoever wishes to gain the highest good and happiness should recite this hymn to the Lord Viṣṇu, composed by Vyāsa.
If you want true good and happiness, recite this hymn to Viṣṇu that Vyāsa gave us.
WordMeans
imam stavamthis hymn
vyāsena kīrtitamsung by Vyāsa
paṭhetshould recite
icchetwho wishes
śreyaḥthe highest good
sukhānihappiness
Vyāsa's gift. The seer named in Part 1 (opening prayers and pūrvabhāga 14) is credited again. The two goals, śreyas (the good) and sukha (happiness), are both promised.
Vyāsa's hymn: for the good and for happiness.

PHALAŚRUTI 22

NEVER DEFEATED
viśveśvaram ajaṃ devaṃjagataḥ prabhum avyayam |bhajanti ye puṣkarākṣaṃna te yānti parābhavam ‖22‖na te yānti parābhavamoṃ nama iti |
Those who worship the lotus-eyed God, the Lord of the universe, unborn, the imperishable master of the world, never meet with defeat.
WordMeans
viśva-īśvaramLord of the universe
ajamunborn
jagataḥ prabhummaster of the world
bhajantiworship
Bhīṣma's last word. The old warrior, who knew defeat at the end of his own life, promises: His devotees are never ultimately defeated. The repeated line and "oṃ nama iti" mark the end of Bhīṣma's phalaśruti.
Those who worship Him are never defeated.

CLOSING 23 · ARJUNA

ARJUNA'S PRAYER
padmapatraviśālākṣapadmanābha surottama |bhaktānām anuraktānāṃtrātā bhava janārdana ‖23‖
Arjuna said: O lotus-eyed, lotus-navelled, best of the gods, O Janārdana: be the protector of Your loving devotees.
Arjuna prays: Lotus-eyed Lord, please protect those who love You.
WordMeans

Other voices join. From here, others speak. First Arjuna, Kṛṣṇa's closest friend, asks for protection: not for himself but for all loving devotees.
Arjuna asks: protect all who love You.

CLOSING 24 · KṚṢṆA

ONE VERSE IS ENOUGH
yo māṃ nāmasahasreṇastotum icchati pāṇḍava |so'ham ekena ślokenastuta eva na saṃśayaḥ ‖24‖stuta eva na saṃśayaoṃ nama iti |
The Lord said: O Pāṇḍava, whoever wishes to praise Me with the thousand names, I am praised even by a single verse; there is no doubt.
WordMeans
yaḥ māmwhoever, Me
stotum icchatiwishes to praise
pāṇḍavaO son of Pāṇḍu
ekena ślokenaby a single verse
Grace over quantity. Kṛṣṇa's answer is wonderfully kind: the wish to praise Him with a thousand names is already enough; one sincere verse counts as all. The Lord looks at the heart, not the length.
One sincere verse counts as the whole thousand.

CLOSING 25 · VYĀSA

THE THREE WORLDS ARE HIS HOME
vāsanād vāsudevasyavāsitaṃ bhuvanatrayam |sarvabhūtanivāso'sivāsudeva namo'stu te ‖25‖śrī vāsudeva namo'stutaoṃ nama iti |
Vyāsa said: Because Vāsudeva dwells in them, the three worlds are His dwelling; You are the dwelling of all beings; O Vāsudeva, salutations to You.
WordMeans
vāsanātby dwelling
vāsitamis inhabited
bhuvana-trayamthe three worlds
sarva-bhūta-nivāsaḥdwelling of all beings
The seer speaks. Vyāsa explains the name Vāsudeva by its root vas, "to dwell": He lives in all, and all live in Him (names 332, 695, 709).
Vāsudeva: He lives in all, all live in Him.

CLOSING 26 · PĀRVATĪ

PĀRVATĪ'S QUESTION
kenopāyena laghunāviṣṇor nāmasahasrakam |paṭhyate paṇḍitair nityaṃśrotum icchāmy ahaṃ prabho ‖26‖
Pārvatī said: By what easy way do the wise recite the thousand names of Viṣṇu every day? I wish to hear it, my Lord.
Pārvatī asks Śiva: Is there a short way the wise use to chant all thousand names of Viṣṇu daily? Please tell me.
WordMeans
kena upāyenaby what means
laghunāeasy, short
nāma-sahasrakamthe thousand names
paṭhyateis recited
paṇḍitaiḥby the wise
śrotum icchāmiI wish to hear
A practical question. Pārvatī asks what many of us ask: is there a shorter way? Her husband Śiva, himself a great devotee of Rāma, answers in the next verse.
Pārvatī asks: is there a shorter way?

CLOSING 27 · ŚIVA

RĀMA RĀMA RĀMA
śrīrāma rāma rāmetirame rāme manorame |sahasranāma tattulyaṃrāmanāma varānane ‖27‖śrīrāma nāma varānanaoṃ nama iti |
Īśvara (Śiva) said: "Śrī Rāma, Rāma, Rāma": so I delight in the delightful Rāma, O lovely one. The name of Rāma is equal to the thousand names, O fair-faced one.
WordMeans
rāma rāma rāma iti"Rāma, Rāma, Rāma"
rameI delight
rāme manoramein Rāma, the delightful
tat-tulyamequal to that
The famous shortcut. Many recite this verse three times when time is short. A traditional explanation: in the Sanskrit letter-number system, "Rāma" counts as 2 × 5 = 10, so Rāma × Rāma × Rāma = 1,000. Whatever the arithmetic, the message is that one beloved name, said with love, contains all.
"Śrī Rāma Rāma Rāma": one name holds all thousand.
The numerical explanation is a traditional one, not part of the text.

CLOSING 28 · BRAHMĀ

A THOUSAND OF EVERYTHING

Brahmā said: Salutations to the Endless One of a thousand forms, with a thousand feet, eyes, heads, thighs and arms; to the eternal Person of a thousand names, who upholds a thousand crores of ages: salutations.

The creator bows. Brahmā himself praises Viṣṇu, echoing the Puruṣa Sūkta and names 224–227: a thousand heads, eyes, feet. And "of a thousand names": the hymn names itself.
A thousand forms, a thousand names, countless ages.

CLOSING 29 · SAÑJAYA

WHERE KṚṢṆA AND ARJUNA ARE
yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇoyatra pārtho dhanurdharaḥ |tatra śrīr vijayo bhūtirdhruvā nītir matir mama ‖29‖
Sañjaya said: Wherever there is Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of yoga, and wherever there is Arjuna, the archer, there, I believe, are fortune, victory, prosperity and firm justice.
Sañjaya says: Wherever Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are together, there will be prosperity, victory, wellbeing and lasting justice.
WordMeans

The Gītā's last verse (18.78). Sañjaya, who narrated the Gītā to the blind king, ends with this. Where the Lord (grace) and the devotee who acts (effort) come together, success is certain.
Grace and effort together: victory is certain.

Kṛṣṇa says: Those who think of Me alone and worship Me, I take care of: I bring what they need and protect what they have.

He brings what we lack, and keeps what we have.

CLOSING 31 · KṚṢṆA

AGE AFTER AGE

To protect good people, to end evil and to set right living firmly in place, I come again and again, in every age.

Age after age, He comes.

CLOSING 32

MAY ALL BE FREE OF SORROW
ārtā viṣaṇṇāḥ śithilāś ca bhītāḥghoreṣu ca vyādhiṣu vartamānāḥ |saṅkīrtya nārāyaṇaśabdamātraṃvimuktaduḥkhāḥ sukhino bhavantu ‖32‖
The afflicted, the dejected, the weak and the frightened, and those suffering terrible illnesses: by merely chanting the word "Nārāyaṇa", may they be freed of sorrow and be happy.
May everyone who is suffering, sad, weak, afraid or seriously ill be freed of pain and find happiness, just by saying "Nārāyaṇa".
WordMeans
ārtāḥthe afflicted
viṣaṇṇāḥthe dejected

A prayer for others. The hymn ends not with "may I" but "may they": all who suffer. It is a prayer anyone can say for the sick and the sorrowing.
May all who suffer be free and happy.

CLOSING 33

I OFFER IT ALL
kāyena vācā manasendriyair vābuddhyātmanā vā prakṛteḥ svabhāvāt |karomi yad yat sakalaṃ parasmainārāyaṇāyeti samarpayāmi ‖33‖
Whatever I do with my body, speech, mind or senses, with my intellect or my self, or out of my natural tendencies: all of it I offer to the Supreme, to Nārāyaṇa.
Everything I do, with body, words, mind, senses, intellect or habit, I offer to Nārāyaṇa.
WordMeans
kāyena · vācāby body · by speech
manasā · indriyaiḥby mind · by senses
buddhyā · ātmanāby intellect · by self
prakṛteḥ svabhāvātfrom natural tendency

The final offering. The last verse turns the whole of life into worship: every act of body, speech and mind is offered to the Lord. Many families close every prayer with it. The thousand names end where the Prapatti ended: in surrender.
Whatever I do, I offer to Nārāyaṇa.

THE WHOLE SAHASRANĀMA ON ONE PAGE

Ten parts, a thousand names, one answer
Part 1 · The question. On his bed of arrows, Bhīṣma is asked: who is the one God, and what frees us? His answer: the thousand names.
Parts 2–5 · Names 1–512. The universe and its Witness; the avatāras; the healer and the bridge; the friend and the mother's love; name 500.
Parts 6–9 · Names 513–1000. Śrī everywhere; one and many; hard and easy to reach; the bearer of our burden; the Wonderful; everything is His weapon to protect us.
Part 10 · The fruits. Health, courage and peace; one verse is enough; "Śrī Rāma Rāma Rāma".
The last word. Whatever I do, I offer to Nārāyaṇa.
