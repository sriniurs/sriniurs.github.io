"Ananto hutabhug bhoktā" · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: First verses 95–108 for chanting in one go. Then one page for each verse: the chant, a picture, every name with its number and meaning, one insight and the one thing to remember.

PREVIOUSLY · AND NOW

In Part 8, names 765–885: hard to reach and easy to reach, the thread of the worlds, Sundara, name 800, the rain cloud, the bearer of our burden, and the sun.

Part 9 completes the thousand: verses 95–107, names 886 to 1000, and the closing prayer, verse 108. Here are the Wonderful, name 900 and the names of well-being, the One beyond words, the best of the forgiving, the destroyer of bad dreams, Lakṣmī among the names, fourteen names of sacrifice, Devakī's joy, and finally His weapons, ending with name 1000.

ZAPPY: We actually reach 1000?

PROF. BOLT: Name 1000 is in verse 107: Sarvapraharaṇāyudha, 'whose weapons are all things'. Then verse 108 gathers it all into one prayer.

THE LORD'S FIVE WEAPONS

How the thousand names end
WeaponNameWhat it stands for
Pāñcajanya, the conchŚaṅkhabhṛt (993)The call of dharma; the sacred sound
Nandaka, the swordNandakī (994)Knowledge that cuts ignorance
Sudarśana, the discusCakrī (995)The wheel of time; the mind
Śārṅga, the bowŚārṅgadhanvā (996)The senses, held in control
Kaumodakī, the maceGadādhara (997)Strength; the intellect

Symbols. The meanings in the last column are traditional readings that link each weapon to a power of the mind and cosmos; texts differ in the details. Then name 1000 says the rest: everything is His weapon to protect us.

ZAPPY: Why end a thousand names with weapons?

PROF. BOLT: Because the hymn began with 'what frees us from fear?'. It ends: nothing can stop Him from protecting you.

VERSE 95 · NAMES 886–895

THE WONDERFUL
ananto hutabhug bhoktāsukhado naikajo'grajaḥ |anirviṇṇaḥ sadāmarṣīlokādhiṣṭhānam adbhutaḥ ‖95‖
He is endless, the eater of oblations, the enjoyer, the giver of happiness, born many times, the first-born; never weary, ever forgiving, the support of the world, the wonderful.
No.NameMeans
886anantaḥthe endless (again)
887huta-bhukeater of oblations
888bhoktāthe enjoyer (again)
889sukha-daḥgiver of happiness
890naika-jaḥborn many times (avatāras)
891agra-jaḥthe first-born
892anirviṇṇaḥnever weary (again)
893sadā-amarṣīever forgiving
894loka-adhiṣṭhānamsupport of the world
895adbhutaḥthe wonderful
Adbhuta (895). "The wonderful": after nearly 900 names, the hymn simply says He is a wonder. Sadāmarṣī (893): ever forgiving, read by some as "ever patient with the wrongs of devotees".
After 900 names: He is simply wonderful.
Some texts read naikadaḥ for naikajaḥ (890).

VERSE 96 · NAMES 896–905

NAME 900 · WELL-BEING
sanāt sanātanatamaḥkapilaḥ kapir avyayaḥ |svastidaḥ svastikṛt svastisvastibhuk svastidakṣiṇaḥ ‖96‖
He is time eternal, the most ancient, the tawny (Kapila), the sun (Kapi), the imperishable; the giver of well-being, the maker of well-being, well-being itself, the enjoyer of well-being, the bestower of well-being.
No.NameMeans
896sanāteternal time
897sanātana-tamaḥthe most ancient
898kapilaḥthe tawny (Kapila)
899kapiḥthe sun; the boar
900avyayaḥthe imperishable
901svasti-daḥgiver of well-being
902svasti-kṛtmaker of well-being
903svastiwell-being itself
904svasti-bhukenjoyer of well-being
905svasti-dakṣiṇaḥbestower of well-being
Name 900: Avyaya. The imperishable. Then five svasti names: He gives, makes, is, enjoys and bestows well-being. Svasti, "it is well", is the root of the auspicious svastika symbol.
Svasti: He is well-being itself.

VERSE 97 · NAMES 906–914

BEYOND WORDS
araudraḥ kuṇḍalī cakrīvikramy ūrjitaśāsanaḥ |śabdātigaḥ śabdasahaḥśiśiraḥ śarvarīkaraḥ ‖97‖
He is not fierce, wears earrings, bears the discus, is valiant, of mighty command; beyond words, praised by all words, cool, the maker of night.
No.NameMeans
906araudraḥnot fierce, gentle
907kuṇḍalīwearing earrings
908cakrībearer of the discus
909vikramīvaliant (again)
910ūrjita-śāsanaḥof mighty command
911śabda-atigaḥbeyond words
912śabda-sahaḥpraised by all words

Beyond words, yet praised by all words (911–912). The perfect description of this hymn: a thousand names, and still He is beyond them all. Every name points; none captures. Araudra (906): He is never cruel.
Beyond all words, yet every word praises Him.

VERSE 98 · NAMES 915–922

BEST OF THE FORGIVING
akrūraḥ peśalo dakṣodakṣiṇaḥ kṣamiṇāṃ varaḥ |vidvattamo vītabhayaḥpuṇyaśravaṇakīrtanaḥ ‖98‖
He is not cruel, gentle, skilful, generous, the best of the forgiving; the wisest, free from fear, the one whose hearing and singing are holy.
No.NameMeans
915akrūraḥnever cruel
916peśalaḥgentle, lovely
917dakṣaḥskilful (again)
918dakṣiṇaḥgenerous, kind
919kṣamiṇāṃ varaḥbest of the forgiving
920vidvat-tamaḥthe wisest
921vīta-bhayaḥfree from fear
922puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtanaḥholy to hear of and sing
Kṣamiṇāṃ varaḥ (919). "Best of the forgiving": more patient than the earth, which bears everything. And Puṇyaśravaṇakīrtana (922): simply hearing of Him and singing about Him is holy, which is exactly what we are doing.
Best of the forgiving: hearing of Him is holy.

VERSE 99 · NAMES 923–931

DESTROYER OF BAD DREAMS
uttāraṇo duṣkṛtihāpuṇyo duḥsvapnanāśanaḥ |vīrahā rakṣaṇaḥ santojīvanaḥ paryavasthitaḥ ‖99‖
He lifts us across, destroys evil deeds, is holy, destroys bad dreams; slays mighty demons, is the protector, the good, the life-giver, all-present.

Duḥsvapnanāśana (926). "Destroyer of bad dreams": a very human name. Śaṅkara also reads it as the bad dream of saṃsāra itself, from which the Lord wakes us. Rakṣaṇa (928): the protector.
He chases away our bad dreams.

VERSE 100 · NAMES 932–940

VERSE 100 · REMOVER OF FEAR
anantarūpo'nantaśrīrjitamanyur bhayāpahaḥ |caturaśro gabhīrātmāvidiśo vyādiśo diśaḥ ‖100‖
He has endless forms, endless glory, has conquered anger, removes fear; He is just, of profound nature, who gives to each their due, who commands, who directs.
No.NameMeans
932ananta-rūpaḥof endless forms
933ananta-śrīḥof endless glory

937gabhīra-ātmāof profound nature

Verse 100. Jitamanyu (934): He has no anger. Bhayāpaha (935): He takes away fear. Caturaśra (936): "four-square", fair and just to all. The directions (938–940): He guides every being in every direction.
No anger, no fear: just and true in every direction.

VERSE 101 · NAMES 941–949

LAKṢMĪ AMONG THE NAMES
anādir bhūrbhuvo lakṣmīḥsuvīro rucirāṅgadaḥ |janano janajanmādirbhīmo bhīmaparākramaḥ ‖101‖
He is without beginning, the support of earth and sky, Lakṣmī (glory) Herself, the great hero, wearing beautiful armlets; the begetter, the origin of beings' birth, the awesome, of awesome valour.
No.NameMeans
941anādiḥwithout beginning
942bhūr-bhuvaḥsupport of earth and sky
943lakṣmīḥLakṣmī: glory itself
944su-vīraḥthe great hero
945rucira-aṅgadaḥwearing beautiful armlets
946jananaḥthe begetter
947jana-janma-ādiḥorigin of all births
948bhīmaḥthe awesome
949bhīma-parākramaḥof awesome valour
Lakṣmī (943). A name of Viṣṇu that is the name of His consort: Śaṅkara reads it as "the glory of the earth and sky". In Śrīvaiṣṇava thought, the Lord and Śrī are one inseparable couple, so Her name belongs among His.
Even Lakṣmī's name is among His.

VERSE 102 · NAMES 950–958

THE SMILE OF A FLOWER
ādhāranilayo'dhātāpuṣpahāsaḥ prajāgaraḥ |ūrdhvagaḥ satpathācāraḥprāṇadaḥ praṇavaḥ paṇaḥ ‖102‖
He is the abode of the supports, the one without support, who smiles like a blossoming flower, ever awake; who rises highest, whose conduct is the good path, the giver of life, Oṃ, the one who deals (with all justly).
No.NameMeans
950ādhāra-nilayaḥabode of the supports
951adhātāwho needs no support
952puṣpa-hāsaḥwho smiles like a flower
953prajāgaraḥever awake
954ūrdhva-gaḥwho rises highest
955sat-patha-ācāraḥwho walks the good path
956prāṇa-daḥgiver of life (again)
957praṇavaḥOṃ (again)
958paṇaḥwho deals justly with all
Puṣpahāsa (952). "Whose smile is a blooming flower": creation unfolds from Him as a bud opens into a flower. Prajāgara (953): ever awake, watching over us while we sleep (recall the Suprabhātam: we "wake" Him, but He never sleeps).
He smiles like a flower, and never sleeps.
Name 951 is adhātā (shlokam.org); some texts read dhātā.

VERSE 103 · NAMES 959–966

BEYOND BIRTH, AGE AND DEATH
pramāṇaṃ prāṇanilayaḥprāṇabhṛt prāṇajīvanaḥ |tattvaṃ tattvavid ekātmājanmamṛtyujarātigaḥ ‖103‖
He is the proof (of all knowledge), the abode of life, the sustainer of life, the life of life; the Reality, the knower of Reality, the one Self, beyond birth, death and old age.
No.NameMeans
959pramāṇamthe proof, consciousness
960prāṇa-nilayaḥabode of life
961prāṇa-bhṛtsustainer of life
962prāṇa-jīvanaḥthe life of life
963tattvamthe Reality
964tattva-vitknower of Reality
965eka-ātmāthe one Self
966janma-mṛtyu-jarā-atigaḥbeyond birth, death and old age
Janmamṛtyujarātiga (966). "Beyond birth, death and old age": the three things every human fears. He is beyond them, and the hymn's promise (pūrvabhāga 3) is that by chanting His names we too are freed from "the bondage of birth and worldly life".
Beyond birth, age and death, and He leads us there.

VERSE 104 · NAMES 967–975

THE TREE OF THE THREE WORLDS
bhūrbhuvaḥsvastarus tāraḥsavitā prapitāmahaḥ |yajño yajñapatir yajvāyajñāṅgo yajñavāhanaḥ ‖104‖
He is the tree of earth, sky and heaven; the saviour, Savitā, the great-grandfather; the sacrifice, the lord of sacrifice, the sacrificer, whose limbs are the sacrifice, who carries the sacrifice.
No.NameMeans
967bhūr-bhuvaḥ-svaḥ-taruḥtree of the three worlds
968tāraḥthe saviour
969savitāSavitā (again)
970pra-pitāmahaḥthe great-grandfather (of all)
971yajñaḥthe sacrifice
972yajña-patiḥlord of sacrifice
973yajvāthe sacrificer
974yajña-aṅgaḥwhose limbs are the sacrifice (Varāha)

The yajña names begin. From here to 984, fourteen names on yajña, sacrifice. He is the sacrifice, its lord, its doer, its carrier, its fruit. Prapitāmaha (970): Brahmā is the grandfather of the world; Viṣṇu, from whom Brahmā was born, is the great-grandfather.
The tree of the three worlds, and the sacrifice itself.

VERSE 105 · NAMES 976–984

THE FOOD AND THE EATER
yajñabhṛd yajñakṛd yajñīyajñabhug yajñasādhanaḥ |yajñāntakṛd yajñaguhyamannam annāda eva ca ‖105‖
He upholds the sacrifice, performs the sacrifice, is the master of sacrifice, enjoys the sacrifice, is the means of sacrifice; the one who completes the sacrifice, the secret of the sacrifice; the food, and the eater of food.
No.NameMeans
976yajña-bhṛtupholder of the sacrifice
977yajña-kṛtperformer of the sacrifice
978yajñīmaster of the sacrifice
979yajña-bhukenjoyer of the sacrifice
980yajña-sādhanaḥthe means of sacrifice
981yajña-anta-kṛtwho completes the sacrifice
982yajña-guhyamthe secret of the sacrifice
983annamthe food
984anna-adaḥthe eater of food
Annam and Annāda (983–984). "The food and the eater", from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad's joyful song: "I am food, I am food, I am the eater of food!" Every meal, offered first, becomes worship (compare 142–143).
He is the food and the one who eats it.

VERSE 106 · NAMES 985–992

DEVAKĪ'S JOY
ātmayoniḥ svayañjātovaikhānaḥ sāmagāyanaḥ |devakīnandanaḥ sraṣṭākṣitīśaḥ pāpanāśanaḥ ‖106‖
He is His own source, self-born, the one who digs (as Varāha), the singer of Sāma; the joy of Devakī, the creator, the lord of the earth, the destroyer of sin.
No.NameMeans
985ātma-yoniḥHis own source
986svayam-jātaḥself-born
987vaikhānaḥwho dug (Varāha, into the earth)
988sāma-gāyanaḥsinger of the Sāma
989devakī-nandanaḥthe joy of Devakī

Devakīnandana (989). Kṛṣṇa, born to Devakī in Kaṃsa's prison. The hymn's deity, named in the pūrvabhāga as "Devakī's son", appears by name at the very end of the thousand. Then Pāpanāśana (992): the destroyer of sin.
Devakī's joy: the hymn's deity, named at last.

VERSE 107 · NAMES 993–1000

NAMES 993–1000: THE WEAPONS
śaṅkhabhṛn nandakī cakrīśārṅgadhanvā gadādharaḥ |rathāṅgapāṇir akṣobhyaḥsarvapraharaṇāyudhaḥ ‖107‖śrī sarvapraharaṇāyudhaoṃ nama iti |
He bears the conch, the sword Nandaka, the discus; the bow Śārṅga, the mace; holds the chariot-wheel in His hand, is undisturbed, and has every weapon as His weapon. Salutations, Oṃ, to Him whose weapons are all things.
No.NameMeans
993śaṅkha-bhṛtbearer of the conch (Pāñcajanya)
994nandakībearer of the sword Nandaka
995cakrībearer of the discus (again)
996śārṅga-dhanvābearer of the bow Śārṅga
997gadā-dharaḥbearer of the mace (Kaumodakī)
998ratha-aṅga-pāṇiḥwith the chariot-wheel in hand
999akṣobhyaḥundisturbed (again)
1000sarva-praharaṇa-āyudhaḥwhose weapons are all things
Name 1000. The thousand names end with His weapons: conch, sword, discus, bow and mace, which protect His devotees. Rathāṅgapāṇi (998): Kṛṣṇa once lifted a chariot-wheel to protect Arjuna. And the last: Sarvapraharaṇāyudha, "everything is His weapon": nothing can stop Him from protecting us.
Name 1000: everything is His weapon to protect us.

VERSE 108 · THE CLOSING PRAYER

MAY HE PROTECT US
vanamālī gadī śārṅgīśaṅkhī cakrī ca nandakī |śrīmān nārāyaṇo viṣṇurvāsudevo'bhirakṣatu ‖108‖
May He who wears the forest garland, who holds the mace, the bow, the conch, the discus and the sword, the glorious Nārāyaṇa, Viṣṇu, Vāsudeva, protect us.
No.NameMeans
—vanamālīwearing the forest garland
—gadī · śārṅgīwith mace · with bow
—śaṅkhī · cakrī · nandakīwith conch, discus, sword
—śrīmān nārāyaṇaḥglorious Nārāyaṇa
—viṣṇuḥ vāsudevaḥViṣṇu, Vāsudeva
—abhirakṣatumay He protect
The closing prayer. This verse adds no new names; it gathers the Lord's weapons and three great names, Nārāyaṇa, Viṣṇu, Vāsudeva, into a single prayer: abhirakṣatu, "may He protect". Many recite it three times. Part 10 completes the hymn with its closing section, the uttarabhāga.
"Abhirakṣatu": may the Lord protect us all.
