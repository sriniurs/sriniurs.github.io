FOREWORD

"Viśvaṃ viṣṇur vaṣaṭkāraḥ…" In millions of homes and temples, the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma, the "thousand names of Viṣṇu", is chanted every day. Many know it by heart; many more hear it every morning without knowing what the names mean.

It comes from the Mahābhārata. At the end of the great war, the dying Bhīṣma answers Yudhiṣṭhira's question, "What should one chant to be free?", with a thousand names of the Lord, while Kṛṣṇa Himself stands listening.

This series takes it one verse at a time, in ten parts. Part 1 covers everything before the names: the opening prayers, Bhīṣma's teaching (the pūrvabhāga) and the meditation verses (dhyāna).

Hari Oṃ,
srini.presents 🙏

PROF. BOLT: With the story: why Bhīṣma gave them. Once you know the question, every name becomes part of the answer.

THE STORY · ON A BED OF ARROWS

How the thousand names were given
The story in 5 frames
The war is over. The Pāṇḍavas have won at Kurukṣetra, but Yudhiṣṭhira, the new king, is crushed by grief at the cost.
A dying teacher. Bhīṣma, the great-uncle of both sides, lies on a bed of arrows, waiting for the sun to turn north before he dies.
Kṛṣṇa's advice. Kṛṣṇa takes Yudhiṣṭhira to Bhīṣma to learn: kingship, dharma, how to live and how to let go.
The last question. After many days of teaching, Yudhiṣṭhira asks: who is the one God, and what should one chant to be free?
The answer. Bhīṣma recites the thousand names of Viṣṇu, while Kṛṣṇa stands before him. The Mahābhārata places this in the Anuśāsana Parva.

ZAPPY: Kṛṣṇa was right there while Bhīṣma praised Him?

PROF. BOLT: Yes! Bhīṣma was praising the very Lord standing in front of him. That's why the hymn names 'Devakī's son' as its deity.

ABOUT THE SAHASRANĀMA

A thousand names in four sections

📜 Where it comes from

The Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva (chapter 149 in the popular edition; numbered differently in the Critical Edition). Seer: Vyāsa.

🎵 The sound

The names are in anuṣṭubh: four lines of eight syllables. Some dhyāna verses use longer metres.

Two great commentaries. Ādi Śaṅkara wrote a famous commentary on the names from the Advaita view; Parāśara Bhaṭṭar wrote one from the Śrīvaiṣṇava view. They sometimes read the same name differently; where they do, this series will say so.

OPENING PRAYER 1

REMOVE ALL OBSTACLES
śuklāmbaradharaṃ viṣṇuṃśaśivarṇaṃ caturbhujam |prasannavadanaṃ dhyāyetsarvavighnopaśāntaye ‖
One should meditate on the all-pervading One, clad in white, moon-coloured, four-armed, with a gracious face, for the removal of all obstacles.
To clear every obstacle, picture the Lord dressed in white, bright as the moon, with four arms and a kind, smiling face.
WordMeans
śukla-ambara-dharamwearing white
viṣṇumthe all-pervading
śaśi-varṇammoon-coloured
catur-bhujamfour-armed
prasanna-vadanamwith a gracious face
vighna-upaśāntayefor removing obstacles
Who is this? Many families chant this verse to Gaṇeśa before any work; in the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition it is addressed to Viṣṇu or to Viṣvaksena, the commander of His hosts (next verse). The word viṣṇu, "all-pervading", fits both readings.
Begin with a calm, smiling Lord: obstacles melt away.

OPENING PRAYER 2

VIṢVAKSENA, THE COMMANDER
yasya dviradavaktrādyāḥpāriṣadyāḥ paraḥ śatam |vighnaṃ nighnanti satataṃviṣvaksenaṃ tam āśraye ‖
I take refuge in Viṣvaksena, whose more than a hundred attendants, led by the elephant-faced one, constantly destroy every obstacle.
I take shelter in Viṣvaksena, whose hundred-plus helpers, led by the elephant-faced one, keep clearing every obstacle away.
WordMeans
dvirada-vaktraelephant-faced
pāriṣadyāḥattendants
paraḥ śatammore than a hundred
vighnaṃ nighnantidestroy obstacles
viṣvaksenathe commander of Viṣṇu's hosts
āśrayeI take refuge
Viṣvaksena is the chief of Viṣṇu's attendants in Vaikuṇṭha. In Śrīvaiṣṇava temples he is worshipped first, as the remover of obstacles, much as Gaṇeśa is elsewhere. Here an elephant-faced attendant leads his hosts.
The Lord's commander clears the way.

OPENING PRAYER 3

SALUTATIONS TO VYĀSA
vyāsaṃ vasiṣṭhanaptāraṃśakteḥ pautram akalmaṣam |parāśarātmajaṃ vandeśukatātaṃ taponidhim ‖
I bow to Vyāsa, great-grandson of Vasiṣṭha, grandson of Śakti, son of Parāśara, father of Śuka: the stainless one, a treasure of austerity.
I bow to the sage Vyāsa, from a great line of sages, Vasiṣṭha, Śakti and Parāśara, and father of Śuka: pure and rich in spiritual discipline.
WordMeans
vasiṣṭha-naptāramgreat-grandson of Vasiṣṭha
śakteḥ pautramgrandson of Śakti
akalmaṣamstainless
parāśara-ātmajamson of Parāśara
śuka-tātamfather of Śuka
tapo-nidhimtreasure of austerity
Why Vyāsa? The Sahasranāma is part of the Mahābhārata, which tradition credits to Vyāsa. Before the hymn, we honour its seer. Five generations appear in one verse: Vasiṣṭha, Śakti, Parāśara, Vyāsa, Śuka.
Honour the seer first: Vyāsa, of a great line of sages.

OPENING PRAYER 4

VYĀSA IS VIṢṆU
vyāsāya viṣṇurūpāyavyāsarūpāya viṣṇave |namo vai brahmanidhayevāsiṣṭhāya namo namaḥ ‖
Salutations to Vyāsa, who is a form of Viṣṇu, and to Viṣṇu, who took the form of Vyāsa; salutations again and again to the treasure-house of the Veda, the descendant of Vasiṣṭha.
I bow to Vyāsa, who is Viṣṇu Himself, and to Viṣṇu, who became Vyāsa: the keeper of the Vedas, born in Vasiṣṭha's line.
WordMeans
viṣṇu-rūpāyain the form of Viṣṇu
vyāsa-rūpāyain the form of Vyāsa
brahma-nidhayetreasure of the Veda
vāsiṣṭhāyadescendant of Vasiṣṭha
namo namaḥsalutations again and again
vaitruly
A mirror verse. "Vyāsa-as-Viṣṇu, Viṣṇu-as-Vyāsa": the words swap places. Tradition counts Vyāsa among the Lord's manifestations, the one who divided the Veda and wrote it down for our age.
Vyāsa and Viṣṇu: the teacher and the Lord are one.

OPENING PRAYER 5

THE CHANGELESS ONE
avikārāya śuddhāyanityāya paramātmane |sadaikarūparūpāyaviṣṇave sarvajiṣṇave ‖
To the changeless, the pure, the eternal, the Supreme Self; to Him whose one form is always the same; to Viṣṇu, the conqueror of all.
To Viṣṇu, who never changes, is pure and eternal, the Supreme Self, always one and the same, and victorious over all.
WordMeans
avikārāyachangeless
śuddhāyapure
nityāyaeternal
param-ātmaneSupreme Self
sadā-eka-rūpaalways of one form
sarva-jiṣṇaveconqueror of all
Philosophy in a verse. Everything in the world changes; the Lord does not (avikāra). He takes many forms in the avatāras, yet is "always of one form" in His essence. This is the God whose thousand names follow.
Everything changes; He does not.

OPENING PRAYER 6

FREED JUST BY REMEMBERING
yasya smaraṇamātreṇajanmasaṃsārabandhanāt |vimucyate namas tasmaiviṣṇave prabhaviṣṇave ‖oṃ namo viṣṇaveprabhaviṣṇave ‖
Salutations to Him, the all-pervading, the mighty, by merely remembering whom one is freed from the bondage of birth and worldly life. Oṃ, salutations to Viṣṇu, the mighty.
WordMeans
smaraṇa-mātreṇaby mere remembrance
janma-saṃsārabirth and worldly life
bandhanātfrom the bondage
vimucyateone is freed
The promise up front. Before the story begins, the opening prayers end with the hymn's whole promise: remembering the Lord frees us. The rest of the Sahasranāma gives a thousand ways to remember Him.
Just remember Him, and the chains fall away.

PŪRVABHĀGA 1

YUDHIṢṬHIRA ASKS AGAIN
śrutvā dharmān aśeṣeṇapāvanāni ca sarvaśaḥ |yudhiṣṭhiraḥ śāntanavaṃpunar evābhyabhāṣata ‖1‖
Vaiśampāyana said: Having heard all the teachings on dharma, and every means of purification, Yudhiṣṭhira spoke again to Bhīṣma, the son of Śantanu.
After hearing everything about right living and every way to purify oneself, Yudhiṣṭhira turned to Bhīṣma with one more question.
WordMeans
śrutvāhaving heard
dharmān aśeṣeṇaall the dharmas, completely
pāvanānipurifying practices
śāntanavamthe son of Śantanu: Bhīṣma
punar evaonce again
abhyabhāṣataspoke to
The setting. The great war is over. Bhīṣma, the grand old warrior, lies on a bed of arrows, waiting for the auspicious time to die. For days he has taught Yudhiṣṭhira everything about dharma. Now comes the last, simplest question. Vaiśampāyana, Vyāsa's disciple, narrates.
After all the teachings, one more question.

PŪRVABHĀGA 2–3

THE SIX QUESTIONS
kim ekaṃ daivataṃ lokekiṃ vāpy ekaṃ parāyaṇam |stuvantaḥ kaṃ kam arcantaḥprāpnuyur mānavāḥ śubham ‖2‖ko dharmaḥ sarvadharmāṇāṃbhavataḥ paramo mataḥ |kiṃ japan mucyate janturjanmasaṃsārabandhanāt ‖3‖
Yudhiṣṭhira said: (1) Who is the one God in the world? (2) What is the one highest refuge? (3) By praising whom, and (4) by worshipping whom, may people attain good? (5) What, in your view, is the highest dharma of all? (6) By chanting what is a living being freed from the bondage of birth and worldly life?
WordMeans
ekaṃ daivatamthe one God
ekaṃ parāyaṇamthe one supreme goal
stuvantaḥ · arcantaḥpraising · worshipping
paramo dharmaḥthe highest dharma
Six questions, one answer. Yudhiṣṭhira asks six questions, but Bhīṣma will give a single answer: Viṣṇu, and the chanting of His thousand names. Many teachers say the whole Sahasranāma is the answer to these two verses.
Six great questions: who, what, whom, which, how.

PŪRVABHĀGA 4

BHĪṢMA ANSWERS
jagatprabhuṃ devadevamanantaṃ puruṣottamam |stuvan nāmasahasreṇapuruṣaḥ satatotthitaḥ ‖4‖
Bhīṣma said: A person who, always alert, praises with the thousand names the Lord of the world, the God of gods, the endless one, the Supreme Person…
Bhīṣma replied: whoever, always ready and wakeful, praises the Lord of the universe, the God of gods, the infinite Supreme Person, with these thousand names…
WordMeans
jagat-prabhumLord of the world
deva-devamGod of gods
anantamendless
puruṣa-uttamamSupreme Person
nāma-sahasreṇawith the thousand names
satata-utthitaḥalways alert
The answer begins. Verses 4–7 form one long sentence. Bhīṣma answers "whom to praise" (the Supreme Person) and "how" (with the thousand names). Satatotthitaḥ: "always risen up", eager and wakeful, not lazy in devotion.
Praise the Lord of all with a thousand names.

PŪRVABHĀGA 5

FOUR WAYS TO WORSHIP
tam eva cārcayan nityaṃbhaktyā puruṣam avyayam |dhyāyan stuvan namasyaṃś cayajamānas tam eva ca ‖5‖
…worshipping that same imperishable Person daily with devotion, meditating on Him, praising Him, bowing to Him, and offering sacrifice to Him alone…
…worshipping Him every day with love, meditating on Him, singing His praise, bowing to Him and making offerings to Him…
WordMeans
arcayanworshipping
nityamdaily
bhaktyāwith devotion
dhyāyanmeditating
stuvan · namasyanpraising · bowing
yajamānaḥoffering sacrifice
Body, mind and speech. Worship (with hands), meditation (with mind), praise (with voice), bowing (with body), offering (with possessions): every part of us can worship. And bhaktyā, "with love", runs through all of it.
Worship, meditate, praise, bow: with love.

PŪRVABHĀGA 6

BEYOND ALL SORROW
anādinidhanaṃ viṣṇuṃsarvalokamaheśvaram |lokādhyakṣaṃ stuvan nityaṃsarvaduḥkhātigo bhavet ‖6‖
…praising daily Viṣṇu, who has no beginning and no end, the great Lord of all worlds, the overseer of the world, such a person goes beyond all sorrow.
…praising every day Viṣṇu, who never began and never ends, the Lord who watches over all worlds: such a person rises above every sorrow.
WordMeans
anādi-nidhanamwithout beginning or end
sarva-loka-maheśvaramgreat Lord of all worlds
loka-adhyakṣamoverseer of the world
stuvan nityampraising daily
sarva-duḥkha-atigaḥbeyond all sorrow
bhavetbecomes
The first fruit. Here Bhīṣma answers "how to be blessed": daily praise of the endless Lord carries a person "across all sorrow" (duḥkha-atiga). Not that sorrows stop coming, but that they no longer drown us.
Praise the endless Lord daily: rise above all sorrow.

PŪRVABHĀGA 7

SOURCE OF ALL BEINGS
brahmaṇyaṃ sarvadharmajñaṃlokānāṃ kīrtivardhanam |lokanāthaṃ mahadbhūtaṃsarvabhūtabhavodbhavam ‖7‖
…the friend of the Vedas and the wise, the knower of all dharma, who increases the glory of the worlds; the Lord of the world, the great Being, the source of the birth of all beings.
…the protector of sacred knowledge, who knows all that is right, who makes the world shine; the Lord of all, the great Being from whom every creature comes.
WordMeans
brahmaṇyamdevoted to the Veda and its keepers
sarva-dharma-jñamknower of all dharma
kīrti-vardhanamincreasing glory
loka-nāthamLord of the world
mahat-bhūtamthe great Being
sarva-bhūta-bhava-udbhavamsource of all beings
Who is this God? Bhīṣma piles up descriptions: He is not a remote power but the knower of dharma and the origin of every creature. To praise Him is to praise the source of everything.
The great Being, source of every creature.

PŪRVABHĀGA 8

THE HIGHEST DHARMA
eṣa me sarvadharmāṇāṃdharmo'dhikatamo mataḥ |yad bhaktyā puṇḍarīkākṣaṃstavair arcen naraḥ sadā ‖8‖
This, in my view, is the highest dharma of all dharmas: that a person should always worship the lotus-eyed Lord with devotion, through hymns of praise.
Of all duties, this is the highest, says Bhīṣma: to worship the lotus-eyed Lord with love, always, by singing His praise.

stavaiḥwith hymns
sadāalways
Answer to question 5. "What is the highest dharma?" Bhīṣma, the greatest expert on dharma, could have named a sacrifice or a vow. He chooses something open to everyone: loving praise of the Lord.
The highest dharma: praise Him with love, always.

PŪRVABHĀGA 9

THE HIGHEST OF ALL
paramaṃ yo mahat tejaḥparamaṃ yo mahat tapaḥ |paramaṃ yo mahad brahmaparamaṃ yaḥ parāyaṇam ‖9‖
He who is the supreme great light; He who is the supreme great austerity; He who is the supreme great Brahman; He who is the supreme refuge…
He is the greatest light, the greatest discipline, the greatest Reality, the final refuge…
WordMeans
paramamsupreme
mahat tejaḥgreat radiance
mahat tapaḥgreat austerity
mahad brahmagreat Brahman
parāyaṇamthe final refuge
yaḥHe who
Answer to question 2. "What is the one supreme goal?" Four times Bhīṣma says paramam, "supreme". Light, tapas, Brahman, refuge: whatever you seek most, He is its highest form.
Light, discipline, Reality, refuge: He is the highest of each.

PŪRVABHĀGA 10

HOLIEST OF THE HOLY
pavitrāṇāṃ pavitraṃ yomaṅgalānāṃ ca maṅgalam |daivataṃ devatānāṃ cabhūtānāṃ yo'vyayaḥ pitā ‖10‖
He who is the purifier of all that purifies, the auspiciousness of all that is auspicious, the God of the gods, and the imperishable father of all beings…
He makes holy things holy and blessed things blessed; He is God even to the gods, and the undying father of every creature…
WordMeans
pavitrāṇām pavitrampurest of the pure
maṅgalānām maṅgalamblessing of blessings
daivatam devatānāmGod of the gods
bhūtānāmof all beings
avyayaḥimperishable
pitāfather
Answer to question 1. "Who is the one God?" He is God even to the gods (daivataṃ devatānām). And yet the last word is tender: He is our pitā, father, who never dies.
God of gods, and father of every being.

PŪRVABHĀGA 11

FROM HIM, INTO HIM
yataḥ sarvāṇi bhūtānibhavanty ādiyugāgame |yasmiṃś ca pralayaṃ yāntipunar eva yugakṣaye ‖11‖
…from whom all beings come forth at the dawn of the first age, and into whom they dissolve again at the end of the age…
…from whom every creature comes at the start of creation, and into whom everything returns when the age ends…
WordMeans
yataḥfrom whom
sarvāṇi bhūtāniall beings
ādi-yuga-āgameat the coming of the first age
yasmininto whom
pralayam yāntigo to dissolution
yuga-kṣayeat the end of the age
Cycles of time. Indian cosmology sees creation as cycles: worlds arise, last for ages, and dissolve, again and again. The Lord is the one constant: the source at every dawn and the resting place at every night of the cosmos.
Everything comes from Him and returns to Him.

PŪRVABHĀGA 12

"LISTEN, O KING"
tasya lokapradhānasyajagannāthasya bhūpate |viṣṇor nāmasahasraṃ meśṛṇu pāpabhayāpaham ‖12‖
O King, hear from me the thousand names of Viṣṇu, the foremost of the worlds, the Lord of the universe: names that take away sin and fear.
Listen, King, says Bhīṣma: I will tell you the thousand names of Viṣṇu, Lord of the universe, which take away wrongdoing and fear.
WordMeans
loka-pradhānasyathe foremost of the worlds
jagat-nāthasyaLord of the universe
bhūpateO King
nāma-sahasramthe thousand names
śṛṇulisten!
pāpa-bhaya-apahamremoving sin and fear
Answer to question 6. "By chanting what is one freed?" The thousand names. Bhīṣma now promises to recite them. Notice "sin and fear": the names cleanse the past and calm the future.
"Listen": the names take away sin and fear.

PŪRVABHĀGA 13

NAMES FROM HIS QUALITIES
yāni nāmāni gauṇānivikhyātāni mahātmanaḥ |ṛṣibhiḥ parigītānitāni vakṣyāmi bhūtaye ‖13‖
Those names of the Great One that arise from His qualities, that are well known, and that have been sung by the sages: these I will tell you, for the good of all.
I will tell you His famous names, the ones that describe His qualities and that the sages have always sung, for everyone's welfare.
WordMeans
gauṇānibased on qualities
vikhyātāniwell known
mahātmanaḥof the Great One
ṛṣibhiḥby the sages
parigītānisung all around
bhūtayefor well-being
Why names matter. These are gauṇa names, "from qualities": each one describes something true about the Lord. So learning the names is learning who He is. That's why the commentators explain every one.
Each name describes a quality of the Lord.

PŪRVABHĀGA 14

SEER, METRE, DEITY
ṛṣir nāmnāṃ sahasrasyavedavyāso mahāmuniḥ |chando'nuṣṭup tathā devobhagavān devakīsutaḥ ‖14‖
The seer of the thousand names is the great sage Veda Vyāsa; the metre is anuṣṭubh; and the deity is the Lord, the son of Devakī.
Who saw this hymn? Vyāsa. Its rhythm? Anuṣṭubh, the eight-syllable line. Whom does it praise? Kṛṣṇa, Devakī's son.
WordMeans
ṛṣiḥthe seer
veda-vyāsaḥVeda Vyāsa
mahā-muniḥgreat sage
chandaḥ anuṣṭupthe metre is anuṣṭubh
devaḥthe deity
devakī-sutaḥson of Devakī: Kṛṣṇa
Like a Vedic mantra. Every Vedic hymn has a seer (ṛṣi), a metre (chandas) and a deity (devatā). Naming them here treats the Sahasranāma as a mantra. And the deity is Kṛṣṇa, who stood beside Bhīṣma as he spoke.
Seer Vyāsa, metre anuṣṭubh, deity Kṛṣṇa.

PŪRVABHĀGA 15

SEED, POWER, HEART
amṛtāṃśūdbhavo bījaṃśaktir devakīnandanaḥ |trisāmā hṛdayaṃ tasyaśāntyarthe viniyujyate ‖15‖
The "seed" is Amṛtāṃśūdbhava; the "power" is Devakīnandana; the "heart" is Trisāmā; and it is employed for peace.
This mantra has a seed, a power and a heart, each a name of the Lord; and it is chanted for peace.
WordMeans
amṛta-aṃśu-udbhavaḥsource of the moon, a name
bījamthe seed
śaktiḥthe power
devakī-nandanaḥDevakī's joy
trisāmāpraised in three Sāmans
śānti-arthefor peace
Mantra structure. A mantra is said to have a seed (bīja), a power (śakti) and a heart (hṛdaya). Each is one of the thousand names. Its purpose (viniyoga) is stated plainly: peace.
Chanted with a seed, a power, a heart: for peace.

PŪRVABHĀGA 16

I BOW TO THE SUPREME PERSON
viṣṇuṃ jiṣṇuṃ mahāviṣṇuṃprabhaviṣṇuṃ maheśvaram |anekarūpadaityāntaṃnamāmi puruṣottamam ‖16‖
I bow to the Supreme Person: Viṣṇu, the victorious, the great Viṣṇu, the mighty, the great Lord, the destroyer of demons of many forms.
I bow to the Supreme Person: all-pervading, victorious, great, mighty, the great Lord, who ends evil in all its forms.
WordMeans
viṣṇumthe all-pervading
jiṣṇumthe victorious
prabhaviṣṇumthe mighty
mahā-īśvaramthe great Lord
aneka-rūpa-daitya-antamender of many-formed demons
namāmiI bow
Many-formed evil. Evil, too, takes many forms: anger, greed, pride, fear. The Lord who ends "demons of many forms" ends these within us as well. With this bow, the preparation is complete.
I bow to the Supreme Person.

THE NYĀSA

PREPARING TO CHANT
asya śrī viṣṇor divya-sahasranāma-stotra-mahāmantrasyaśrī vedavyāso bhagavān ṛṣiḥ …
"Of this great mantra, the divine hymn of the thousand names of Śrī Viṣṇu, the seer is the revered Veda Vyāsa…": the nyāsa continues by naming the metre, deity, seed, power and other parts, and the purpose of the chanting.
Before chanting, the reciter formally names the hymn's seer, metre, deity and parts, and states its purpose: to please the Lord.
WordMeans
nyāsaplacing (the mantra in oneself)
ṛṣithe seer
chandasthe metre
devatāthe deity
bīja · śakti · kīlakaseed · power · pin
viniyogathe purpose
A prose ritual. The nyāsa is not a verse but a formal declaration, often with touches to the hands and body. Its seed, power and other parts are themselves names from the thousand. Many reciters say it before the dhyāna; some traditions omit it.
Before the names, the reciter prepares body and mind.
The full nyāsa is not reproduced here; texts differ slightly in its wording.

DHYĀNA 1

ON THE SHORE OF THE MILK OCEAN
kṣīrodanvatpradeśe śucimaṇi-vilasatsaikate mauktikānāṃmālākḷptāsanasthaḥ sphaṭika-maṇinibhair mauktikair maṇḍitāṅgaḥ |śubhrair abhrair adabhrair upariviracitair muktapīyūṣavarṣaiḥānandī naḥ punīyād arinalina-gadāśaṅkhapāṇir mukundaḥ ‖
On the shore of the ocean of milk, sparkling with pure gems, seated on a throne made of garlands of pearls, His body adorned with pearls clear as crystal; with plentiful white clouds above showering drops of nectar: may Mukunda, full of bliss, holding discus, lotus, mace and conch, purify us.
WordMeans
kṣīra-udanvatthe ocean of milk
saikateon the sandy shore
mauktikapearls
mukta-pīyūṣa-varṣaiḥraining nectar
Dhyāna: seeing before chanting. These verses give the mind a picture to hold while chanting. All white and sparkling: milk ocean, pearls, crystal, white clouds. The Lord is Mukunda, "giver of liberation". This verse is in the long sragdharā metre.
See Him by the milk ocean, and be purified.

DHYĀNA 2

THE COSMIC FORM
bhūḥ pādau yasya nābhir viyadasuranilaś candrasūryau ca netrekarṇāv āśāḥ śiro dyaur mukham apidahano yasya vāsteyam abdhiḥ |antaḥsthaṃ yasya viśvaṃ sura-narakhagagobhogigandharvadaityaiḥcitraṃ raṃramyate taṃ tribhuvana-vapuṣaṃ viṣṇum īśaṃ namāmi ‖
Whose feet are the earth, whose navel is the sky, whose breath is the wind, whose eyes are the moon and sun, whose ears are the directions, whose head is heaven, whose mouth is fire, whose belly is the ocean; within whom the whole universe, with gods, humans, birds, cattle, serpents, gandharvas and demons, plays in wonderful variety: to that Lord Viṣṇu, whose body is the three worlds, I bow.
WordMeans
bhūḥ pādauearth is His feet
nābhiḥ viyatsky is His navel
candra-sūryau netremoon and sun are His eyes
vāsteyam abdhiḥthe ocean is His belly
The Viśvarūpa. This is the cosmic form, like the one Kṛṣṇa showed Arjuna in Gītā chapter 11 and the Puruṣa of the Vedas. The universe is not separate from Him: it is His body, and all of life "plays" within it.
The universe is His body.

DHYĀNA 3

ŚĀNTĀKĀRAM: THE PEACEFUL ONE
oṃ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya ‖śāntākāraṃ bhujagaśayanaṃpadmanābhaṃ sureśaṃviśvādhāraṃ gaganasadṛśaṃmeghavarṇaṃ śubhāṅgam |lakṣmīkāntaṃ kamalanayanaṃyogibhir dhyānagamyaṃ vandeviṣṇuṃ bhavabhayaharaṃ sarvalokaikanātham ‖
Oṃ, salutations to the Lord Vāsudeva. I worship Viṣṇu: of peaceful form, resting on the serpent, lotus-navelled, Lord of the gods, support of the universe, vast as the sky, cloud-coloured, of beautiful limbs; beloved of Lakṣmī, lotus-eyed, reached by yogis in meditation; who removes the fear of worldly existence, the one Lord of all the worlds.
WordMeans
śānta-ākāramof peaceful form
bhujaga-śayanamresting on the serpent
padma-nābhamlotus-navelled
gagana-sadṛśamlike the sky
The most famous dhyāna. "Śāntākāraṃ bhujagaśayanam" is one of the best-known Sanskrit verses: chanted on its own in many homes. The Lord rests on Ādiśeṣa on the cosmic ocean, perfectly at peace, while holding up the whole universe. Calm and power together. It is in mandākrāntā, a slow, stately metre.
Perfect peace, holding up the universe.

DHYĀNA 4

DARK AS A RAIN CLOUD
meghaśyāmaṃpītakauśeyavāsaṃśrīvatsāṅkaṃkaustubhodbhāsitāṅgam |puṇyopetaṃpuṇḍarīkāyatākṣaṃviṣṇuṃ vandesarvalokaikanātham ‖
I worship Viṣṇu, the one Lord of all worlds: dark as a rain cloud, clad in yellow silk, marked with the Śrīvatsa, His body shining with the Kaustubha gem; full of holiness, with long lotus eyes.
WordMeans
megha-śyāmamdark as a rain cloud
pīta-kauśeya-vāsamwearing yellow silk
śrīvatsa-aṅkammarked with Śrīvatsa

Two treasures on His chest. The Śrīvatsa, a curl-mark where Lakṣmī dwells, and the Kaustubha, the gem that rose from the churning of the ocean. A shorter verse in śālinī metre, easy to hold in mind.
Rain-cloud dark, Kaustubha-bright.

DHYĀNA 5

FIRST OF ALL BEINGS
namaḥ samastabhūtānāmādibhūtāya bhūbhṛte |anekarūparūpāyaviṣṇave prabhaviṣṇave ‖
Salutations to Viṣṇu, the mighty: the first being of all beings, the bearer of the earth, whose forms are many.
I bow to the mighty Viṣṇu, the first of all beings, who holds up the earth and takes countless forms.
WordMeans
samasta-bhūtānāmof all beings

aneka-rūpa-rūpāyawhose forms are many

Many forms. As Varāha He lifted the earth; as Kūrma He held up the mountain; as the avatāras He took many shapes. Yet He is the ādi-bhūta, the first, before all forms.
The first of all, in countless forms.

DHYĀNA 6

I BOW MY HEAD
saśaṅkhacakraṃsakirīṭakuṇḍalaṃsapītavastraṃsarasīruhekṣaṇam |sahāravakṣaḥsthala-kaustubhaśriyaṃnamāmi viṣṇuṃśirasā caturbhujam ‖
With my head bowed, I salute the four-armed Viṣṇu: with conch and discus, with crown and earrings, in yellow garments, lotus-eyed, with the beauty of the necklace and Kaustubha on His chest.

Ten "with"s. The verse strings together sa- words: "with conch, with discus, with crown, with earrings, with yellow silk…". Then the devotee's own action, the simplest of all: namāmi śirasā, "I bow my head".
Conch, discus, crown: I bow my head.

DHYĀNA 7

KṚṢṆA UNDER THE PĀRIJĀTA TREE

In the shade of the Pārijāta tree, seated on a golden lion-throne; dark as a rain cloud, long-eyed, adorned; moon-faced, four-armed, His chest marked with Śrīvatsa; accompanied by Rukmiṇī and Satyabhāmā: in that Kṛṣṇa I take refuge.
WordMeans
chāyāyāmin the shade

Back to Kṛṣṇa. The dhyāna ends with Kṛṣṇa as King of Dvārakā, with the Pārijāta, the heavenly wish-tree He brought down from Indra's garden for Satyabhāmā. The deity of the hymn, "Devakī's son" (Pūrvabhāga 14), is now before our eyes. Now the names can begin.
Under the wish-tree, I take refuge in Kṛṣṇa.

PART 1 ON ONE PAGE

The opening in five lines
Opening prayers. To the obstacle-remover, to Viṣvaksena, to Vyāsa who is Viṣṇu, to the changeless Lord whose remembrance frees us.
The questions. After the war, Yudhiṣṭhira asks Bhīṣma six questions: who is God, what is the goal, whom to praise and worship, what is the highest dharma, what frees us?
The answer. Praise the endless Lord, source and end of all beings, with love, through His thousand names: that is the highest dharma.
The mantra. Seer Vyāsa, metre anuṣṭubh, deity Kṛṣṇa; chanted for peace.
The meditation. The Lord by the milk ocean, as the universe, resting on the serpent, rain-cloud dark, under the wish-tree.
