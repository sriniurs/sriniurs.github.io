THE LORD AS A FRIEND

The tender names in verses 41–54
Neya (398). No one leads Him (400), yet He lets Himself be led by His devotees' love: Kṛṣṇa as Arjuna's charioteer.
Viśrāma (424). After all the turning of time, He is the place where the tired soul rests.
Anirviṇṇa (435). He never tires of us, never gives up on us, however often we fall.
Suhṛt (460) and Vatsala (471). The good friend of all beings, with a mother cow's love that does not mind the dirt.
Satyasandha (510). True to His word: "My devotee never perishes" (Gītā 9.31).

ZAPPY: I thought the names would all be grand cosmic titles!

PROF. BOLT: Many are. But tucked between them are names like these, which show a God who is close, patient and kind.

VERSE 41 · NAMES 373–383

THE HIDDEN CAUSE
udbhavaḥ kṣobhaṇo devaḥśrīgarbhaḥ parameśvaraḥ |karaṇaṃ kāraṇaṃ kartāvikartā gahano guhaḥ ‖41‖
He is the origin, the stirrer (of nature), the shining God, who holds Śrī within, the supreme Lord; the instrument, the cause, the doer, the varied maker, the unfathomable, the hidden.
No.NameMeans
373udbhavaḥthe origin
374kṣobhaṇaḥwho stirs nature into creation
375devaḥthe shining God
376śrī-garbhaḥholding Śrī within
377parama-īśvaraḥthe supreme Lord
378karaṇamthe instrument
379kāraṇamthe cause
380kartāthe doer
381vikartāmaker of variety
382gahanaḥthe unfathomable
383guhaḥthe hidden one
Every kind of cause. Names 378–381 cover them all: the tool, the material, the maker and the shaper of variety. Yet the cause of all is gahana and guha (382–383): deep and hidden, found within, "in the cave of the heart".
The cause of everything, hidden in the cave of the heart.

VERSE 42 · NAMES 384–393

DHRUVA'S STAR
vyavasāyo vyavasthānaḥsaṃsthānaḥ sthānado dhruvaḥ |parardhiḥ paramaspaṣṭastuṣṭaḥ puṣṭaḥ śubhekṣaṇaḥ ‖42‖
He is resolve itself, the support of order, the ultimate state, the giver of places (to His devotees), the fixed; of supreme glory, utterly clear, content, full, whose glance is auspicious.
No.NameMeans
384vyavasāyaḥresolve, knowledge
385vyavasthānaḥthe basis of order
386saṃsthānaḥthe final resting place
387sthāna-daḥgiver of a place (to Dhruva)
388dhruvaḥthe fixed, unchanging
389para-ṛddhiḥof supreme glory
390parama-spaṣṭaḥutterly clear
391tuṣṭaḥcontent
392puṣṭaḥfull
393śubha-īkṣaṇaḥwhose glance is auspicious
Dhruva's place. Sthānada (387): the Lord who gave the child Dhruva a fixed place in the sky, the pole star, after his single-minded penance. The next name, Dhruva (388), means "the fixed". Śubhekṣaṇa (393): one glance from Him is a blessing.
He gives us a place that never moves.

VERSE 43 · NAMES 394–404

RĀMA, NAME 394
rāmo virāmo virajomārgo neyo nayo'nayaḥ |vīraḥ śaktimatāṃ śreṣṭhodharmo dharmavid uttamaḥ ‖43‖
He is Rāma, the delight; the end (of all); the passionless; the path, the one to be led to, the leader, without a leader; the hero, best of the powerful, dharma, the best of the knowers of dharma.
No.NameMeans
394rāmaḥRāma: in whom yogis delight
395virāmaḥthe end, final rest
396virajaḥstainless (some texts: virataḥ)
397mārgaḥthe path
398neyaḥwho can be led (by devotees' love)
399nayaḥthe leader
400anayaḥwho has no leader
401vīraḥthe hero
402śaktimatāṃ śreṣṭhaḥbest of the powerful
403dharmaḥdharma
404dharma-vid-uttamaḥbest of the knowers of dharma
Rāma (394). "The one in whom yogis delight (ramante)". Neya (398) is beautiful: the Lord of all, who has no leader (400), lets Himself be "led" by His devotees' love, as Rāma by Sītā's wish for the golden deer, or Kṛṣṇa by Arjuna as his charioteer.
No one leads Him, yet love can lead Him anywhere.

VERSE 44 · NAMES 405–415

OṂ, THE PRAṆAVA
vaikuṇṭhaḥ puruṣaḥ prāṇaḥprāṇadaḥ praṇavaḥ pṛthuḥ |hiraṇyagarbhaḥ śatrughnovyāpto vāyur adhokṣajaḥ ‖44‖
He is Vaikuṇṭha, the Person, life, the giver of life, the sacred syllable Oṃ, the vast; the golden womb, the slayer of foes, the pervader, the wind, beyond the senses.
No.NameMeans
405vaikuṇṭhaḥVaikuṇṭha: who removes obstacles
406puruṣaḥthe Person
407prāṇaḥlife
408prāṇa-daḥgiver of life
409praṇavaḥOṃ, the sacred syllable
410pṛthuḥthe vast
411hiraṇya-garbhaḥthe golden womb
412śatru-ghnaḥslayer of foes
413vyāptaḥthe pervader
414vāyuḥthe wind
415adhokṣajaḥbeyond the senses
Praṇava (409). Oṃ is the sound that stands for the Absolute; the Lord is Oṃ itself. Vaikuṇṭha (405): read as "He who removes obstacles (kuṇṭha)", and also the name of His own world.
He is Oṃ itself.

VERSE 45 · NAMES 416–425

SUDARŚANA AND TIME
ṛtuḥ sudarśanaḥ kālaḥparameṣṭhī parigrahaḥ |ugraḥ saṃvatsaro dakṣoviśrāmo viśvadakṣiṇaḥ ‖45‖
He is the season, the beautiful sight (and the discus), time; the one in the highest place, the one who receives (all offerings); the fierce, the year, the skilful, the place of rest, the most generous.
No.NameMeans
416ṛtuḥthe season
417sudarśanaḥof beautiful sight; the discus
418kālaḥtime
419parameṣṭhīin the highest place
420parigrahaḥwho accepts all offerings
421ugraḥthe fierce
422saṃvatsaraḥthe year
423dakṣaḥthe skilful
424viśrāmaḥthe place of rest
425viśva-dakṣiṇaḥmost generous, skilful in all
Sudarśana (417). "Beautiful to see", and the name of His discus, the wheel of time that cuts down evil. Next to it, Kāla, time itself. And then a gentle name: Viśrāma (424), the place where the weary soul finally rests.
Time itself, and the rest at its end.

VERSE 46 · NAMES 426–434

THE IMPERISHABLE SEED
vistāraḥ sthāvarasthāṇuḥpramāṇaṃ bījam avyayam |artho'nartho mahākośomahābhogo mahādhanaḥ ‖46‖
He is the expanse, the steady ground, the measure (of truth), the imperishable seed; the goal sought, the one who seeks nothing, the great treasury, the great enjoyment, the great wealth.
No.NameMeans
426vistāraḥthe expanse
427sthāvara-sthāṇuḥsteady and still
428pramāṇamthe measure, the proof
429bījam avyayamthe imperishable seed
430arthaḥthe goal everyone seeks
431anarthaḥwho seeks nothing
432mahā-kośaḥthe great treasury
433mahā-bhogaḥthe great enjoyment
434mahā-dhanaḥthe great wealth
Artha and anartha (430–431). A paradox pair: everyone seeks Him (artha), and He seeks nothing (anartha), being full. And the seed that never perishes (429): the whole tree of the world grows from Him and returns to Him.
Everyone seeks Him; He seeks nothing.

VERSE 47 · NAMES 435–444

HUB OF THE STARS
anirviṇṇaḥ sthaviṣṭho'bhūrdharmayūpo mahāmakhaḥ |nakṣatranemir nakṣatrīkṣamaḥ kṣāmaḥ samīhanaḥ ‖47‖
He never tires, is the most vast, unborn; the post of dharma, the great sacrifice; the hub of the stars, the moon among stars; patient, the one who reduces (all at dissolution), the one who strives (for creation).
No.NameMeans
435anirviṇṇaḥnever weary, never dejected
436sthaviṣṭhaḥthe most vast
437abhūḥunborn
438dharma-yūpaḥthe post of dharma
439mahā-makhaḥthe great sacrifice
440nakṣatra-nemiḥhub of the stars
441nakṣatrīthe moon, lord of stars
442kṣamaḥpatient, capable
443kṣāmaḥwho reduces all at dissolution
444samīhanaḥwho strives for creation
Anirviṇṇa (435). "Never weary, never discouraged": He never tires of saving us, however often we fall. Nakṣatranemi (440): like the hub around which the stars wheel, the still centre of the cosmos.
He never tires of us.

VERSE 48 · NAMES 445–454

SACRIFICE AND KNOWLEDGE
yajña ijyo mahejyaś cakratuḥ satraṃ satāṃ gatiḥ |sarvadarśī vimuktātmāsarvajño jñānam uttamam ‖48‖
He is the sacrifice, the one worshipped, the most worshipped, the rite, the long sacrifice, the goal of the good; all-seeing, of free nature, all-knowing, the highest knowledge.
No.NameMeans
445yajñaḥthe sacrifice
446ijyaḥthe one worshipped
447mahā-ijyaḥmost worshipped
448kratuḥthe rite
449satramthe long sacrifice; protector of the good
450satāṃ gatiḥgoal of the good
451sarva-darśīall-seeing
452vimukta-ātmāever free
453sarva-jñaḥall-knowing
454jñānam uttamamthe highest knowledge
From ritual to knowledge. The verse moves from sacrifice (445–449) to knowledge (451–454): the old Vedic journey. The Lord is both the worship and the One who is known through it.
He is the worship, and the knowledge it leads to.

VERSE 49 · NAMES 455–464

THE GOOD FRIEND
suvrataḥ sumukhaḥ sūkṣmaḥsughoṣaḥ sukhadaḥ suhṛt |manoharo jitakrodhovīrabāhur vidāraṇaḥ ‖49‖
He keeps good vows, has a pleasing face, is subtle, of beautiful voice, the giver of happiness, the friend; the enchanter of the mind, the conqueror of anger, of heroic arms, the destroyer (of the wicked).
No.NameMeans
455su-vrataḥof good vows
456su-mukhaḥof pleasing face
457sūkṣmaḥsubtle
458su-ghoṣaḥof beautiful voice
459sukha-daḥgiver of happiness
460su-hṛtthe good friend
461mano-haraḥenchanter of the mind
462jita-krodhaḥconqueror of anger
463vīra-bāhuḥof heroic arms
464vidāraṇaḥdestroyer of the wicked
Suhṛt (460). "The good-hearted friend": in the Gītā (5.29) Kṛṣṇa calls Himself "the friend of all beings". Suvrata (455): His vow is to protect anyone who comes to Him, as Rāma vowed to Vibhīṣaṇa.
Suhṛt: the friend of every being.

VERSE 50 · NAMES 465–474

VATSALA: A MOTHER'S LOVE
svāpanaḥ svavaśo vyāpīnaikātmā naikakarmakṛt |vatsaro vatsalo vatsīratnagarbho dhaneśvaraḥ ‖50‖
He puts beings to sleep (in māyā), is self-governed, all-pervading, of many forms, of many deeds; the dwelling of all, the loving, the protector of calves, the jewel-wombed, the lord of wealth.
No.NameMeans
465svāpanaḥwho puts to sleep (in māyā)
466sva-vaśaḥself-governed
467vyāpīall-pervading
468naika-ātmāof many forms
469naika-karma-kṛtdoer of many deeds
470vatsaraḥthe dwelling of all
471vatsalaḥloving, like a mother cow
472vatsīprotector of calves
473ratna-garbhaḥholding jewels within
474dhana-īśvaraḥlord of wealth
Vatsala (471). The love of a cow for her calf, which licks it clean without minding the dirt. In Śrīvaiṣṇava thought, vātsalya is the quality that makes the Lord overlook our faults (Suprabhātam Stotram, verse 2).
Vatsala: love that does not mind our faults.

VERSE 51 · NAMES 475–485

BEING AND BECOMING
dharmagub dharmakṛd dharmīsad asat kṣaram akṣaram |avijñātā sahasrāṃśurvidhātā kṛtalakṣaṇaḥ ‖51‖
He guards dharma, practises dharma, upholds dharma; He is being and non-being, the perishable and the imperishable; the non-knower (of anything other), the thousand-rayed, the arranger, the one of perfect marks.
No.NameMeans
475dharma-gupguardian of dharma
476dharma-kṛtwho practises dharma
477dharmīupholder of dharma
478satbeing, the real
479asatnon-being, the unmanifest
480kṣaramthe perishable
481akṣaramthe imperishable
482avijñātāthe non-knower (of limits)
483sahasra-aṃśuḥthe thousand-rayed
484vidhātāthe arranger
485kṛta-lakṣaṇaḥof perfect marks; who wrote the scriptures
Both sides of reality. Names 478–481: being and non-being, perishable and imperishable, are all Him. The Gītā (9.19) says the same: "I am immortality and death, being and non-being, Arjuna."
Being and becoming: all are He.

VERSE 52 · NAMES 486–493

GREAT GOD OF THE GODS
gabhastinemiḥ sattvasthaḥsiṃho bhūtamaheśvaraḥ |ādidevo mahādevodeveśo devabhṛd guruḥ ‖52‖
He is the hub of the wheel of rays, dwells in goodness, the lion, the great Lord of beings; the first God, the great God, Lord of the gods, the teacher who sustains the gods.
No.NameMeans
486gabhasti-nemiḥhub of the wheel of rays
487sattva-sthaḥdwelling in goodness
488siṃhaḥthe lion (again)
489bhūta-mahā-īśvaraḥgreat Lord of beings
490ādi-devaḥthe first God
491mahā-devaḥthe great God
492deva-īśaḥLord of the gods
493deva-bhṛt-guruḥteacher who sustains the gods
Mahādeva (491). Usually a name of Śiva; here Śaṅkara explains it as "the great God, who gives up everything to be known as the Self". Another meeting of Śiva and Viṣṇu names, read in more than one way.
The first God, the great God of all gods.

VERSE 53 · NAMES 494–502

NAME 500 · LORD OF THE MONKEYS
uttaro gopatir goptājñānagamyaḥ purātanaḥ |śarīrabhūtabhṛd bhoktākapīndro bhūridakṣiṇaḥ ‖53‖
He is the higher (who lifts up), the lord of cows (and the earth), the protector, reached by knowledge, the ancient; the sustainer of the elements of the body, the enjoyer, the Lord of the monkeys, the most generous.
No.NameMeans
494uttaraḥwho lifts us up, the highest
495go-patiḥlord of cows, of the earth
496goptāthe protector
497jñāna-gamyaḥreached by knowledge
498purātanaḥthe ancient
499śarīra-bhūta-bhṛtsustainer of the body's elements
500bhoktāthe enjoyer (of offerings)
501kapi-indraḥLord of the monkeys: Rāma; Varāha
502bhūri-dakṣiṇaḥmost generous
The halfway mark. Name 500, Bhoktā: the Lord accepts and enjoys every offering. Then Kapīndra (501): Rāma, Lord of Hanumān and the monkey army (or Varāha, as kapi also means boar). Gopati and Goptā: the cowherd who protects.
Halfway: the Lord who accepts every offering.

VERSE 54 · NAMES 503–512

TRUE TO HIS WORD
somapo'mṛtapaḥ somaḥpurujit purusattamaḥ |vinayo jayaḥ satyasandhodāśārhaḥ sātvatāṃ patiḥ ‖54‖
He drinks the Soma, drinks nectar, is Soma itself; conqueror of many, best of the many; the trainer (of the wicked), victory, true to His word, the descendant of Daśārha, the Lord of the Sātvatas.
No.NameMeans
503soma-paḥdrinker of Soma
504amṛta-paḥdrinker of nectar
505somaḥSoma, the moon
506puru-jitconqueror of many
507puru-sattamaḥbest of the many
508vinayaḥwho disciplines the wicked
509jayaḥvictory
510satya-sandhaḥtrue to His word
511dāśārhaḥof the Daśārha clan: Kṛṣṇa
512sātvatāṃ patiḥLord of the Sātvatas
Satyasandha (510). "True to His promise". Rāma promised protection to all who come to Him; Kṛṣṇa promised Arjuna, "My devotee never perishes" (Gītā 9.31). Dāśārha and Sātvata are names of Kṛṣṇa's Yādava clans.
Satyasandha: He always keeps His promise.
