PREVIOUSLY · AND NOW

In Part 5, names 373–512: the hidden cause, Rāma, Oṃ, time and rest, the good friend, a mother's love, name 500, and the Lord who keeps His word.

Part 6 covers verses 55–67, names 513 to 632. Here come many avatāras (Kapila, Matsya, Varāha, Paraśurāma, Balarāma), the Lord as medicine and doctor, a dozen Śrī names in a row, name 600, and the One who cuts our doubts and destroys our grief.

VERSE 55 · NAMES 513–520

MUKUNDA, GIVER OF FREEDOM
jīvo vinayitā sākṣīmukundo'mitavikramaḥ |ambhonidhir anantātmāmahodadhiśayo'ntakaḥ ‖55‖
He is the living soul, the witness of discipline, the giver of liberation, of boundless valour; the ocean, the infinite Self, who lies on the great ocean, and the end of all.
No.NameMeans
513jīvaḥthe living one, life in all
514vinayitā-sākṣīwitness of humility and discipline
515mukundaḥgiver of liberation
516amita-vikramaḥof boundless valour
517ambho-nidhiḥthe ocean
518ananta-ātmāthe infinite Self
519mahā-udadhi-śayaḥwho lies on the great ocean
520antakaḥthe end of all
Mukunda (515). Read as "giver of mukti, liberation". Kulaśekhara Āḻvār's famous hymn Mukundamālā is named after it. Mahodadhiśaya (519): the Lord resting on Ādiśeṣa on the cosmic ocean.
Mukunda: the giver of freedom.

VERSE 56 · NAMES 521–530

BLISS AND THE THREE STRIDES
ajo mahārhaḥ svābhāvyojitāmitraḥ pramodanaḥ |ānando nandano nandaḥsatyadharmā trivikramaḥ ‖56‖
He is the unborn, the most worthy, of His own nature, the conqueror of enemies, the giver of joy; bliss, the delight, the joyful, of true dharma, the one of three strides.
No.NameMeans
521ajaḥthe unborn
522mahā-arhaḥmost worthy of worship
523svābhāvyaḥof His own nature
524jita-amitraḥconqueror of foes (inner and outer)
525pramodanaḥwho makes joyful
526ānandaḥbliss
527nandanaḥwho delights others
528nandaḥthe joyful
529satya-dharmāof true dharma
530tri-vikramaḥof three strides
Three joy-names. Ānanda, Nandana, Nanda (526–528): He is bliss, gives bliss, and enjoys bliss. Trivikrama (530): Vāmana's three strides, already met in names 151–153, now named directly.
Bliss itself, who gives bliss to all.

VERSE 57 · NAMES 531–537

KAPILA AND THE GREAT HORN
maharṣiḥ kapilācāryaḥkṛtajño medinīpatiḥ |tripadas tridaśādhyakṣomahāśṛṅgaḥ kṛtāntakṛt ‖57‖
He is the great sage Kapila, the teacher; the grateful, the lord of the earth; of three steps, overseer of the gods, the great-horned (fish), the ender of death.
No.NameMeans
531mahā-ṛṣiḥ kapila-ācāryaḥthe great sage Kapila
532kṛta-jñaḥgrateful (again)
533medinī-patiḥlord of the earth
534tri-padaḥof three steps
535tridaśa-adhyakṣaḥoverseer of the gods
536mahā-śṛṅgaḥthe great-horned fish (Matsya)
537kṛtānta-kṛtender of death
Two avatāras. Kapila (531), the sage of Sāṅkhya philosophy, counted in the Bhāgavatam among the Lord's forms. Mahāśṛṅga (536): Matsya, the great fish whose horn towed Manu's boat through the flood. Kṛtajña returns (532): grateful again.
The sage-teacher and the great fish: both His forms.

VERSE 58 · NAMES 538–546

THE GREAT BOAR
mahāvarāho govindaḥsuṣeṇaḥ kanakāṅgadī |guhyo gabhīro gahanoguptaś cakragadādharaḥ ‖58‖
He is the great Boar, Govinda; with a fine army, wearing golden armlets; secret, deep, unfathomable, hidden, the bearer of discus and mace.
No.NameMeans
538mahā-varāhaḥthe great Boar
539govindaḥGovinda: who found the earth
540su-ṣeṇaḥwith a fine army
541kanaka-aṅgadīwearing golden armlets
542guhyaḥsecret
543gabhīraḥdeep
544gahanaḥunfathomable
545guptaḥhidden
546cakra-gadā-dharaḥbearer of discus and mace
Varāha and Govinda. The great Boar (538) lifted the earth from the ocean; so the very next name, Govinda (539), is read here as "who found (vinda) the earth (go)". Then four names of depth: secret, deep, unfathomable, hidden.
The Boar who found and lifted the earth.

VERSE 59 · NAMES 547–557

THE TREE OF REFUGE
vedhāḥ svāṅgo'jitaḥ kṛṣṇodṛḍhaḥ saṅkarṣaṇo'cyutaḥ |varuṇo vāruṇo vṛkṣaḥpuṣkarākṣo mahāmanāḥ ‖59‖
He is the creator, the beautiful-limbed, the unconquered, Kṛṣṇa, the firm, Saṅkarṣaṇa the unfailing; Varuṇa, Varuṇa's son, the tree, lotus-eyed, great-minded.
No.NameMeans
547vedhāḥthe creator
548sva-aṅgaḥof beautiful limbs
549ajitaḥthe unconquered
550kṛṣṇaḥKṛṣṇa (again)
551dṛḍhaḥthe firm
552saṅkarṣaṇaḥ acyutaḥSaṅkarṣaṇa, the unfailing
553varuṇaḥVaruṇa, lord of waters
554vāruṇaḥVaruṇa's son (Vasiṣṭha)
555vṛkṣaḥthe tree
556puṣkara-akṣaḥlotus-eyed
557mahā-manāḥgreat-minded
Vṛkṣa (555): the tree. Like a great tree, the Lord stands firm and shelters all who come to Him, giving shade and fruit. Saṅkarṣaṇa (552) is Balarāma, and the second vyūha (see name 138).
Like a great tree, He shelters all who come.

VERSE 60 · NAMES 558–566

BHAGAVĀN
bhagavān bhagahā'nandīvanamālī halāyudhaḥ |ādityo jyotirādityaḥsahiṣṇur gatisattamaḥ ‖60‖
He is Bhagavān, the remover (of the six glories, at dissolution), the blissful, wearing the forest garland, bearing the plough as weapon; the son of Aditi, the sun of light, the patient, the best refuge.
No.NameMeans
558bhagavānBhagavān: of six glories
559bhaga-hāwho withdraws them at dissolution
560ānandīthe blissful
561vana-mālīwearing the forest garland
562hala-āyudhaḥwith the plough as weapon (Balarāma)
563ādityaḥAditi's son (Vāmana)
564jyotiḥ-ādityaḥthe sun of light
565sahiṣṇuḥthe patient (again)
566gati-sattamaḥthe best refuge
Bhagavān (558). The Viṣṇu Purāṇa defines bhaga as six glories: lordship, strength, fame, prosperity, knowledge and detachment, in full. The one who has all six is Bhagavān. Then the brothers: Kṛṣṇa with His forest garland (561), Balarāma with His plough (562).
Bhagavān: full of all six glories.

VERSE 61 · NAMES 567–573

VYĀSA, AND THE AXE
sudhanvā khaṇḍaparaśurdāruṇo draviṇapradaḥ |divaḥspṛk sarvadṛg vyāsovācaspatir ayonijaḥ ‖61‖
He has a fine bow, the broken axe (Paraśurāma), is fierce (to the wicked), the giver of wealth; touching heaven, the all-seeing Vyāsa, the lord of speech, not born from a womb.
No.NameMeans
567su-dhanvāwith a fine bow (Śārṅga)
568khaṇḍa-paraśuḥwith the axe (Paraśurāma)
569dāruṇaḥfierce to the wicked
570draviṇa-pradaḥgiver of wealth
571divaḥ-spṛktouching heaven
572sarva-dṛk-vyāsaḥthe all-seeing Vyāsa
573vācaspatiḥ ayonijaḥlord of speech, not womb-born
Vyāsa among the names (572). The seer of the hymn appears inside it: "the all-seeing Vyāsa", who divided the Veda in four. Remember Opening Prayer 4: "Vyāsa is Viṣṇu, Viṣṇu is Vyāsa". Khaṇḍaparaśu (568) is Paraśurāma.
The seer of the hymn is one of its names.

VERSE 62 · NAMES 574–585

MEDICINE AND DOCTOR, PEACE
trisāmā sāmagaḥ sāmanirvāṇaṃ bheṣajaṃ bhiṣak |saṃnyāsakṛc chamaḥ śāntoniṣṭhā śāntiḥ parāyaṇam ‖62‖
He is praised in three Sāmans, the singer of Sāman, the Sāman itself; liberation, the medicine, the physician; the founder of renunciation, calm, peaceful, the foundation, peace, the final goal.
No.NameMeans
574tri-sāmāpraised in three Sāmans
575sāma-gaḥsinger of the Sāma Veda
576sāmathe Sāma Veda
577nirvāṇamliberation, supreme bliss
578bheṣajamthe medicine
579bhiṣakthe physician
580saṃnyāsa-kṛtfounder of renunciation
581śamaḥcalm
582śāntaḥpeaceful
583niṣṭhāthe foundation
584śāntiḥpeace
585parāyaṇamthe final goal
Medicine and doctor together (578–579). After Vaidya (164) and Auṣadham (287), the Lord is now both the cure and the healer of the disease of saṃsāra. Then five names of peace: calm, peaceful, foundation, peace, final goal.
The cure and the doctor: He heals, and He is peace.

VERSE 63 · NAMES 586–595

FRIEND OF THE COWS
śubhāṅgaḥ śāntidaḥ sraṣṭākumudaḥ kuvaleśayaḥ |gohito gopatir goptāvṛṣabhākṣo vṛṣapriyaḥ ‖63‖
He has auspicious limbs, gives peace, is the creator, the delighter of the earth, who rests on the waters; the friend of cows, the lord of cows, the protector, bull-eyed, the lover of dharma.
No.NameMeans
586śubha-aṅgaḥof auspicious limbs
587śānti-daḥgiver of peace
588sraṣṭāthe creator
589ku-mudaḥdelighter of the earth
590kuvale-śayaḥwho rests on the waters
591go-hitaḥfriend of the cows
592go-patiḥlord of cows (again)
593goptāthe protector (again)
594vṛṣabha-akṣaḥbull-eyed; whose eyes rain blessings
595vṛṣa-priyaḥwho loves dharma
Gohita (591). "Kind to cows": the cowherd boy of Vraja who protected the cows from Indra's storm. Go also means the earth and the Vedas: He is friend to all three. Śāntida (587), the giver of peace, follows the peace names of verse 62.
The cowherd who cares for every creature.

VERSE 64 · NAMES 596–604

THE ŚRĪ NAMES BEGIN
anivartī nivṛttātmāsaṅkṣeptā kṣemakṛc chivaḥ |śrīvatsavakṣāḥ śrīvāsaḥśrīpatiḥ śrīmatāṃ varaḥ ‖64‖
He never turns back (from His dharma), His nature is detached; He compresses (the universe), brings well-being, is auspicious; with Śrīvatsa on His chest, the dwelling of Śrī, the lord of Śrī, the best of the glorious.
No.NameMeans
596anivartīwho never turns back
597nivṛtta-ātmādetached in nature
598saṅkṣeptāwho draws the universe in
599kṣema-kṛtbringer of well-being
600śivaḥthe auspicious (again)
601śrīvatsa-vakṣāḥwith Śrīvatsa on His chest
602śrī-vāsaḥdwelling of Śrī
603śrī-patiḥLord of Śrī
604śrīmatāṃ varaḥbest of the glorious
Name 600: Śiva. The auspicious one returns (first at 27). Then a cluster of Śrī names begins, running through the next verse: Lakṣmī on His chest (601), His dwelling (602), His wife (603). Kṣemakṛt (599): He takes care of our welfare.
Name 600, the auspicious; then Śrī on His chest.

VERSE 65 · NAMES 605–614

TEN NAMES OF ŚRĪ
śrīdaḥ śrīśaḥ śrīnivāsaḥśrīnidhiḥ śrīvibhāvanaḥ |śrīdharaḥ śrīkaraḥ śreyaḥśrīmān lokatrayāśrayaḥ ‖65‖
He gives Śrī, is Lord of Śrī, the dwelling of Śrī, the treasure of Śrī, who distributes Śrī; who bears Śrī, makes Śrī, is the highest good, possesses Śrī, and is the refuge of the three worlds.
No.NameMeans
605śrī-daḥgiver of Śrī (prosperity)
606śrī-śaḥLord of Śrī
607śrī-nivāsaḥdwelling of Śrī
608śrī-nidhiḥtreasure of Śrī
609śrī-vibhāvanaḥwho distributes Śrī
610śrī-dharaḥbearer of Śrī
611śrī-karaḥwho makes Śrī (for devotees)
612śreyaḥthe highest good
613śrīmānpossessing Śrī
614loka-traya-āśrayaḥrefuge of the three worlds
Śrī everywhere. Nine of these ten names contain Śrī: Lakṣmī, glory, prosperity. Together with verse 64 that's twelve Śrī names in a row. The Lord and Lakṣmī are never apart, as the Suprabhātam and Prapatti also taught.
Śrī, Śrī, Śrī: the Lord is never without Lakṣmī.

VERSE 66 · NAMES 615–623

REMOVER OF DOUBTS
svakṣaḥ svaṅgaḥ śatānandonandir jyotirgaṇeśvaraḥ |vijitātmā'vidheyātmāsatkīrtiś chinnasaṃśayaḥ ‖66‖
He has beautiful eyes and limbs, a hundred joys, is joy, the lord of the hosts of lights; self-conquered, whose nature none can dictate, of true fame, the remover of doubts.
No.NameMeans
615su-akṣaḥof beautiful eyes
616su-aṅgaḥof beautiful limbs
617śata-ānandaḥof a hundred joys
618nandiḥjoy itself
619jyotiḥ-gaṇa-īśvaraḥlord of the hosts of lights
620vijita-ātmāself-conquered
621avidheya-ātmāwhose nature none can command
622sat-kīrtiḥof true fame
623chinna-saṃśayaḥremover of doubts
Chinnasaṃśaya (623). "Whose doubts are cut", and who cuts our doubts. As Kṛṣṇa cut Arjuna's doubts in the Gītā (18.73: "my delusion is gone"). Avidheyātmā (621): no one can command Him, yet He gives Himself freely to love.
He cuts through every doubt.

VERSE 67 · NAMES 624–632

DESTROYER OF SORROW
udīrṇaḥ sarvataścakṣuranīśaḥ śāśvatasthiraḥ |bhūśayo bhūṣaṇo bhūtirviśokaḥ śokanāśanaḥ ‖67‖
He is exalted, with eyes on every side, has no lord above Him, eternal and steady; who lay on the ground (as Rāma), the ornament (of the world), prosperity itself, free from sorrow, the destroyer of sorrow.
No.NameMeans
624udīrṇaḥexalted
625sarvataḥ-cakṣuḥwith eyes on every side
626anīśaḥwith no lord above Him
627śāśvata-sthiraḥeternal and steady
628bhū-śayaḥwho lay on the ground (Rāma at the sea)
629bhūṣaṇaḥthe ornament of the world
630bhūtiḥprosperity itself
631vi-śokaḥfree from sorrow
632śoka-nāśanaḥdestroyer of sorrow
Bhūśaya (628). Rāma lay on the bare ground by the sea for three days, praying to the ocean for a path to Laṅkā: the Lord of all, sleeping on the earth for His devotee. The verse ends with comfort: Śokanāśana (632), destroyer of grief.
Śokanāśana: He takes away our grief.
