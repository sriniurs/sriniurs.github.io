PREVIOUSLY · AND NOW

In Part 3, names 133–255: the fourfold Lord, Vāmana, the healer, the mountain-lifter, Śrīnivāsa and Govinda, the lion (200), the Cosmic Person and Nārāyaṇa.

Part 4 covers verses 28–40, names 256 to 372. Themes here: dharma as His body, the Lord as medicine and bridge, desire ended and fulfilled, the peacock-feathered Kṛṣṇa, Vāsudeva, the ferryman across saṃsāra, and Dāmodara, bound by a mother's love.

THREE THREADS IN THIS PART

What to listen for in verses 28–40
Dharma (vṛṣa). Verse 28 uses "vṛṣa", the bull of dharma, five times: dharma is not a rule outside the Lord but His very body and steps.
Healing. After Vaidya the physician (164), He is now Auṣadham, the medicine (287), and Setu, the bridge (288): cure and crossing for the disease of saṃsāra.
Desire (kāma). Verse 32 holds five names on desire: He ends it, fulfils it, gives it, and is Himself the beloved we most desire.
Kṛṣṇa of Vṛndāvana. Śikhaṇḍī, the peacock feather (311); Śauri (340); and Dāmodara (367), tied to a mortar by Yaśodā.
Repeats. Viṣṇu (258), Acyuta (318), Padmanābha (346), Mahīdhara (317, 369), Vāsudeva (332, again at 695 and 709): each time with a fresh meaning.

ZAPPY: Why repeat names at all?

PROF. BOLT: Because one meaning is never enough for the Lord. Each repeat opens another door.

VERSE 28 · NAMES 256–264

DHARMA IN EVERY LIMB
vṛṣāhī vṛṣabho viṣṇurvṛṣaparvā vṛṣodaraḥ |vardhano vardhamānaś caviviktaḥ śrutisāgaraḥ ‖28‖
He shines in dharma, is dharma itself, all-pervading; dharma is His limbs and His being; He makes all grow and grows with them; He stands apart, and is the ocean of scripture.
No.NameMeans
256vṛṣa-ahīwho shines in dharma
257vṛṣabhaḥdharma itself; who showers blessings
258viṣṇuḥthe all-pervading (again)
259vṛṣa-parvāwhose steps are dharma
260vṛṣa-udaraḥwhose being is dharma
261vardhanaḥwho makes grow
262vardhamānaḥwho grows (as the world)
263viviktaḥwho stands apart
264śruti-sāgaraḥocean of scripture
Vṛṣa = dharma. Five names here use vṛṣa, "bull", the classic symbol of dharma. Viṣṇu returns as name 258 (first at 2): Śaṅkara reads it this time as "who pervades through His steps".
Dharma is His very body.

VERSE 29 · NAMES 265–274

PRESENT IN THE RAYS
subhujo durdharo vāgmīmahendro vasudo vasuḥ |naikarūpo bṛhadrūpaḥśipiviṣṭaḥ prakāśanaḥ ‖29‖
He has beautiful arms, cannot be held, is eloquent, the great Indra, the giver of wealth, wealth itself; of many forms, of vast form, present in the rays, the revealer.
No.NameMeans
265su-bhujaḥof beautiful arms
266durdharaḥwho cannot be held (by others)
267vāgmīeloquent
268mahā-indraḥthe great Indra
269vasu-daḥgiver of wealth
270vasuḥwealth itself
271naika-rūpaḥof many forms
272bṛhat-rūpaḥof vast form
273śipiviṣṭaḥpresent in the rays
274prakāśanaḥthe revealer
Giver and gift. Vasuda (269) "gives wealth", and Vasu (270) "is wealth". Śaṅkara's note: He gives Himself to His devotees, the greatest wealth. Śipiviṣṭa is an old Vedic name of Viṣṇu: "pervading the rays".
He gives wealth, and is the wealth He gives.

VERSE 30 · NAMES 275–282

MOONBEAM AND SUNLIGHT
ojas tejo dyutidharaḥprakāśātmā pratāpanaḥ |ṛddhaḥ spaṣṭākṣaro mantraścandrāṃśur bhāskaradyutiḥ ‖30‖
He holds vigour, splendour and light; His nature is radiance; He burns (the wicked); He is prosperous, the clearly sounded syllable (Oṃ), the mantra, the moonbeam, the sun's light.
No.NameMeans
275ojas-tejo-dyuti-dharaḥholder of vigour, splendour, light
276prakāśa-ātmāwhose nature is radiance
277pratāpanaḥwho scorches (the wicked)
278ṛddhaḥprosperous, full
279spaṣṭa-akṣaraḥthe clear syllable: Oṃ
280mantraḥthe mantra
281candra-aṃśuḥthe moonbeam
282bhāskara-dyutiḥthe sun's light
Moon and sun. The same Lord is the cool moonbeam that soothes and the hot sunlight that sustains. And He is mantra itself (280): the sacred sound, Oṃ (279), is His name.
Cool as moonlight, bright as the sun.

VERSE 31 · NAMES 283–289

MEDICINE AND BRIDGE
amṛtāṃśūdbhavo bhānuḥśaśabinduḥ sureśvaraḥ |auṣadhaṃ jagataḥ setuḥsatyadharmaparākramaḥ ‖31‖
He is the source of the moon, the sun, the moon's mark (who nourishes plants), Lord of the gods; the medicine, the bridge of the world, whose valour is truth and dharma.
No.NameMeans
283amṛta-aṃśu-udbhavaḥsource of the moon
284bhānuḥthe sun, the shining
285śaśa-binduḥthe moon (marked by a hare)
286sura-īśvaraḥLord of the gods
287auṣadhamthe medicine
288jagataḥ setuḥthe bridge of the world
289satya-dharma-parākramaḥwhose valour is truth and dharma
Auṣadham: medicine. After Vaidya the physician (164), now the Lord is the medicine itself (287) for the disease of saṃsāra. And Setu (288): the bridge across it, and the dam that keeps the worlds in order.
He is the medicine and the bridge.

VERSE 32 · NAMES 290–299

DESIRE: ENDER AND GIVER
bhūtabhavyabhavannāthaḥpavanaḥ pāvano'nalaḥ |kāmahā kāmakṛt kāntaḥkāmaḥ kāmapradaḥ prabhuḥ ‖32‖
He is the Lord of past, future and present; the wind, the purifier, the fire; destroyer of desire, fulfiller of desire, the beloved, the desired, giver of what is desired, the master.
No.NameMeans
290bhūta-bhavya-bhavat-nāthaḥLord of past, future, present
291pavanaḥthe wind, purifier
292pāvanaḥwho makes pure
293analaḥfire; never satisfied
294kāma-hādestroyer of desire
295kāma-kṛtfulfiller of desire
296kāntaḥthe beloved, beautiful
297kāmaḥthe desired
298kāma-pradaḥgiver of what is desired
299prabhuḥthe master
Five names on desire. He destroys desire in seekers of liberation (294), fulfils it for those who ask (295), is the beautiful one we desire (296–297), and gives every wish (298). The same Lord meets each of us where we are.
He ends desire, fulfils it, and is the One we truly desire.

VERSE 33 · NAMES 300–307

MAKER OF THE AGES
yugādikṛd yugāvartonaikamāyo mahāśanaḥ |adṛśyo vyaktarūpaś casahasrajid anantajit ‖33‖
He begins the ages and turns them; of many māyās, the great consumer; unseen, yet of manifest form; victor over thousands, victor over the endless.
No.NameMeans
300yuga-ādi-kṛtwho begins the ages
301yuga-āvartaḥwho turns the ages
302naika-māyaḥof many māyās
303mahā-aśanaḥthe great consumer (at dissolution)
304adṛśyaḥunseen
305vyakta-rūpaḥof manifest form
306sahasra-jitvictor over thousands
307ananta-jitever victorious
Unseen and seen. Names 304–305: He is invisible to the senses, yet manifest in His forms, in temples and in the world. Name 300 is a milestone: the maker of the four yugas, Kṛta, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali.
Unseen, yet seen everywhere.

VERSE 34 · NAMES 308–317

THE PEACOCK FEATHER
iṣṭo'viśiṣṭaḥ śiṣṭeṣṭaḥśikhaṇḍī nahuṣo vṛṣaḥ |krodhahā krodhakṛt kartāviśvabāhur mahīdharaḥ ‖34‖
He is the beloved, the same in all, dear to the good, wearing a peacock feather, the binder (by māyā), dharma; destroyer of anger, who acts (against the wicked), the doer, whose arms are everywhere, the bearer of the earth.
No.NameMeans
308iṣṭaḥthe beloved; worshipped
309aviśiṣṭaḥthe same in all
310śiṣṭa-iṣṭaḥdear to the good
311śikhaṇḍīwearing a peacock feather
312nahuṣaḥwho binds by māyā
313vṛṣaḥdharma
314krodha-hādestroyer of anger
315krodha-kṛt-kartādoer, who acts against wrong
316viśva-bāhuḥwhose arms are everywhere
317mahī-dharaḥbearer of the earth
Śikhaṇḍī (311). "The one with the peacock feather": Kṛṣṇa of Vṛndāvana. Krodhahā (314): He removes anger from His devotees. "Whose arms are everywhere" (316): He can help anyone, anywhere.
The peacock-feathered One, who takes away our anger.
Name 315 is read as one compound, krodha-kṛt-kartā, as on shlokam.org.

VERSE 35 · NAMES 318–326

NEVER CARELESS
acyutaḥ prathitaḥ prāṇaḥprāṇado vāsavānujaḥ |apāṃnidhir adhiṣṭhānamapramattaḥ pratiṣṭhitaḥ ‖35‖
He is the unfailing, the renowned, life, the giver of life, Indra's younger brother; the ocean, the foundation of all, never careless, established in Himself.
No.NameMeans
318acyutaḥthe unfailing (again)
319prathitaḥrenowned
320prāṇaḥlife
321prāṇa-daḥgiver of life
322vāsava-anujaḥIndra's younger brother (Vāmana)
323apāṃ-nidhiḥthe ocean
324adhiṣṭhānamthe foundation of all
325apramattaḥnever careless
326pratiṣṭhitaḥestablished in Himself
Apramatta (325). "Never careless": He never forgets to give each being the fruit of its actions, and never neglects His devotees. Acyuta returns (318), with the same promise as name 100.
He never forgets, never neglects His own.

VERSE 36 · NAMES 327–335

VĀSUDEVA
skandaḥ skandadharo dhuryovarado vāyuvāhanaḥ |vāsudevo bṛhadbhānurādidevaḥ purandaraḥ ‖36‖
He is the flow (of nectar), the upholder of dharma's path, the bearer of burdens, the giver of boons, who moves the winds; Vāsudeva, of vast radiance, the first God, the destroyer of cities (of demons).
No.NameMeans
327skandaḥthe flowing (as nectar)
328skanda-dharaḥupholder of dharma's path
329dhuryaḥbearer of the burden
330vara-daḥgiver of boons
331vāyu-vāhanaḥwho moves the winds
332vāsudevaḥVāsudeva: dwelling in all
333bṛhat-bhānuḥof vast radiance
334ādi-devaḥthe first God
335puram-daraḥdestroyer of (demons') cities
Vāsudeva (332). Read as "the God (deva) who dwells (vasu) in all", or Kṛṣṇa as son of Vasudeva. The famous twelve-syllable mantra is "oṃ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya". The name comes again at 695 and 709 with other senses.
Vāsudeva: the God who dwells in all.

VERSE 37 · NAMES 336–345

WITHOUT SORROW
aśokas tāraṇas tāraḥśūraḥ śaurir janeśvaraḥ |anukūlaḥ śatāvartaḥpadmī padmanibhekṣaṇaḥ ‖37‖
He is without sorrow, the one who carries across, the saviour, the valiant, Śauri (Kṛṣṇa), Lord of beings; ever favourable, of a hundred descents, holding a lotus, with lotus eyes.
No.NameMeans
336aśokaḥwithout sorrow
337tāraṇaḥwho carries across
338tāraḥthe saviour
339śūraḥthe valiant
340śauriḥŚauri: Kṛṣṇa, of Śūra's line
341jana-īśvaraḥLord of beings
342anukūlaḥever favourable
343śata-āvartaḥof a hundred descents
344padmīholding a lotus
345padma-nibha-īkṣaṇaḥlotus-eyed
Anukūla (342). "Favourable to all": the Lord is never against anyone; even His punishments correct rather than destroy. Tāraṇa and Tāra (337–338): He is the ferryman across the river of saṃsāra.
The ferryman who is never against us.

LOTUS AND EAGLE

He is lotus-navelled, lotus-eyed, seated in the lotus (of the heart), the bearer of bodies; of great prosperity, prosperous, of ancient nature, of great eyes, with Garuḍa on His banner.

349śarīra-bhṛtbearer of bodies

He sits in the lotus of the heart.

VERSE 39 · NAMES 355–362

BEYOND COMPARE
atulaḥ śarabho bhīmaḥsamayajño havirhariḥ |sarvalakṣaṇalakṣaṇyolakṣmīvān samitiñjayaḥ ‖39‖
He is beyond compare, who dwells in bodies, the awesome; knower of the right time, the oblation itself and Hari; known by every sign of knowledge, with Lakṣmī, victorious in battle.
No.NameMeans
355atulaḥbeyond compare
356śarabhaḥwho dwells in bodies
357bhīmaḥthe awesome
358samaya-jñaḥknower of the right time
359haviḥ-hariḥthe oblation, and Hari
360sarva-lakṣaṇa-lakṣaṇyaḥknown by every means of knowing
361lakṣmīvānwith Lakṣmī
362samitim-jayaḥvictorious in battle
Samayajña (358). "Knower of the right time": He knows when to act for His devotees, and for the world: not too early, not too late. Lakṣmīvān (361): He is never without Lakṣmī.
Beyond compare, and always on time.

VERSE 40 · NAMES 363–372

DĀMODARA: BOUND BY LOVE
vikṣaro rohito mārgohetur dāmodaraḥ sahaḥ |mahīdharo mahābhāgovegavān amitāśanaḥ ‖40‖
He is imperishable, red (as the fish avatāra), the path, the cause, Dāmodara, the patient; bearer of the earth, the most fortunate, swift, of boundless appetite.
No.NameMeans
363vikṣaraḥimperishable
364rohitaḥthe red one (Matsya)
365mārgaḥthe path
366hetuḥthe cause
367dāmodaraḥDāmodara: rope round the waist
368sahaḥthe patient; who overcomes
369mahī-dharaḥbearer of the earth (again)
370mahā-bhāgaḥmost fortunate
371vegavānswift
372amita-aśanaḥof boundless appetite
Dāmodara (367). Little Kṛṣṇa, tied by Yaśodā with a rope (dāma) round His waist (udara) for stealing butter: the Lord of the universe bound by a mother's love. Next to it, Mārga (365): He is Himself the path.
Dāmodara: the infinite Lord, bound by love.
