PREVIOUSLY · AND NOW

In Part 6, names 513–632: Mukunda, the hidden avatāras, the Lord as medicine and doctor, twelve Śrī names, name 600, and the destroyer of sorrow.

Part 7 covers verses 68–81, names 633 to 764. Here are Keśava and Hari, Dhanañjaya, the verse of ten "Brahma" names, the hymn that praises itself, name 700, the one-syllable names "who, what, which, that", and the Lord who is without pride yet gives honour to all.

ZAPPY: Can 'what' really be a name of God?

PROF. BOLT: In verse 78, yes: 'kaḥ, kim, yat, tat'. The Upaniṣads point to God with the smallest words: That.

PAIRS OF OPPOSITES

The Lord holds both sides
PairNos.Meaning
Eka · Naika725–726The One · the many
Mūrti · Amūrtimān717–720Of every form · formless
Acala · Cala745–746Unmoving · moving
Darpahā · Darpada712–713Humbles pride · gives dignity
Amānī · Mānada747–748Without pride · gives honour
Pragraha · Nigraha760–761Holds the reins · restrains
Stavya · Stotā679–683The praised · the one who praises

Not a contradiction. The Upaniṣads describe Brahman in pairs like these: "It moves, and It moves not; It is far, and It is near" (Īśa Upaniṣad 5). The names hold both sides at once.

ZAPPY: How can He be one and many at once?

PROF. BOLT: Like the sun reflected in a hundred bowls of water: one sun, many reflections.

VERSE 68 · NAMES 633–641

THE PURE AND PURIFYING
arciṣmān arcitaḥ kumbhoviśuddhātmā viśodhanaḥ |aniruddho'pratirathaḥpradyumno'mitavikramaḥ ‖68‖
He is radiant, worshipped, the pot (that holds all), of pure nature, the purifier; Aniruddha, without rival, Pradyumna, of boundless valour.
No.NameMeans
633arciṣmānradiant
634arcitaḥworshipped
635kumbhaḥthe pot that holds all
636viśuddha-ātmāof pure nature
637viśodhanaḥthe purifier
638aniruddhaḥAniruddha, the unobstructed
639apratirathaḥwithout rival
640pradyumnaḥPradyumna, of great wealth
641amita-vikramaḥof boundless valour
Two vyūhas. Aniruddha (638) and Pradyumna (640), with Saṅkarṣaṇa (552) and Vāsudeva, complete the four vyūhas of the Pāñcarātra (see name 138). Kumbha (635): like a pot holds water, the Lord holds the whole universe within.
Pure Himself, He makes us pure.

VERSE 69 · NAMES 642–650

KEŚAVA, HARI
kālaneminihā vīraḥśauriḥ śūrajaneśvaraḥ |trilokātmā trilokeśaḥkeśavaḥ keśihā hariḥ ‖69‖
He slew Kālanemi, is the hero, Śauri, the Lord of the heroes of Śūra's line; the Self of the three worlds, their Lord; Keśava, the slayer of Keśī, Hari.
No.NameMeans
642kālanemi-nihāslayer of the demon Kālanemi
643vīraḥthe hero
644śauriḥŚauri: Kṛṣṇa (again)
645śūra-jana-īśvaraḥLord of the heroes
646tri-loka-ātmāSelf of the three worlds
647tri-loka-īśaḥLord of the three worlds
648keśavaḥKeśava (again)
649keśi-hāslayer of Keśī
650hariḥHari: who takes away (sorrow)
Keśava and Keśihā (648–649). Side by side: "beautiful-haired" and "slayer of Keśī", the horse-demon young Kṛṣṇa overcame. Hari (650): "the one who takes away", our sins and sorrows, and also our hearts (see the Madhurāṣṭakam).
Hari: He takes away our sorrow.

VERSE 70 · NAMES 651–660

DHANAÑJAYA
kāmadevaḥ kāmapālaḥkāmī kāntaḥ kṛtāgamaḥ |anirdeśyavapur viṣṇurvīro'nanto dhanañjayaḥ ‖70‖
He is the god desired, the protector of desires, the one who desires (His devotees), the beautiful, the author of the scriptures; of indescribable form, Viṣṇu, the hero, the endless, Dhanañjaya.
No.NameMeans
651kāma-devaḥthe God who is desired
652kāma-pālaḥprotector of desires
653kāmīwho desires (His devotees)
654kāntaḥthe beautiful
655kṛta-āgamaḥauthor of the scriptures
656anirdeśya-vapuḥof indescribable form
657viṣṇuḥViṣṇu (third time)
658vīraḥthe hero
659anantaḥthe endless
660dhanañjayaḥDhanañjaya: Arjuna, "winner of wealth"
Dhanañjaya (660). A name of Arjuna. In the Gītā (10.37) Kṛṣṇa says: "among the Pāṇḍavas, I am Dhanañjaya". The Lord counts His dearest devotee as Himself. Kāmī (653): God, too, longs for His devotees.
"I am Arjuna": the Lord counts His devotee as Himself.

VERSE 71 · NAMES 661–670

TEN BRAHMA NAMES
brahmaṇyo brahmakṛd brahmābrahma brahmavivardhanaḥ |brahmavid brāhmaṇo brahmībrahmajño brāhmaṇapriyaḥ ‖71‖
He is the friend of the Veda and its keepers, the maker of the Veda, Brahmā, Brahman itself, the one who makes the Veda grow; the knower of the Veda, the brāhmaṇa, the possessor of the Veda, the knower of Brahman, dear to the wise.
No.NameMeans
661brahmaṇyaḥfriend of the Veda and its keepers
662brahma-kṛtmaker of the Veda
663brahmāBrahmā, the creator
664brahmaBrahman, the Absolute
665brahma-vivardhanaḥwho makes the Veda grow
666brahma-vitknower of the Veda
667brāhmaṇaḥthe teacher of the Veda
668brahmīin whom the Veda lives
669brahma-jñaḥknower of Brahman
670brāhmaṇa-priyaḥdear to the wise
"Brahma" in every name. The word has many senses: the Veda, the Absolute (Brahman), the creator (Brahmā), the sacred word. This verse plays on all of them: the Lord is the knowledge, the knower and the known.
The knowledge, the knower and the known.

VERSE 72 · NAMES 671–678

EIGHT TIMES "GREAT"
mahākramo mahākarmāmahātejā mahoragaḥ |mahākratur mahāyajvāmahāyajño mahāhaviḥ ‖72‖
He is of great strides, great deeds, great splendour; the great serpent; the great rite, the great sacrificer, the great sacrifice, the great offering.
No.NameMeans
671mahā-kramaḥof great strides
672mahā-karmāof great deeds
673mahā-tejāḥof great splendour
674mahā-uragaḥthe great serpent (Śeṣa)
675mahā-kratuḥthe great rite
676mahā-yajvāthe great sacrificer
677mahā-yajñaḥthe great sacrifice
678mahā-haviḥthe great offering
Mahā, mahā, mahā. Eight names begin with "great". The second half is all sacrifice: He is the rite, the one who performs it, the sacrifice itself and the offering poured in. The Gītā (9.16) says the same: "I am the rite, I am the sacrifice, I am the offering".
He is the rite, the priest, the sacrifice and the offering.

VERSE 73 · NAMES 679–689

THE PRAISE AND THE PRAISED
stavyaḥ stavapriyaḥ stotraṃstutiḥ stotā raṇapriyaḥ |pūrṇaḥ pūrayitā puṇyaḥpuṇyakīrtir anāmayaḥ ‖73‖
He is worthy of praise, loves praise, is the hymn, the act of praise, the one who praises; who delights in battle (against evil); full, the one who fills, holy, of holy fame, free of illness.
No.NameMeans
679stavyaḥworthy of praise
680stava-priyaḥwho loves praise
681stotramthe hymn itself
682stutiḥthe act of praise
683stotāthe one who praises
684raṇa-priyaḥwho delights in battle (against evil)
685pūrṇaḥfull
686pūrayitāwho fills (our wishes)
687puṇyaḥholy
688puṇya-kīrtiḥof holy fame
689anāmayaḥfree of illness
The hymn praises itself. Names 679–683 are a wonderful loop: He is the one praised, who loves praise, the hymn, the praising and the one who praises. So when we chant the Sahasranāma, He is in every part of it.
When we chant, He is the hymn and the singer.

VERSE 74 · NAMES 690–698

SWIFT AS THE MIND
manojavas tīrthakarovasuretā vasupradaḥ |vasuprado vāsudevovasur vasumanā haviḥ ‖74‖
He is swift as the mind, the maker of fords (teacher of the paths), of golden seed, the giver of wealth, the giver of liberation; Vāsudeva, the dwelling, the one who dwells in all, the offering.
No.NameMeans
690mano-javaḥswift as the mind
691tīrtha-karaḥmaker of fords; teacher of the ways
692vasu-retāḥof golden seed
693vasu-pradaḥgiver of wealth
694vasu-pradaḥgiver of liberation, the highest wealth
695vāsudevaḥVāsudeva: son of Vasudeva
696vasuḥthe dwelling
697vasu-manāḥwho dwells in all minds
698haviḥthe offering
The same name twice in a row (693–694). Vasuprada, "giver of wealth", appears twice. Śaṅkara: first, He gives ordinary wealth; then, the greatest wealth, liberation. Tīrthakara (691): He makes the fords, the safe crossings, across the river of life.
He gives wealth, and then the greatest wealth: freedom.

VERSE 75 · NAMES 699–707

NAME 700 · BY THE YAMUNĀ
sadgatiḥ satkṛtiḥ sattāsadbhūtiḥ satparāyaṇaḥ |śūraseno yaduśreṣṭhaḥsannivāsaḥ suyāmunaḥ ‖75‖
He is the goal of the good, of good deeds, existence itself, of true glory, the highest refuge of the good; the hero of the Śūrasenas, best of the Yadus, the dwelling of the good, surrounded by the people of the Yamunā.
No.NameMeans
699sat-gatiḥgoal of the good
700sat-kṛtiḥof good deeds
701sattāexistence itself
702sat-bhūtiḥof true glory
703sat-parāyaṇaḥhighest refuge of the good
704śūra-senaḥhero of the Śūrasenas
705yadu-śreṣṭhaḥbest of the Yadus
706sat-nivāsaḥdwelling of the good
707su-yāmunaḥof the Yamunā: loved by its people
Name 700: Satkṛti. "Of good deeds". Five sat names open the verse: good, real, true. Then Kṛṣṇa of the Yādava clan (704–705) and Suyāmuna (707): the Lord surrounded by the cowherds and gopīs of the Yamunā banks.
Name 700: the Lord of good deeds, by the Yamunā.

VERSE 76 · NAMES 708–716

HUMBLER OF PRIDE
bhūtāvāso vāsudevaḥsarvāsunilayo'nalaḥ |darpahā darpado dṛptodurdharo'thāparājitaḥ ‖76‖
He is the dwelling of all beings, Vāsudeva, the abode of all life-breath, the fire; the destroyer of pride, the giver of pride (in dharma), the joyful, the hard to grasp, and the unconquered.
No.NameMeans
708bhūta-āvāsaḥdwelling of all beings
709vāsudevaḥVāsudeva: who covers all with māyā
710sarva-asu-nilayaḥabode of all life
711analaḥthe fire; boundless
712darpa-hādestroyer of pride
713darpa-daḥgiver of rightful pride
714dṛptaḥever joyful
715durdharaḥhard to grasp
716aparājitaḥunconquered
Darpahā and Darpada (712–713). He destroys the arrogance of the wicked, and gives rightful self-respect to the good. Vāsudeva appears for the third time (709; also 332 and 695), each time with a fresh meaning.
He humbles pride, and gives the good their dignity.

VERSE 77 · NAMES 717–724

FORMS WITHOUT NUMBER
viśvamūrtir mahāmūrtirdīptamūrtir amūrtimān |anekamūrtir avyaktaḥśatamūrtiḥ śatānanaḥ ‖77‖
His form is the universe; He has a great form, a shining form, and is formless; of many forms, unmanifest, of a hundred forms, a hundred faces.
No.NameMeans
717viśva-mūrtiḥwhose form is the universe
718mahā-mūrtiḥof great form
719dīpta-mūrtiḥof shining form
720amūrtimānformless
721aneka-mūrtiḥof many forms
722avyaktaḥthe unmanifest
723śata-mūrtiḥof a hundred forms
724śata-ānanaḥof a hundred faces
Form and formless. Seven "form" names, and in the middle Amūrtimān (720): formless. He is the universe, takes countless forms, and yet is beyond all form. Both are true; neither cancels the other.
Every form, and beyond all form.

VERSE 78 · NAMES 725–736

ONE AND MANY · FRIEND OF ALL
eko naikaḥ savaḥ kaḥ kiṃyat tat padam anuttamam |lokabandhur lokanāthomādhavo bhaktavatsalaḥ ‖78‖
He is the One, the many, the sacrifice, the one of joy (ka), the "what" (to be sought), the "which", the "that", the supreme state; the friend of the world, the Lord of the world, Mādhava, who loves His devotees.
No.NameMeans
725ekaḥthe One
726naikaḥthe many
727savaḥthe sacrifice (Soma)
728kaḥ"Ka": joy
729kim"What?": the one to be sought
730yat"Which": from whom all comes
731tat"That": the Absolute
732padam anuttamamthe supreme state
733loka-bandhuḥfriend of the world
734loka-nāthaḥLord of the world
735mādhavaḥMādhava (again)
736bhakta-vatsalaḥwho loves His devotees
One-syllable names. Kaḥ, kim, yat, tat (728–731): the smallest words in Sanskrit, "who, what, which, that", become names of God. The Upaniṣads point to Him as "That" (tat tvam asi). And then the tender ending: Bhaktavatsala (736), who loves His devotees.
One and many, "That", and the lover of His devotees.

VERSE 79 · NAMES 737–746

MOVING AND UNMOVING
suvarṇavarṇo hemāṅgovarāṅgaś candanāṅgadī |vīrahā viṣamaḥ śūnyoghṛtāśīr acalaś calaḥ ‖79‖
He is golden in colour, golden-limbed, of excellent limbs, wearing sandal paste and armlets; slayer of the mighty (demons), unequalled, empty (of qualities), who needs no blessings; unmoving, and moving.
No.NameMeans
737suvarṇa-varṇaḥgolden in colour
738hema-aṅgaḥgolden-limbed
739vara-aṅgaḥof excellent limbs
740candana-aṅgadīwith sandal paste and armlets
741vīra-hāslayer of mighty demons
742viṣamaḥunequalled
743śūnyaḥempty of all limiting qualities
744ghṛta-āśīḥwho needs no blessings
745acalaḥunmoving
746calaḥmoving (as the wind)
Acala and Cala (745–746). The unmoving and the moving, side by side: still as the Absolute, moving as all the life of the world. The Īśa Upaniṣad says the same: "It moves, and It moves not".
Still as a mountain, moving as the wind.

VERSE 80 · NAMES 747–756

WITHOUT PRIDE, GIVING HONOUR
amānī mānado mānyolokasvāmī trilokadhṛk |sumedhā medhajo dhanyaḥsatyamedhā dharādharaḥ ‖80‖
He is without pride, gives honour, is worthy of honour; the master of the world, the bearer of the three worlds; of great wisdom, born of sacrifice, blessed, of true wisdom, the bearer of the earth.
No.NameMeans
747amānīwithout pride
748māna-daḥwho gives honour
749mānyaḥworthy of honour
750loka-svāmīmaster of the world
751tri-loka-dhṛkbearer of the three worlds
752su-medhāḥof great wisdom
753medha-jaḥborn of sacrifice
754dhanyaḥblessed, fulfilled
755satya-medhāḥof true wisdom
756dharā-dharaḥbearer of the earth
Amānī, Mānada, Mānya (747–749). "Without pride, giving honour, worthy of honour": the perfect order. The greatest has no ego, gives respect to others, and so is truly respected. A lesson in leadership as much as theology.
Without pride, He gives honour to all.

VERSE 81 · NAMES 757–764

HOLDER OF THE REINS
tejovṛṣo dyutidharaḥsarvaśastrabhṛtāṃ varaḥ |pragraho nigraho vyagronaikaśṛṅgo gadāgrajaḥ ‖81‖
He showers splendour, bears radiance, is the best of all who bear weapons; He holds the reins, restrains (the wicked), is ever active (for His devotees), many-horned, the elder brother of Gada.
No.NameMeans
757tejo-vṛṣaḥwho showers splendour
758dyuti-dharaḥbearer of radiance
759sarva-śastra-bhṛtāṃ varaḥbest of all warriors
760pragrahaḥwho holds the reins (of the senses)
761nigrahaḥwho restrains

Pragraha (760). "Who holds the reins": like Kṛṣṇa holding the horses of Arjuna's chariot, the Lord holds the reins of our senses (see Hṛṣīkeśa, 47). Gadāgraja (764): Kṛṣṇa, elder brother of Gada.
He holds the reins of our chariot.
