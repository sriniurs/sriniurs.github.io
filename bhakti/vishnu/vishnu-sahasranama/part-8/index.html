"Caturmūrtiś caturbāhuḥ" · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: First verses 82–94 for chanting in one go. Then one page for each verse: the chant, a picture, every name with its number and meaning, one insight and the one thing to remember.

PREVIOUSLY · AND NOW

In Part 7, names 633–764: Keśava and Hari, the hymn that praises itself, name 700, one and many, form and formless, and the Lord without pride who gives honour.

Part 8 covers verses 82–94, names 765 to 885. Here are the "four" names, "hard to reach" and "easy to reach", the thread on which the worlds are strung, Sundara the beautiful, name 800, the rain cloud, the sacred trees, smaller than an atom and greater than the universe, the bearer of our burden, and the sun.

ZAPPY: Hard to reach AND easy to reach?

PROF. BOLT: Name 777 and name 817! The difference, say the commentators, is love.

VERSE 82 · NAMES 765–772

FOUR, FOUR, FOUR
caturmūrtiś caturbāhuścaturvyūhaś caturgatiḥ |caturātmā caturbhāvaścaturvedavid ekapāt ‖82‖
He has four forms, four arms, four emanations, is the goal of the four (stages of life); of four natures, four aspects, knower of the four Vedas, and with one foot (the world is a quarter of Him).
No.NameMeans
765catur-mūrtiḥof four forms
766catur-bāhuḥfour-armed
767catur-vyūhaḥof four emanations
768catur-gatiḥgoal of the four stages of life
769catur-ātmāof fourfold nature
770catur-bhāvaḥsource of the four aims of life
771catur-veda-vitknower of the four Vedas
772eka-pātone-footed: the world is one quarter of Him
"Four" seven times. Four arms, four vyūhas, four āśramas (stages of life), four puruṣārthas (dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa), four Vedas. Then Ekapāt (772): the Puruṣa Sūkta says the whole universe is only one quarter of Him; three quarters are beyond.
The whole universe is only one quarter of Him.

VERSE 83 · NAMES 773–781

HARD TO REACH, WITHOUT LOVE
samāvarto'nivṛttātmādurjayo duratikramaḥ |durlabho durgamo durgodurāvāso durārihā ‖83‖
He turns the wheel of saṃsāra, yet His nature never turns away; hard to conquer, hard to transgress; hard to reach, hard to attain, a fortress, hard to dwell in, the slayer of evil foes.
No.NameMeans
773samāvartaḥwho turns the wheel of saṃsāra
774anivṛtta-ātmāwhose nature never turns away
775durjayaḥhard to conquer
776duratikramaḥwhose command none can transgress
777durlabhaḥhard to reach (without devotion)
778durgamaḥhard to attain
779durgaḥthe fortress
780durāvāsaḥhard to dwell in (for the restless mind)
781durāri-hāslayer of evil foes
Durlabha (777). "Hard to reach": but Śaṅkara adds, hard to reach without devotion. Compare Sulabha (817) in verse 88: "easy to reach". The same Lord is far for the proud and near for the loving.
Hard to reach without love, easy to reach with it.

VERSE 84 · NAMES 782–789

THE GREAT WEB
śubhāṅgo lokasāraṅgaḥsutantus tantuvardhanaḥ |indrakarmā mahākarmākṛtakarmā kṛtāgamaḥ ‖84‖
He has auspicious limbs, takes the essence of the world (as a bee); the beautiful thread, the one who extends the thread; whose deeds are like Indra's, of great deeds, who has done all that needs doing, who gave the scriptures.
No.NameMeans
782śubha-aṅgaḥof auspicious limbs (again)
783loka-sāraṅgaḥwho takes the world's essence
784su-tantuḥthe beautiful thread
785tantu-vardhanaḥwho extends the thread
786indra-karmāwhose deeds are like Indra's
787mahā-karmāof great deeds
788kṛta-karmāwho has done all that needs doing
789kṛta-āgamaḥwho gave the scriptures
Sutantu (784). "The beautiful thread": the Lord is the thread on which the worlds are strung, as Kṛṣṇa says in the Gītā (7.7): "all this is strung on Me like pearls on a thread".
All the worlds are strung on Him like pearls on a thread.

VERSE 85 · NAMES 790–799

THE BEAUTIFUL ONE
udbhavaḥ sundaraḥ sundoratnanābhaḥ sulocanaḥ |arko vājasanaḥ śṛṅgījayantaḥ sarvavijjayī ‖85‖
He is the origin, the beautiful, the compassionate, with a jewel-like navel, of lovely eyes; the sun, the giver of food, the horned (fish), victorious, the all-knowing victor.
No.NameMeans
790udbhavaḥthe origin (again)
791sundaraḥthe beautiful
792sundaḥthe compassionate
793ratna-nābhaḥof jewel-like navel
794su-locanaḥof lovely eyes
795arkaḥthe sun; worthy of worship
796vāja-sanaḥgiver of food
797śṛṅgīthe horned (Matsya)
798jayantaḥvictorious
799sarva-vit-jayīthe all-knowing victor
Sundara (791). Simply "beautiful". In the middle of cosmic names, the hymn stops to say: He is lovely to look at. The Madhurāṣṭakam would say the same in one word, madhuram. Vājasana (796): He gives food to all.
Sundara: He is simply beautiful.

VERSE 86 · NAMES 800–806

NAME 800 · THE GREAT LAKE
suvarṇabindur akṣobhyaḥsarvavāgīśvareśvaraḥ |mahāhrado mahāgartomahābhūto mahānidhiḥ ‖86‖
He is the golden drop (Oṃ), undisturbed, the lord of all the lords of speech; the great lake (of bliss), the great abyss (of māyā), the great being, the great treasure.
No.NameMeans
800suvarṇa-binduḥthe golden drop: Oṃ
801akṣobhyaḥundisturbed
802sarva-vāg-īśvara-īśvaraḥLord of all lords of speech
803mahā-hradaḥthe great lake of bliss
804mahā-gartaḥthe great abyss (of māyā)
805mahā-bhūtaḥthe great being
806mahā-nidhiḥthe great treasure
Name 800: Suvarṇabindu. "The golden drop", read as the bindu of Oṃ. Mahāhrada (803): the great lake into which yogis dive and rest in bliss. Akṣobhya (801): nothing can disturb Him; and in Him we too can be still.
Name 800: a golden drop, a great lake of peace.

VERSE 87 · NAMES 807–816

THE RAIN CLOUD
kumudaḥ kundaraḥ kundaḥparjanyaḥ pāvano'nilaḥ |amṛtāśo'mṛtavapuḥsarvajñaḥ sarvatomukhaḥ ‖87‖
He delights the earth, gives the earth (to Kaśyapa), is pure as jasmine; the rain cloud, the purifier, the wind; who feeds on nectar, whose body is nectar, the all-knowing, facing every direction.
No.NameMeans
807ku-mudaḥwho delights the earth (again)
808kundaraḥwho gives the earth (as Paraśurāma)
809kundaḥpure as jasmine
810parjanyaḥthe rain cloud
811pāvanaḥthe purifier
812anilaḥthe wind
813amṛta-āśaḥwho feeds on nectar
814amṛta-vapuḥwhose body is nectar
815sarva-jñaḥall-knowing (again)
816sarvataḥ-mukhaḥfacing every direction
Parjanya (810). "The rain cloud": He rains blessings on all, just as a cloud rains on every field without choosing. In a land that waits for the monsoon, few images say "grace" more clearly.
Like the rain cloud, He showers on all without choosing.

VERSE 88 · NAMES 817–825

EASY TO REACH
sulabhaḥ suvrataḥ siddhaḥśatrujic chatrutāpanaḥ |nyagrodho'dumbaro'śvatthaścāṇūrāndhraniṣūdanaḥ ‖88‖
He is easy to reach, of good vows, perfect; the conqueror of foes, the scorcher of foes; the banyan, the fig, the peepal tree; the slayer of Cāṇūra of Andhra.
No.NameMeans
817sulabhaḥeasy to reach
818su-vrataḥof good vows (again)
819siddhaḥperfect (again)
820śatru-jitconqueror of foes
821śatru-tāpanaḥscorcher of foes
822nyagrodhaḥthe banyan
823udumbaraḥthe fig; above the sky
824aśvatthaḥthe peepal (sacred fig)
825cāṇūra-andhra-niṣūdanaḥslayer of Cāṇūra, the wrestler
Sulabha (817), after Durlabha (777). Forty names ago He was "hard to reach"; now He is "easy to reach". The difference is love. Aśvattha (824): the Gītā's world-tree (15.1), "with roots above and branches below". Cāṇūra was Kaṃsa's wrestler, defeated by young Kṛṣṇa.
Sulabha: for the loving, He is easy to reach.

VERSE 89 · NAMES 826–834

FIRE AND SUN · REMOVER OF FEAR
sahasrārciḥ saptajihvaḥsaptaidhāḥ saptavāhanaḥ |amūrtir anagho'cintyobhayakṛd bhayanāśanaḥ ‖89‖
He has a thousand rays, seven tongues (as fire), seven flames, seven horses (as the sun); formless, sinless, unthinkable, who causes fear (to the wicked), who destroys fear (in the good).
No.NameMeans
826sahasra-arciḥof a thousand rays
827sapta-jihvaḥseven-tongued (fire)
828sapta-edhāḥof seven flames
829sapta-vāhanaḥdrawn by seven horses (the sun)
830amūrtiḥformless
831anaghaḥsinless (again)
832acintyaḥunthinkable
833bhaya-kṛtwho causes fear (to the wicked)
834bhaya-nāśanaḥdestroyer of fear
Fire and sun. The seven-tongued fire and the seven-horsed sun: the two great lights of the Vedic world are His forms. Then Bhayanāśana (834): whatever we fear, He removes it, as He promised in the pūrvabhāga (names that "take away sin and fear").
Bhayanāśana: He takes away our fear.

VERSE 90 · NAMES 835–846

SMALLEST AND GREATEST
aṇur bṛhat kṛśaḥ sthūloguṇabhṛn nirguṇo mahān |adhṛtaḥ svadhṛtaḥ svāsyaḥprāgvaṃśo vaṃśavardhanaḥ ‖90‖
He is the smallest, the vast, the thin, the stout; the bearer of qualities, beyond qualities, the great; unsupported, self-supported, of beautiful face, of the first lineage, the one who makes lineages grow.
No.NameMeans
835aṇuḥthe smallest
836bṛhatthe vast
837kṛśaḥthe thin
838sthūlaḥthe stout
839guṇa-bhṛtbearer of qualities
840nirguṇaḥbeyond all qualities
841mahānthe great
842adhṛtaḥunsupported
843sva-dhṛtaḥself-supported
844su-āsyaḥof beautiful face
845prāg-vaṃśaḥof the first lineage
846vaṃśa-vardhanaḥwho makes families grow
Smaller than the smallest, greater than the greatest. The Kaṭha Upaniṣad: "aṇor aṇīyān mahato mahīyān". Names 835–840 pair opposites: tiny and vast, thin and stout, with qualities and without. Vaṃśavardhana (846): He blesses families and lineages.
Smaller than an atom, greater than the universe.

VERSE 91 · NAMES 847–856

BEARER OF OUR BURDEN
bhārabhṛt kathito yogīyogīśaḥ sarvakāmadaḥ |āśramaḥ śramaṇaḥ kṣāmaḥsuparṇo vāyuvāhanaḥ ‖91‖
He bears the burden (of the world), is proclaimed (in the Vedas), the yogī, the lord of yogīs, the giver of every wish; the resting place, the one who wearies (the worldly), who wastes all at dissolution, the fair-winged, who moves the wind.
No.NameMeans
847bhāra-bhṛtbearer of the burden
848kathitaḥproclaimed by the Vedas
849yogīthe yogī
850yoga-īśaḥlord of yogīs
851sarva-kāma-daḥgiver of every wish
852āśramaḥthe resting place, shelter
853śramaṇaḥwho wearies the worldly
854kṣāmaḥwho wastes all at dissolution
855su-parṇaḥfair-winged (again)
856vāyu-vāhanaḥwho moves the wind (again)
Bhārabhṛt (847). "Bearer of the burden": He carries the weight of the worlds, and the weight of His devotees. In the Gītā (9.22): "I carry what they lack and keep what they have". Āśrama (852): like a hermitage, He is a shelter for the weary.
He carries our burden.

VERSE 92 · NAMES 857–866

THE ARCHER WHO DISCIPLINES
dhanurdharo dhanurvedodaṇḍo damayitā damaḥ |aparājitaḥ sarvasahoniyantā'niyamo'yamaḥ ‖92‖
He bears the bow, is the science of archery, the rod (of justice), the one who disciplines, discipline itself; unconquered, all-enduring, the controller, beyond all control, beyond death.
No.NameMeans
857dhanuḥ-dharaḥbearer of the bow (Rāma)
858dhanuḥ-vedaḥthe science of archery
859daṇḍaḥthe rod of justice
860damayitāwho disciplines
861damaḥself-restraint itself
862aparājitaḥunconquered (again)
863sarva-sahaḥall-enduring
864niyantāthe controller
865aniyamaḥunder no rule
866ayamaḥbeyond death (Yama)
Sarvasaha (863). "All-enduring": He bears with all our failings, again and again (compare Sahiṣṇu, 144). Discipline (859–861) is not anger: it is the Lord correcting us like a loving teacher, for our good.
He disciplines, and endures us with patience.

VERSE 93 · NAMES 867–875

HE WHO MAKES LOVE GROW
sattvavān sāttvikaḥ satyaḥsatyadharmaparāyaṇaḥ |abhiprāyaḥ priyārho'rhaḥpriyakṛt prītivardhanaḥ ‖93‖
He is full of goodness, pure in nature, the Truth, devoted to truth and dharma; the one all seek, worthy of love, worthy of worship, the one who does what pleases, who makes love grow.
No.NameMeans
867sattvavānfull of goodness
868sāttvikaḥpure in nature
869satyaḥthe Truth (again)
870satya-dharma-parāyaṇaḥdevoted to truth and dharma
871abhiprāyaḥthe one all seek
872priya-arhaḥworthy of our love
873arhaḥworthy of worship
874priya-kṛtwho does what pleases (His devotees)
875prīti-vardhanaḥwho makes love grow
The love names. Priyārha (872), worthy of love; Priyakṛt (874), who does what pleases His devotees; Prītivardhana (875), who makes love grow. The more we love Him, the more He increases that love: devotion feeds itself.
Prītivardhana: He makes our love grow.

VERSE 94 · NAMES 876–885

THE SUN NAMES
vihāyasagatir jyotiḥsurucir hutabhug vibhuḥ |ravir virocanaḥ sūryaḥsavitā ravilocanaḥ ‖94‖
He moves in the sky, is light itself, of lovely radiance, the eater of oblations (as fire), all-pervading; the sun, the shining, Sūrya, Savitā, whose eye is the sun.
No.NameMeans
876vihāyasa-gatiḥwho moves in the sky
877jyotiḥlight itself
878su-ruciḥof lovely radiance
879huta-bhukeater of oblations (fire)
880vibhuḥall-pervading (again)
881raviḥthe sun
882virocanaḥthe shining one
883sūryaḥSūrya, the sun
884savitāSavitā, the life-giver
885ravi-locanaḥwhose eye is the sun
Savitā (884). The deity of the Gāyatrī mantra: "tat savitur vareṇyam". The verse ends in a cluster of sun-names: Ravi, Virocana, Sūrya, Savitā. The sun is the most visible form of the Lord, giving light and life to all.
The sun is His most visible form.
