MADHURĀṢṬAKAM

ŚRĪ VALLABHĀCĀRYA · THE SWEETNESS OF KṚṢṆA
“Adharaṃ madhuram” · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: First the whole hymn for chanting in one go. Then the story of how the hymn came to be and its background, and two pages for each verse: the chant, a picture, what it says and the key words; then a deeper look, background facts, the inner teaching and the one thing to remember.

FOREWORD

"Adharaṃ madhuraṃ, vadanaṃ madhuraṃ…" Few Sanskrit hymns are as easy to love. Even a child can sing it, and many do: one word, madhuram, "sweet", sung again and again.

The Madhurāṣṭakam was composed by Śrī Vallabhācārya, founder of the Puṣṭimārga, the "Path of Grace". In eight short verses, he looks at Kṛṣṇa from head to foot, from His smile to His flute, His river, His cows and the whole creation, and finds only one word for all of it.

This is not a hymn that argues or asks. It simply gazes, and is overwhelmed.

Jai Śrī Kṛṣṇa,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is it really just one word?

PROF. BOLT: Almost! Six sweet things in each verse, and every verse ends: 'everything about the Lord of Sweetness is sweet'.

THE STORY · A MIDNIGHT VISION

Vallabhācārya and the Path of Grace
The story in 5 frames
A Telugu boy. Vallabhācārya (1479–1531, by most accounts) was born into a Telugu brāhmaṇa family and became a great scholar of the Vedānta and the Bhāgavatam.
A teacher who walked. He travelled across India on foot, debating and teaching, and visited Vraja, Kṛṣṇa's land, many times.
A vision at midnight. According to tradition, Kṛṣṇa appeared to him at midnight on the eleventh day of the bright half of Śrāvaṇa, and Vallabha composed the Madhurāṣṭakam in His praise.
The Path of Grace. He founded the Puṣṭimārga, where the Lord is loved and served (sevā) as a beloved child, by singing, remembering, seeing and serving Him.
Every Janmāṣṭamī. The hymn is sung wherever Kṛṣṇa is loved, especially on His birthday; M. S. Subbulakshmi's rendition is widely loved.

ZAPPY: Why 'Path of Grace'?

PROF. BOLT: Puṣṭi means nourishment, grace. Vallabha taught that we are held by the Lord's grace, not by our own efforts. And grace tastes sweet!

ABOUT THE MADHURĀṢṬAKAM

Eight verses, forty-eight sweet things

✍️ The author

Śrī Vallabhācārya, founder of the Puṣṭimārga and the philosophy of Śuddhādvaita, "pure non-dualism".

🍯 The word

Madhura means sweet, lovely, charming. Mādhurya, the Lord's sweetness, is set against His aiśvarya, majesty.

VerseWhat is sweet
1Lips, face, eyes, smile, heart, walk
2Words, deeds, clothes, posture, movement, wandering
3Flute, dust of His feet, hands, feet, dance, friendship
4Singing, drinking, eating, sleeping, form, tilaka
5Deeds, saving, stealing, play, giving, calm
6Berry necklace, garland, Yamunā, waves, water, lotuses
7Gopīs, play, union, parting, glance, courtesy
8Cowherds, cows, staff, creation, crushing evil, fruit

The movement. From His face (1) to His life (2–5), to what is near Him (6–7), to the whole creation (8): sweetness spreading outward like ripples.

VERSE 1 · GOING DEEPER

HIS FACE, HIS SMILE, HIS HEART

Beginning with the face. The poet looks at Kṛṣṇa as a lover looks: lips first, then face, eyes, smile. Then he goes inward, to the heart, and back out, to the way He walks.

The refrain. Every verse ends madhurādhipater akhilaṃ madhuram: "everything about the Lord of Sweetness is sweet". Each verse lists six sweet things and then gives up: it is all sweet.

A pun. Madhurā is also an old name of Mathurā, Kṛṣṇa's birthplace; so madhurādhipati can be read as "Lord of Mathurā" too.

💡 Mādhurya

Sweetness: the loving, intimate side of the Lord, as against His majesty (aiśvarya).

💡 Mathurā

Kṛṣṇa's birthplace on the Yamunā, also called Madhurā or Madhupurī.

When you truly love someone, you stop listing reasons. Everything about them is dear.

ZAPPY: The same word, over and over?

PROF. BOLT: That's the magic! "Madhuram, madhuram": like a child who can only say "sweet!" because there's no better word.

"Adharaṃ madhuram": everything about Kṛṣṇa is sweet.

VERSE 2 · GOING DEEPER

HIS WORDS, HIS WAYS, HIS WANDERINGS

From face to life. Verse 1 was about how He looks; verse 2 is about how He lives: what He says, does and wears, and where He goes.

Valita. "The bend": Kṛṣṇa is often shown in the tribhaṅga pose, bent at neck, waist and knee, playing His flute.

Pītāmbara. His garment is the yellow silk (pītāmbara), one of His names. Even His clothes are sweet because He wears them (compare Veṅkaṭeśa Maṅgalāśāsanam verse 11).

💡 Tribhaṅga

The graceful three-bend pose of Kṛṣṇa playing the flute.

💡 Pītāmbara

"Yellow-clad": Kṛṣṇa's silk garment, and a name for Him.

Love sees sweetness in ordinary things: how someone speaks, stands, walks.

ZAPPY: His clothes are sweet too?

PROF. BOLT: Everything is! What He says, what He wears, even how He wanders about Vṛndāvana.

Words, deeds, clothes, wanderings: all sweet.

VERSE 3 · GOING DEEPER

HIS FLUTE, THE DUST OF HIS FEET

The flute. Kṛṣṇa's flute (veṇu, muralī) called the cows, the gopīs and all Vṛndāvana. In the Bhāgavatam, even rivers stopped to listen.

The dust. Devotees long for the dust of His feet (pāda-reṇu), or of His devotees' feet. Vraja's dust is still held sacred.

Friendship. Sakhya, friendship with God, is one of the moods of bhakti: the cowherd boys loved Him as their friend, wrestling and eating with Him.

💡 Muralī

Kṛṣṇa's bamboo flute; Muralīdhara, "flute-bearer", is His name.

💡 Sakhya-bhāva

Loving God as a friend: the mood of the cowherd boys and Arjuna.

Even the dust of His path is sacred. Walk where He walked.

ZAPPY: Even the dust is sweet?

PROF. BOLT: The dust from His dancing feet! Devotees still treasure the dust of Vraja.

Flute, dust, dance, friendship: all sweet.

VERSE 4 · GOING DEEPER

SINGING, EATING, SLEEPING

Everyday life. Singing, drinking, eating, sleeping: the most ordinary acts. In Kṛṣṇa they are lovely because they are His. This is the heart of mādhurya bhakti: God as a beloved child or friend, not a distant king.

The butter thief. Kṛṣṇa's eating means, above all, the stolen butter of Gokula: the favourite childhood story.

Puṣṭimārga sevā. In Vallabha's tradition, the Lord is served through the day like a beloved child: woken, fed, dressed, put to rest.

💡 Tilaka

A sacred mark on the forehead; Vaiṣṇavas wear the ūrdhva-puṇḍra.

💡 Navanīta-cora

"Butter thief": Kṛṣṇa as the mischievous child of Gokula.

God is found in ordinary moments, not only in temples.

ZAPPY: Even His sleeping is sweet?

PROF. BOLT: Think of a sleeping child! In Vallabha's path, the Lord is cared for like a child: fed, sung to, put to sleep.

Even eating and sleeping: sweet, because it is He.

VERSE 5 · GOING DEEPER

HIS DEEDS, HIS RESCUE, HIS MISCHIEF

A harder verse. These words have several meanings, and commentators differ. Taraṇa: carrying across, as He carries devotees across saṃsāra. Haraṇa: stealing: butter, clothes, and hearts. Ramaṇa: His play, His love.

Vamita and śamita. Read variously: vamita as what He "lets out", such as the prasāda of chewed betel He shares, or His giving; śamita as His peace, or His calming of devotees' troubles.

Even mischief. Stealing is wrong; but His stealing of hearts is sweet. Love sees everything differently.

💡 Hari

"The one who takes away": His name, from the same root as haraṇa.

💡 Variant

Some texts read smaraṇam, "remembering", for ramaṇam.

Let Him steal your heart. That theft makes you richer.

ZAPPY: Stealing is sweet?

PROF. BOLT: When He steals hearts, yes! Hari literally means "the one who takes away", our sorrows and our hearts.

Rescuing, stealing, playing: His every act is sweet.
Interpretations of vamitam and śamitam vary between commentators.

VERSE 6 · GOING DEEPER

HIS GARLAND, HIS RIVER

Beyond His body. The sweetness now spreads outward: to what He wears, and then to His river. Things become sweet by being near Him.

Guñjā. A cowherd boy's necklace of bright red-and-black seeds: simple jewellery for a village Lord.

The Yamunā. The river of Vṛndāvana, where Kṛṣṇa played, bathed, subdued Kāliya and danced the rāsa. In Vallabha's tradition the Yamunā is honoured as a goddess; he wrote the Yamunāṣṭakam to her.

💡 Guñjā-mālā

A necklace of guñjā seeds, worn by Kṛṣṇa as a cowherd.

💡 Yamunāṣṭakam

Vallabha's eight verses in praise of the Yamunā.

Whatever is close to Him becomes sweet. Stay close.

ZAPPY: Now the river is sweet too?

PROF. BOLT: Anything near Him becomes sweet: His necklace, His garland, the Yamunā, even her lotuses!

Garland, river, waves, lotuses: sweet by being near Him.

VERSE 7 · GOING DEEPER

THE GOPĪS AND HIS PLAY

The gopīs. The cowherd women of Vraja, whose love for Kṛṣṇa is, for many traditions, the highest form of devotion: total, selfless, without calculation.

Even separation. Yuktaṃ … muktaṃ: meeting Him is sweet, and so is parting. In the bhakti tradition, the longing of separation (viraha) is itself a deep form of love; the gopīs' longing after Kṛṣṇa left Vraja is celebrated in song.

Līlā. Everything He does is play: free, joyful, without need.

💡 Viraha

The pain of separation from the beloved: a key mood of bhakti.

💡 Variant

Some texts read bhuktam ("eating") for muktam here.

Even missing Him is a way of loving Him.

ZAPPY: Being apart is sweet?

PROF. BOLT: Longing for Him keeps Him in your heart. The gopīs' longing is one of the deepest forms of love.

Union and separation: both are sweet with Him.

VERSE 8 · GOING DEEPER

HIS COWS, HIS STAFF, HIS WHOLE CREATION

From the cowshed to the cosmos. The last verse moves from His friends, His cows and His stick to sṛṣṭi, the whole creation. The cowherd boy is the creator of the universe.

Dalita and phalita. He crushes evil (as He crushed Kāliya and Kaṃsa) and brings everything to fruit. Destruction and fulfilment, both sweet in His hands.

The end of the list. After 48 sweet things in 8 verses, the refrain says it all: akhilaṃ madhuram, everything is sweet.

💡 Gopāla

"Protector of cows": the cowherd Kṛṣṇa.

💡 Govinda

"One who finds joy in cows", or "who is known through the Vedas".

If the creator is sweet, the creation is too. Look at the world with love.

ZAPPY: The whole creation is sweet?

PROF. BOLT: If He made it, it's His, and everything of His is sweet. That's where the hymn ends!

From cows to the cosmos: akhilaṃ madhuram, all is sweet.

THE MADHURĀṢṬAKAM ON ONE PAGE

Eight verses in four lines
Verses 1–2. His face, smile, heart, words, deeds, clothes and wanderings: all sweet.
Verses 3–5. His flute, dance, friendship, eating, sleeping, stealing and saving: all sweet.

Verse 8. His cows, His staff, His whole creation: akhilaṃ madhuram, everything is sweet.

ZAPPY: What's the one thing to remember?

PROF. BOLT: When you love the Lord, everything about Him, and everything He touches, becomes sweet. Madhuram!

"Madhurādhipater akhilaṃ madhuram": everything about Him is sweet. 🙏
