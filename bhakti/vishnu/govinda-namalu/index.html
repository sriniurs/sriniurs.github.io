GOVINDA NĀMĀLU

ŚRĪ ŚRĪNIVĀSA GOVINDĀ · ŚRĪ VEṄKAṬEŚA GOVINDĀ
The pilgrims' chant of the Seven Hills · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: First the whole chant to sing along. Then what it is and how to sing it, and one page for every two stanzas: the chant, a picture, every name with its meaning, the story behind it and the one thing to remember.

FOREWORD

"Śrī Śrīnivāsā Govindā, Śrī Veṅkaṭeśā Govindā…" Anyone who has climbed the Seven Hills to Tirumala has heard it: pilgrims of every age calling out the Lord's names, one after another, each ending in a joyful "Govindā!"

The Govinda Nāmālu, "names of Govinda" (nāmālu is Telugu for "names"), is a simple chant of about 150 names of Śrīnivāsa, mostly in Sanskrit with a few Telugu ones, set to a refrain everyone can join: Govindā Hari Govindā, Gokulanandana Govindā.

No learning is needed to sing it. That is its beauty.

Govindā Govindā,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Why does every line end with Govindā?

PROF. BOLT: Because it's a call and an answer. One person sings the name, everyone shouts 'Govindā!'. It turns a crowd into a single voice.

WHAT IS GOVINDA NĀMĀLU?

A chant of the people

🗣 The language

Mostly Sanskrit names, sung in the Telugu style, with a few Telugu names like Ēḍukoṇḍalavāḍā (Lord of the Seven Hills) and Vaḍḍikāsulavāḍā (the interest collector).

✍️ The author

Traditional: no known author or date. It grew among the pilgrims and devotees of Tirumala, and versions vary in length and order.

Simple by design. Each stanza has four names, each followed by "Govindā", then the refrain. No metre to learn, no grammar to follow: just names and love.

What's in the names. Śrīnivāsa and Veṅkaṭeśa; the avatāras, especially Rāma and Kṛṣṇa; the Lord's ornaments at Tirumala (diamond crown, musk tilaka, Vaijayantī garland); and devotees and teachers linked with the hill: Padmāvatī, Rāmānuja, Hātīrāma.

Govinda. "Finder of cows" or "known through the Vedas": a name of Kṛṣṇa, and the great name of Tirumala, shouted as "Govindā! Govindā!".

ZAPPY: Is it the same as Bhaja Govindam?

PROF. BOLT: No! Bhaja Govindam is Śaṅkara's teaching poem. Govinda Nāmālu is a pilgrims' chant of names. Both love the name Govinda.

HOW TO SING IT

Call, answer, refrain
The leader calls. One person sings the name: "Śrī Śrīnivāsā…"
Everyone answers. All respond: "…Govindā!"
Four names, then the refrain. After every four names, everyone sings together: "Govindā Hari Govindā, Gokulanandana Govindā".
Speed it up. Many groups start slowly and get faster and louder as the chant goes on, ending in a joyful rush.
Alone, too. Sing both parts yourself, softly, while walking, cooking or before sleep. It needs no setting and no special time.
govindā hari govindā · gokulanandana govindā

ZAPPY: Can I sing it without knowing what the names mean?

PROF. BOLT: Of course! But the next pages will make every 'Govindā' mean something.

Āśritapakṣa (22). "Who takes the side of those who come to Him": the Lord is not neutral about His devotees. He is self-luminous (21), needing no other light, and the abode of bliss: the Ānanda Nilaya is also the name of the gilded tower over His shrine.
He is on the side of all who come to Him.

GOVINDA NĀMĀLU ON ONE PAGE

38 stanzas in five lines
Stanzas 1–4. Śrīnivāsa, Lord of Veṅkaṭa, loving to devotees; dark as a rain cloud; the butter thief who protects the good.
Stanzas 5–8, 25–26. All the avatāras, from the Fish to Kalki, with Rāma and Kṛṣṇa again and again.
Stanzas 9–18. Friend in trouble, ocean of compassion, the fear-not hand, rescuer of the elephant, God made visible.
Stanzas 19–31. The Lord of Tirumala: diamond crown, the interest collector, Lord of the Seven Hills, on the side of His own.
Stanzas 32–38. Śiva and Keśava in one; dear to Hātīrāma and Rāmānuja; and back to Śrī Śrīnivāsa.
