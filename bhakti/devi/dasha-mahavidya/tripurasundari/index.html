TRIPURASUNDARĪ

ṢOḌAŚĪ · LALITĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The most beautiful in the three worlds

FOREWORD

After the darkness of Kālī and the blue night of Tārā comes the dawn: Tripurasundarī, red as the rising sun, the most beautiful being in the three worlds. In the south she is Lalitā, whose Thousand Names are chanted in homes every Friday; in Tantra she is Ṣoḍaśī, forever sixteen.

She holds a bow of sugarcane and arrows of flowers. She was born from the gods’ sacrificial fire to destroy a demon made from the ashes of Love, and to bring Love back. Her body is a diagram: the Śrī Cakra.

This guide tells her story, her texts, her yantra and her temples.

Śrī Mātre Namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is the Lalitā Sahasranāma about this Goddess?

PROF. BOLT: The very same. Lalitā, Tripurasundarī and Ṣoḍaśī are three names for the third Mahāvidyā.

Part of Daśa Mahāvidyā, the ten Great Wisdoms. Previous: Tārā.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
NameTripurasundarī: “the most beautiful in the three worlds” (or “of the three cities”)
AlsoṢoḍaśī, Lalitā, Rājarājeśvarī, Kāmeśvarī, Śrī Mātā, Kāmākṣī
PlaceThird of the ten Mahāvidyās; supreme Goddess of the Śrīkula
LookRed and radiant; four arms with sugarcane bow, five flower arrows, noose and goad; crescent moon
ConsortKāmeśvara, Śiva as Lord of Desire
TextsLalitopākhyāna of the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitā Sahasranāma, Saundarya Laharī, Tripura Rahasya
SymbolThe Śrī Cakra (Śrī Yantra)
TemplesTripura Sundarī, Udaipur (Tripurā); Kāmākṣī, Kāñcī; Rājarājeśvarī shrines across the south

ZAPPY: The fierce Kālī and the beautiful Lalitā in one list?

PROF. BOLT: That's the Mahāvidyās. Kālī dissolves, Lalitā delights: the same Mother, at both ends.

HER NAMES

Sixteen, sport, queen of queens
Tripurasundarītri-pura “three cities / three worlds” + sundarī “beautiful”
Ṣoḍaśī“Sixteen”: the ever-sixteen-year-old; also her sixteen-syllable mantra
Lalitā“She who plays”: the universe is her game
Rājarājeśvarī“Queen of kings of kings”
KāmeśvarīWife of Kāmeśvara, Lord of Desire
Śrī Mātā“Holy Mother”: the very first name in her Thousand Names
BālāHer child form, Bālā Tripurasundarī

ZAPPY: Why sixteen?

PROF. BOLT: The moon has sixteen digits; the sixteenth never wanes. She is the part of us that never fades.

HER FORM

Desire turned divine
Sugarcane bowThe mind: sweet, bendable
Five flower arrowsThe five senses, and the five arrows of Kāma
Noose (pāśa)Attachment, which she binds
Goad (aṅkuśa)Anger, which she steers
ThroneBrahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra and Īśvara as its legs, Sadāśiva as the seat
ColourRed, like the rising sun, like the hibiscus

ZAPPY: She sits on the gods?

PROF. BOLT: In Śrīvidyā the five great gods are her furniture. That's how highly Śrīkula ranks her.

LALITOPĀKHYĀNA · BHAṆḌĀSURA

A demon from the ashes of love
From ashes. When Śiva burned Kāma, the god of love, one of Śiva’s attendants shaped a figure from the ashes. It came to life as the demon Bhaṇḍāsura, and won a boon that made him almost unbeatable.
A world without desire. Bhaṇḍa conquered the worlds; with love gone, nothing grew.
From the fire. The gods offered themselves into a great sacrifice. From the fire rose Lalitā, on the Śrī Cakra chariot.
Victory. With her generals Mantriṇī (Śyāmalā) and Daṇḍanāthā (Vārāhī), she destroyed Bhaṇḍa and his armies, and brought Kāma back to life.

ZAPPY: She kills a demon born of dead desire, and revives desire?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly. Love without wisdom becomes a demon; wisdom brings love back to life.

THE GREAT TEXTS

Thousand names and a wave of beauty
LalitopākhyānaThe tale of Lalitā in the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, told by Hayagrīva to the sage Agastya
Lalitā SahasranāmaHer Thousand Names, in the same Purāṇa: one of the most-chanted hymns in India, beginning śrī mātā
Lalitā TriśatīHer three hundred names, built on her mantra’s syllables
Saundarya Laharī“The Wave of Beauty”, a hundred verses attributed to Ādi Śaṅkara, on her form and the Śrī Cakra
Tripura RahasyaA dialogue on her as pure consciousness
See also: Lalitā Pañcaratnam on this site

THE ŚRĪ CAKRA

Her diagram, her body
TrianglesNine interlocking triangles: four pointing up (Śiva), five pointing down (Śakti), making 43 small triangles
LotusesRings of eight and sixteen petals
GatesA square outer wall with four gates
BinduThe central dot: the Goddess herself, in union with Śiva
MeruA three-dimensional, mountain-shaped Śrī Cakra is also worshipped
WorshipIn Śrīvidyā the Śrī Cakra is her subtle form

ZAPPY: A drawing that is the Goddess?

PROF. BOLT: As a map is the city for the traveller. Śrīvidyā walks inward from the gates to the dot.

ŚRĪVIDYĀ

The path of the Śrīkula
WhatThe Tantric tradition centred on Lalitā Tripurasundarī and the Śrī Cakra
MantraThe secret pañcadaśī (fifteen-syllable) and ṣoḍaśī (sixteen) mantras, given by a guru
WorshipNavāvaraṇa pūjā: worship of the nine enclosures of the Śrī Cakra
WhereStrong in the south: Kāñcī, Śṛṅgeri, and households of Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka
ŚaṅkaraŚaṅkara’s maṭhas are linked to Śrīvidyā worship by tradition

ZAPPY: Can I worship the Śrī Cakra at home?

PROF. BOLT: Many do, with simple offerings and the Sahasranāma. The full rites and mantras come from a teacher.

BĀLĀ TRIPURASUNDARĪ

The girl Goddess
WhoLalitā’s child form, a girl of about nine
StoryIn the Lalitopākhyāna, Bālā rides out and kills Bhaṇḍa’s thirty sons
WorshipOften the first step in Śrīvidyā; Kumārī pūjā honours her in girls
TempleNemili in Tamil Nadu is famous for Bālā

TRIPURA SUNDARĪ, UDAIPUR

The Goddess of Tripurā
WhereUdaipur (old Rangamati), Gomati district, Tripurā
BuiltBy Mahārāja Dhanya Mānikya of Tripurā, c. 1501
Kūrma PīṭhaThe low hill is shaped like a tortoise (kūrma)
Śakti PīṭhaCounted among the 51: Satī’s right foot, by common lists
ImagesTwo: the large Tripura Sundarī and the small Choṭi Mā (Caṇḍī), in black stone
Name of the stateOne theory derives “Tripurā” from the Goddess
See also: Kāmākṣī of Kāñcī, Lalitā in the south

IN THE SOUTH

Kāmākṣī and Rājarājeśvarī
KāmākṣīAt Kāñcī she sits as Lalitā in yoga, before a Śrī Cakra installed, by tradition, by Śaṅkara
RājarājeśvarīTemples in Bengaluru, Kerala and elsewhere
NavarātriIn the south, Navarātri ends with Lalitā’s worship
HomesLalitā Sahasranāma is chanted on Fridays and full moons in countless homes

WHAT SHE TEACHES

The wisdom of beauty
Beauty is divineThe world’s beauty is her play (līlā), not a trap
Desire purifiedThe bow and arrows of love, held by wisdom
Inward journeyThe Śrī Cakra: from the outer gates of the senses to the inner dot of the Self
Third wisdomAfter Kālī’s dissolution and Tārā’s rescue, Tripurasundarī’s bliss
