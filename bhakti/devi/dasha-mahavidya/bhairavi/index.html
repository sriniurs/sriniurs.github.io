BHAIRAVĪ

DAŚA MAHĀVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The fire that burns and purifies

FOREWORD

Bhuvaneśvarī makes room for the world. The fifth Great Wisdom, Bhairavī, is the fire that clears it: red as a thousand suns, bride of the fierce Bhairava, queen of the sixty-four yoginīs. She holds a garland of heads in one telling and a book and rosary in another.

Her name is also a title. In 1861 a woman ascetic called the Bhairavī Brāhmaṇī stepped off a boat at Dakṣiṇeśvar and became Śrī Rāmakṛṣṇa’s first guru. And every Hindustani concert traditionally ends with a rāga that bears her name.

This guide tells her stories, her meaning and her temples.

Jai Bhairavī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is she scary or kind?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. Fire is both, and she is fire.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
NameBhairavī: “the terrifying”, “the awe-inspiring”; also Tripurabhairavī
PlaceFifth of the ten Mahāvidyās
She isThe Goddess as fire: the heat that destroys, purifies and transforms
LookRed as a thousand rising suns; three eyes; crescent moon; garland of heads; rosary, book and blessing hands
ConsortBhairava, the fierce Śiva
CourtQueen of the sixty-four yoginīs
TitleA woman adept in Tantra is also called a bhairavī
TemplesTripurabhairavī at Kāśī; Bhairabī shrines across Odisha

ZAPPY: Terrifying, but she holds a book and a rosary?

PROF. BOLT: Both. The fire that burns the house down also cooks the food. She is that fire.

HER NAMES

Fear, awe and the three cities
BhairavīFeminine of bhairava, “terrible, awe-inspiring”
Tripurabhairavītri-pura, “three cities”: the Bhairavī of the three worlds
Śubhaṅkarī“She who does good”: the kind face behind the fierce one
Kālarātri“Night of time”: a name she shares with Kālī
Ṣaṭkūṭā, Kauleśī, RudrabhairavīSome of her forms named in the Tantras
Bhairavī (title)A woman who has mastered Tantric practice

ZAPPY: Is Tripurabhairavī the same as Tripurasundarī?

PROF. BOLT: Close relatives, not twins. Teachers say they are linked, but distinct.

HER FORM

Two faces of one Goddess
ColourRed, “like the light of a thousand rising suns”
FaceThree eyes, crescent moon, smiling or fierce
Gentle formRosary and book, with the gestures of boon-giving and “fear not”; seated on a red lotus
Fierce formRed garments, a garland of severed heads, blood on her breast, sometimes in the cremation ground
MeaningThe same power that creates also destroys: both forms are true

THE GODDESS OF FIRE

Tapas, the heat of practice
TapasLiterally “heat”: the inner fire of discipline, fasting and meditation
She is that heatThe burning that clears away ignorance and old habits
DestroyerKinsley notes her strong link with destruction: she is the end of every created thing
KuṇḍalinīTeachers place her at the root of the body, the fire that rises upward
Fifth wisdomAfter Bhuvaneśvarī makes room for the world, Bhairavī burns away what does not belong

ZAPPY: Why would anyone pray to a destroyer?

PROF. BOLT: Because some things should end: anger, fear, pride. You ask her to burn those.

THE THREE CITIES

Why “Tripura”?
The legendTripura was three flying cities of demons; Śiva burnt them with a single arrow
In KāśīLocal tradition says Bhairavī took her fierce form to destroy the three cities
Inner meaningTeachers read the three cities as waking, dream and deep sleep, or body, mind and causal self
BeyondBurn all three and what is left is pure awareness

ZAPPY: A goddess who burns cities?

PROF. BOLT: The cities are inside us. She burns the walls we build around the self.

BHAIRAVA AND THE YOGINĪS

Her court
BhairavaŚiva in his fierce form, guardian of Kāśī and of every temple: Bhairavī is his power
Sixty-four yoginīsFemale deities of Tantra; Bhairavī is called their leader
Yoginī templesCircular, open-air shrines such as Hirāpur (Odisha) and Mitāolī (Madhya Pradesh) honour the yoginīs
Charnel groundA 17th-century Mughal-period painting in the Metropolitan Museum shows Bhairavī and Śiva as skull-bearing ascetics
Other consortSome texts pair her with Dakṣiṇāmūrti, Śiva as teacher
See also: The Devī Māhātmya series

THE BHAIRAVĪ BRĀHMAṆĪ

A living bhairavī
WhoYogeśvarī, known as the Bhairavī Brāhmaṇī, a woman ascetic from Jessore, Bengal
1861She arrived by boat at the Dakṣiṇeśvar Kālī temple and met Śrī Rāmakṛṣṇa
TeacherShe became his first formal guru and led him through the sixty-four Tantric practices
WitnessShe argued before pandits that his states were spiritual, not madness
AfterHer later life and death are not recorded

ZAPPY: So a bhairavī is also a person?

PROF. BOLT: A title earned by mastery. This one taught the saint of Dakṣiṇeśvar.

THE RĀGA BHAIRAVĪ

The Goddess in music
HindustaniBhairavī is the morning rāga, and by custom the last piece of a concert
MoodDevotion, tenderness, longing: the gentle face of the Goddess
CarnaticBhairavi is also a major Carnatic rāga, with a different scale
FamilyThe rāga Bhairav is named for Bhairava; Bhairavī is its feminine partner
Name onlyThe rāgas carry her name; they are not hymns to her

ZAPPY: Concerts end with a fierce Goddess?

PROF. BOLT: With her sweetest side. Even the terrible one sings softly at dawn.

TRIPURABHAIRAVĪ AT KĀŚĪ

Her temple in Varanasi
WhereNear Mīr Ghāṭ, in the lanes by the Viśvanātha temple; the steps below are Tripurabhairavī Ghāṭ
DeityA self-manifested (svayambhū) image; the Tripureśvara liṅga, her partner, is in the same shrine
Kāśī KhaṇḍaSkanda Purāṇa, Kāśī Khaṇḍa ch. 72: she is one of the powers the Goddess released in her battle with Durgāsura
The ghāṭOnce Vṛddhāditya Ghāṭ; renamed after an 18th-century temple to her
More Kāśī: Viśālākṣī

BHAIRABĪ IN ODISHA

Shrines of the east
PuruṇākaṭakBoudh district: Bhairabī is the presiding deity of the town
BoudhA second Bhairabī temple in the district town
MantridiNear Berhampur, Ganjam: Ek-pāda Bhairavī, a one-legged image in a temple said to date to the 8th century
MalkangiriA Bhairabī temple on the edge of the town, tied to the old royal house
KāmākhyāBhairavī has her own shrine on the Nīlācala hill with the other Mahāvidyās

ZAPPY: Why so many in Odisha?

PROF. BOLT: Odisha kept its village goddesses and its Tantric temples. Bhairabī belongs to both.

THROUGH THE YEAR

Her days
Bhairavī JayantīThe full moon of Mārgaśīrṣa (November–December)
NavarātriBoth seasons; she is honoured among the ten Mahāvidyās
Kālabhairava AṣṭamīThe day of her consort, Kālabhairava, in the same month
Gupta NavarātriThe “hidden” Navarātris of Māgha and Āṣāḍha, kept by Tantric worshippers

WHAT SHE TEACHES

The wisdom of fire
Burn what bindsDiscipline is heat; it hurts, and it frees
Fear and aweWhat terrifies the ego is liberating to the self
Speech and knowledgeHer book and rosary: learning and practice go together
Women as teachersThe title bhairavī honours women who master the path
