DAŚA MAHĀVIDYĀ

DEVĪ · AN ILLUSTRATED OVERVIEW
One Mother, ten faces

FOREWORD

When Śiva forbade Satī to go to her father’s sacrifice, she did not argue. She grew dark and terrible, and when he turned to leave, he found her in front of him, and behind him, and on every side: ten Goddesses, one in each direction.

These are the Daśa Mahāvidyā, the ten Great Wisdoms: Kālī of time, Tārā who carries across, Tripurasundarī of beauty, and seven more, down to golden Kamalā on her lotus.

This overview introduces all ten. A full guide to each follows in the series.

Jai Mā,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Why are some of them so scary?

PROF. BOLT: Because some truths are. The fierce ones cut away fear; the gentle ones give what's left room to bloom.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
Mahāvidyā“Great Wisdom”, “Great Knowledge”: a form of the Goddess who is herself a path to liberation
DaśaTen of them, in the usual order:
The tenKālī, Tārā, Tripurasundarī, Bhuvaneśvarī, Bhairavī, Chinnamastā, Dhūmāvatī, Bagalāmukhī, Mātaṅgī, Kamalā
One GoddessTen faces of the one Mahādevī, from terrifying to beautiful
Origin storySatī becomes ten to stop Śiva from blocking her way to Dakṣa’s sacrifice
TextsMahābhāgavata and Bṛhaddharma Purāṇas (c. 10th c. onward); many Tantras

ZAPPY: Ten goddesses or one?

PROF. BOLT: One Mother, ten windows. Each shows a different truth about her: time, sound, beauty, space, fire, sacrifice, emptiness, stillness, speech, plenty.

HOW SATĪ BECAME TEN

The best-known story
No invitation. Dakṣa held a great sacrifice and did not invite Śiva. Satī wanted to go anyway; Śiva forbade it.
Her fury. Satī grew terrible: she became Kālī, dark and wild. Śiva, alarmed, tried to flee.
Ten directions. Wherever he turned, a different Goddess blocked his way: she had become ten, one in each of the ten directions (the eight points of the compass, above and below).
He yields. Śiva let her go. She went to the sacrifice, and what followed is the story of the Śakti Pīṭhas.

ZAPPY: The same Satī whose body became the Pīṭhas?

PROF. BOLT: The very same. Before she died she showed Śiva all ten of her faces.

OTHER BEGINNINGS

More than one story
PārvatīIn another telling, Pārvatī, after a quarrel with Śiva, takes ten forms to stop him leaving
DurgāElsewhere they arise in the Goddess’s battles with demons, as her helpers
ŚatākṣīOne account links them to the hundred-eyed Goddess who ended a famine
TextsThe Mahābhāgavata (c. 10th c.) and Bṛhaddharma Purāṇas tell the Satī version; Tantras such as the Toḍala and Śākta-pramoda describe each Goddess
Lists varySome Purāṇic lists name Ṣoḍaśī for Tripurasundarī and order them differently

ZAPPY: Which story is true?

PROF. BOLT: All of them point the same way: the Goddess is never just one thing.

FIERCE TO GENTLE

A spectrum of the Goddess
Fierce (ugra)Kālī, Tārā, Chinnamastā, Bhairavī, Dhūmāvatī, Bagalāmukhī: they face death, fear and the ego head on
Gentle (saumya)Tripurasundarī, Bhuvaneśvarī, Mātaṅgī, Kamalā: beauty, space, art and plenty
One cycleSome teachers read them as stages: from Kālī’s dissolution to Kamalā’s full bloom
Why fierce?The fierce forms are mothers too: they destroy what binds the devotee

KĀLĪKULA AND ŚRĪKULA

Two families of worship
KālīkulaThe “family of Kālī”: Kālī, Tārā, Chinnamastā and the fierce forms; strong in Bengal and the east
ŚrīkulaThe “family of Śrī”: Tripurasundarī (Lalitā), with the Śrī Cakra and Śrīvidyā; strong in the south
MātaṅgīIn Śrīvidyā she is Lalitā’s minister, Śyāmalā
BagalāmukhīLalitā’s general in some lists; Vārāhī also holds that role
Meeting pointThe Mahāvidyās bring both families into one set of ten

ZAPPY: So Śrīvidyā in Kāñcī and Kālī worship in Kolkata are cousins?

PROF. BOLT: Same family tree, two branches. Tripurasundarī is the third Mahāvidyā.

ZAPPY: Viṣṇu's avatāras come from the Goddess?

PROF. BOLT: That's the Śākta view: every form of God is her power at work.

HOW TO RECOGNISE THEM

Colours and objects
KālīBlack; tongue out; garland of heads; sword; standing on Śiva
TārāBlue; short; tiger skin; scissors and skull-cup; on a cremation ground
TripurasundarīRed-gold; sugarcane bow and flower arrows; noose and goad
BhuvaneśvarīGolden; crescent moon; noose and goad; gestures of blessing
BhairavīRed; red garments; rosary and book, or noose and goad
ChinnamastāHolds her own cut head; three streams of blood feed her and two attendants
DhūmāvatīGrey widow in white; crow banner; winnowing basket; horseless chariot
BagalāmukhīYellow; pulls a demon’s tongue; raises a club
MātaṅgīGreen; vīṇā; parrot
KamalāGolden; seated on a lotus; elephants pour water over her

A WORD ON WORSHIP

Tantra and initiation
TantricThe Mahāvidyās belong to Tantric worship; their mantras and yantras are traditionally given by a guru, with initiation (dīkṣā)
For everyoneStories, images, names and temple darśana are open to all
Fierce, not evilTheir fearsome looks stand for truths (death, desire, the ego) faced without flinching
NavarātriGupta Navarātris in Māgha and Āṣāḍha are often kept for the Mahāvidyās
This seriesTen guides, one per Goddess: myth, form, meaning, temples. No secret mantras

ZAPPY: Can I chant their mantras from a book?

PROF. BOLT: Tradition says take them from a teacher. This series sticks to what is meant for everyone.
