BAGALĀMUKHĪ

DAŚA MAHĀVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The yellow Goddess who stills

FOREWORD

The eighth Great Wisdom is dressed in gold. Bagalāmukhī, Pītāmbarā, “the yellow-robed”, rose from a lake of turmeric to still a storm that was tearing the world apart. In her best-known image she holds a demon by the tongue, club raised, stopping his deadly words mid-sentence.

Her power is stambhana, stillness. Across North India people seek her for victory in disputes; teachers say the first tongue she stills is our own. Her great temples stand at Datiā, Nalkheḍā and Bankhaṇḍī.

This guide tells her stories, her meaning and her temples.

Jai Pītāmbarā,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Why is everything yellow?

PROF. BOLT: She is Pītāmbarā. Yellow clothes, yellow flowers, even a yellow lake.

Part of Daśa Mahāvidyā, the ten Great Wisdoms. Previous: Dhūmāvatī.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
NameBagalāmukhī: “crane-faced”, or “she whose face is the bridle”
AlsoPītāmbarā, “she who wears yellow”
PlaceEighth of the ten Mahāvidyās
PowerStambhana: stilling, stopping, paralysing; above all, stopping speech
LookGolden-yellow; yellow clothes, flowers and ornaments; club raised in one hand, a demon’s tongue in the other
BirthFrom the Haridrā Sarovara, the lake of turmeric, to calm a cosmic storm
TemplesDatiā and Nalkheḍā (Madhya Pradesh), Bankhaṇḍī (Himachal)
Sought forVictory in disputes and protection from enemies

ZAPPY: A Goddess who grabs a tongue?

PROF. BOLT: The tongue that lies and curses. She stops it mid-sentence.

HER NAMES

Crane or bridle?
Crane (baka)Kinsley translates her name “she who has the face of a crane”; the crane stands utterly still before it strikes
But…Kinsley also notes she is rarely shown with a crane’s head
Bridle (valgā)Others say “bagalā” is a worn-down form of valgā, the bit in a horse’s mouth: she controls
A Kāśī priestTold Kinsley her true name is Valgāmukhī, for her power to control
Pītāmbarā · Pītāmbarī“Yellow-robed”: her name across North India
Kalyāṇī“The auspicious”: another reading of her name

ZAPPY: Which one is right?

PROF. BOLT: Both are told. A crane's stillness and a horse's bit both mean control.

HER FORM

The golden Goddess
ColourGolden-yellow, with yellow clothes, garland and ornaments
HandsLeft: the demon’s tongue; right: a raised club
Four-armedSome descriptions give her four arms and a third eye
SeatA golden throne; some images show a corpse beneath her

THE STORM AND THE TURMERIC LAKE

Why she was born
The storm. In the Satya Yuga a great storm began to destroy creation.
The austerity. Viṣṇu, troubled, performed tapas on the shore of the Haridrā Sarovara, the lake of turmeric, in Saurāṣṭra.
The Goddess. Pleased, the Goddess brought forth Bagalāmukhī from the lake. She stilled the storm and order returned.

ZAPPY: Why turmeric?

PROF. BOLT: Yellow is her colour. Turmeric, gold, the yellow robe: all of it points to her.

THE DEMON MADAN

Seizing the tongue
The boonMadan won vāk-siddhi by austerity: whatever he said came true
The misuseHe used it to harm and kill people
The godsEnraged, they turned to Bagalāmukhī
The tongueShe caught his tongue and stilled his speech
His wishBefore she slew him, he asked to be worshipped with her; she granted it. That is the figure at her feet

ZAPPY: He got worshipped after all that?

PROF. BOLT: Even a demon who meets her is changed. His wish was to stay near her.

A THIRD STORY

Shared with Dhūmāvatī
HungerKinsley records a myth in which the hungry Goddess swallows Śiva
Two GoddessesThe same story is told of Dhūmāvatī; in this telling both Dhūmāvatī and Bagalāmukhī arise from it
The craneLike a crane swallowing its prey whole, she swallowed her husband: one reading of “crane-faced”
NeighboursAt Datiā the two Goddesses share a temple complex
Previous: Dhūmāvatī

THE POWER TO STILL

Stambhana
Stambhana“Making rigid”: stopping motion, speech, thought
OutwardDevotees pray to her for victory in lawsuits, debates and disputes
InwardTeachers say the real enemy is our own tongue and restless mind; she stills them
SpeechShe silences false speech and gives the power of true speech
Eighth wisdomAfter Dhūmāvatī’s emptiness, Bagalāmukhī’s stillness

ZAPPY: So she helps you win court cases?

PROF. BOLT: Many believe so. The sages add: first win against your own tongue.

PĪTĀMBARĀ PĪṬHA, DATIĀ

Her most famous temple
WhereDatiā, Madhya Pradesh, at the entrance to the town
FoundedBy a Swāmījī who settled here in the 1920s; the Bagalāmukhī shrine is dated to 1935 by some sources
Haridrā SarovaraA pond in front of the temple recalls the turmeric lake
DhūmāvatīHe also founded her temple in the same āśrama
No moneyThe temple of Pītāmbarā does not accept money offerings
AlsoA Sanskrit library, and the Vanakhaṇḍeśvara Śiva temple

NALKHEḌĀ

The riverbank temple of Mālwā
WhereNalkheḍā, Āgar Mālwā district, Madhya Pradesh, on the Lakhundar, a tributary of the Narmadā
LegendSaid to have been founded by Yudhiṣṭhira on Kṛṣṇa’s advice, to win the Mahābhārata war
ImageA self-manifested image with three faces, called Triśakti Mātā
GroundBuilt on a cremation ground, as befits a Tantric seat
HistoryRestored in 1815
DaysNavarātri and Bagalāmukhī Jayantī; Tuesdays and Fridays

ZAPPY: Three old temples?

PROF. BOLT: Datiā, Nalkheḍā and Bankhaṇḍī are the three most often named.

WHAT SHE TEACHES

The wisdom of stillness
Hold your tongueSpeech can kill, as Madan’s did; restraint is power
Be stillThe crane waits motionless; the mind must too
Stop the stormInside or outside, chaos can be stilled
Truth winsFalse words are seized; true words prevail
