MĀTAṄGĪ

DAŚA MAHĀVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The green Goddess of speech and music

FOREWORD

Bagalāmukhī stops the tongue. The ninth Great Wisdom, Mātaṅgī, sets it free. She is emerald-green, plays the vīṇā with a parrot at her side, and rules speech, music and the sixty-four arts: the Tantric Sarasvatī.

Yet she was born from the food the gods left on their plates, and devotees offer her leftovers that no other deity would accept. In Śrīvidyā she is Śyāmalā, the minister of Queen Lalitā, praised in a hymn ascribed to Kālidāsa.

This guide tells her stories, her meaning and her temples.

Jai Mātaṅgī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is she like Sarasvatī?

PROF. BOLT: Sarasvatī in green, who also eats with the outcastes.

Part of Daśa Mahāvidyā, the ten Great Wisdoms. Previous: Bagalāmukhī.

ZAPPY: A Goddess who wants leftovers?

PROF. BOLT: The gods' leftovers. She turns what others throw away into grace.

PROF. BOLT: That's her secret. The palace and the cremation ground are both hers.

HER FORM

Two Mātaṅgīs
Rāja-MātaṅgīGreen, crescent-crowned, playing a ruby-studded vīṇā; seated on an altar flanked by two parrots; conch earrings and flower garlands
Ucchiṣṭa-MātaṅgīCarries a noose, sword, goad and club

ParrotRepeats speech: the bird of words
VīṇāThe instrument of Sarasvatī: music and learning

BORN FROM LEFTOVERS

The Ucchiṣṭa story
The feast. Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī visited Śiva and Pārvatī, and a banquet of fine food was served.
The fallen food. As the gods ate, some food fell to the ground. From it rose a beautiful maiden, who asked for their leftovers.
The blessing. The four gave her their leftovers as prasāda. Śiva declared that worship of her would bear fruit, and that leftovers offered to her are sacred. She is Ucchiṣṭa-Mātaṅgī.

ZAPPY: So the gods' plates made a Goddess?

PROF. BOLT: What touched the gods became holy. She is that holiness, rising from the floor.

OTHER TELLINGS

Sources differ
Sage’s daughterThe sage Mātaṅga won a boon that the Divine Mother would be born as his daughter: Mātaṅgī
Pārvatī in disguiseIn another story Pārvatī comes to Śiva as a Cāṇḍāla (outcaste) woman, and he is drawn to her
HuntressThe Svatantra Tantra has her help Mātaṅga subdue all creatures; scholars link her with hunter and forest peoples
Ten formsWhen Satī blocked Śiva’s path, Mātaṅgī stood in the north-west
AvatārasThe Muṇḍamālā list pairs her with the Buddha; others with Rāma

THE TANTRIC SARASVATĪ

Speech and the arts
SpeechShe rules the spoken word, from the first sound to eloquence
MusicThe vīṇā is hers; musicians honour her
Sixty-four artsMusic, dance, painting, poetry and the rest: all are hers
DifferenceSarasvatī is pure and white; Mātaṅgī embraces the impure and the outsider
Ninth wisdomAfter Bagalāmukhī stills speech, Mātaṅgī gives it

ZAPPY: Two Goddesses of speech in a row?

PROF. BOLT: One stops false speech, the next gives true speech. A neat pair.

ŚYĀMALĀ, THE MINISTER

Her place in Śrīvidyā
MantriṇīIn the Lalitopākhyāna she is Lalitā’s chief counsellor and minister
In battleShe leads the army against Bhaṇḍāsura alongside Vārāhī, the commander
Lalitā SahasranāmaPraises Lalitā as pleased when Mantriṇī Ambā slew Viṣaṅga, Bhaṇḍa’s brother
KālidāsaThe Śyāmalā Daṇḍakam, ascribed to the poet, praises her ruby-studded vīṇā and sweet speech
NavarātriŚyāmalā Navarātri is kept in Māgha by some Śrīvidyā families
See also: Tripurasundarī

WHY LEFTOVERS?

Turning pollution upside down
The ruleIn orthodox practice, food touched by the mouth is impure, never offered to a deity
Her ruleTo Mātaṅgī, devotees offer ucchiṣṭa: the order is reversed
OutsidersShe belongs to the edges: forests, outcastes, those left over
MeaningSome say she is herself the “leftover”: the Self that remains when all else perishes
LessonNothing and no one is beneath the Goddess

ZAPPY: Isn't that against all the rules?

PROF. BOLT: On purpose. Tantra breaks a rule to show that the divine is everywhere.

MĀTAṄGĪ MODHEŚVARĪ

Modhera, Gujarat
WhereModhera, North Gujarat, beside the famous Sun Temple
GoddessModheśvarī, called Mātaṅgī because the sage Mātaṅga worshipped her
Clan goddessKuladevī of the Modh communities
LegendShe appeared to destroy a demon, Karṇāṭa, who troubled the brahmins at prayer
HistoryDamaged in the invasions of the 13th–14th c.; the present temple was built 1962–66
NavarātriA major state-supported festival

IN THE SOUTH

Madurai and beyond
MaduraiŚrīvidyā tradition sees Mīnākṣī, green and parrot-bearing, as Rāja-Mātaṅgī
Śrīvidyā homesŚyāmalā is worshipped alongside Lalitā and Vārāhī
HampiThe Mātaṅga hill at Hampi is named for the sage Mātaṅga of the Rāmāyaṇa, not for the Goddess
MusiciansCarnatic musicians traditionally honour her as the Goddess of music

ZAPPY: Mīnākṣī is Mātaṅgī?

PROF. BOLT: In Śrīvidyā reading, yes: the green Queen with the parrot. The temple's own tradition calls her Mīnākṣī.
