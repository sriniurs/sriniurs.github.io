KAMALĀ

DAŚA MAHĀVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The lotus Goddess of fortune

FOREWORD

The ten Great Wisdoms began with Kālī in the cremation ground. They end on a lotus. Kamalā, the tenth, is golden and smiling, holding lotuses, while white elephants pour water over her: the Gaja-Lakṣmī image found from the gateways of Sāñcī to the doorways of Indian homes.

She is Śrī-Lakṣmī, risen from the ocean of milk. But in the Mahāvidyā circle she sits alone, without Viṣṇu: not a god’s consort but the source of fortune herself. At Kāmākhyā she lives in the inner sanctum.

This guide tells her stories and meaning, and closes the series with all ten together.

Jai Kamalā,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is this the last one?

PROF. BOLT: The tenth and last. After the storm of the fierce ones, the lotus.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
NameKamalā, “she of the lotus”; also Kamalātmikā, “whose very self is the lotus”
PlaceTenth and last of the Mahāvidyās
She isThe Tantric form of Śrī-Lakṣmī: prosperity, beauty, fertility and grace
LookGolden; seated on a lotus; holding two lotuses; giving boons and “fear not”
ElephantsTwo or four elephants pour water over her: the Gaja-Lakṣmī image
Vishnu?Unlike Lakṣmī, she is shown alone; Viṣṇu is mostly absent
PopularityKinsley: the best known Mahāvidyā, with the oldest worship outside the group
KāmākhyāShe sits in the main sanctum, with Tripurasundarī and Mātaṅgī

ZAPPY: The last Wisdom is just Lakṣmī?

PROF. BOLT: Lakṣmī without Viṣṇu, on her own throne. The series ends in abundance.

ZAPPY: Why a lotus?

PROF. BOLT: It grows from mud and stays clean. Wealth without stain.

HER FORM

The golden Goddess
ColourGolden, shining like the sun

FROM THE OCEAN OF MILK

Her birth
Lost glory. When Śrī left the gods, they grew weak and the demons strong.
The churning. On Viṣṇu’s advice, gods and demons churned the ocean of milk, with Mount Mandara as the rod and the serpent Vāsuki as the rope.
Her rising. Among the treasures rose Lakṣmī on a lotus. She chose Viṣṇu, and the gods’ fortunes returned.

ZAPPY: Where did she go when she left?

PROF. BOLT: Into the ocean. Fortune hides when it is not respected.

WHY ALONE?

Kamalā versus Lakṣmī
LakṣmīIn Vaiṣṇava art she sits at Viṣṇu’s feet or by his side, with two hands
KamalāIn the Mahāvidyā set she has four arms and her own throne; Viṣṇu is mostly absent
MeaningThe Goddess is not wealth belonging to a god: she is the source of it
Not the sameSome writers insist Kamalā and Lakṣmī are distinct; most, like Kinsley, treat them as one
Tenth wisdomAfter nine fierce and strange forms, the Goddess as grace and fullness

ZAPPY: So she is independent here?

PROF. BOLT: In this series every Goddess is Śakti herself. Kamalā too.

GAJA-LAKṢMĪ

A very old image
Sāñcī and BharhutGaja-Lakṣmī is carved on the early Buddhist stūpa gateways and railings
CoinsShe appears on ancient Indian coins
SharedBuddhists, Jains and Hindus all used the image
TodayAbove doorways and shop counters across India

ZAPPY: A Buddhist Lakṣmī?

PROF. BOLT: The image of fortune belonged to everyone. Sāñcī's gates show it.

THE TEN TOGETHER

The whole series on one page
1. Kālī: time, death and transformation.
2. Tārā: the saviour who carries across.
3. Tripurasundarī (Ṣoḍaśī): beauty of the three worlds.
4. Bhuvaneśvarī: queen of the worlds, space.
5. Bhairavī: fierce heat of tapas.
6. Chinnamastā: the self-beheaded, life feeding life.
7. Dhūmāvatī: the smoky widow, emptiness.
8. Bagalāmukhī: the stopper of speech and foes.
9. Mātaṅgī: the outcaste Sarasvatī, speech and art.
10. Kamalā: the lotus Lakṣmī, abundance.

ZAPPY: From Kālī to Kamalā?

PROF. BOLT: From the darkness of time to the lotus of fortune. Ten faces, one Mother.

WHAT SHE TEACHES

The wisdom of fullness

Stay cleanLike the lotus, live in the world without its mud sticking
Respect fortuneShe leaves where she is disrespected
Full circleThe Mother who devours is also the Mother who feeds
