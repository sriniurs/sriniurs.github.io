FOREWORD

“Yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śaktirūpeṇa saṃsthitā…” Few lines in Sanskrit are better known. Every Navarātri it rings out from pandals, temples and television sets: “to the Goddess who dwells in all beings as power: salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations to her, again and again.”

It comes from the Devī Māhātmya, the great Purāṇic text of the Goddess. Driven from heaven, the gods stand on the Himālaya and praise her, not as a distant queen but as something inside every creature: its awareness, its hunger, its sleep, its faith, its mother, even its mistakes.

Twenty-one forms. By the end, there is nowhere she is not.

With the blessings of the Devī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Why say 'salutations to her' three times?

PROF. BOLT: Each form gets three bows. The tradition even counts each line as three separate mantras, which is how the Devī Māhātmya reaches its famous total of 700.

THE STORY · THE GODS ON THE HIMĀLAYA

Why the gods sang this hymn
The story in 5 frames
Heaven seized. Two demon brothers, Śumbha and Niśumbha, take over the three worlds and strip the gods of their powers.
A promise remembered. After slaying Mahiṣa (chapter 4), the Goddess had promised: “Remember me in distress, and I will come.”
The hymn. The gods go to the Himālaya and sing this praise: to the Goddess in every being.
Ambikā appears. Pārvatī comes to bathe in the Gaṅgā and asks whom they are praising. From her own body shines out Ambikā, who answers: “This hymn is for me.”
The battle begins. Śumbha’s servants see her beauty; the demon king sends a proposal. Her reply opens the great war of chapters 5–10.

ZAPPY: She was hiding inside Pārvatī?

PROF. BOLT: That's the point: the Goddess is inside every being. The gods just sang it, and here she shows it.

ABOUT THE HYMN

One hymn, many names

📜 Where it comes from

Devī Māhātmya (Durgā Saptaśatī), chapter 5, part of the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa.

🏷 Its names

Aparājitā Stuti, “praise of the Unconquered One”; also called the Tāntrika Devī Sūktam.

VersesWhat they do
1–5Salutations to the Goddess by her many names: Nature, Gaurī, Durgā, gentle and fierce
6–26The 21 forms: “the Goddess who dwells in all beings as…”
27–28The summing up: ruler of the senses, consciousness filling the world

When it is chanted. Every Navarātri, in daily Devī worship, and at the Aparājitā pūjā on Vijayadaśamī. Not to be confused with the Ṛg Vedic Devī Sūkta (10.125), “ahaṃ rudrebhir…”, which the Saptaśatī recitation also uses.

VERSE 1

SALUTATIONS TO THE GREAT GODDESS
namo devyai mahādevyaiśivāyai satataṃ namaḥ |namaḥ prakṛtyai bhadrāyainiyatāḥ praṇatāḥ sma tām ‖1‖
Salutations to the Devī, to the Great Devī, to the auspicious one, always salutations. Salutations to Prakṛti, to the gracious one: with minds controlled, we bow to her.
We bow to the Goddess, the Great Goddess, the Kind One; to Nature herself, the Gracious One. With quiet minds, we bow.
WordMeans
devyai · mahādevyaito the Goddess · to the Great Goddess
śivāyaito the auspicious one
prakṛtyaito Prakṛti: Nature, the source of all
bhadrāyaito the gracious, kind one
niyatāḥ praṇatāḥ smaself-controlled, we have bowed
She is Nature itself. Prakṛti is the stuff of the world: matter, energy, mind. The gods begin by saluting the Goddess not as a distant queen but as the very nature of everything they see.
The first word of worship: she is Nature herself.

VERSE 2

FIERCE AND GENTLE, MOON AND JOY
raudrāyai namo nityāyaigauryai dhātryai namo namaḥ |jyotsnāyai cendurūpiṇyaisukhāyai satataṃ namaḥ ‖2‖
Salutations to the fierce one, to the eternal one; to Gaurī, to the sustainer, salutations again and again. To moonlight, to her whose form is the moon, to joy, always salutations.
We bow to the Fierce One and the Eternal One, to fair Gaurī who holds up the world, to the cool moonlight, and to happiness itself.
WordMeans
raudrāyaito the fierce one
nityāyaito the eternal one
gauryaito Gaurī, the fair one, Pārvatī
dhātryaito the sustainer, the nurse of all
jyotsnāyai · indu-rūpiṇyaito moonlight · to her moon-form
sukhāyaito joy
Opposites in one breath. Fierce (raudrā) and fair (gaurī), fire and moonlight: the hymn will keep holding opposites together. The Goddess is not only the pleasant half of life.
Fierce and gentle: both are the Mother.

VERSE 3

FORTUNE AND MISFORTUNE
kalyāṇyai praṇatāṃ vṛddhyaisiddhyai kurmo namo namaḥ |nairṛtyai bhūbhṛtāṃ lakṣmyaiśarvāṇyai te namo namaḥ ‖3‖
To the auspicious one, to the growth of those who bow, to success, we offer salutations again and again. To Nairṛtī, to the fortune of kings, to Śarvāṇī: to you, salutations again and again.
We bow to her as goodness, as growth for her devotees, as success; and as misfortune too, as the glory of kings, and as Śiva’s wife.
WordMeans
kalyāṇyaito the auspicious one
praṇatāṃ vṛddhyaito the growth of those who bow
siddhyaito success
nairṛtyaito Nairṛtī: decay, misfortune
bhūbhṛtāṃ lakṣmyaito the fortune of kings
śarvāṇyaito Śarvāṇī, wife of Śiva
Good luck and bad. Lakṣmī is fortune; Nairṛtī is her opposite, the power of decline. The gods bow to both in one line. Whatever comes, prosperity or loss, comes through the same Mother.
Fortune and misfortune both come from her hand.
shlokam.org reads praṇatā for praṇatāṃ.

VERSE 4

DURGĀ, WHO CARRIES US ACROSS
durgāyai durgapārāyaisārāyai sarvakāriṇyai |khyātyai tathaiva kṛṣṇāyaidhūmrāyai satataṃ namaḥ ‖4‖
To Durgā, to her who carries us across difficulties, to the essence, to the cause of all; to fame, and to the dark one, to the smoky one, always salutations.
We bow to Durgā, who ferries us across every hardship; the essence and maker of all; renowned, dark, smoke-coloured.
WordMeans
durgāyaito Durgā: the hard-to-reach fortress
durga-pārāyaito her who takes us across hardship
sārāyaito the essence
sarva-kāriṇyaito the cause of all
khyātyaito fame, renown
kṛṣṇāyai · dhūmrāyaito the dark one · the smoky one
The name Durgā. Durga means a place hard to reach, a fortress, a difficulty. She is the fortress no one can take, and also the one who carries us through what is hard: durga-pārā, the “ferry across difficulty”.
Durgā: the fortress, and the ferry across.

VERSE 5

MOST GENTLE, MOST FIERCE
atisaumyātiraudrāyainatās tasyai namo namaḥ |namo jagatpratiṣṭhāyaidevyai kṛtyai namo namaḥ ‖5‖
To her who is most gentle and most fierce, we bow, salutations again and again. Salutations to the support of the world, to the Devī who is action itself, salutations again and again.
We bow to her who is the gentlest and the fiercest, who holds the world up, and who is every action.
WordMeans
ati-saumyāmost gentle
ati-raudrāmost fierce
natāḥwe have bowed
jagat-pratiṣṭhāthe foundation of the world
kṛtiaction, deed, creation
The whole hymn in one line. Ati-saumyā ati-raudrā: the most gentle and the most fierce, at once. A mother is soft to her child and terrible to anything that threatens it. Next the gods name the 21 forms in which she lives in every being.
Gentlest and fiercest, at the same time.

VERSES 6–8 · THE MIND’S THREE POWERS

SHE IS IN ALL BEINGS AS…

6. VIṢṆUMĀYĀ · Viṣṇu’s māyā

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu viṣṇumāyeti śabditā

Called Viṣṇumāyā: the power that makes the One appear as the many. In chapter 1 she even put Viṣṇu himself to sleep.

7. CETANĀ · consciousness

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu cetanety abhidhīyate

Called consciousness: the awareness by which you are reading this right now.

8. BUDDHI · intelligence

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu buddhirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As intelligence: the power to understand, weigh and decide.

after each: namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ

“To her: salutations, salutations, salutations, again and again.”

Two names, then a pattern. For the first two the gods say “she is called”: Viṣṇumāyā, consciousness. From buddhi on, the line becomes “she dwells in the form of…”. Nineteen times more.

Māyā, awareness, understanding: the light of the mind is the Mother.

VERSES 9–11 · THE BODY’S NEEDS

IN SLEEP, IN HUNGER, IN SHADOW

9. NIDRĀ · sleep

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu nidrārūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As sleep: the rest that restores us each night. Even switching off is her.

10. KṢUDHĀ · hunger

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu kṣudhārūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As hunger: the drive that keeps every creature alive. That is why food is offered first to her.

11. CHĀYĀ · shadow, shade

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu chāyārūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As shadow: the reflection of all things, and the shade that shelters the tired.

after each: namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ

“To her: salutations, salutations, salutations, again and again.”

The ordinary is holy. No grand qualities here: sleep, hunger, shade. The hymn finds the Goddess in what every animal shares.

Every night’s sleep and every meal: she is there too.

VERSES 12–14 · ENERGY AND ITS USE

POWER, DESIRE, PATIENCE

12. ŚAKTI · power

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śaktirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As power: the energy in every movement, every growing seed, every heartbeat.

13. TṚṢṆĀ · thirst, desire

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu tṛṣṇārūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As thirst and desire. Even longing is her; what matters is where we turn it.

14. KṢĀNTI · patience

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu kṣāntirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As forbearance: the strength to bear and forgive without hitting back.

after each: namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ

“To her: salutations, salutations, salutations, again and again.”

Thirst between power and patience. Desire sits between energy and forbearance: the force that moves us, and the strength to hold it.

Power and patience: both are her strength.

VERSES 15–17 · HOW WE LIVE WITH OTHERS

BIRTH, MODESTY, PEACE

15. JĀTI · birth, kind

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu jātirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As birth: the kind of life each being is born into, bird, fish, tree or human.

16. LAJJĀ · modesty

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu lajjārūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As modesty: the inner sense of shame that stops us before we do wrong.

17. ŚĀNTI · peace

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śāntirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As peace: the stillness that remains when the storm inside settles.

after each: namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ

“To her: salutations, salutations, salutations, again and again.”

From birth to peace. We are born into a kind; modesty keeps us decent among others; peace is where it leads.

She is in our birth, our conscience and our peace.

VERSES 18–20 · WHAT MAKES LIFE BRIGHT

FAITH, RADIANCE, FORTUNE

18. ŚRADDHĀ · faith

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śraddhārūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As faith: the trust that lets us take the next step before we can see the end of the road.

19. KĀNTI · radiance, beauty

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu kāntirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As radiance: the glow of beauty in a face, a flower, a sunrise.

20. LAKṢMĪ · fortune

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu lakṣmīrūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As Lakṣmī: prosperity, well-being, everything that makes a home flourish.

after each: namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ

“To her: salutations, salutations, salutations, again and again.”

Lakṣmī among the forms. The goddess of fortune is named as one form of the one Devī: in this hymn, every goddess is her.

Faith, beauty, prosperity: gifts of one Mother.

VERSES 21–23 · THE MIND AT WORK

WORK, MEMORY, COMPASSION

21. VṚTTI · livelihood, activity

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu vṛttirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As activity: our work and livelihood, and the ceaseless movement of the mind.

22. SMṚTI · memory

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu smṛtirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As memory: what we hold of the past, and the remembering of God.

23. DAYĀ · compassion

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu dayārūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As compassion: the urge to help when another suffers.

after each: namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ

“To her: salutations, salutations, salutations, again and again.”

Smṛti. Memory also means remembering God. The gods are doing just that, in this hymn, in their hour of need.

Our work, our memory, our kindness: all her forms.

VERSES 24–26 · THE BOLDEST ENDING

CONTENTMENT, MOTHER, ERROR

24. TUṢṬI · contentment

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu tuṣṭirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As contentment: the quiet joy of “this is enough”.

25. MĀTṚ · mother

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu mātṛrūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As the mother: in every mother, of every creature.

26. BHRĀNTI · error, confusion

yā devī sarvabhūteṣu bhrāntirūpeṇa saṃsthitā

As error and confusion. Even our mistakes happen within her: there is nowhere she is not.

after each: namas tasyai namas tasyai namas tasyai namo namaḥ

“To her: salutations, salutations, salutations, again and again.”

Why end with error? After the mother comes bhrānti, confusion. Nothing is outside the Goddess, not even our mistakes. That is the hymn’s most daring claim.

From the mother to our mistakes: nothing is outside her.

VERSE 27

RULER OF THE SENSES
indriyāṇām adhiṣṭhātrībhūtānāṃ cākhileṣu yā |bhūteṣu satataṃ tasyaivyāptyai devyai namo namaḥ ‖27‖
She who presides over the senses of all beings, and who always pervades all beings: to her, the all-pervading Devī, salutations again and again.
She rules every sense of every creature and fills them all. We bow to the Goddess who is everywhere.
WordMeans
indriyāṇāmof the senses
adhiṣṭhātrīpresiding ruler
akhileṣu bhūteṣuin all beings
satatamalways
vyāptyai devyaito the all-pervading Goddess
Inside every sense. Seeing, hearing, tasting: none of it works on its own. The gods now sum up the 21 forms: she is the power behind every sense, and she fills every being.
Every sense works by her power.
Some editions read vyāptidevyai as one word.

VERSE 28

CONSCIOUSNESS FILLING THE WORLD
citirūpeṇa yā kṛtsnametad vyāpya sthitā jagat |namas tasyai namas tasyainamas tasyai namo namaḥ ‖28‖
She who, in the form of consciousness, pervades this whole world and abides: salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations again and again.
She fills the whole universe as pure consciousness. We bow to her, again and again.
WordMeans
citi-rūpeṇain the form of consciousness
kṛtsnam etad jagatthis whole world
vyāpyapervading
sthitāabides
From cetanā to citi. The forms began with cetanā, consciousness in each being. They end with citi, consciousness filling the whole universe. What is in you is what is everywhere.
The awareness in you fills the whole world.

THE HYMN ON ONE PAGE

28 verses in five lines
The setting. Driven from heaven by Śumbha and Niśumbha, the gods remember the Goddess’s promise and praise her on the Himālaya.
Verses 1–5. They bow to her as Nature, Gaurī, moonlight, fortune and misfortune, Durgā who carries us across, the gentlest and the fiercest.
Verses 6–14. She is in every being as Viṣṇu’s māyā, awareness, intelligence, sleep, hunger, shadow, power, desire, patience.
Verses 15–26. As birth, modesty, peace, faith, radiance, fortune, work, memory, compassion, contentment, the mother, and even error.
Verses 27–28. Ruler of every sense, she fills the whole world as consciousness. Then Ambikā steps out of Pārvatī to answer them.
