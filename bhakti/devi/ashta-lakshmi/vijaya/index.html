VIJAYA LAKṢMĪ

AṢṬA LAKṢMĪ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Lakṣmī of victory

FOREWORD

Vijaya means victory. Vijaya Lakṣmī, also called Jaya Lakṣmī, is the Goddess of success: winning a battle, a case, an exam or a hard inner struggle.

This guide follows victory through Indian tradition: Vijayadaśamī, Mysuru’s great Dasara, Arjuna whose name was Vijaya, the royal Lakṣmī on Gupta coins, and Karnataka’s own “city of victory”, Vijayanagara.

Jai Vijaya Lakṣmī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Victory over what?

PROF. BOLT: Over enemies, over obstacles, and over yourself.

Part of Aṣṭa Lakṣmī, the eight forms of Lakṣmī. Previous: Santāna Lakṣmī. Next: Vidyā Lakṣmī.

AT A GLANCE

Vijaya Lakṣmī on one page
NameVijaya Lakṣmī, also Jaya Lakṣmī: “Lakṣmī of victory”
GiftSuccess, victory and overcoming obstacles
FormRed sari; eight arms with discus, conch, sword, shield, lotus and noose; boon and fear-not gestures
SeatedOften on a lotus, sometimes shown with an elephant
Prayed toBefore contests, exams, interviews and court cases

ZAPPY: Is it OK to pray to win?

PROF. BOLT: For a fair fight, yes. Victory over wrong is her gift.

Vijaya Lakṣmī is close to Dhairya Lakṣmī, courage, and to Gaja Lakṣmī, royal power. Where courage is the means, victory is the result. Her weapons are those of the warrior Goddess.

She is prayed to before any contest, from a cricket match to a court hearing. Tradition adds a condition: she blesses fair fights.

VIJAYADAŚAMĪ

The day of victory
WhenThe tenth day of Āśvina’s bright fortnight, after Navarātri
StoriesRāma’s victory over Rāvaṇa; Durgā’s over Mahiṣa; the Pāṇḍavas taking back their weapons from the śamī tree
ŚamīLeaves of the śamī tree are exchanged as “gold”
New beginningsChildren start school (vidyārambha); work and journeys begin

Vijayadaśamī gathers several victory stories into one day. In the Mahābhārata tradition, the Pāṇḍavas hid their weapons in a śamī tree during their year in disguise and recovered them on this day before the battle against the Kauravas’ cattle raid.

In Karnataka and Maharashtra, śamī (bannī) leaves are exchanged as “gold” with greetings. The day is also auspicious for beginning studies and new ventures.

MYSURU DASARA

Karnataka’s great victory festival
Nāḍa HabbaKarnataka’s state festival
CāmuṇḍeśvarīThe Goddess of Mysuru, slayer of Mahiṣa, is taken in a golden howdah on an elephant
Jambū SavārīThe great procession on Vijayadaśamī
SinceKept with royal splendour since the Vijayanagara and Wodeyar kings

ZAPPY: Why Mysuru?

PROF. BOLT: Mahiṣa's town, by tradition: Mahiṣūru. Victory over him is the city's story.

Mysuru Dasara is Karnataka’s Nāḍa Habba, state festival. Its climax, the Jambū Savārī, carries Goddess Cāmuṇḍeśvarī in a golden howdah on an elephant through the city on Vijayadaśamī, followed by the torchlight parade.

The festival’s royal style continues the Mahānavamī celebrations of the Vijayanagara kings, which foreign visitors to Hampi described in wonder in the 16th century.

ARJUNA, “VIJAYA”

The victorious one
NameOne of Arjuna’s ten names is Vijaya, because he always won
GītāArjuna says: I want no victory, Kṛṣṇa (na kāṅkṣe vijayaṃ, 1.32)
Last verseThe Gītā ends: where Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are, there are Śrī, victory (vijaya), prosperity and justice (18.78)
MeaningVictory follows dharma and skill together
See: Gītā 18.78

In the Virāṭa Parva, Arjuna explains his ten names to Prince Uttara. He is Vijaya because he never returned from battle without winning.

The Gītā’s last verse, spoken by Sañjaya, names victory with Śrī: where Kṛṣṇa, the master of yoga, and Arjuna, the archer, are together, there are fortune, victory, prosperity and justice. It is often taken as the Gītā’s own blessing.

RĀJA LAKṢMĪ

Fortune of kings
KingshipLakṣmī was the king’s fortune: if she left, the kingdom fell
Gupta coinsGold coins show the king with Lakṣmī on the reverse
CoronationThe abhiṣeka bath, like Gaja Lakṣmī’s
Vijaya and RājyaSome lists name Rājya Lakṣmī; others fold her into Vijaya

In classical Indian political thought, the king’s fortune, rājalakṣmī or rājyaśrī, was a goddess who chose a king and could abandon him. Poets wrote of her as a fickle beloved who must be kept by righteous rule.

Gupta gold coins, among the finest of ancient India, often show Lakṣmī on the reverse, sometimes holding a cornucopia or lotus, as the king’s fortune.

VIJAYANAGARA

Karnataka’s city of victory
Name“City of Victory”, capital at Hampi from the 14th to 16th century
FoundedBy Harihara and Bukka, traditionally with the blessing of the sage Vidyāraṇya
GodsVirūpākṣa its guardian; Lakṣmī Narasiṃha, Viṭhṭhala and Rāma among its great shrines
LegacyThe Mysuru Dasara continues its Mahānavamī tradition

Vijayanagara, founded in the 14th century, became one of the great cities of the world. Its name announced its purpose. Its temples at Hampi, Virūpākṣa, Viṭhṭhala, Hazāra Rāma and the giant monolithic Lakṣmī Narasiṃha, are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The Wodeyars of Mysuru, once Vijayanagara’s feudatories, carried on its Dasara, which is why Mysuru’s celebration has imperial roots.

HER VERSE

In the Aṣṭalakṣmī Stotram
ThemeHer verse calls her victorious, worshipped by all, giver of knowledge and liberation
ToneTriumphant, like a victory song
RefrainIt asks her to protect the singer always
UseBefore exams, contests and new ventures

Her verse in the Stotram is triumphant, with drums and conches in its sounds. Students often recite it on exam mornings.

We describe rather than reproduce it.

THE INNER VICTORY

Over oneself
Gītā 6.6The self is friend to one who has conquered himself
Anger and greedThe six inner enemies are the hardest foes
JayaThe epic itself was first called Jaya, “Victory”
Quiet winsKeeping a promise, breaking a bad habit

ZAPPY: Is that also Vijaya Lakṣmī?

PROF. BOLT: The biggest victory of all, the teachers say.

The Gītā says that for one who has conquered the self, the self is a friend; for one who has not, it acts like an enemy (6.6). The traditional six inner enemies, lust, anger, greed, delusion, pride and envy, are the hardest to defeat.

The Mahābhārata itself, scholars note, was once known as Jaya, “Victory”, and its invocation verse still ends with that word.

WHAT SHE TEACHES

Win rightly
Effort and graceVictory needs both
Fair meansVictory by wrong means is not her gift
Humility in winningRāma honoured Rāvaṇa in death
BeginningsEvery new start is a small victory

Vijaya Lakṣmī teaches that victory is a gift and a responsibility: won through effort and grace, by fair means, and carried with humility. Rāma ordered full funeral honours for Rāvaṇa: enmity ends with death.

Every beginning, a new school year, a new job, is a small victory, which is why Vijayadaśamī is a day for starting things.
