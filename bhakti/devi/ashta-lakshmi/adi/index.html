ĀDI LAKṢMĪ

AṢṬA LAKṢMĪ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The first, the source of all fortune

FOREWORD

Ādi Lakṣmī means “the first” or “primordial Lakṣmī”. She is not one particular blessing but the Goddess as she always is, inseparable from Viṣṇu, before and beyond any of the gifts the other seven bring.

This guide tells who she is: her birth as the sage Bhṛgu’s daughter, her place in Śrīvaiṣṇava theology as the one who pleads for us, and her presence on Viṣṇu’s chest.

ZAPPY: Why is she 'first'?

PROF. BOLT: She is Lakṣmī before any gift: the Goddess herself, always with Viṣṇu.

AT A GLANCE

Ādi Lakṣmī on one page
NameĀdi Lakṣmī, “the primordial Lakṣmī”; also Mahālakṣmī, Ramā
GiftNot one gift but the source: grace and union with the Divine
FormFour arms, holding a lotus and a white flag; the others in boon and fear-not gestures; often beside Viṣṇu
ColourUsually pink-red sari on a lotus
Place in the eightFirst
Seen atShrines of the Aṣṭalakṣmī temples; every Viṣṇu temple as his consort

ZAPPY: A flag?

PROF. BOLT: A white flag: a sign of peace and of her rule. The lotus is her own.

Of the eight, Ādi Lakṣmī is the hardest to picture as a “gift” because she is the giver. Many temples show her with Viṣṇu or with his symbols, to make the point that she is not a separate goddess of a single blessing but Śrī herself.

In some lists she is replaced or joined by Mahālakṣmī; in others she is called Ramā, “the delightful”. All these names point to the same idea: the Lakṣmī behind all Lakṣmīs.

BHṚGU’S DAUGHTER

Her first birth
Viṣṇu PurāṇaLakṣmī was born to the sage Bhṛgu and his wife Khyāti, so she is called Bhārgavī
LaterLost through Durvāsā’s curse, she rose again from the ocean of milk
AlwaysThe Purāṇa says she is eternal: born again whenever Viṣṇu descends
AvatārasPadmā with Vāmana, Dharaṇī with Paraśurāma, Sītā with Rāma, Rukmiṇī with Kṛṣṇa

The Viṣṇu Purāṇa explains Lakṣmī’s two births: first as daughter of Bhṛgu and Khyāti, then, after Durvāsā’s curse, from the churned ocean. The text reconciles them by saying she is eternal, like Viṣṇu, and simply appears in new ways.

Her name Bhārgavī, “Bhṛgu’s daughter”, is common in hymns. It also ties her to one of the great sage families, the Bhārgavas, who include Paraśurāma.

INSEPARABLE

Viṣṇu and Śrī
Viṣṇu PurāṇaAs he is meaning, she is speech; as he is the sun, she is its light
ŚrīvatsaThe mark on Viṣṇu’s chest where she dwells
Śrīnivāsa“Abode of Śrī”: Viṣṇu’s name at Tirumala
Lakṣmī-NārāyaṇaThe pair worshipped together in countless temples

ZAPPY: So she's never apart from him?

PROF. BOLT: Never, the texts say. Even when they seem apart in a story, it's play.

The images of speech and meaning, light and sun, are classic. They say that Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī are not two separate beings who happen to be married, but two aspects of one reality, as a word and its sense cannot be pulled apart.

Śrīvaiṣṇavas name their tradition after her, and say Viṣṇu is never worshipped without Śrī. The śrīvatsa mark on images of Viṣṇu is her seat on his chest.

IN THE PĀÑCARĀTRA

Śrī as Viṣṇu’s power
Lakṣmī TantraA Pāñcarātra text in which Lakṣmī herself teaches Indra
ŚaktiShe is Viṣṇu’s śakti, his creative power; through her he acts
Six qualitiesKnowledge, lordship, power, strength, valour and splendour belong to both
ĀdiIn this view the “first Lakṣmī” is this cosmic power

The Pāñcarātra Āgamas are the ritual and theological texts behind most Vaiṣṇava temple worship in South India. In the Lakṣmī Tantra she describes herself as the power by which Viṣṇu creates, sustains and dissolves the world.

This Ādi or primordial Lakṣmī is close to the Śākta idea of the Great Goddess, but always paired with Viṣṇu. The Kamalā of the Mahāvidyās, by contrast, sits alone.

THE ONE WHO PLEADS

Puruṣakāra in Śrīvaiṣṇavism
RoleŚrī intercedes with Viṣṇu for the soul; she is the merciful mediator
RāmānujaHis Śaraṇāgati Gadya begins with surrender to Śrī before Nārāyaṇa
Dvaya mantra“I take refuge in the feet of Nārāyaṇa, with Śrī”
DebateThe Vaḍakalai and Teṅkalai schools differ on whether she can grant liberation herself or only mediate

ZAPPY: Why go through her?

PROF. BOLT: Like asking a mother to speak to a father. She is pure mercy.

Puruṣakāra, the mediator, is central to Śrīvaiṣṇava devotion. The soul, burdened with faults, approaches the Lord through his consort, whose compassion softens his justice. The Dvaya mantra, at the heart of the tradition, names Śrī with Nārāyaṇa.

The two later Śrīvaiṣṇava schools, Vaḍakalai and Teṅkalai, differ on whether Lakṣmī is fully equal to Viṣṇu and can grant liberation herself, or is the supreme mediator only. We present both without choosing.

PADMĀVATĪ OF TIRUMALA

Ādi Lakṣmī on earth
StoryWhen Lakṣmī left Vaikuṇṭha after Bhṛgu kicked Viṣṇu’s chest, Viṣṇu came to earth as Śrīnivāsa
MarriageHe married Padmāvatī, born on a lotus to King Ākāśa Rāja
TiruchānūrHer temple near Tirupati; pilgrims visit her before or after the Lord
DebtBy legend Śrīnivāsa borrowed from Kubera for the wedding; devotees help repay it

ZAPPY: Bhṛgu kicked Viṣṇu?

PROF. BOLT: To test which god was greatest. Viṣṇu forgave; Lakṣmī was offended, since his chest is her home.

The Tirumala legends, in the Veṅkaṭācala Māhātmya and later local texts, tell that the sage Bhṛgu, testing the gods, kicked Viṣṇu on the chest, where Lakṣmī lives. Viṣṇu apologised to the sage; Lakṣmī, hurt, left for earth. Viṣṇu followed as Śrīnivāsa.

Padmāvatī, born on a lotus, is taken as a form of Lakṣmī. Her temple at Tiruchānūr draws huge crowds, and a common saying is that a pilgrimage to Tirumala is not complete without visiting her.

HOW SHE IS WORSHIPPED

Ways to honour her
Śrī SūktaRecited at her abhiṣeka
Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa pūjāWith Viṣṇu, especially on Ekādaśī and Fridays
LampA ghee lamp at dusk at the threshold, for her to enter
Clean homeTradition: Ādi Lakṣmī enters a clean, peaceful home

Ādi Lakṣmī worship is simply Lakṣmī worship in its fullest form. The Śrī Sūkta is recited during her abhiṣeka in temples, and many homes keep a small Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa image for daily prayer.

Lighting a lamp at the doorway at dusk, an old custom across India, is said to welcome Lakṣmī into the house as the day ends.

WHAT SHE TEACHES

Grace before gifts
Source firstSeek the giver, not just the gifts
PartnershipŚrī and Viṣṇu: power and goodness together
MercyShe pleads for the undeserving
PeaceThe white flag: fortune grows in peace

ZAPPY: What's the lesson?

PROF. BOLT: The other seven are gifts. She is the one who gives.

The lesson of the first Lakṣmī is about order: if the relationship with the source is right, the gifts follow. Chasing the gifts alone, the other seven, can lead to Alakṣmī.

Her pairing with Viṣṇu also teaches balance: prosperity joined to goodness. Wealth without dharma is not Lakṣmī.
