DEVĪ KAVACAM

DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 1 · THE NINE DURGĀS
The Goddess’s armour, verses 1–17 · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: This series follows the Devī Māhātmya in the order it is chanted, in 19 parts. Part 1 starts with the Devī Kavacam: first verses 1–17 for chanting, then the story, and one page for each verse or group, with the Nine Durgās and the Mothers each on a page of their own.

FOREWORD

The Devī Māhātmya, also called the Durgā Saptaśatī or Caṇḍī Pāṭha, is the great scripture of the Goddess: 13 chapters of the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa, traditionally counted as 700 mantras. Through every Navarātri it is chanted in temples and homes across India.

Before the story begins, the reader puts on armour. The Devī Kavacam, “the Goddess’s armour”, asks her powers to guard every direction and every part of the body. It opens with one of the best-loved lists in Hindu worship: the Nine Durgās of the nine nights.

This series will take you through the whole text, in the order it is chanted.

THE STORY · HOW THE TEXT IS CHANTED

Three limbs, thirteen chapters, three secrets
The recitation in 5 frames
The core. The Devī Māhātmya proper is chapters 81–93 of the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa: 13 chapters, three great battles of the Goddess.
The three limbs (aṅgas). Before it, readers chant the Kavacam (armour), the Argalā (the bolt) and the Kīlakam (the pin).
Who speaks. In the Kavacam, the sage Mārkaṇḍeya asks Brahmā, the creator, for the most secret protection.
The 13 chapters. The story of King Suratha, the merchant Samādhi and the sage Medhas, who tells them of the Goddess.
The three secrets (rahasyas). After the text come three short “secret” teachings on her forms.

ZAPPY: Is the Kavacam in the Purāṇa?

PROF. BOLT: Not in its 13 chapters. It belongs to the recitation tradition around it, like the Argalā and Kīlakam, and is chanted as part of the whole.

VERSE 1 · MĀRKAṆḌEYA ASKS

TELL ME THE SECRET
yad guhyaṃ paramaṃ lokesarvarakṣākaraṃ nṛṇām |yan na kasyacid ākhyātaṃtan me brūhi pitāmaha ‖1‖
Mārkaṇḍeya said: O Grandsire, tell me that which is supremely secret in the world, which gives every protection to people, and which you have told to no one.
The sage asks Brahmā: tell me the great secret that protects people completely, the one you have never shared.
WordMeans
guhyam paramamthe supreme secret
sarva-rakṣā-karamgiving all protection
nṛṇāmfor people
na kasyacit ākhyātamtold to no one
pitāmahaGrandsire: Brahmā
Where the Kavacam comes from. It is not part of the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa’s 13 chapters. It is one of three hymns, the aṅgas or “limbs”, chanted before the Devī Māhātmya: Kavacam, Argalā, Kīlakam. Here the sage Mārkaṇḍeya, narrator of the Purāṇa, asks Brahmā for it.
The recitation begins with a request for protection.

VERSE 2 · BRAHMĀ ANSWERS

THE GODDESS’S ARMOUR
asti guhyatamaṃ viprasarvabhūtopakārakam |devyās tu kavacaṃ puṇyaṃtac chṛṇuṣva mahāmune ‖2‖
Brahmā said: There is, O brahmin, a most secret thing that helps all beings: the holy armour of the Devī. Listen to it, great sage.
Brahmā replies: yes, there is such a secret, and it helps every creature. It is the Goddess’s own armour. Listen.
WordMeans
guhyatamammost secret
vipraO brahmin
sarva-bhūta-upakārakamhelping all beings
kavacamarmour, a protective shield
puṇyamholy, meritorious
śṛṇuṣvalisten!
What a kavaca is. A kavaca is armour. A kavaca hymn “puts on” the deity limb by limb, asking a different power to guard each part of the body. Part 2 of this series covers that head-to-foot armour.
The Goddess’s protection is armour you put on by chanting.

VERSES 3–5 · THE NINE DURGĀS

NINE NAMES, NINE NIGHTS
prathamaṃ śailaputrī cadvitīyaṃ brahmacāriṇī |tṛtīyaṃ candraghaṇṭetikūṣmāṇḍeti caturthakam ‖3‖pañcamaṃ skandamātetiṣaṣṭhaṃ kātyāyanīti ca |saptamaṃ kālarātrītimahāgaurīti cāṣṭamam ‖4‖navamaṃ siddhidātrī canavadurgāḥ prakīrtitāḥ |uktāny etāni nāmānibrahmaṇaiva mahātmanā ‖5‖
First is Śailaputrī, second Brahmacāriṇī, third Candraghaṇṭā, fourth Kūṣmāṇḍā; fifth Skandamātā, sixth Kātyāyanī, seventh Kālarātri, eighth Mahāgaurī; and ninth Siddhidātrī. These are proclaimed the Nine Durgās. These names were spoken by the great Brahmā himself.
WordMeans
nava-durgāḥthe Nine Durgās
prakīrtitāḥare proclaimed
uktāni etāni nāmānithese names were spoken
brahmaṇā evaby Brahmā himself
The source of the nine nights. These three verses are the classic list of the Navadurgā, the nine forms worshipped one per night of Navarātri, especially in North India. The next page meets all nine.
Nine forms, one for each night of Navarātri.

VERSES 6–7 · NO HARM COMES

IN FIRE, IN BATTLE, IN FEAR
agninā dahyamānas tuśatrumadhye gato raṇe |viṣame durgame caivabhayārtāḥ śaraṇaṃ gatāḥ ‖6‖na teṣāṃ jāyate kiṃcidaśubhaṃ raṇasaṅkaṭe |nāpadaṃ tasya paśyāmiśokaduḥkhabhayaṃ na hi ‖7‖
One being burned by fire, one fallen among enemies in battle, one in a dangerous or impassable place, those stricken with fear who take refuge in her: nothing inauspicious befalls them in the crisis of battle. I see no calamity for such a one; no grief, no pain, no fear.
WordMeans
agninā dahyamānaḥbeing burned by fire
śatru-madhye raṇeamong enemies in battle
viṣame durgamein danger, in places hard to cross
bhaya-ārtāḥstricken with fear
śaraṇaṃ gatāḥwho have taken refuge
śoka-duḥkha-bhayamgrief, pain, fear
Refuge is the key word. The promise is not for the brave or the strong but for the bhaya-ārta, those “stricken with fear”, who simply turn to her. Notice durgame: “a place hard to cross”, the very root of the name Durgā.
Those who turn to her in fear are not left alone.

VERSE 8 · THOSE WHO REMEMBER

REMEMBER HER, AND GROW
yais tu bhaktyā smṛtā nūnaṃteṣāṃ vṛddhiḥ prajāyate |ye tvāṃ smaranti deveśirakṣase tān na saṃśayaḥ ‖8‖
For those who remember her with devotion, growth surely comes. O Queen of the gods, those who remember you, you protect them: there is no doubt.
Remember her with love and you will flourish. Goddess, you guard all who remember you; of that there is no doubt.
WordMeans
bhaktyā smṛtāremembered with devotion
vṛddhiḥgrowth, prosperity
prajāyatearises
deveśiO Queen of the gods
rakṣaseyou protect
na saṃśayaḥno doubt
Just remember. No complicated ritual is asked for: smṛtā, remembered. It echoes her own promise in the Devī Māhātmya (chapter 4): call on me in distress and I will come. Mid-verse, Brahmā turns and speaks to her directly: “you protect them”.
Remember her with love: that is enough.

VERSES 9–11 · THE MOTHERS ARRIVE

EACH ON HER MOUNT
pretasaṃsthā tu cāmuṇḍāvārāhī mahiṣāsanā |aindrī gajasamārūḍhāvaiṣṇavī garuḍāsanā ‖9‖māheśvarī vṛṣārūḍhākaumārī śikhivāhanā |lakṣmīḥ padmāsanā devīpadmahastā haripriyā ‖10‖śvetarūpadharā devīīśvarī vṛṣavāhanā |brāhmī haṃsasamārūḍhāsarvābharaṇabhūṣitā ‖11‖
Cāmuṇḍā seated on a corpse, Vārāhī seated on a buffalo, Aindrī mounted on an elephant, Vaiṣṇavī seated on Garuḍa; Māheśvarī mounted on a bull, Kaumārī riding a peacock; the goddess Lakṣmī seated on a lotus, lotus in hand, beloved of Hari; the white-formed goddess Īśvarī riding a bull; Brāhmī mounted on a swan, adorned with every ornament.
WordMeans
preta-saṃsthāseated on a corpse
mahiṣa-āsanāseated on a buffalo
gaja-samārūḍhāmounted on an elephant
śikhi-vāhanāwhose vehicle is the peacock
The Mothers (Mātṛkās). Each is the power (śakti) of a god, riding his mount: Aindrī of Indra, Vaiṣṇavī of Viṣṇu, Māheśvarī of Śiva, Kaumārī of Kumāra, Brāhmī of Brahmā, Vārāhī of the Boar. In chapter 8 they leave the gods’ bodies to fight beside the Devī. Meet them on the next page.
The gods’ own powers, as goddesses, come to guard us.

VERSES 12–15 · ARMED FOR US

SIXTEEN WEAPONS
ityetā mātaraḥ sarvāḥsarvayogasamanvitāḥ |nānābharaṇaśobhāḍhyānānāratnopaśobhitāḥ ‖12‖dṛśyante ratham ārūḍhādevyaḥ krodhasamākulāḥ |śaṅkhaṃ cakraṃ gadāṃ śaktiṃhalaṃ ca musalāyudham ‖13‖kheṭakaṃ tomaraṃ caivaparaśuṃ pāśam eva ca |kuntāyudhaṃ triśūlaṃ caśārṅgam āyudham uttamam ‖14‖daityānāṃ dehanāśāyabhaktānām abhayāya ca |dhārayanty āyudhānītthaṃdevānāṃ ca hitāya vai ‖15‖
All these Mothers, endowed with every power of yoga, rich in many ornaments and shining with many jewels, are seen mounted on chariots, the goddesses full of wrath. Conch, discus, mace, spear, plough and pestle; shield, javelin, axe and noose; lance, trident and the excellent bow Śārṅga: they hold these weapons to destroy the bodies of demons, to free their devotees from fear, and for the good of the gods.
Wrath on our side. The Mothers are fierce, but the verse says why: to destroy what harms, so that devotees need not fear.
Every weapon is held so that we need not fear.

VERSE 16 · SALUTATIONS

GREAT AND TERRIBLE
namas te’stu mahāraudremahāghoraparākrame |mahābale mahotsāhemahābhayavināśini ‖16‖
Salutations to you, O greatly fierce one, of terrifying valour; of great strength, of great energy, destroyer of great fear.
We bow to you, mighty and fierce, unstoppable in strength and courage, who destroys our greatest fears.
WordMeans
mahā-raudreO greatly fierce one
mahā-ghora-parākrameof terrifying valour
mahā-baleof great strength
mahā-utsāheof great energy, zeal
mahā-bhaya-vināśinidestroyer of great fear
Five times “great”. Mahā rings through the line: fierce, terrible, strong, energetic. And it ends where the hymn always ends: she is fierce so that she can destroy our fear.
Her fierceness destroys our fear.

VERSE 17 · THE ARMOUR BEGINS

EAST AND SOUTH-EAST
trāhi māṃ devi duṣprekṣyeśatrūṇāṃ bhayavardhini |prācyāṃ rakṣatu mām aindrīāgneyyām agnidevatā ‖17‖
Protect me, O Devī, you who are hard to look upon, who increase the fear of enemies. In the east may Aindrī protect me; in the south-east, the goddess of fire.
Guard me, Goddess too bright to look at, terror of all enemies. Let Aindrī guard my east, and the fire goddess my south-east.
WordMeans
trāhi māmprotect me
duṣprekṣyeO you hard to look upon
śatrūṇāṃ bhaya-vardhiniincreaser of enemies’ fear
prācyāmin the east
āgneyyāmin the south-east
agni-devatāthe goddess of fire
From here, the armour. The prayer now turns to space itself: one goddess for each of the ten directions, then one for every part of the body, head to foot. That is Part 2.
The armour starts: a guardian for every direction.

PART 1 ON ONE PAGE

Verses 1–17 in five lines
Verses 1–2. Mārkaṇḍeya asks Brahmā for the supreme secret of protection; Brahmā gives him the Goddess’s armour.
Verses 3–5. The Nine Durgās, Śailaputrī to Siddhidātrī: the goddesses of the nine nights.
