THE GODDESS IS BORN

DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 7 · CHAPTER 2
From the light of all the gods · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The story in comic panels, and the key ślokas of chapter 2 on their own pages: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 6, the first carita ended: Mahāmāyā held Viṣṇu in sleep, then woke him, and Madhu and Kaiṭabha fell.

Now the second carita begins, whose deity is Mahālakṣmī. A buffalo demon, Mahiṣa, has conquered heaven. The gods’ anger becomes light, the light becomes a woman, and every god arms her. This is the birth scene of the Goddess, loved across India.

With the blessings of the Devī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A goddess made of light?

PROF. BOLT: Made of all the gods' light. Each one gives his power, and together they become her. Read chapter 2 as a picture of what unity can do.

HEAVEN LOST

Ślokas 2.1–8
The story
The war. Gods and asuras fight for a full hundred years. Mahiṣa leads the asuras; Indra leads the gods.
The defeat. The gods lose. Mahiṣāsura becomes Indra and sits on the throne of heaven.
To the great gods. Led by Brahmā, the defeated gods go to Śiva and Viṣṇu (“the one with Garuḍa on his banner”).
The complaint. “He has taken over the offices of the Sun, Indra, Fire, Wind, Moon, Yama and Varuṇa. We wander the earth like mortals.”
The plea. “We have come to you for refuge. Think how he can be killed.”

ZAPPY: Why couldn't the great gods just fight him?

PROF. BOLT: Mahiṣa had a boon: in the usual telling, no man or god could kill him. Watch what the gods do instead. They give up their power to someone new.

Heaven is lost; the gods seek refuge.

ŚLOKAS 2.2–3

A HUNDRED YEARS OF WAR
devāsuramabhūdyuddhaṃ pūrṇamabdaśataṃ purā |mahiṣe’surāṇāmadhipe devānāṃ ca purandare ‖2‖tatrāsurairmahāvīryairdevasainyaṃ parājitam |jitvā ca sakalān devānindro’bhūnmahiṣāsuraḥ ‖3‖
Long ago there was a war between gods and asuras for a full hundred years, when Mahiṣa was lord of the asuras and Indra (Purandara) of the gods. In it the gods’ army was defeated by the mighty asuras, and having conquered all the gods, Mahiṣāsura became Indra.
WordMeans
deva-asuram yuddhamwar of gods and asuras
abda-śatama hundred years
purandaraIndra, “breaker of forts”
indraḥ abhūtbecame Indra: took the throne of heaven
The second carita begins. The deity of this episode is Mahālakṣmī. “Indra” is a post, not just a person: whoever wins heaven sits on its throne. Now a buffalo demon holds it.
Evil can win for a while; Mahiṣa sits on heaven’s throne.

ŚLOKAS 2.9–10

THE ANGER OF THE GODS
itthaṃ niśamya devānāṃ vacāṃsi madhusūdanaḥ |cakāra kopaṃ śambhuśca bhrukuṭīkuṭilānanau ‖9‖tato’tikopapūrṇasya cakriṇo vadanāttataḥ |niścakrāma mahattejo brahmaṇaḥ śaṅkarasya ca ‖10‖
Hearing these words of the gods, Madhusūdana (Viṣṇu) grew angry, and so did Śambhu (Śiva), their faces twisted with frowning brows. Then from the face of the discus-bearer, filled with great anger, came forth a great radiance, and from Brahmā and Śaṅkara too.
WordMeans
madhusūdanaḥslayer of Madhu: Viṣṇu (Part 6)
kopaanger
bhrukuṭīa frown of the brows
mahat tejaḥa great radiance
Anger turned into light. The gods tell Viṣṇu and Śiva everything (2.4–8). Their anger does not become a curse or a war; it becomes tejas, radiant power. Righteous anger, gathered together, becomes something new.
Righteous anger becomes radiant light.

ŚLOKAS 2.11–12

A MOUNTAIN OF LIGHT
anyeṣāṃ caiva devānāṃ śakrādīnāṃ śarīrataḥ |nirgataṃ sumahattejastaccaikyaṃ samagacchata ‖11‖atīva tejasaḥ kūṭaṃ jvalantamiva parvatam |dadṛśuste surāstatra jvālāvyāptadigantaram ‖12‖
From the bodies of the other gods, Śakra (Indra) and the rest, a very great radiance came forth, and it all became one. The gods saw there a peak of exceeding radiance, like a blazing mountain, its flames filling every direction.
WordMeans
śakra-ādīnāmof Indra and the others
aikyam samagacchatacame into oneness
tejasaḥ kūṭama peak of radiance
jvālā-vyāpta-digantaflames filling the horizons
Many lights, one fire. Each god gives his own power, and the powers merge. The Devī of this chapter is literally the combined strength of all the gods: no single god could defeat Mahiṣa, but together, as her, they can.
Every god’s power, merged into one.

ŚLOKA 2.13

THE LIGHT BECOMES A WOMAN
atulaṃ tatra tattejaḥ sarvadevaśarīrajam |ekasthaṃ tadabhūnnārī vyāptalokatrayaṃ tviṣā ‖13‖
That matchless radiance, born from the bodies of all the gods, gathered in one place and became a woman, pervading the three worlds with her lustre.
WordMeans
atulammatchless
sarva-deva-śarīra-jamborn of the bodies of all gods
ekasthamgathered in one
nārīa woman
tviṣāwith her lustre
The birth of the Goddess. This is the verse painted on a thousand Navarātri pandals. In chapter 1 she came out of Viṣṇu as sleep; here she is born as a woman of light, and the second carita’s great form, Mahālakṣmī, stands before the gods.
From all the gods’ light, one Goddess.

ŚLOKAS 2.14–15

A BODY MADE OF GODS · I
yadabhūcchāmbhavaṃ tejastenājāyata tanmukham |yāmyena cābhavan keśā bāhavo viṣṇutejasā ‖14‖saumyena stanayoryugmaṃ madhyaṃ caindreṇa cābhavat |vāruṇena ca jaṅghorū nitambastejasā bhuvaḥ ‖15‖
From Śiva’s radiance her face was formed; from Yama’s, her hair; from Viṣṇu’s radiance, her arms. From the Moon’s, her two breasts; from Indra’s, her waist; from Varuṇa’s, her legs and thighs; and from the Earth’s radiance, her hips.
WordMeans
śāmbhavaof Śambhu: Śiva
yāmyaof Yama, lord of death
saumyaof Soma, the Moon
aindra · vāruṇaof Indra · of Varuṇa
bhuvaḥ tejasāby the Earth’s radiance
A map of the body. Each part matches its giver: Śiva, the auspicious, gives the face; Yama, master of time, the dark hair; Viṣṇu, the protector, the many arms; the steady Earth, the hips. The Goddess is a living union of all divine powers.
Each god gives a part of her body.

ŚLOKAS 2.16–18

A BODY MADE OF GODS · II
brahmaṇastejasā pādau tadaṅgulyo’rkatejasā |vasūnāṃ ca karāṅgulyaḥ kaubereṇa ca nāsikā ‖16‖tasyāstu dantāḥ sambhūtāḥ prājāpatyena tejasā |nayanatritayaṃ jajñe tathā pāvakatejasā ‖17‖bhruvau ca sandhyayostejaḥ śravaṇāvanilasya ca |anyeṣāṃ caiva devānāṃ sambhavastejasāṃ śivā ‖18‖
From Brahmā’s radiance her feet; from the Sun’s, her toes; from the Vasus’, her fingers; from Kubera’s, her nose. Her teeth arose from the radiance of Prajāpati; her three eyes were born from the radiance of Fire. Her brows came from the radiance of the two twilights, her ears from the Wind’s; and from the radiance of the other gods too, the auspicious one came into being.
WordMeans
arkathe Sun
vasavaḥthe Vasus, eight gods of the elements
kauberaof Kubera, lord of wealth
nayana-tritayamthe three eyes
pāvakaFire
śivāthe auspicious one
Three eyes of fire. Agni gives her three eyes, like Śiva’s third eye: sun, moon and fire. Her brows come from the two twilights, dawn and dusk. Nothing in her is ordinary; every feature is a god.
Her three eyes are Agni’s fire.

EVERY GOD GIVES

Ślokas 2.19–31 · the full list
The story
Weapons. Śiva a trident, Viṣṇu a discus, Varuṇa a conch and noose, Agni a spear, Vāyu a bow and quivers, Indra a thunderbolt and bell, Yama a staff.
Prayer tools. Brahmā a rosary and water pot. The Sun puts his rays into her skin. Kāla (Time) gives a sword and a spotless shield.
Jewels. The milky ocean gives a necklace, unageing garments, a crest-jewel, earrings, bangles, a crescent moon ornament, armlets, anklets and rings.
More. Viśvakarmā, the divine craftsman, an axe, many missiles and unbreakable armour; the ocean lotus garlands; Śeṣa a serpent necklace set with jewels.
Mount and cup. Himavān gives the lion and jewels; Kubera a cup never empty. Honoured by all, she laughs aloud.

ZAPPY: Why does every god give something?

PROF. BOLT: Because victory needs everyone. Each gives his best, and none is diminished: Śiva and Viṣṇu draw new weapons out of their own. She carries the whole of heaven's strength.

All of heaven’s strength, in her hands.

ŚLOKAS 2.20–21

THE GIFTS · TRIDENT AND DISCUS
tato devā dadustasyai svāni svānyāyudhāni ca |śūlaṃ śūlādviniṣkṛṣya dadau tasyai pinākadhṛk ‖20‖cakraṃ ca dattavān kṛṣṇaḥ samutpāṭya svacakrataḥ |śaṅkhaṃ ca varuṇaḥ śaktiṃ dadau tasyai hutāśanaḥ ‖21‖
Then the gods gave her each their own weapons. The bearer of Pināka (Śiva) drew a trident out of his own trident and gave it to her. Kṛṣṇa (Viṣṇu) gave her a discus, drawing it out of his own discus. Varuṇa gave her a conch, and Fire (Hutāśana) a spear.
WordMeans
svāni svāni āyudhānieach their own weapons
śūlāt viniṣkṛṣyadrawing it out of his trident
pināka-dhṛkholder of Pināka: Śiva’s bow
śaktiḥa spear, Agni’s lance
Weapon out of weapon. Śiva does not hand over his trident; he draws a new one out of it. Viṣṇu does the same with his discus. Giving to her loses nothing: the source stays full. It is the old Upaniṣadic idea: from fullness, fullness comes.
Weapons drawn out of the gods’ own weapons.

ŚLOKAS 2.22–23

THE GIFTS · BOW, THUNDERBOLT, BELL
māruto dattavāṃścāpaṃ bāṇapūrṇe tatheṣudhī |vajramindraḥ samutpāṭya kuliśādamarādhipaḥ ‖22‖dadau tasyai sahasrākṣo ghaṇṭāmairāvatādgajāt |kāladaṇḍādyamo daṇḍaṃ pāśaṃ cāmbupatirdadau ‖23‖
Māruta (the Wind) gave a bow and two quivers full of arrows. Indra, lord of the immortals, the thousand-eyed, drew a thunderbolt out of his own thunderbolt and gave it, and a bell from his elephant Airāvata. Yama gave a staff out of his staff of death, and the lord of waters (Varuṇa) a noose.
WordMeans
mārutathe Wind god
iṣudhītwo quivers
vajra · kuliśathunderbolt
sahasrākṣathousand-eyed: Indra
kāla-daṇḍathe staff of death
ambu-patilord of waters: Varuṇa
Why a bell? Among swords and thunderbolts sits Airāvata’s bell. Its sound will stun demons in the battle (2.57). It is also the bell rung in every pūjā to drive away what is not wanted.
Even the pūjā bell is one of her weapons.

ŚLOKA 2.24

THE GIFTS · ROSARY, WATER POT, SUNLIGHT
prajāpatiścākṣamālāṃ dadau brahmā kamaṇḍalum |samastaromakūpeṣu nijaraśmīn divākaraḥ ‖24‖
Brahmā, lord of creatures, gave her a rosary and a water pot. The Sun (Divākara) put his own rays into all the pores of her skin.
WordMeans
akṣa-mālāa rosary of beads
kamaṇḍalua water pot
roma-kūpathe pores of the skin
nija-raśmīnhis own rays
divākaramaker of the day: the Sun
Tools of the mind too. Not every gift is a weapon. The rosary and water pot are the tools of prayer and austerity. And the Sun does not hand her anything: he fills her with light from within.
She carries weapons and the rosary alike.

ŚLOKA 2.30

THE LION FROM THE HIMĀLAYA
himavān vāhanaṃ siṃhaṃ ratnāni vividhāni ca |dadāvaśūnyaṃ surayā pānapātraṃ dhanādhipaḥ ‖30‖
Himavān (the Himālaya) gave her a lion as her mount, and many kinds of jewels. The lord of wealth (Kubera) gave her a drinking cup ever full of wine.
WordMeans
himavānthe Himālaya, the mountain king
vāhanaa mount
siṃhalion
aśūnyamnever empty
dhana-adhipalord of wealth: Kubera
The lion. Her mount comes from the Himālaya, the mountain who is also, in other stories, father of Pārvatī. The lion is power under control: wild strength that carries the Goddess wherever she goes. Kubera’s cup will matter in chapter 3.
Her lion comes from the Himālaya.

ŚLOKAS 2.32–33

THE ROAR
anyairapi surairdevī bhūṣaṇairāyudhaistathā |sammānitā nanādoccaiḥ sāṭṭahāsaṃ muhurmuhuḥ ‖32‖tasyā nādena ghoreṇa kṛtsnamāpūritaṃ nabhaḥ |amāyatātimahatā pratiśabdo mahānabhūt ‖33‖
Honoured by the other gods too, with ornaments and weapons, the Devī roared aloud with laughter, again and again. The whole sky was filled with her terrible roar, and a great, immeasurable echo arose.
WordMeans
sammānitāhonoured
nanāda uccaiḥroared aloud
sa-aṭṭahāsamwith loud laughter
muhur muhuḥagain and again
pratiśabdaecho
Laughter as a battle cry. Fully armed, she does not ask for a plan. She laughs. It is the laugh of complete confidence, and it shakes the universe: the next verses say the oceans heaved and mountains swayed.
Her first sound is a fearless laugh.

ŚLOKAS 2.34–35

“JAYA!”
cukṣubhuḥ sakalā lokāḥ samudrāśca cakampire|cacāla vasudhā celuḥ sakalāśca mahīdharāḥ ‖34‖jayeti devāśca mudā tāmūcuḥ siṃhavāhinīm |tuṣṭuvurmunayaścaināṃ bhaktinamrātmamūrtayaḥ ‖35‖
All the worlds trembled, the oceans shook, the earth quaked, and all the mountains swayed. “Victory!” cried the gods in joy to her who rides the lion, and the sages praised her, their bodies bowed in devotion.
WordMeans
cukṣubhuḥwere shaken
vasudhāthe earth
mahīdharāḥmountains, “earth-holders”
jayavictory!
siṃha-vāhinīshe who rides the lion
Two reactions to one sound. The worlds shake in fear; the gods shout with joy. The same power is terror to one side and hope to the other. “Jaya” here is the first cheer of a cry still heard at every Navarātri: jaya mātā dī!
Terror for the asuras, joy for the gods.

ŚLOKAS 2.38–39

WHAT MAHIṢA SAW
sa dadarśa tato devīṃ vyāptalokatrayāṃ tviṣā |pādākrāntyā natabhuvaṃ kirīṭollikhitāmbarām ‖38‖kṣobhitāśeṣapātālāṃ dhanurjyāniḥsvanena tām |diśo bhujasahasreṇa samantādvyāpya saṃsthitām ‖39‖
Then he saw the Devī, pervading the three worlds with her lustre; the earth bending under the tread of her feet, her crown scraping the sky, the whole underworld shaken by the twang of her bowstring, standing with her thousand arms filling all the directions.
WordMeans
pāda-ākrāntyā nata-bhuvamthe earth bowed by her tread
kirīṭa-ullikhita-ambarāmher crown scraping the sky
pātālathe underworld
dhanur-jyā-niḥsvanathe twang of the bowstring
bhuja-sahasraa thousand arms
The cosmic form. Mahiṣa rushes in shouting “What is this?” (2.37), and sees a Goddess as big as the universe. Like Arjuna seeing Kṛṣṇa’s cosmic form in the Gītā, the sight should have been enough. Pride makes him fight anyway.
He saw a Goddess as big as the universe, and still fought.

ŚLOKAS 2.50–51

AS IF AT PLAY
sāpi devī tatastāni śastrāṇyastrāṇi caṇḍikā |līlayaiva praciccheda nijaśastrāstravarṣiṇī ‖50‖anāyastānanā devī stūyamānā surarṣibhiḥ |mumocāsuradeheṣu śastrāṇyastrāṇi ceśvarī ‖51‖
And Caṇḍikā, raining down her own weapons and missiles, cut all those weapons and missiles to pieces, as if in play. With an untroubled face, praised by gods and sages, the Ruler hurled weapons and missiles onto the bodies of the asuras.
WordMeans
caṇḍikāthe fierce one: a name of the Devī
līlayā evaas if in play
pracicchedacut to pieces
anāyasta-ānanāwith an untroubled face
śastra · astrahand weapons · thrown missiles
Līlā. The whole war is play to her. Her face shows no strain (anāyasta). This is a key idea: the Divine acts in the world effortlessly, without anxiety. The asuras fight desperately; she fights like someone at play.
She fights without strain, as play.

ŚLOKA 2.53

EVERY BREATH AN ARMY
niḥśvāsān mumuce yāṃśca yudhyamānā raṇe’mbikā |ta eva sadyaḥ sambhūtā gaṇāḥ śatasahasraśaḥ ‖53‖
The breaths that Ambikā breathed out as she fought in battle at once became her troops, in hundreds of thousands.
WordMeans
niḥśvāsaout-breath
ambikāMother
sadyaḥat once
gaṇāḥtroops, attendants
śata-sahasraśaḥby hundreds of thousands
She needs no allies. The gods gave her weapons, but her army comes from her own breath. Her gaṇas fight beside her and even play drums and conches “in that great festival of war” (2.55).
Her very breath becomes her army.

THE ARMIES FALL

Ślokas 2.40–69
The story
The generals. Cikṣura leads the asura army; Cāmara, Udagra, Mahāhanu, Asiloma, Bāṣkala, Biḍāla and others come with chariots, elephants and horses beyond counting.
Mahiṣa himself stands in the middle, surrounded by “thousands of crores of crores” of chariots, elephants and horses.
The Goddess answers. She cuts their weapons as if at play; her breaths become armies; her lion roams the enemy “like fire in a forest”.
A festival of war. Her gaṇas beat drums and blow conches. The bell stuns, the noose binds, the trident and mace cut down the asuras.
The end of the army. In a moment she destroys it all, as fire burns a great heap of grass and wood. The gods shower flowers from heaven.

ZAPPY: All those generals, and she beat them in a moment?

PROF. BOLT: Their numbers are huge on purpose: crores upon crores. The point is not arithmetic. However large the forces of ego, they are like dry grass before her fire.

However vast the army, dry grass before her fire.

PART 7 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 2 in five lines
Heaven lost (2.1–8). After a hundred years of war Mahiṣa becomes Indra; the gods go to Viṣṇu and Śiva for refuge.
The light (2.9–13). The gods’ anger becomes radiance, the radiance a blazing mountain, and the mountain a woman filling the three worlds.
The body (2.14–18). Each god gives a part of her: Śiva her face, Viṣṇu her arms, Agni her three eyes.
The gifts (2.19–35). Every god gives a weapon or jewel; Himavān the lion. She laughs, and the worlds shake. “Jaya!”
The battle (2.36–69). Mahiṣa sees her cosmic form; his generals and armies fall like grass before fire.

ZAPPY: What's next?

PROF. BOLT: Part 8: chapter 3. Mahiṣa himself fights, changing shape again and again: buffalo, lion, man, elephant. The Goddess drinks, laughs, and says: “Roar, fool, roar, while I drink.”
