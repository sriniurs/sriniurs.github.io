MADHU AND KAIṬABHA

DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 6 · CHAPTER 1, BRAHMĀ’S HYMN
The Goddess who held Viṣṇu in sleep · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The story in comic panels, and Brahmā’s hymn (ślokas 73–87) in full, one page for each verse: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 5, a ruined king and a betrayed merchant asked the sage Medhas: why can’t we let go? He answered: Mahāmāyā drags even the wise into delusion; and when she is pleased, she sets us free. “Who is she?” asked the king.

Medhas begins with her first coming, the first carita, whose deity is Mahākālī. Viṣṇu sleeps on the cosmic ocean; two demons attack Brahmā; and Brahmā sings one of the great hymns of the Goddess.

THE SLEEPING LORD

Ślokas 1.67–72
The story
The end of an age. The whole universe has become a single ocean. Lord Viṣṇu lies on the serpent Śeṣa in yoganidrā, the sleep of yoga.
Two demons. From the dirt of Viṣṇu’s ears arise two terrible demons, Madhu and Kaiṭabha.
Brahmā in danger. Brahmā sits on the lotus that grows from Viṣṇu’s navel. The demons come to kill him.
Viṣṇu will not wake. Brahmā sees the Lord asleep and the demons coming.
To whom does he pray? Not to Viṣṇu, but to Yoganidrā, the Goddess dwelling in Viṣṇu’s eyes and holding him in sleep. The hymn that follows is Brahmā’s praise of her.

ZAPPY: Demons from earwax? That's gross!

PROF. BOLT: It's meant to be! Even the dirt of the Lord's ear holds power, and power without wisdom turns demonic. And notice who Brahmā turns to: not the sleeper, but Sleep.

With Viṣṇu asleep, Brahmā prays to the Goddess who holds him.

ŚLOKA 1.73 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

YOU ARE THE SACRED SOUND
tvaṃ svāhā tvaṃ svadhā tvaṃ hivaṣaṭkāraḥ svarātmikā |sudhā tvam akṣare nityetridhā mātrātmikā sthitā ‖73‖
Brahmā said: You are Svāhā, you are Svadhā, you are the Vaṣaṭ cry; you are the soul of sound. You are the nectar, O imperishable, eternal one; you abide as the three measures (of Oṃ).
You are every sacred word of offering, and the sound in all of them. You are the nectar, and the three sounds of Oṃ.
WordMeans
svāhā · svadhā · vaṣaṭthe ritual cries of offering
svara-ātmikāwhose self is sound
sudhānectar
tridhā mātrā-ātmikāas the three measures: A, U, M
Brahmā’s hymn begins. With two demons coming to kill him and Viṣṇu fast asleep, Brahmā does not pray to Viṣṇu. He prays to Yoganidrā, the Goddess who holds Viṣṇu in sleep. And he starts with sound: every ritual word, and the syllable Oṃ itself.
Before anything else, she is the sacred sound.

ŚLOKA 1.74 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

THE SILENCE AFTER OṂ
ardhamātrāsthitā nityāyānuccāryā viśeṣataḥ |tvam eva sandhyā sāvitrītvaṃ devi jananī parā ‖74‖
You are the eternal half-measure, which cannot be uttered in any particular way. You alone are Sandhyā and Sāvitrī; you, O Devī, are the supreme mother.
You are the silent half-beat after Oṃ that no voice can say. You are the twilight prayer and the Gāyatrī; you are the highest Mother.
WordMeans
ardha-mātrāthe half-measure of Oṃ
anuccāryāthat cannot be uttered
sandhyāthe twilight prayer
sāvitrīthe Gāyatrī mantra
jananī parāthe supreme mother
Beyond the sound. After A, U and M, tradition speaks of a fourth, the ardhamātrā: the resonance as Oṃ fades into silence. Brahmā says she is that too. She is in the sound, and in the silence beyond it.
She is the sound, and the silence after it.

ŚLOKAS 1.75–76 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

CREATE, SUSTAIN, DISSOLVE
tvayaitad dhāryate viśvaṃtvayaitat sṛjyate jagat |tvayaitat pālyate devitvam atsy ante ca sarvadā ‖75‖visṛṣṭau sṛṣṭirūpā tvaṃsthitirūpā ca pālane |tathā saṃhṛtirūpāntejagato’sya jaganmaye ‖76‖
By you this universe is borne; by you this world is created; by you, O Devī, it is protected; and at the end you always consume it. At its emanation you are the form of creation; in protecting, the form of preservation; and at the end, the form of dissolution, O you who are this whole world.
WordMeans
dhāryate · sṛjyate · pālyateis borne · is created · is protected
atsi anteyou consume at the end
sṛṣṭi · sthiti · saṃhṛticreation · preservation · dissolution
jagat-mayeO you who are the world
All three jobs are hers. Usually creation is Brahmā’s, preservation Viṣṇu’s, dissolution Śiva’s. Here Brahmā himself says all three are the Goddess. It is a key idea of the Devī Māhātmya: she is the power behind the trinity.
Creation, preservation, dissolution: three forms of one Goddess.

ŚLOKA 1.77 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

SEVEN TIMES GREAT
mahāvidyā mahāmāyāmahāmedhā mahāsmṛtiḥ |mahāmohā ca bhavatīmahādevī maheśvarī ‖77‖
You are the great knowledge, the great illusion, the great intellect, the great memory, and the great delusion; the great Goddess, the great Ruler.
You are the greatest wisdom and the greatest illusion, the greatest mind and memory, the greatest confusion: the great Goddess who rules all.
WordMeans
mahā-vidyāthe great knowledge
mahā-māyāthe great illusion
mahā-medhā · mahā-smṛtiḥgreat intellect · great memory
mahā-mohāthe great delusion
Wisdom and delusion, side by side. Just as Medhas said in śloka 55, she is both mahāvidyā and mahāmohā. The name Mahāvidyā later becomes the title of the ten great tantric forms of the Goddess.
Greatest knowledge and greatest illusion: both are she.
Some editions read mahāsurī for maheśvarī.

ŚLOKA 1.78 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

THE THREE NIGHTS
prakṛtis tvaṃ ca sarvasyaguṇatrayavibhāvinī |kālarātrir mahārātrirmoharātriś ca dāruṇā ‖78‖
You are Prakṛti, the nature of all things, bringing forth the three guṇas. You are the Night of Time, the Great Night, and the terrible Night of Delusion.
You are Nature, from whom the three qualities of everything arise. You are the dark night of destruction, the great night, and the fearful night of confusion.
WordMeans
prakṛtiḥNature, the source of everything
guṇa-traya-vibhāvinīwho brings forth the three guṇas
kāla-rātriḥthe night of time, of dissolution
mahā-rātriḥthe great night
moha-rātriḥthe night of delusion
Three nights. Kālarātri is also the seventh of the Nine Durgās (Part 1). One popular tradition links the three nights to festivals: Mahārātri to Śivarātri, Moharātri to Kṛṣṇa Janmāṣṭamī, Kālarātri to Dīpāvalī. Darkness, too, is a form of the Mother.
Even the darkest night is a form of the Mother.

ŚLOKA 1.79 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

EVERY GOOD QUALITY
tvaṃ śrīs tvam īśvarī tvaṃ hrīstvaṃ buddhir bodhalakṣaṇā |lajjā puṣṭis tathā tuṣṭistvaṃ śāntiḥ kṣāntir eva ca ‖79‖
You are Śrī, you are the Ruler, you are modesty; you are intelligence, whose mark is understanding; shyness, nourishment, contentment; you are peace and patience.
You are fortune and sovereignty, conscience and understanding, nourishment and contentment, peace and patience.
WordMeans
śrīḥ · īśvarīfortune · the ruler
hrīḥ · lajjāmodesty · shyness
buddhiḥ bodha-lakṣaṇāintelligence, marked by understanding
puṣṭiḥ · tuṣṭiḥnourishment · contentment
śāntiḥ · kṣāntiḥpeace · patience
A first sketch of chapter 5. Many of these qualities return in the 21 forms of “yā devī sarvabhūteṣu”: lajjā, tuṣṭi, śānti, kṣānti. Brahmā names them here; in chapter 5 the gods bow to each one.
Every good quality in us is a form of her.

ŚLOKA 1.80 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

ARMED
khaḍginī śūlinī ghorāgadinī cakriṇī tathā |śaṅkhinī cāpinī bāṇa-bhuśuṇḍīparighāyudhā ‖80‖
You are terrible, armed with sword and trident, with mace and discus; with conch and bow, arrows, sling and iron club.
You are fearsome too: sword, trident, mace, discus, conch, bow, arrows, sling and club are in your hands.
WordMeans
khaḍginī · śūlinīsword-bearer · trident-bearer
ghorāterrible
gadinī · cakriṇīmace-bearer · discus-bearer
śaṅkhinī · cāpinīconch-bearer · bow-bearer
bhuśuṇḍī · parighasling · iron club
From qualities to weapons. Right after peace and patience come weapons. It is the Devī Māhātmya’s rhythm everywhere: gentle and fierce side by side. Brahmā needs both right now.
The same Mother holds peace and holds weapons.

ŚLOKA 1.81 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

GENTLER THAN ALL
saumyā saumyatarāśeṣa-saumyebhyas tv atisundarī |parāparāṇāṃ paramātvam eva parameśvarī ‖81‖
You are gentle, gentler than all gentle things, and exceedingly beautiful. Beyond the high and the low, you alone are the Supreme Goddess.
You are the gentlest of the gentle and the most beautiful. Higher than the highest, you are the Supreme.
WordMeans
saumyāgentle
saumya-tarāgentler
ati-sundarīexceedingly beautiful
para-aparāṇām paramāhighest of the high and the low
Right after the weapons. Verse 80 was terrible; verse 81 is the softest line in the hymn. It is the same pairing as “ati-saumyā ati-raudrā” in chapter 5: most gentle, most fierce.
Fiercer than any, and gentler than all.

ŚLOKA 1.82 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

HOW CAN I PRAISE YOU?
yac ca kiṃcit kvacid vastusad asad vākhilātmike |tasya sarvasya yā śaktiḥsā tvaṃ kiṃ stūyase mayā ‖82‖
Whatever thing exists anywhere, real or unreal, O Self of all, the power of all of it is you. How can you be praised by me?
Whatever exists, real or not, its power is you. So how could I ever praise you enough?
WordMeans
yat kiṃcit kvacit vastuwhatever thing, anywhere
sat asatreal or unreal
akhila-ātmikeO Self of all
śaktiḥthe power
kiṃ stūyase mayāhow are you to be praised by me?
Praise runs out. If she is the power in everything, including the one praising, then praise is like a wave praising the ocean. Brahmā sees this and keeps praising anyway.
The power in everything, even in the one who praises.
Some editions read tadā for mayā.

ŚLOKA 1.83 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

EVEN VIṢṆU SLEEPS
yayā tvayā jagatsraṣṭājagatpātyatti yo jagat |so’pi nidrāvaśaṃ nītaḥkas tvāṃ stotum iheśvaraḥ ‖83‖
You by whom even he who creates, protects and consumes the world has been brought under the power of sleep: who here is able to praise you?
You have put even Viṣṇu, maker, keeper and ender of the world, to sleep. Who could praise you?
WordMeans
jagat-sraṣṭācreator of the world
jagat-pātiprotects the world
atticonsumes
nidrā-vaśaṃ nītaḥbrought under sleep’s power
kaḥ … īśvaraḥwho is able?
The situation, plainly. Viṣṇu is asleep and will not wake, because she is holding him in sleep. So Brahmā appeals directly to the power that holds even the preserver.
Even Viṣṇu sleeps in her hands.

ŚLOKA 1.84 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

THE TRINITY TAKES BODY THROUGH HER
viṣṇuḥ śarīragrahaṇamaham īśāna eva ca |kāritās te yato’tas tvāṃkaḥ stotuṃ śaktimān bhavet ‖84‖
Viṣṇu, I and Īśāna (Śiva) are all made to take bodies by you. Therefore, who could be able to praise you?
Viṣṇu, Śiva and I all took our bodies because of you. Who has the power to praise you?
WordMeans
śarīra-grahaṇamthe taking of a body
ahamI: Brahmā
īśānaḥĪśāna: Śiva
kāritāḥ temade to do so by you
The boldest line. The three great gods of the Hindu trinity owe their very bodies to her. This is the heart of Śākta theology, spoken by Brahmā himself.
The trinity itself takes form through her.

ŚLOKAS 1.85–87 · BRAHMĀ’S HYMN

THE TWO REQUESTS
sā tvam itthaṃ prabhāvaiḥ svairudārair devi saṃstutā |mohayaitau durādharṣāvasurau madhukaiṭabhau ‖85‖prabodhaṃ ca jagatsvāmīnīyatām acyuto laghu |bodhaś ca kriyatām asyahantum etau mahāsurau ‖86–87‖
You, O Devī, thus praised for your own noble powers: delude these two invincible demons, Madhu and Kaiṭabha. And let Acyuta, the Lord of the world, be quickly awakened; let him be roused to slay these two great demons.
WordMeans
mohayadelude!
durādharṣauthe two invincible ones
prabodham nīyatāmlet him be awakened
laghuquickly
hantumin order to slay
Two requests, both granted. First: confuse the demons. Second: wake Viṣṇu. Watch both come true on the next pages. The demons’ strange mistake is no accident; it is the Goddess answering Brahmā.
Delude the demons, wake the Lord.

THE LORD WAKES

Ślokas 1.88–93
The story
She leaves. Praised by Brahmā, the dark goddess Tāmasī leaves Viṣṇu’s eyes, mouth, nostrils, arms, heart and chest, and appears before Brahmā.
He wakes. Freed from her, Janārdana (Viṣṇu) rises from his serpent bed on the ocean.
He sees. He sees the two demons, red-eyed with fury, ready to devour Brahmā.
The fight. Viṣṇu fights them with his bare arms, for five thousand years.
No winner. The demons, proud of their great strength, cannot be beaten by force. Something else will decide it.

ZAPPY: Five thousand years and he couldn't win?

PROF. BOLT: Not by strength alone. Remember Brahmā's first request: 'delude them'. The Goddess is about to answer it.

The Lord wakes, but strength alone is not enough.

THE STRANGEST BOON

Ślokas 1.94–104
The story
Deluded. Bewildered by Mahāmāyā, as Brahmā asked, the proud demons say: “We are pleased with your fighting. Choose a boon from us!”
The Lord’s choice. Viṣṇu says: “If you are pleased, then be slain by me, both of you. What other boon do I need?”
Trapped. Too late, they see the whole world is water. They say: “Then kill us where the earth is not flooded.”
The answer. Viṣṇu takes them on his thighs, the one dry place, and cuts off their heads with his discus.
Her first coming. Medhas ends: “Thus she arose, praised by Brahmā himself. Now hear more of her glory.”

ZAPPY: Why would demons offer their enemy a wish?

PROF. BOLT: Because the Goddess muddled their minds: Brahmā's first request, granted. Delusion turned their own strength against them.

She deluded the demons and woke the Lord: her first great deed.

PART 6 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 1, ślokas 67–104, in five lines
The danger. Viṣṇu sleeps on the cosmic ocean; Madhu and Kaiṭabha, born from his ears, attack Brahmā.
The hymn (73–79). Brahmā praises Yoganidrā: the sacred sound and the silence after Oṃ, creator, sustainer and dissolver, knowledge and delusion, every good quality.
The hymn (80–87). Armed and gentle, the power in all things, holding even Viṣṇu in sleep, giving the trinity their bodies: “delude the demons, wake the Lord.”
Both granted. She leaves Viṣṇu; he fights the demons for 5,000 years; deluded, they offer him a boon.
The end. “Be slain by me.” He kills them on his thighs, the only dry place. Thus she first arose.

ZAPPY: What's next?

PROF. BOLT: Part 7: chapter 2, the second carita. The gods have lost heaven to the buffalo demon Mahiṣa, and their fury becomes a blazing light: the Goddess is born.
