YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 10 · CHAPTER 5
The Goddess in all beings · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The whole “yā devī” hymn (ślokas 9–82) in full for chanting, then a page for each form of the Goddess, and the story of Kauśikī and Śumbha’s proposal in comic panels.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 9, the gods sang the Śakrādi stuti and received a boon: “whenever we remember you, end our dangers.” The second carita closed.

Chapter 5 opens the third carita, whose deity is Mahāsarasvatī. New tyrants, Śumbha and Niśumbha, rule heaven. The gods climb the Himālaya and sing the hymn chanted in every home: “yā devī sarvabhūteṣu”, the Goddess in every being.

Our separate article on this hymn is Yā Devī Sarvabhūteṣu; this part reads it inside its story.

With the blessings of the Devī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Twenty-one forms! Which one is the real Goddess?

PROF. BOLT: All of them, and the awareness behind them. The hymn starts with consciousness, ends with consciousness, and finds her in everything between, even hunger and error.

NEW TYRANTS

Ślokas 5.1–8
The story
The third carita. Its deity is Mahāsarasvatī. Two asura brothers, Śumbha and Niśumbha, proud of their strength, take the three worlds and the gods’ shares of sacrifice.
Everything taken. They seize the offices of the Sun, Moon, Kubera, Yama, Varuṇa, Wind and Fire.
Driven out. The gods, defeated and kingdomless, are cast out.
They remember. “She gave us a boon: remember me in danger, and I will end your dangers at once.”
To the mountain. They go to the Himālaya and praise her as Viṣṇumāyā. The hymn they sing is the most famous in all Goddess worship.

ZAPPY: New demons? Didn't she just win?

PROF. BOLT: Every age has its own tyrants. That's the point of the three caritas: she came once, twice, and will come again, whenever she is remembered.

New tyrants; the old promise still holds.

ŚLOKAS 5.5–7

THEY REMEMBERED THE BOON
hṛtādhikārāstridaśāstābhyāṃ sarve nirākṛtāḥ |mahāsurābhyāṃ tāṃ devīṃ saṃsmarantyaparājitām ‖5‖tayāsmākaṃ varo datto yathāpatsu smṛtākhilāḥ |bhavatāṃ nāśayiṣyāmi tatkṣaṇātparamāpadaḥ ‖6‖iti kṛtvā matiṃ devā himavantaṃ nageśvaram |jagmustatra tato devīṃ viṣṇumāyāṃ pratuṣṭuvuḥ ‖7‖
Stripped of their offices and driven out by those two great asuras, the gods all remembered the unconquered Devī: “She gave us a boon: ‘Whenever you remember me in danger, I will at once destroy all your worst dangers.’” Resolved on this, the gods went to Himavān, lord of mountains, and there praised the Devī, Viṣṇumāyā.
WordMeans
hṛta-adhikārāḥstripped of their offices
aparājitāthe unconquered one
smṛtāremembered
tat-kṣaṇātthat very moment
viṣṇu-māyāthe power of Viṣṇu
The boon is used. At the end of chapter 4 the gods asked: “remember you, and you remove our dangers.” Now new tyrants, the brothers Śumbha and Niśumbha, have taken everything, and the gods do the one thing they were promised would work: they remember her.
In danger, the gods remember her promise.

ŚLOKAS 5.9–10

NAMO DEVYAI
namo devyai mahādevyai śivāyai satataṃ namaḥ |namaḥ prakṛtyai bhadrāyai niyatāḥ praṇatāḥ sma tām ‖9‖raudrāyai namo nityāyai gauryai dhātryai namo namaḥ |jyotsnāyai cendurūpiṇyai sukhāyai satataṃ namaḥ ‖10‖
Salutations to the Devī, to the Great Devī, to Śivā, the auspicious, always salutations; salutations to Prakṛti, to Bhadrā; with minds controlled we bow to her. Salutations to the fierce one, to the eternal one, to Gaurī, to the sustainer, salutations again and again; to her who is moonlight, whose form is the moon, to her who is happiness, always salutations.
WordMeans
namaḥsalutation
śivā · bhadrāthe auspicious · the kind
prakṛtiNature
raudrāthe fierce
dhātrīthe sustainer, nurse
jyotsnā · indu-rūpiṇīmoonlight · moon-formed
The hymn opens. The best-known prelude to “yā devī”. Fierce (raudrā) and moonlight-gentle (jyotsnā) sit side by side in one verse, as always in this text.
Salutations to the fierce one, and to the moonlight.

ŚLOKAS 5.11–13

MOST GENTLE, MOST FIERCE
kalyāṇyai praṇatāmṛddhyai siddhyai kurmo namo namaḥ |nairṛtyai bhūbhṛtāṃ lakṣmyai śarvāṇyai te namo namaḥ ‖11‖durgāyai durgapārāyai sārāyai sarvakāriṇyai |khyātyai tathaiva kṛṣṇāyai dhūmrāyai satataṃ namaḥ ‖12‖atisaumyātiraudrāyai natāstasyai namo namaḥ |namo jagatpratiṣṭhāyai devyai kṛtyai namo namaḥ ‖13‖
To Kalyāṇī, who is the prosperity of those who bow, to Siddhi, we make salutations again and again; to Nairṛtī, to the Lakṣmī of kings, to Śarvāṇī, salutations to you. To Durgā, who carries across difficulties; to the essence, the doer of all; to Khyāti (fame), to Kṛṣṇā (the dark one), to Dhūmrā (the smoky one), always salutations. To her who is most gentle and most fierce we bow, salutations; salutations to the foundation of the world, to the Devī who is action, salutations again and again.
WordMeans
kalyāṇīthe auspicious
siddhisuccess, attainment
durga-pārāwho carries across difficulties
sarva-kāriṇīdoer of all
ati-saumyā ati-raudrāmost gentle, most fierce
kṛtiaction
“Atisaumyā atiraudrā”. In two words, the whole Devī Māhātmya: the gentlest and the fiercest. She is also kṛti, action itself, and the ground (pratiṣṭhā) the world stands on.
Most gentle and most fierce, together.

ŚLOKAS 5.14–16 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

SHE IS CALLED VIṢṆUMĀYĀ
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu viṣṇumāyeti śabditā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖14–16‖
To the Devī who in all beings is called Viṣṇumāyā: salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations again and again.
WordMeans
yā devīthe Goddess who
sarva-bhūteṣuin all beings
viṣṇu-māyāViṣṇu’s power
śabditāis called
namas tasyaisalutation to her
The pattern begins. 21 times the gods will say “the Devī who in all beings is…”, each time followed by a triple “namas tasyai”. The first is Viṣṇumāyā: the power that held Viṣṇu in sleep in chapter 1, now found in every being.
The first of 21: she is Viṣṇumāyā in all beings.

ŚLOKAS 5.17–19 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

SHE IS CONSCIOUSNESS
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu cetanetyabhidhīyate |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖17–19‖
To the Devī who in all beings is called Consciousness: salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations again and again.
WordMeans
cetanāconsciousness, awareness
abhidhīyateis named
The deepest one first. Before any quality, she is cetanā: the awareness by which anything is known at all. Every feeling and thought in the list that follows happens inside this awareness.
The awareness in every being is the Goddess.

ŚLOKAS 5.20–25 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

INTELLIGENCE AND SLEEP
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu buddhirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖20–22‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu nidrārūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖23–25‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as intelligence; to the Devī who dwells in all beings as sleep: salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations again and again.
WordMeans
buddhiintelligence, discernment
nidrāsleep
rūpeṇa saṃsthitāabides in the form of
Waking mind and sleep. Buddhi is the clear mind of the day; nidrā is rest, the sleep that restores us. Both are holy. Remember: she was Yoganidrā, the sleep of Viṣṇu, in chapter 1.
Clear thinking and deep sleep are both her.

ŚLOKAS 5.26–31 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

HUNGER AND SHADE
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu kṣudhārūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖26–28‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu chāyārūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖29–31‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as hunger; as shade: salutations to her, again and again.
WordMeans
kṣudhāhunger
chāyāshade, shadow, also reflection
Even hunger? Yes. Hunger keeps every creature alive and moving; it is her power at work. And chāyā, shade, is the cool rest she gives the tired, or, some say, the reflection of the Self in the mind.
Hunger keeps life going: that too is her.

ŚLOKAS 5.32–37 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

POWER AND THIRST
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śaktirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖32–34‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu tṛṣṇārūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖35–37‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as power; as thirst: salutations to her, again and again.
WordMeans
śaktipower, energy
tṛṣṇāthirst, also craving
Śakti in name. “Śakti” is her very name: the energy in every movement. Tṛṣṇā is thirst, and also craving. The text does not hide that desire, too, is her power, which is why the next lines balance it.
All energy, even craving, is her power.

ŚLOKAS 5.38–43 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

PATIENCE AND BIRTH
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu kṣāntirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖38–40‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu jātirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖41–43‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as patience; as birth (or kind): salutations to her, again and again.
WordMeans
kṣāntipatience, forbearance
jātibirth; also one’s kind or nature
Forbearance. Kṣānti is the strength to bear hurt without striking back. Jāti here means birth or kind: the way every creature is born into its own nature.
Patience is her strength in us.

ŚLOKAS 5.44–49 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

CONSCIENCE AND PEACE
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu lajjārūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖44–46‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śāntirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖47–49‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as modesty; as peace: salutations to her, again and again.
WordMeans
lajjāmodesty, conscience, the sense of shame that stops wrong
śāntipeace
The inner brakes. Lajjā is the healthy shame that stops us before we do wrong. Śānti is the peace that follows a clean conscience. Brahmā already named both in chapter 1 (Part 6).
Conscience and peace are her presence within.

ŚLOKAS 5.50–55 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

FAITH AND BEAUTY
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu śraddhārūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖50–52‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu kāntirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖53–55‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as faith; as beauty (radiance): salutations to her, again and again.
WordMeans
śraddhāfaith, trust
kāntibeauty, radiance
Faith. Chapter 4 called her “śraddhā satām”, the faith of the good. Here she is faith in all beings. Kānti is the glow of beauty: in a face, a flower, a sunrise.
Faith and beauty shine with her light.

ŚLOKAS 5.56–61 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

FORTUNE AND LIVELIHOOD
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu lakṣmīrūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖56–58‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu vṛttirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖59–61‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as Lakṣmī (fortune); as livelihood (activity): salutations to her, again and again.
WordMeans
lakṣmīfortune, prosperity
vṛttilivelihood; also activity of the mind
Prosperity and work. Lakṣmī is the fortune we receive; vṛtti is the work by which we earn it. In yoga, vṛtti also means the movements of the mind. Wealth and work are both sacred here.
Fortune and honest work are both her.

ŚLOKAS 5.62–67 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

MEMORY AND COMPASSION
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu smṛtirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖62–64‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu dayārūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖65–67‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as memory; as compassion: salutations to her, again and again.
WordMeans
smṛtimemory; also tradition
dayācompassion, mercy
Why memory matters here. The gods are on the Himālaya precisely because they remembered her (5.5–6). Memory is the thread that brings her back. Dayā, compassion, is why she answers.
We remember her; she answers with compassion.

ŚLOKAS 5.68–73 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

CONTENTMENT AND MOTHER
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu tuṣṭirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖68–70‖yā devī sarvabhūteṣu mātṛrūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖71–73‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as contentment; as mother: salutations to her, again and again.
WordMeans
tuṣṭicontentment
mātṛmother
The heart of the list. Many find this the most moving line: in every being she is the Mother. Every mother’s love, in humans and animals, is her. And tuṣṭi, contentment, is the end of craving (tṛṣṇā) named earlier.
In every being, she is the Mother.

ŚLOKAS 5.74–76 · YĀ DEVĪ SARVABHŪTEṢU

SHE IS EVEN ERROR
yā devī sarvabhūteṣu bhrāntirūpeṇa saṃsthitā |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖74–76‖
To the Devī who dwells in all beings as delusion (error): salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations again and again.
WordMeans
bhrāntidelusion, error, mistaking one thing for another
The last form is the boldest. Even bhrānti, error, is her: the Mahāmāyā of Part 5 who “drags even the wise into delusion”. Nothing, not even our mistakes, is outside her. That is why only she can free us from them.
Even our errors are held by her.

ŚLOKAS 5.77–80

CONSCIOUSNESS FILLS ALL
indriyāṇāmadhiṣṭhātrī bhūtānāṃ cākhileṣu yā |bhūteṣu satataṃ tasyai vyāptyai devyai namo namaḥ ‖77‖citirūpeṇa yā kṛtsnametad vyāpya sthitā jagat |namastasyai namastasyai namastasyai namo namaḥ ‖78–80‖
To her who presides over the senses of beings, who is in all beings always, to the all-pervading Devī, salutations again and again. To her who, in the form of consciousness (citi), pervades this whole world and abides in it: salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations to her, salutations again and again.
WordMeans
indriyāṇām adhiṣṭhātrīpresiding over the senses
vyāptipervasion
citi-rūpeṇain the form of pure consciousness
kṛtsnamwhole
The hymn closes the circle. It began with cetanā (awareness) and ends with citi (pure consciousness) filling the whole world. Everything between, intelligence and hunger, faith and error, was that one consciousness in many forms.
One consciousness, filling the whole world.

ŚLOKAS 5.81–82

MAY SHE END OUR DANGERS
stutā suraiḥ pūrvamabhīṣṭasaṃśrayā-ttathā surendreṇa dineṣu sevitā |karotu sā naḥ śubhaheturīśvarīśubhāni bhadrāṇyabhihantu cāpadaḥ ‖81‖yā sāmprataṃ coddhatadaityatāpitai-rasmābhirīśā ca surairnamasyate |yā ca smṛtā tatkṣaṇameva hanti naḥsarvāpado bhaktivinamramūrtibhiḥ ‖82‖
Praised long ago by the gods for the help they sought, and worshipped daily by the lord of gods: may she, the Ruler, the source of all good, bring us good and auspicious things and destroy our dangers. She who is now bowed to by us gods, tormented by arrogant demons; who, remembered by us with bodies bowed in devotion, at that very moment destroys all our dangers.

Back to the promise. The gods end by naming exactly what they are counting on: “remembered, she destroys all dangers at that very moment.” The boon of chapter 4, word for word.
Remembered with devotion, she ends dangers at once.

ŚLOKAS 5.84–88

KAUŚIKĪ AND KĀLIKĀ
evaṃ stavābhiyuktānāṃ devānāṃ tatra pārvatī |snātumabhyāyayau toye jāhnavyā nṛpanandana ‖84‖sābravīttān surān subhrūrbhavadbhiḥ stūyate’tra kā |śarīrakośataścāsyāḥ samudbhūtābravīcchivā ‖85‖stotraṃ mamaitatkriyate śumbhadaityanirākṛtaiḥ |devaiḥ sametaiḥ samare niśumbhena parājitaiḥ ‖86‖śarīrakośādyattasyāḥ pārvatyā niḥsṛtāmbikā |kauśikīti samasteṣu tato lokeṣu gīyate ‖87‖tasyāṃ vinirgatāyāṃ tu kṛṣṇābhūtsāpi pārvatī |kāliketi samākhyātā himācalakṛtāśrayā ‖88‖
As the gods praised, Pārvatī came to bathe in the Gaṅgā. “Whom are you praising?” she asked. From the sheath of her body came forth Śivā and said: “This hymn is to me, from the gods driven out by Śumbha and defeated by Niśumbha.” Born from the sheath (kośa) of Pārvatī, she is sung as Kauśikī. Pārvatī herself then became dark, and is known as Kālikā, dwelling on the Himālaya.
WordMeans
jāhnavīthe Gaṅgā
śarīra-kośathe sheath of the body
kauśikī“from the sheath”
kṛṣṇā abhūtbecame dark
kālikāthe dark one
A strange, beautiful answer. Pārvatī asks who is being praised; the answer steps out of her own body. The hymn was to her all along. Bright Kauśikī fights this carita; dark Pārvatī becomes Kālikā.
The Goddess they praised steps out of Pārvatī.

THE PROPOSAL

Ślokas 5.89–129
The story
Spies. Śumbha’s servants Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa see Ambikā on the Himālaya in her most lovely form.
The report. “You own every jewel of the three worlds: Indra’s elephant Airāvata, the Pārijāta tree, the horse Uccaiḥśravas, Brahmā’s swan-chariot, Kubera’s treasure, Varuṇa’s umbrella. Why not this jewel among women?”
The messenger. Śumbha sends the asura Sugrīva: “Śumbha rules the three worlds and enjoys all the sacrifices. Marry me or my brother Niśumbha, and all that power is yours.”
The vow. Smiling inwardly, she answers: “Whoever conquers me in battle shall be my husband. Let him come.”
The threat. “Proud woman! Gods could not face them; will you, alone? Go, or be dragged by your hair.” She replies calmly: “Tell your king exactly what I said.”

ZAPPY: A proposal? In the middle of a war story?

PROF. BOLT: Śumbha thinks everything beautiful should belong to him: elephant, tree, horse, and now the Goddess. Greed treats even the Divine as a jewel to own.

Greed wants to own the Goddess; she offers a fight instead.

ŚLOKAS 5.119–121

THE VOW
kiṃ tvatra yatpratijñātaṃ mithyā tatkriyate katham |śrūyatāmalpabuddhitvātpratijñā yā kṛtā purā ‖119‖yo māṃ jayati saṅgrāme yo me darpaṃ vyapohati |yo me pratibalo loke sa me bhartā bhaviṣyati ‖120‖tadāgacchatu śumbho’tra niśumbho vā mahābalaḥ |māṃ jitvā kiṃ cireṇātra pāṇiṃ gṛhṇātu me laghu ‖121‖
The Devī said: “But I made a vow long ago, out of little understanding; how can I make it false? Hear it: He who conquers me in battle, who removes my pride, who is my equal in strength in the world, he shall be my husband. So let Śumbha come here, or the mighty Niśumbha. Let him conquer me and quickly take my hand. Why delay?”

Hidden smile. The text says she spoke “smiling deeply within” (5.116). Śumbha’s messenger offered her marriage to the lord of the three worlds. Her answer sounds humble and is a challenge: “Beat me first.” No one in the universe can, and she knows it.
“Whoever conquers me in battle”: a challenge dressed as a vow.

PART 10 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 5 in five lines
New tyrants (5.1–7). Śumbha and Niśumbha take the three worlds; the gods remember her boon and go to the Himālaya.
The prelude (5.9–13). Salutations to the Devī: fierce and moonlike, Durgā who carries across, most gentle and most fierce.
The 21 forms (5.14–76). In all beings she is Viṣṇumāyā, consciousness, intelligence, sleep, hunger… mother, and even error.
The close (5.77–82). She is pure consciousness filling the world; remembered, she ends dangers at once.
Kauśikī (5.84–129). She steps out of Pārvatī, who becomes Kālikā. Śumbha proposes marriage; she replies with her vow: “Conquer me first.”
