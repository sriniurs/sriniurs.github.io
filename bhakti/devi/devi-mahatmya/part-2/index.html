DEVĪ KAVACAM

DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 2 · ARMOUR FOR EVERY LIMB
Verses 18–56 · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: Verses 18–56 for chanting, then the whole armour on one picture, then one page for each group of verses: the directions, the body from crest to toes, the breath and mind, family and road, and the fruits of reciting.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 1, the sage Mārkaṇḍeya asked Brahmā for the supreme secret of protection, and Brahmā began the Devī Kavacam: the Nine Durgās, the Mothers on their mounts, and in verse 17 the first two guardians of space.

Now the armour goes on. First every direction, then the body from the crest of the head to the soles of the feet, then breath, mind, family and the road ahead. Last come the fruits of reciting it, and a rule: chant this before the Saptaśatī.

With the blessings of the Devī,

THE ARMOUR AT A GLANCE

A goddess for each part of you

The Kavacam names more than 60 guardians. This map shows 24 of them, from Udyotinī at the crest of the head to Śrī at the toes, with Abhedyā, “the Unbreakable”, in every joint. Breath, mind and the three guṇas have guardians too (verses 36–38).

VERSES 18–20 · THE TEN DIRECTIONS

A GODDESS ON EVERY SIDE
dakṣiṇe’vatu vārāhīnairṛtyāṃ khaḍgadhāriṇī |pratīcyāṃ vāruṇī rakṣedvāyavyāṃ mṛgavāhinī ‖18‖udīcyāṃ pātu kaumārīaiśānyāṃ śūladhāriṇī |ūrdhvaṃ brahmāṇi me rakṣedadhastād vaiṣṇavī tathā ‖19‖evaṃ daśa diśo rakṣeccāmuṇḍā śavavāhanā |jayā me cāgrataḥ pātuvijayā pātu pṛṣṭhataḥ ‖20‖
In the south may Vārāhī protect me; in the south-west, the sword-bearer; in the west, Vāruṇī; in the north-west, she who rides the deer. In the north may Kaumārī protect; in the north-east, the trident-bearer; above, Brahmāṇī; below, Vaiṣṇavī. So may Cāmuṇḍā, who rides a corpse, guard all ten directions. May Jayā protect me in front, and Vijayā behind.
WordMeans
dakṣiṇe · pratīcyāmin the south · in the west
udīcyām · aiśānyāmin the north · in the north-east
ūrdhvam · adhastātabove · below
daśa diśaḥthe ten directions
Ten directions, not four. Indian thought counts eight compass points plus up and down. With verse 17 (east and south-east) every one now has a guardian. Then come front and back: Jayā and Vijayā, “Victory” and “Triumph”.
No direction is left open: she guards all ten.

VERSES 21–23 · HEAD AND FACE

FROM THE CREST DOWN
ajitā vāmapārśve tudakṣiṇe cāparājitā |śikhām udyotinī rakṣedumā mūrdhni vyavasthitā ‖21‖mālādharī lalāṭe cabhruvau rakṣed yaśasvinī |trinetrā ca bhruvor madhyeyamaghaṇṭā ca nāsike ‖22‖śaṅkhinī cakṣuṣor madhyeśrotrayor dvāravāsinī |kapolau kālikā rakṣetkarṇamūle tu śāṅkarī ‖23‖
Ajitā on my left side, Aparājitā on my right; may Udyotinī guard my crest, and Umā dwell on my head. Mālādharī on my forehead, Yaśasvinī my brows, Trinetrā between the brows, Yamaghaṇṭā my nostrils; Śaṅkhinī my eyes, Dvāravāsinī my ears, Kālikā my cheeks, Śāṅkarī the roots of my ears.
WordMeans
vāma · dakṣiṇa-pārśveon the left · on the right side
śikhām · mūrdhnithe crest · the head
lalāṭe · bhruvauforehead · brows
cakṣuṣoḥ · śrotrayoḥthe eyes · the ears
Names that fit their place. Trinetrā, the “three-eyed”, guards the spot between the brows, where the third eye is placed. Dvāravāsinī, “she who dwells at the door”, guards the ears, the doors of sound.
From crest to cheek, each part has its guardian.

VERSES 24–26 · MOUTH AND THROAT

SPEECH IS GUARDED TOO
nāsikāyāṃ sugandhā cauttaroṣṭhe ca carcikā |adhare cāmṛtakalājihvāyāṃ ca sarasvatī ‖24‖dantān rakṣatu kaumārīkaṇṭhadeśe tu caṇḍikā |ghaṇṭikāṃ citraghaṇṭā camahāmāyā ca tāluke ‖25‖kāmākṣī cibukaṃ rakṣedvācaṃ me sarvamaṅgalā |grīvāyāṃ bhadrakālī capṛṣṭhavaṃśe dhanurdharī ‖26‖
Sugandhā on my nose, Carcikā on my upper lip, Amṛtakalā on my lower lip, Sarasvatī on my tongue. May Kaumārī guard my teeth, Caṇḍikā my throat, Citraghaṇṭā my uvula, Mahāmāyā my palate. Kāmākṣī my chin, Sarvamaṅgalā my speech, Bhadrakālī my neck, Dhanurdharī my spine.
WordMeans
jihvāyām sarasvatīSarasvatī on the tongue
kaṇṭha-deśein the throat
vācamspeech
pṛṣṭha-vaṃśealong the spine
Not just body parts. Sarasvatī, goddess of learning, guards the tongue; Sarvamaṅgalā, “all-auspicious”, guards vāc, speech itself. The armour protects what we say as well as what we are.
Sarasvatī on the tongue: may our words be guarded.

VERSES 27–29 · ARMS AND HEART

LALITĀ IN THE HEART
nīlagrīvā bahiḥkaṇṭhenalikāṃ nalakūbarī |skandhayoḥ khaḍginī rakṣedbāhū me vajradhāriṇī ‖27‖hastayor daṇḍinī rakṣedambikā cāṅgulīṣu ca |nakhāñ chūleśvarī rakṣetkukṣau rakṣet kuleśvarī ‖28‖stanau rakṣen mahādevīmanaḥśokavināśinī |hṛdaye lalitā devīudare śūladhāriṇī ‖29‖
Nīlagrīvā the outer throat, Nalakūbarī the windpipe; Khaḍginī my shoulders, Vajradhāriṇī my arms; Daṇḍinī my hands, Ambikā my fingers; Śūleśvarī my nails, Kuleśvarī my sides. Mahādevī, destroyer of the mind’s grief, my chest; the goddess Lalitā my heart; Śūladhāriṇī my belly.
WordMeans
skandhayoḥ · bāhūthe shoulders · the arms
hastayoḥ · aṅgulīṣuthe hands · the fingers
manaḥ-śoka-vināśinīdestroyer of the mind’s grief
hṛdaye lalitāLalitā in the heart
The heart belongs to Lalitā. The gentlest, most beautiful form of the Goddess is placed in the heart. Her neighbour is the one who “destroys the mind’s grief”: the armour reaches past the body to sorrow itself.
Lalitā guards the heart; grief itself is driven out.

VERSES 30–32 · DOWN TO THE FEET

NAVEL TO TOES
nābhau ca kāminī rakṣedguhyaṃ guhyeśvarī tathā |pūtanā kāmikā meḍhraṃgude mahiṣavāhinī ‖30‖kaṭyāṃ bhagavatī rakṣejjānunī vindhyavāsinī |jaṅghe mahābalā rakṣetsarvakāmapradāyinī ‖31‖gulphayor nārasiṃhī capādapṛṣṭhe tu taijasī |pādāṅgulīṣu śrī rakṣetpādādhastalavāsinī ‖32‖
Kāminī my navel, Guhyeśvarī my private parts, and the goddesses below; Bhagavatī my hips, Vindhyavāsinī my knees; Mahābalā, giver of every wish, my shins; Nārasiṃhī my ankles, Taijasī the tops of my feet, Śrī my toes, and Talavāsinī the soles of my feet.
WordMeans
nābhauat the navel
kaṭyām · jānunīthe hips · the knees
gulphayoḥthe ankles
pāda-aṅgulīṣu · talathe toes · the soles
Nothing is too low. The armour does not skip the parts we do not mention or the feet that touch the dust. Vindhyavāsinī, “she who dwells in the Vindhyas”, guards the knees; Śrī herself, Lakṣmī, guards the toes.
Every part of us, down to the soles, is hers to guard.

VERSES 33–35 · INSIDE THE BODY

BLOOD, BONE AND JOINTS
nakhān daṃṣṭrākarālī cakeśāṃś caivordhvakeśinī |romakūpeṣu kauberītvacaṃ vāgīśvarī tathā ‖33‖raktamajjāvasāmāṃsā-nyasthimedāṃsi pārvatī |antrāṇi kālarātriś capittaṃ ca mukuṭeśvarī ‖34‖padmāvatī padmakośekaphe cūḍāmaṇis tathā |jvālāmukhī nakhajvālāmabhedyā sarvasandhiṣu ‖35‖
Daṃṣṭrākarālī my nails, Ūrdhvakeśinī my hair; Kauberī my pores, Vāgīśvarī my skin. Pārvatī my blood, marrow, fat, flesh, bone and tissue; Kālarātri my intestines, Mukuṭeśvarī my bile. Padmāvatī the lotus of my heart, Cūḍāmaṇi my phlegm, Jvālāmukhī the glow of my nails, Abhedyā every joint.
WordMeans
roma-kūpeṣu · tvacamthe pores · the skin
rakta · asthiblood · bone
padma-kośein the lotus-bud (of the heart)
abhedyā sarva-sandhiṣuthe Unbreakable, in every joint
Into the tissues. The armour goes past the skin: blood, marrow, bone, the body’s humours of bile and phlegm, as the āyurvedic body was understood. Abhedyā, “the Unbreakable”, holds every joint.
Inside and out, she holds the body together.

VERSES 36–38 · BREATH AND MIND

THE SUBTLE BODY
śukraṃ brahmāṇi me rakṣecchāyāṃ chatreśvarī tathā |ahaṅkāraṃ mano buddhiṃrakṣen me dharmadhāriṇī ‖36‖prāṇāpānau tathā vyānamudānaṃ ca samānakam |vajrahastā ca me rakṣetprāṇaṃ kalyāṇaśobhanā ‖37‖rase rūpe ca gandhe caśabde sparśe ca yoginī |sattvaṃ rajas tamaś caivarakṣen nārāyaṇī sadā ‖38‖
Brahmāṇī my vital seed, Chatreśvarī my shadow; Dharmadhāriṇī my ego, mind and intellect. Vajrahastā my five breaths, prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna and samāna; Kalyāṇaśobhanā my life itself. Yoginī in taste, form, smell, sound and touch; and may Nārāyaṇī always guard my sattva, rajas and tamas.
WordMeans
ahaṅkāraṃ mano buddhimego, mind, intellect
prāṇa … samānathe five vital breaths
rasa rūpa gandha śabda sparśataste, form, smell, sound, touch
sattvaṃ rajas tamaḥthe three guṇas
Beyond flesh. Now the armour covers the subtle body: the five breaths of yoga, the five sense-experiences, the mind and ego, and finally the three guṇas, the qualities of all nature. Nārāyaṇī, the Goddess as Viṣṇu’s power, guards them.
Breath, senses, mind: the inner self is armoured too.

VERSES 39–41 · LIFE AND LOVED ONES

MY FAMILY, MY ROAD
āyū rakṣatu vārāhīdharmaṃ rakṣatu vaiṣṇavī |yaśaḥ kīrtiṃ ca lakṣmīṃ cadhanaṃ vidyāṃ ca cakriṇī ‖39‖gotram indrāṇi me rakṣetpaśūn me rakṣa caṇḍike |putrān rakṣen mahālakṣmīrbhāryāṃ rakṣatu bhairavī ‖40‖panthānaṃ supathā rakṣenmārgaṃ kṣemakarī tathā |rājadvāre mahālakṣmīrvijayā sarvataḥ sthitā ‖41‖
Vārāhī my life span, Vaiṣṇavī my dharma; Cakriṇī my fame, honour, fortune, wealth and learning. Indrāṇī my family line; guard my cattle, O Caṇḍikā; Mahālakṣmī my children; Bhairavī my wife. Supathā my path, Kṣemakarī my road; Mahālakṣmī at the king’s gate; Vijayā everywhere.
WordMeans
āyuḥ · dharmamlife span · dharma
dhanaṃ vidyāmwealth, learning
gotram · putrānfamily line · children
panthānam · mārgammy path · my road
Armour for a whole life. The prayer widens from the body to everything a person cares about: years, values, name, money, learning, family, animals, the journey, even one’s case at the king’s court. The language is of its time (“my wife”); the care is for everyone we love.
Not just me: my family, my work, my road.

VERSE 42 · THE GAPS

WHATEVER WAS MISSED
rakṣāhīnaṃ tu yat sthānaṃvarjitaṃ kavacena tu |tat sarvaṃ rakṣa me devijayantī pāpanāśinī ‖42‖
Whatever place is left without protection, not covered by this armour: protect all of it for me, O Devī Jayantī, destroyer of sin.
And anything I forgot to name, any gap in the armour: Goddess, guard that too.
WordMeans
rakṣā-hīnamwithout protection
varjitaṃ kavacenaleft out by the armour
tat sarvam rakṣaprotect all of that
jayantīthe Victorious
pāpa-nāśinīdestroyer of sin
The humble clause. After naming every direction, limb, breath and loved one, the prayer admits it cannot list everything. So it hands the rest to her. It is a beautiful model for any prayer: name what you can, then trust.
Whatever I missed, she covers.

VERSES 43–45 · THE FRUITS BEGIN

NOT ONE STEP WITHOUT IT
padam ekaṃ na gacchet tuyadīcchec chubham ātmanaḥ |kavacenāvṛto nityaṃyatra yatraiva gacchati ‖43‖tatra tatrārthalābhaś cavijayaḥ sārvakāmikaḥ |yaṃ yaṃ cintayate kāmaṃtaṃ taṃ prāpnoti niścitam |paramaiśvaryam atulaṃprāpsyate bhūtale pumān ‖44‖nirbhayo jāyate martyaḥsaṅgrāmeṣv aparājitaḥ |trailokye tu bhavet pūjyaḥkavacenāvṛtaḥ pumān ‖45‖
If one wishes oneself well, one should not take even a single step without this armour. Covered by it always, wherever one goes, one gains wealth there, and victory in every aim. Whatever wish one thinks of, one surely attains; such a person gains matchless greatness on earth. One becomes fearless, unconquered in battle; covered by this armour, one is honoured in the three worlds.
The phalaśruti. From here the Kavacam lists the fruits of reciting it. Like every phalaśruti, it speaks in the language of praise: its central promise is the first word of verse 45, nirbhaya, fearless.
Its first gift: to walk without fear.

VERSES 46–47 · RARE EVEN FOR GODS

THREE TIMES A DAY
idaṃ tu devyāḥ kavacaṃdevānām api durlabham |yaḥ paṭhet prayato nityaṃtrisandhyaṃ śraddhayānvitaḥ ‖46‖daivī kalā bhavet tasyatrailokyeṣv aparājitaḥ |jīved varṣaśataṃ sāgramapamṛtyuvivarjitaḥ ‖47‖
This armour of the Devī is hard to obtain even for the gods. Whoever recites it daily, purified and full of faith, at the three junctions of the day, gains a divine spark and is unconquered in the three worlds; such a one lives a full hundred years, free from untimely death.
WordMeans
devānām api durlabhamrare even for gods
prayataḥpurified, self-controlled
trisandhyamat the three junctions: dawn, noon, dusk
daivī kalāa divine spark
apamṛtyu-vivarjitaḥfree from untimely death
Faith and regularity. Two conditions frame the promise: śraddhā, faith, and nityam, every day. The three sandhyās, dawn, noon and dusk, are the classic Vedic times of prayer.
Recited daily with faith, it becomes part of you.

VERSES 48–52 · NOTHING CAN HARM

THE ARMOUR IN THE HEART
naśyanti vyādhayaḥ sarvelūtāvisphoṭakādayaḥ |sthāvaraṃ jaṅgamaṃ caivakṛtrimaṃ cāpi yad viṣam ‖48‖abhicārāṇi sarvāṇimantrayantrāṇi bhūtale |bhūcarāḥ khecarāś caivajalajāś caupadeśikāḥ ‖49‖sahajā kulajā mālāḍākinī śākinī tathā |antarikṣacarā ghorāḍākinyaś ca mahābalāḥ ‖50‖grahabhūtapiśācāś cayakṣagandharvarākṣasāḥ |brahmarākṣasavetālāḥkuṣmāṇḍā bhairavādayaḥ ‖51‖naśyanti darśanāt tasyakavace hṛdi saṃsthite |mānonnatir bhaved rājñastejovṛddhikaraṃ param ‖52‖
All diseases perish, such as spider-bites and boils, and every poison, natural or man-made; all sorcery, spells and charms; and every harmful being of land, sky and water, from ḍākinīs and ghosts to rākṣasas and vetālas: all perish at the very sight of one who holds this armour in the heart. One’s honour before the king rises, and one’s radiance grows.
A traditional list of fears. Disease, poison, sorcery, spirits: everything people then feared. These verses are a statement of faith, not medical claims. Note where the armour is worn: hṛdi, in the heart.
Worn in the heart, the armour drives out fear.

VERSES 53–56 · THE LAST WORD

FIRST THE ARMOUR, THEN THE CAṆḌĪ
yaśasā vardhate so’pikīrtimaṇḍitabhūtale |japet saptaśatīṃ caṇḍīṃkṛtvā tu kavacaṃ purā ‖53‖yāvad bhūmaṇḍalaṃ dhattesaśailavanakānanam |tāvat tiṣṭhati medinyāṃsantatiḥ putrapautrikī ‖54‖dehānte paramaṃ sthānaṃyat surair api durlabham |prāpnoti puruṣo nityaṃmahāmāyāprasādataḥ ‖55‖labhate paramaṃ rūpaṃśivena saha modate ‖56‖
Such a one grows in fame on an earth adorned with his renown. One should chant the Saptaśatī, the Caṇḍī, only after first doing the Kavacam. As long as the earth bears its mountains, woods and forests, so long will his line of children and grandchildren endure. At the body’s end, by the grace of Mahāmāyā, he attains the supreme abode, rare even for the gods; he gains the highest form, and rejoices with Śiva.
Why it comes first. Verse 53 states the rule this series follows: chant the Kavacam before the Saptaśatī. The final promise reaches past this life: the supreme abode, and joy in the company of Śiva.
Armour first; then the story of the Goddess.

PART 2 ON ONE PAGE

Verses 18–56 in five lines
Verses 18–20. A guardian for each of the ten directions, with Cāmuṇḍā over all; Jayā in front, Vijayā behind.
Verses 21–35. The body, crest to soles: Sarasvatī on the tongue, Lalitā in the heart, Śrī at the toes, Abhedyā in every joint.
Verses 36–41. Breath, senses, mind and the three guṇas; then life, dharma, wealth, family and the road ahead.
Verse 42. Whatever was missed: “Jayantī, guard that too.”
Verses 43–56. The fruits: fearlessness, long life, freedom from harm; and the rule, Kavacam first, then the Saptaśatī.
