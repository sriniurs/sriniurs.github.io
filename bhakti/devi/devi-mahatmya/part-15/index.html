“I alone exist” · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The whole of chapter 10 in full for chanting, the story in comic panels, and the key ślokas on their own pages: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 14, Niśumbha fell, and the man who stepped out of his heart was cut down with a laugh. The Mothers and Kālī finished the asura army.

Chapter 10 is the last battle of the Devī Māhātmya. Śumbha stands alone, and so, by choice, does the Goddess. Its most famous line is her reply: “ekaivāhaṃ jagatyatra”, I alone am here in the world.

ZAPPY: The final battle! Is it the biggest one?

PROF. BOLT: It's the smallest: one against one. That's the point. The whole war comes down to the ego that thinks it is everything, facing the One who really is.

THE ONE AND THE TYRANT

Ślokas 10.1–32
The story
The taunt. Niśumbha is dead. Śumbha sneers: “You fight with the strength of others.”
The answer. “I am alone in this world. Who else is there besides me?” Brahmāṇī and all the Mothers dissolve into her. “I stand alone. Stand firm.”
The duel. Divine weapons against divine weapons; she breaks his with “huṃ”. Bow, spear, sword, shield, chariot, hammer: all cut. His fist meets her slap.
The sky. He carries her into the sky; she fights “without support”. She whirls him round, hurls him down and pierces his chest with her trident.
Peace. The sky clears, rivers flow in their courses, pure winds blow, the sun shines. Gods sing and dance.

ZAPPY: So all the goddesses were really one?

PROF. BOLT: That's what she says, in her own words: ekaivāham, I alone. Brahmāṇī, Kālī, Vaiṣṇavī, all of them. The many forms are real, and they are one.

Many forms, one Goddess.

ŚLOKAS 10.2–3

“YOU RELY ON OTHERS”
niśumbhaṃ nihataṃ dṛṣṭvā bhrātaraṃ prāṇasammitam |hanyamānaṃ balaṃ caiva śumbhaḥ kruddho’bravīdvacaḥ ‖2‖balāvalepaduṣṭe tvaṃ mā durge garvamāvaha |anyāsāṃ balamāśritya yuddhyase cātimāninī ‖3‖
Seeing his brother Niśumbha, dear as his own life, slain, and his army being slaughtered, Śumbha said in anger: “Durgā, puffed up with pride in your strength, don’t be so arrogant. Most haughty one, you fight relying on the strength of others.”
WordMeans
prāṇa-sammitamdear as life
bala-avalepapride in strength
garvam mā āvahadon’t be arrogant
anyāsām balam āśrityarelying on the strength of others
ati-māninīmost haughty
The tyrant’s complaint. Śumbha has lost everything, and now calls it unfair: “You have helpers!” It is the voice of the ego that sees only separate beings. Her answer is the philosophical heart of the whole text.
The ego sees many separate powers.

ŚLOKAS 10.5–6

“I AM ALONE IN THIS WORLD”
ekaivāhaṃ jagatyatra dvitīyā kā mamāparā |paśyaitā duṣṭa mayyeva viśantyo madvibhūtayaḥ ‖5‖tataḥ samastāstā devyo brahmāṇīpramukhā layam |tasyā devyāstanau jagmurekaivāsīttadāmbikā ‖6‖
The Devī said: “I am alone here in the world. Who else is there besides me? See, wicked one, these, my own powers, entering into me.” Then all those goddesses, Brahmāṇī and the rest, dissolved into the body of the Devī. Ambikā alone remained.
WordMeans
ekā eva ahamI alone
dvitīyā kāwhat second?
mat-vibhūtayaḥmy own powers, my manifestations
layam jagmuḥdissolved
ekā eva āsītshe alone remained
The great answer. “Ekaivāhaṃ jagatyatra, dvitīyā kā mamāparā”: I am alone; what second is there? The Mothers were never outside helpers. They were her own forms, and she draws them back. This is the non-dual (advaita) teaching of the Devī Māhātmya in one line.
“I alone exist; who else is there?”

ŚLOKA 10.8

“STAND FIRM”
ahaṃ vibhūtyā bahubhiriha rūpairyadāsthitā |tatsaṃhṛtaṃ mayaikaiva tiṣṭhāmyājau sthiro bhava ‖8‖
The Devī said: “The many forms in which I stood here by my power, I have withdrawn. Now I stand in battle alone. Stand firm.”
WordMeans
vibhūtyāby my power
bahubhiḥ rūpaiḥin many forms
saṃhṛtamwithdrawn
tiṣṭhāmi ājauI stand in battle
sthiraḥ bhavastand firm!
One against one. She removes Śumbha’s excuse. If he wanted a fair fight, here it is: the One against the one who thinks he is the greatest.
She gathers her forms and stands alone.

ŚLOKAS 10.12–13

DIVINE WEAPONS
divyānyastrāṇi śataśo mumuce yānyathāmbikā |babhañja tāni daityendrastatpratīghātakartṛbhiḥ ‖12‖muktāni tena cāstrāṇi divyāni parameśvarī |babhañja līlayaivograhuṅkāroccāraṇādibhiḥ ‖13‖
The divine missiles that Ambikā let fly in hundreds, the lord of the daityas broke with counter-weapons. And the divine missiles he released, the Supreme Goddess broke as if in play, with fierce sounds like “huṃ”.
WordMeans
divya astradivine missiles
pratīghātacounter-weapons
babhañjabroke
līlayā evaas if in play
huṃ-kārathe sound “huṃ”
A real duel. Śumbha is no Dhūmralocana: he can actually counter her weapons. But note the difference. He needs weapons to break hers; she breaks his with a sound, “as if in play”.
He needs weapons; she needs only a sound.

ŚLOKAS 10.14–17

BOW, SPEAR, SWORD, SHIELD
tataḥ śaraśatairdevīmācchādayata so’suraḥ |sāpi tatkupitā devī dhanuściccheda ceṣubhiḥ ‖14‖chinne dhanuṣi daityendrastathā śaktimathādade |ciccheda devī cakreṇa tāmapyasya kare sthitām ‖15‖tataḥ khaḍgamupādāya śatacandraṃ ca bhānumat |abhyadhāvata tāṃ devīṃ daityānāmadhipeśvaraḥ ‖16‖tasyāpatata evāśu khaḍgaṃ ciccheda caṇḍikā |dhanurmuktaiḥ śitairbāṇaiścarma cārkakarāmalam ‖17‖
Then the asura covered the Devī with hundreds of arrows; and she, angered, cut his bow with her arrows. His bow cut, the daitya king took up a spear; the Devī cut that too with her discus while it was still in his hand. Then the lord of the daitya lords seized a shining sword and a shield with a hundred moons and rushed at the Devī. As he came on, Caṇḍikā at once cut his sword with sharp arrows from her bow, and his shield, bright as the sun’s rays.
WordMeans
śara-śataiḥwith hundreds of arrows
dhanuḥ cicchedacut the bow
kare sthitāmstill in his hand
śata-candramwith a hundred moons
arka-kara-amalambright as the sun’s rays
Stripped, piece by piece. Like Mahiṣa’s forms, Śumbha’s weapons are taken away one at a time: bow, spear, sword, shield. Each is the next thing he relies on.
One by one, everything he relies on is cut.

ŚLOKAS 10.18–21

BARE FISTS
aśvāṃśca pātayāmāsa rathaṃ sārathinā saha |hatāśvaḥ sa tadā daityaśchinnadhanvā visārathiḥ ‖18‖jagrāha mudgaraṃ ghoramambikānidhanodyataḥ |cicchedāpatatastasya mudgaraṃ niśitaiḥ śaraiḥ ‖19‖tathāpi so’bhyadhāvattāṃ muṣṭimudyamya vegavān |sa muṣṭiṃ pātayāmāsa hṛdaye daityapuṅgavaḥ ‖20‖devyastaṃ cāpi sā devī talenorasyatāḍayat|talaprahārābhihato nipapāta mahītale ‖21‖
She felled his horses and his chariot with its charioteer. Horses slain, bow cut, without a charioteer, the daitya seized a terrible hammer, set on killing Ambikā; as it fell she cut it with sharp arrows. Even so he rushed at her, fist raised, and the bull among daityas brought his fist down on the Devī’s heart. And the Devī struck him on the chest with the flat of her hand; struck by that slap, he fell to the ground.
WordMeans
hata-aśvaḥhorses slain
mudgaraa hammer, club
muṣṭim udyamyaraising his fist
hṛdayeon the heart
tala-prahāraa slap with the open hand
Fist against palm. Stripped of everything, Śumbha still fights with his bare fist. She answers with an open hand, a slap, not a blow. The mightiest tyrant of the three worlds is knocked flat by a slap.
His fist meets her open hand.

ŚLOKAS 10.22–24

A FIGHT IN THE SKY
sa daityarājaḥ sahasā punareva tathotthitaḥ |utpatya ca pragṛhyoccairdevīṃ gaganamāsthitaḥ ‖22‖tatrāpi sā nirādhārā yuyudhe tena caṇḍikā ‖23‖niyuddhaṃ khe tadā daityaścaṇḍikā ca parasparam |cakratuḥ prathamaṃ siddhamunivismayakārakam ‖24‖
The daitya king suddenly rose again, leapt up, seized the Devī and went high into the sky. There too Caṇḍikā, without any support, fought him. Then the daitya and Caṇḍikā fought hand to hand in the sky, a combat such as never was before, which amazed the siddhas and sages.
WordMeans
utpatyaleaping up
gaganam āsthitaḥwent into the sky
nirādhārāwithout support
niyuddhahand-to-hand combat
vismaya-kārakacausing amazement
Nirādhārā. She fights in the sky “without support”, standing on nothing. That one word says who she is: she needs no ground, because she is the ground of everything (“jagat-pratiṣṭhā”, chapter 5).
She fights in the sky, standing on nothing.

ŚLOKAS 10.25–28

ŚUMBHA FALLS
tato niyuddhaṃ suciraṃ kṛtvā tenāmbikā saha |utpāṭya bhrāmayāmāsa cikṣepa dharaṇītale ‖25‖sa kṣipto dharaṇīṃ prāpya muṣṭimudyamya vegavān |abhyadhāvata duṣṭātmā caṇḍikānidhanecchayā ‖26‖tamāyāntaṃ tato devī sarvadaityajaneśvaram |jagatyāṃ pātayāmāsa bhittvā śūlena vakṣasi ‖27‖sa gatāsuḥ papātorvyāṃ devī śūlāgravikṣataḥ |cālayan sakalāṃ pṛthvīṃ sābdhidvīpāṃ saparvatām ‖28‖
After long fighting with him, Ambikā lifted him up, whirled him around and hurled him to the ground. Thrown down, reaching the earth, the evil one raised his fist and rushed at her again, wanting to kill Caṇḍikā. As the lord of all the daityas came on, the Devī pierced his chest with her trident and felled him to the earth. Pierced by the point of the Devī’s trident, he fell lifeless to the ground, shaking the whole earth with its oceans, islands and mountains.
WordMeans
bhrāmayāmāsawhirled around
cikṣepa dharaṇī-talehurled to the ground
vakṣasiin the chest
gata-asuḥlifeless
sa-abdhi-dvīpām sa-parvatāmwith oceans, islands and mountains
He never gives up. Thrown to the ground, Śumbha rises again with his fist. The ego fights to the very last breath. Her trident ends it, and his fall shakes the whole earth: so big was the place he took up.
The last of the tyrants falls, and the earth shakes.

ŚLOKAS 10.29–32

THE WORLD AT PEACE
tataḥ prasannamakhilaṃ hate tasmin durātmani |jagatsvāsthyamatīvāpa nirmalaṃ cābhavannabhaḥ ‖29‖utpātameghāḥ solkā ye prāgāsaṃste śamaṃ yayuḥ |sarito mārgavāhinyastathāsaṃstatra pātite ‖30‖tato devagaṇāḥ sarve harṣanirbharamānasāḥ |babhūvurnihate tasmin gandharvā lalitaṃ jaguḥ ‖31‖avādayaṃstathaivānye nanṛtuścāpsarogaṇāḥ |vavuḥ puṇyāstathā vātāḥ suprabho’bhūddivākaraḥ ‖32‖
When that evil one was slain, the whole universe became serene and gained perfect health, and the sky became clear. The portentous clouds and meteors that had appeared before were calmed, and the rivers flowed in their courses when he was felled. Then all the hosts of gods were overjoyed at his death; the Gandharvas sang sweetly, others played music and the Apsarases danced. Pure winds blew, and the sun shone brightly.
WordMeans
prasannam akhilamall became serene
svāsthyamhealth, well-being
nirmalam nabhaḥa clear sky
utpāta-meghaportentous clouds
mārga-vāhinyaḥflowing in their courses
puṇyāḥ vātāḥpure winds
Nature heals. The last verses say nothing about armies. They say the sky cleared, rivers found their courses, winds blew pure and the sun shone. When tyranny ends, the world itself becomes healthy again.
When the tyrant falls, the whole world heals.

The sky (10.22–28). She fights “without support”, hurls him down, pierces him with her trident. The earth shakes.
Peace (10.29–32). The sky clears, rivers flow, pure winds blow, the sun shines; gods sing and dance.
