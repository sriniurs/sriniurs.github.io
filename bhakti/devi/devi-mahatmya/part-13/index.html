RAKTABĪJA

DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 13 · CHAPTER 8
The Seven Mothers and the blood-seed demon · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The story in comic panels, and the key ślokas of chapter 8 on their own pages: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 12, Kālī leapt from the Goddess’s brow, devoured the army, and brought back the heads of Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa. She was named Cāmuṇḍā.

Chapter 8 is the great battle of the third carita. The powers of the gods come out as the Seven Mothers (Saptamātṛkās), and the Devī faces Raktabīja, the demon who multiplies from his own blood.

With the blessings of the Devī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A demon who multiplies from his blood? How do you beat that?

PROF. BOLT: Not by hitting harder. Watch how the Goddess changes the method. It's one of the cleverest moments in the whole text.

THE SEVEN MOTHERS

Ślokas 8.1–39
The story
Total war. His generals dead, Śumbha calls up every asura clan: the Udāyudhas, Kambus, Koṭivīryas, Dhaumras, Kālakas, Daurhṛdas, Mauryas and Kālakeyas.
Sound. The Goddess fills the sky with her bowstring, lion and bell; Kālī out-roars them all.
The Mothers. The śaktis of Brahmā, Śiva, Skanda, Viṣṇu, Varāha, Narasiṃha and Indra come forth as Brahmāṇī, Māheśvarī, Kaumārī, Vaiṣṇavī, Vārāhī, Nārasiṃhī and Aindrī.
Śivadūtī. A fierce śakti leaps from the Devī and sends Śiva himself with terms of peace. The asuras refuse and attack.
The rout. The Mothers fight each in her own way; the asura army begins to flee.

ZAPPY: Seven Mothers? Aren't they in the Kavacam too?

PROF. BOLT: Yes! In Part 1 the Kavacam lists the Mothers on their mounts. This chapter is where they come from. Many lists add an eighth, Cāmuṇḍā herself.

The powers of all the gods fight as Mothers.

ŚLOKAS 8.8–10

THREE SOUNDS AND A ROAR
āyāntaṃ caṇḍikā dṛṣṭvā tatsainyamatibhīṣaṇam |jyāsvanaiḥ pūrayāmāsa dharaṇīgaganāntaram ‖8‖tataḥ siṃho mahānādamatīva kṛtavānnṛpa |ghaṇṭāsvanena tānnādānambikā copabṛṃhayat ‖9‖dhanurjyāsiṃhaghaṇṭānāṃ nādāpūritadiṅmukhā |ninādairbhīṣaṇaiḥ kālī jigye vistāritānanā ‖10‖
Seeing that most terrible army approaching, Caṇḍikā filled the space between earth and sky with the twang of her bowstring. Then the lion roared a mighty roar, O king, and Ambikā swelled those roars with the sound of her bell. Kālī, her mouth opened wide, filling the directions, overpowered the sounds of bowstring, lion and bell with her fearsome roars.
WordMeans
jyā-svanatwang of the bowstring
upabṛṃhayatswelled, amplified
ghaṇṭābell
vistārita-ānanāwith mouth opened wide
jigyeoverpowered
A battle of sound. Śumbha has called out every clan of asuras (8.2–7). The Goddess answers with three sounds: bow, lion, bell, the same three asked for in the Śakrādi stuti (“protect us with the sound of your bell and bowstring”). Kālī out-roars them all.
Before a blow is struck, her sound fills the world.

ŚLOKAS 8.12–14

THE ŚAKTIS OF THE GODS COME
etasminnantare bhūpa vināśāya suradviṣām |bhavāyāmarasiṃhānāmativīryabalānvitāḥ ‖12‖brahmeśaguhaviṣṇūnāṃ tathendrasya ca śaktayaḥ |śarīrebhyo viniṣkramya tadrūpaiścaṇḍikāṃ yayuḥ ‖13‖yasya devasya yadrūpaṃ yathā bhūṣaṇavāhanam |tadvadeva hi tacchaktirasurānyoddhumāyayau ‖14‖
Meanwhile, O king, for the destruction of the gods’ enemies and the welfare of those lions among the gods, the śaktis of Brahmā, Īśa (Śiva), Guha (Skanda), Viṣṇu and Indra, of exceeding valour and strength, came forth from their bodies and went to Caṇḍikā in their own forms. Whatever form each god had, with his ornaments and mount, in that very form his śakti came to fight the asuras.
WordMeans
śaktayaḥpowers, the female energies of the gods
guhaSkanda, Kumāra
viniṣkramyacoming forth
tat-rūpaiḥin their (gods’) forms
bhūṣaṇa-vāhanaornaments and mount
The Mothers are born. Each god’s power steps out as a woman who looks like him: same weapons, same mount. These are the Mātṛkās, the Mothers, worshipped in temples across India in rows of seven.
Each god’s power steps out as a Mother.

ŚLOKAS 8.15–18

BRĀHMĪ, MĀHEŚVARĪ, KAUMĀRĪ, VAIṢṆAVĪ
haṃsayuktavimānāgre sākṣasūtrakamaṇḍaluḥ |āyātā brahmaṇaḥ śaktirbrahmāṇītyabhidhīyate ‖15‖māheśvarī vṛṣārūḍhā triśūlavaradhāriṇī |mahāhivalayā prāptā candrarekhāvibhūṣaṇā ‖16‖kaumārī śaktihastā ca mayūravaravāhanā |yoddhumabhyāyayau daityānambikā guharūpiṇī ‖17‖tathaiva vaiṣṇavī śaktirgaruḍopari saṃsthitā |śaṅkhacakragadāśārṅgakhaḍgahastābhyupāyayau ‖18‖
In a chariot drawn by swans, with rosary and water pot, came Brahmā’s śakti; she is called Brahmāṇī. Māheśvarī came riding a bull, holding the best of tridents, with great serpents for bracelets, adorned with the crescent moon. Kaumārī, spear in hand, riding the best of peacocks, came to fight the daityas, Ambikā in the form of Guha. Likewise Vaiṣṇavī śakti came, seated on Garuḍa, with conch, discus, mace, bow and sword in her hands.
WordMeans
haṃsa-yukta-vimānaa chariot drawn by swans
vṛṣa-ārūḍhāriding a bull
mahā-ahi-valayāwith great serpents as bracelets
mayūrapeacock
śārṅgaViṣṇu’s bow
Mirror images. Brahmāṇī has Brahmā’s swan, rosary and pot; Māheśvarī has Śiva’s bull, trident, snakes and moon; Kaumārī has Skanda’s peacock and spear; Vaiṣṇavī has Viṣṇu’s Garuḍa, conch and discus. Each is the god’s power, now acting on its own.
Each Mother carries her god’s signs.

ŚLOKAS 8.19–21

VĀRĀHĪ, NĀRASIṂHĪ, AINDRĪ
yajñavārāhamatulaṃ rūpaṃ yā bibhrato hareḥ |śaktiḥ sāpyāyayau tatra vārāhīṃ bibhratī tanum ‖19‖nārasiṃhī nṛsiṃhasya bibhratī sadṛśaṃ vapuḥ |prāptā tatra saṭākṣepakṣiptanakṣatrasaṃhatiḥ ‖20‖vajrahastā tathaivaindrī gajarājopari sthitā |prāptā sahasranayanā yathā śakrastathaiva sā ‖21‖
The śakti of Hari (Viṣṇu), who bears the matchless form of the sacrificial Boar, came there too, in a boar’s body: Vārāhī. Nārasiṃhī came, bearing a body like Nṛsiṃha’s, scattering the hosts of stars with tosses of her mane. Aindrī came, thunderbolt in hand, seated on the king of elephants, thousand-eyed, just like Śakra (Indra).
WordMeans
yajña-vārāhathe sacrificial Boar: Viṣṇu’s avatāra
vārāhī tanua boar’s body
saṭā-ākṣepatossing her mane
nakṣatra-saṃhaticlusters of stars
gaja-rājaking of elephants: Airāvata
Avatāras as Mothers. Two Mothers come from Viṣṇu’s avatāras: Vārāhī from the Boar and Nārasiṃhī from the Man-Lion (see our Daśāvatāra series). Nārasiṃhī’s mane is so wild it knocks the stars around.
Even the avatāras send their power as Mothers.

ŚLOKAS 8.22–23

CAṆḌIKĀ ŚAKTI, HOWLING LIKE JACKALS
tataḥ parivṛtastābhirīśāno devaśaktibhiḥ |hanyantāmasurāḥ śīghraṃ mama prītyāha caṇḍikām ‖22‖tato devīśarīrāttu viniṣkrāntātibhīṣaṇā |caṇḍikā śaktiratyugrā śivāśataninādinī ‖23‖
Then Īśāna (Śiva), surrounded by these powers of the gods, said to Caṇḍikā: “Let the asuras be slain at once, for my pleasure.” Then from the Devī’s body came forth the śakti of Caṇḍikā, most terrible, exceedingly fierce, howling like a hundred jackals.
WordMeans
īśānaŚiva
mama prītyāfor my pleasure
ati-ugrāexceedingly fierce
śivā-śata-ninādinīhowling like a hundred jackals
The Devī’s own śakti. The gods have sent their powers; now the Goddess sends out hers. Jackals (śivā) were thought to haunt battlefields. This fierce power will soon do something bold: send Śiva himself as a messenger.
From the Devī comes her own fiercest power.

ŚLOKAS 8.24–28

ŚIVA AS MESSENGER
sā cāha dhūmrajaṭilamīśānamaparājitā |dūta tvaṃ gaccha bhagavan pārśvaṃ śumbhaniśumbhayoḥ ‖24‖brūhi śumbhaṃ niśumbhaṃ ca dānavāvatigarvitau |ye cānye dānavāstatra yuddhāya samupasthitāḥ ‖25‖trailokyamindro labhatāṃ devāḥ santu havirbhujaḥ |yūyaṃ prayāta pātālaṃ yadi jīvitumicchatha ‖26‖balāvalepādatha cedbhavanto yuddhakāṅkṣiṇaḥ |tadāgacchata tṛpyantu macchivāḥ piśitena vaḥ ‖27‖yato niyukto dautyena tayā devyā śivaḥ svayam |śivadūtīti loke’smiṃstataḥ sā khyātimāgatā ‖28‖
And she, the Unconquered, said to Īśāna of the smoky matted locks: “Go, Lord, as my messenger to Śumbha and Niśumbha. Tell those two very arrogant dānavas, and the others gathered there for war: ‘Let Indra have the three worlds; let the gods enjoy their offerings; go down to Pātāla if you wish to live. But if, proud of your strength, you long for battle, then come: let my jackals feast on your flesh.’” Because Śiva himself was sent by her as messenger, she became famed in this world as Śivadūtī.
WordMeans
dhūmra-jaṭilawith smoky matted hair
dūtamessenger
havir-bhujaḥenjoyers of offerings
pātālathe underworld
śiva-dūtīshe whose messenger is Śiva
The last offer of peace. Even now the terms are generous: give back heaven, go to Pātāla and live. And the messenger is Śiva himself. Hence the name Śivadūtī. The asuras, of course, refuse.
Even now she offers peace; Śiva carries the message.

ŚLOKAS 8.33–35

THE MOTHERS FIGHT
kamaṇḍalujalākṣepahatavīryān hataujasaḥ |brahmāṇī cākarocchatrūnyena yena sma dhāvati ‖33‖māheśvarī triśūlena tathā cakreṇa vaiṣṇavī |daityāñjaghāna kaumārī tathā śaktyātikopanā ‖34‖aindrī kuliśapātena śataśo daityadānavāḥ |peturvidāritāḥ pṛthvyāṃ rudhiraughapravarṣiṇaḥ ‖35‖
Brahmāṇī, wherever she ran, made the enemies powerless and lustreless by sprinkling water from her pot. Māheśvarī slew daityas with her trident, Vaiṣṇavī with her discus, and furious Kaumārī with her spear. Under the blows of Aindrī’s thunderbolt, daityas and dānavas fell in hundreds, torn open, streaming blood.
WordMeans
kamaṇḍalu-jalawater from her pot
hata-ojasaḥrobbed of vigour
triśūlatrident
cakradiscus
kuliśathunderbolt
Each fights her own way. Gentle Brahmāṇī does not strike at all: a sprinkle of holy water drains the enemy’s strength. Vārāhī gores with her tusks, Nārasiṃhī tears with her claws (8.36–37). The asura army breaks and flees.
Each Mother fights in her own way.

THE BLOOD-SEED

Ślokas 8.40–63
The story
Raktabīja. As the asuras flee, the great asura Raktabīja comes forward, mace in hand. He has a terrible power: every drop of his blood that touches the ground becomes another Raktabīja.
The more they strike… Aindrī’s thunderbolt, Vaiṣṇavī’s discus, Kaumārī’s spear, Vārāhī’s sword, Māheśvarī’s trident: every wound makes thousands more.
Despair. The world fills with Raktabījas; the gods are terrified.
The plan. Caṇḍikā laughs: “Cāmuṇḍā, open your mouth wide and drink every drop and every demon born from it.”
The end. She strikes; Kālī drinks. Raktabīja falls bloodless. The Mothers dance, drunk with victory.

ZAPPY: What if you can't stop something that keeps coming back?

PROF. BOLT: Then change how you fight it. The Devī doesn't strike harder; she cuts off the seed. Many teachers read this as how to end a habit: catch it before it lands.

Cut off the seed, and the endless enemy ends.

ŚLOKAS 8.41–44

RAKTABĪJA
raktabinduryadā bhūmau patatyasya śarīrataḥ |samutpatati medinyāṃ tatpramāṇo mahāsuraḥ ‖41‖kuliśenāhatasyāśu bahu susrāva śoṇitam |samuttasthustato yodhāstadrūpāstatparākramāḥ ‖43‖yāvantaḥ patitāstasya śarīrādraktabindavaḥ |tāvantaḥ puruṣā jātāstadvīryabalavikramāḥ ‖44‖
When a drop of blood from his body fell to the ground, a great asura of the same size rose up from the earth. When Raktabīja was struck by the thunderbolt, much blood flowed from him at once, and from it rose warriors of his form and his valour. As many drops of blood as fell from his body, so many men were born, of his strength, valour and might.
WordMeans
rakta-bīja“blood-seed”
rakta-bindua drop of blood
tat-pramāṇaḥof the same size
yāvantaḥ … tāvantaḥas many … so many
vīrya-bala-vikramastrength, power, valour
The hardest enemy yet. Every wound multiplies him. Raktabīja is often read as desire or a bad habit: each time you strike at it carelessly, it spreads. The harder the Mothers fight, the worse it gets.
Strike him, and he multiplies.

ŚLOKAS 8.52–55

“CĀMUṆḌĀ, OPEN YOUR MOUTH WIDE”
taiścāsurāsṛksambhūtairasuraiḥ sakalaṃ jagat |vyāptamāsīttato devā bhayamājagmuruttamam ‖52‖tān viṣaṇṇān surān dṛṣṭvā caṇḍikā prāhasatvarā |uvāca kālīṃ cāmuṇḍe vistīrṇaṃ vadanaṃ kuru ‖53‖macchastrapātasambhūtān raktabindūn mahāsurān |raktabindoḥ pratīccha tvaṃ vaktreṇānena veginā ‖54‖bhakṣayantī cara raṇe tadutpannānmahāsurān |evameṣa kṣayaṃ daityaḥ kṣiṇarakto gamiṣyati ‖55‖
With those asuras born from the asura’s blood the whole world was filled, and the gods fell into the greatest fear. Seeing the gods dejected, Caṇḍikā laughed and quickly said to Kālī: “Cāmuṇḍā, open your mouth wide. With this swift mouth take in the drops of blood born of my weapons’ blows, and the great asuras born from Raktabīja’s blood. Roam the battle devouring the asuras born from it. So this daitya, drained of blood, will perish.”
WordMeans
sakalaṃ jagat vyāptamthe whole world was filled
viṣaṇṇadejected
prāhasatlaughed
vistīrṇaṃ vadanaṃ kurumake your mouth wide
kṣīṇa-raktaḥdrained of blood
The answer is to catch the seed. Fighting harder only makes more Raktabījas. The Goddess laughs (she is never worried) and changes the method: catch every drop before it reaches the ground. Kālī, who swallowed armies in chapter 7, is the one for the job.
Don’t fight harder: catch the seed before it lands.

ŚLOKAS 8.59–62

DRAINED
tasyāhatasya dehāttu bahu susrāva śoṇitam |yatastatastadvaktreṇa cāmuṇḍā sampratīcchati ‖59‖mukhe samudgatā ye’syā raktapātānmahāsurāḥ |tāṃścakhādātha cāmuṇḍā papau tasya ca śoṇitam ‖60‖devī śūlena vajreṇa bāṇairasibhirṛṣṭibhiḥ |jaghāna raktabījaṃ taṃ cāmuṇḍāpītaśoṇitam ‖61‖sa papāta mahīpṛṣṭhe śastrasaṅghasamāhataḥ|nīraktaśca mahīpāla raktabījo mahāsuraḥ ‖62‖
Much blood flowed from his body as he was struck, and wherever it flowed, Cāmuṇḍā took it in with her mouth. The great asuras that sprang up in her mouth from the falling blood, Cāmuṇḍā devoured, and she drank his blood. The Devī struck Raktabīja, whose blood Cāmuṇḍā had drunk, with trident, thunderbolt, arrows, swords and spears. Struck by that mass of weapons, the great asura Raktabīja fell to the ground, bloodless, O king.

Teamwork. The Devī strikes; Kālī catches. Neither alone could end Raktabīja. With nothing left to seed new demons, he falls “bloodless”. The Mothers dance in joy (8.63).
Strike and catch together: the seed is ended.

PART 13 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 8 in five lines
Total war (8.1–11). Śumbha calls up every asura clan; bow, lion and bell answer, and Kālī out-roars them.
The Mothers (8.12–21). The śaktis of Brahmā, Śiva, Skanda, Viṣṇu, Varāha, Narasiṃha and Indra come forth in their gods’ forms.
Śivadūtī (8.22–28). The Devī’s fierce śakti sends Śiva as messenger: give back heaven, or fight.
Raktabīja (8.29–52). The Mothers rout the army; Raktabīja multiplies from every drop of blood until the world is full of him.
The end (8.53–63). “Cāmuṇḍā, open your mouth wide.” The Devī strikes, Kālī drinks; Raktabīja falls bloodless.
