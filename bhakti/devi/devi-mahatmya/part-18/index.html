WHERE WE ARE

In Part 17, the Devī told the fruits of reading her story, and Medhas summed up: eternal, she comes again and again.

Chapter 13 closes the frame that opened in Part 5. The ruined king and the betrayed merchant asked: why can’t we let go? Now they act on the answer. This is the last chapter of the Devī Māhātmya.

THE KING AND THE MERCHANT

Ślokas 13.1–29
The story
The end of the story. Medhas finishes: this is her glory; she deludes even the wise; go to her for refuge.
To the river. Weary of “mine”, the king and the merchant go to a riverbank. The merchant chants the Devī Sūkta.
Worship. They make her image of earth and worship it for three years, fasting, with flowers, incense, fire, and offerings of their own blood.
She appears. “Ask, and receive it all.” The king asks for his kingdom, now and in the next life; the merchant for the knowledge that cuts “I” and “mine”.
Both granted. The king will rule again and become Sāvarṇi, the eighth Manu; the merchant receives knowledge. She vanishes.

ZAPPY: So who got the better deal?

PROF. BOLT: The text won't say! Both asked sincerely, and both received. Many readers notice the merchant's wish is the one that answers their first question, 'why can't we let go?'

Two wishes, both granted.

ŚLOKAS 13.2–5

“GO TO HER FOR REFUGE”
etatte kathitaṃ bhūpa devīmāhātmyamuttamam ‖2‖evaṃ prabhāvā sā devī yayedaṃ dhāryate jagat|vidyā tathaiva kriyate bhagavadviṣṇumāyayā ‖3‖tayā tvayeṣa vaiśyaśca tathaivānye vivekinaḥ |mohyante mohitāścaiva mohameṣyanti cāpare ‖4‖tāmupaihi mahārāja śaraṇaṃ parameśvarīm |ārādhitā saiva nṛṇāṃ bhogasvargāpavargadā ‖5‖
The sage said: “I have told you, O king, this supreme glory of the Devī. Such is the power of that Devī by whom this world is upheld. Knowledge too is given by her, the Viṣṇumāyā of the Lord. By her, you, this merchant and other men of discernment are deluded; others have been deluded, and others will be. Go to her for refuge, great king, to the supreme Ruler. Worshipped, she grants people enjoyment, heaven and final liberation.”
WordMeans
dhāryate jagatthe world is upheld
vidyā kriyateknowledge is given
vivekinaḥmen of discernment
upaihi śaraṇamgo for refuge
bhoga-svarga-apavarga-dāgiver of enjoyment, heaven, liberation
The answer to the first question. In Part 5, the king asked: “Why can’t we let go, though we know better?” After thirteen chapters, Medhas’s answer: she deludes even the wise, and she alone frees them. So: go to her for refuge.
The answer to “why can’t I let go?”: take refuge in her.

ŚLOKAS 13.7–9

TO THE RIVERBANK
iti tasya vacaḥ śrutvā surathaḥ sa narādhipaḥ |praṇipatya mahābhāgaṃ tamṛṣiṃ saṃśitavratam ‖7‖nirviṇṇo’timamatvena rājyāpaharaṇena ca|jagāma sadyastapase sa ca vaiśyo mahāmune ‖8‖sandarśanārthamambāyā nadīpulinamāsthitaḥ |sa ca vaiśyastapastepe devīsūktaṃ paraṃ japan ‖9‖
Hearing his words, King Suratha bowed to the illustrious sage of rigorous vows and, weary of his excessive sense of “mine” and of the loss of his kingdom, went at once to practise austerity, and so did the merchant, O great sage. To have a vision of the Mother, the king stayed on a riverbank, and the merchant too practised austerity, reciting the supreme Devī Sūkta.
WordMeans
nirviṇṇaḥweary, disenchanted
ati-mamatvenaof too much “mine”
tapasefor austerity
nadī-pulinaa riverbank
devī-sūktamthe Devī Sūkta: the Ṛgvedic hymn of the Goddess
Weary of “mine”. The phrase ati-mamatva recalls “the pit of mine” in the Nārāyaṇī stuti (Part 16). The story has done its work: the two men are tired of clinging. They go to act, not just to listen. The merchant chants the Devī Sūkta (Ṛgveda 10.125); see Part 19.
Tired of clinging, they go to the river to pray.

ŚLOKAS 13.10–11

A GODDESS OF CLAY
tau tasmin puline devyāḥ kṛtvā mūrtiṃ mahīmayīm |arhaṇāṃ cakratustasyāḥ puṣpadhūpāgnitarpaṇaiḥ ‖10‖nirāhārau yatātmānau tanmanaskau samāhitau |dadatustau baliṃ caiva nijagātrāsṛgukṣitam ‖11‖
On that riverbank the two made an image of the Devī of earth and worshipped her with flowers, incense, fire and water offerings. Fasting, self-controlled, their minds fixed on her, collected, they also offered her bali sprinkled with blood from their own bodies.
WordMeans
mūrtim mahī-mayīman image made of earth
arhaṇāworship
nir-āhāraufasting
tat-manaskauminds fixed on her
nija-gātra-asṛkblood of their own bodies
The clay image. They make her image from the riverbank’s earth: the root of the clay Durgā images still made each autumn and given back to the river at the end of the festival. And they offer not animals but their own blood: the self, not others, is the sacrifice.
A clay image on a riverbank: the first Durgā pūjā.

ŚLOKAS 13.12–14

THREE YEARS, THEN SHE APPEARS
evaṃ samārādhayatostribhirvarṣairyatātmanoḥ |parituṣṭā jagaddhātrī pratyakṣaṃ prāha caṇḍikā ‖12‖yatprārthyate tvayā bhūpa tvayā ca kulanandana |mattastatprāpyatāṃ sarvaṃ parituṣṭā dadāmi te ‖14‖
When the two self-controlled ones had worshipped her in this way for three years, Caṇḍikā, sustainer of the world, well pleased, appeared before them and said: “Whatever you ask, O king, and you, joy of your family, receive it all from me. I am well pleased; I give it to you.”
WordMeans
tribhiḥ varṣaiḥfor three years
jagat-dhātrīsustainer of the world
pratyakṣamin person, before their eyes
kula-nandanajoy of your family
parituṣṭāwell pleased
She came. Medhas told them about her; now they see her. The gods saw her in battle; two ordinary, broken men see her after three years of quiet worship by a river.
After three years of devotion, she appears in person.

ŚLOKA 13.16

THE KING’S WISH
tato vavre nṛpo rājyamavibhraṃśyanyajanmani |atraiva ca nijaṃ rājyaṃ hataśatrubalaṃ balāt ‖16‖
Then the king chose a kingdom that would not be lost, in another birth, and, in this very life, his own kingdom, the power of his enemies destroyed by force.
WordMeans
vavrechose
avibhraṃśinever to be lost
anya-janmaniin another birth
atra evain this very life
hata-śatru-balamhis enemies’ power destroyed
He still wants the kingdom. After everything, Suratha asks for his throne: in this life and the next. The text does not scold him. Worldly wishes are allowed; the Goddess gives “enjoyment, heaven and liberation”.
The king asks for his kingdom.

ŚLOKA 13.17

THE MERCHANT’S WISH
so’pi vaiśyastato jñānaṃ vavre nirviṇṇamānasaḥ |mametyahamiti prājñaḥ saṅgavicyutikārakam ‖17‖
Then the merchant too, his mind disenchanted, wise, chose knowledge: the knowledge that cuts off attachment, the sense of “mine” and “I”.
WordMeans
jñānamknowledge
nirviṇṇa-mānasaḥwith a disenchanted mind
prājñaḥwise
mama iti aham iti“mine” and “I”
saṅga-vicyuti-kārakathat cuts off attachment
The other answer. Samādhi, betrayed by his family yet still worried for them (Part 5), asks for the one thing that ends the worry: knowledge that cuts “I” and “mine”. His name means deep meditation; his wish fits it.
The merchant asks for freedom from “I” and “mine”.

ŚLOKAS 13.19–22

BOON FOR THE KING
svalpairahobhirnṛpate svaṃ rājyaṃ prāpsyate bhavān ‖19‖hatvā ripūnaskhalitaṃ tava tatra bhaviṣyati ‖20‖mṛtaśca bhūyaḥ samprāpya janma devādvivasvataḥ ‖21‖sāvarṇiko manurnāma bhavānbhuvi bhaviṣyati ‖22‖
The Devī said: “In a few days, O king, you will regain your own kingdom. Having slain your enemies, it will remain yours, unshaken. And after death you will be born again from the god Vivasvat (the Sun), and on earth you will be the Manu named Sāvarṇi.”
WordMeans
svalpaiḥ ahobhiḥin a few days
askhalitamunshaken
vivasvatthe Sun
sāvarṇika manuSāvarṇi, the eighth Manu
The frame closes. Chapter 1 began: “Hear how Sāvarṇi, son of the Sun, became the eighth Manu” (Part 5). This is how: the broken king, by the Goddess’s grace, rules again and in a future age becomes Manu, lord of a whole world-age.
The broken king becomes the future Manu.

ŚLOKAS 13.23–24

BOON FOR THE MERCHANT
vaiśyavarya tvayā yaśca varo’smatto’bhivāñchitaḥ ‖23‖taṃ prayacchāmi saṃsiddhyai tava jñānaṃ bhaviṣyati ‖24‖
“And you, best of merchants, the boon you have desired from me, I give for your perfection: knowledge will be yours.”
WordMeans
vaiśya-varyabest of merchants
abhivāñchitaḥdesired
saṃsiddhyaifor perfection
jñānam bhaviṣyatiknowledge will be yours
Two paths, both blessed. One asked for the world, one for freedom from it; she grants both without judging. That is the Devī Māhātmya’s last teaching: bhukti and mukti, enjoyment and liberation, are both her gifts.
The world for one, freedom for the other: she gives both.

ŚLOKAS 13.26–29

THE END
Having given each of them the boon they desired, the Devī, praised by them with devotion, vanished at once. Thus, having received the boon from the Devī, Suratha, bull among kṣatriyas, will be born of the Sun and become Sāvarṇi, the Manu.
WordMeans
yathā-abhilaṣitamas each desired
antarhitāvanished
kṣatriya-ṛṣabhabull among warriors
sāvarṇiḥ bhavitā manuḥwill become Sāvarṇi, the Manu
Seven hundred verses. Here ends the Devī Māhātmya, the Durgā Saptaśatī, “seven hundred” verses by the traditional count. The last syllables in the edition are the seed “klīṃ” and “oṃ”.
Saptaśatī samāptā: the seven hundred are complete.

PART 18 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 13 in five lines
The answer (13.1–5). She upholds the world and deludes even the wise; “go to her for refuge”.
The river (13.6–11). Weary of “mine”, they worship a clay image on a riverbank, fasting, offering their own blood.
She appears (13.12–14). After three years: “Ask, and receive it all.”
Two wishes (13.15–17). The king asks for his kingdom; the merchant for knowledge that cuts “I” and “mine”.
Both granted (13.18–29). The king will rule again and become Sāvarṇi Manu; the merchant gains knowledge. The Saptaśatī is complete.
