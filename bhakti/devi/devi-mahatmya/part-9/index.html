DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 9 · CHAPTER 4
The gods’ hymn after victory · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The whole hymn (ślokas 2–27) in full for chanting, then one page for each verse or group: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 8, Mahiṣāsura changed shape again and again, and fell under the Goddess’s foot. The second carita’s battle is won.

Chapter 4 is pure praise. Indra and the gods sing the Śakrādi stuti, one of the three great hymns of the Devī Māhātmya (with Brahmā’s hymn in chapter 1 and the Nārāyaṇī stuti in chapter 11). It is often chanted on its own.

With the blessings of the Devī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: The battle's over. Why a whole chapter of praise?

PROF. BOLT: Because the gods finally see who she is. And their hymn ends with a boon for us: she will come whenever she is remembered.

ŚLOKA 4.2 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

THE GODS BOW
śakrādayaḥ suragaṇā nihate’tivīryetasmindurātmani surāribale ca devyā |tāṃ tuṣṭuvuḥ praṇatinamraśirodharāṃsāvāgbhiḥ praharṣapulakodgamacārudehāḥ ‖2‖
When that most valiant, evil-souled Mahiṣa and the army of the gods’ enemy had been slain by the Devī, Śakra (Indra) and the hosts of gods praised her with words, their necks and shoulders bent low in reverence, their bodies beautiful with the thrill of joy.
WordMeans
śakra-ādayaḥIndra and the others: hence “Śakrādi stuti”
praṇati-namrabent low in bowing
praharṣa-pulakathe thrill of joy, hair standing on end
tuṣṭuvuḥthey praised
The name of the hymn. It is called the Śakrādi stuti, the hymn of “Śakra (Indra) and the others”. Its long, flowing metre (vasantatilakā, “ornament of spring”) is unlike the plain narrative verses: the gods sing in their best voice.
Victory is followed by praise.

ŚLOKA 4.3 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

SHE WHO SPREAD THROUGH ALL
devyā yayā tatamidaṃ jagadātmaśaktyāniḥśeṣadevagaṇaśaktisamūhamūrtyā |tāmambikāmakhiladevamaharṣipūjyāṃbhaktyā natāḥ sma vidadhātu śubhāni sā naḥ ‖3‖
To that Devī, who by her own power pervades this world, whose form is the sum of the powers of all the hosts of gods; to that Ambikā, worthy of worship by all gods and great sages, we bow in devotion. May she grant us all that is auspicious.
WordMeans
tatamspread out, pervaded
ātma-śaktyāby her own power
śakti-samūha-mūrtiwhose form is the sum of the powers
vidadhātu śubhānimay she grant good things
Chapter 2 in one line. “The sum of the powers of all the gods” describes her birth (Part 7). But the gods add: she pervades the world by her own power. Their light did not make her; it revealed her.
The gods’ power was always hers.

ŚLOKA 4.4 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

BEYOND ALL DESCRIPTION
yasyāḥ prabhāvamatulaṃ bhagavānanantobrahmā haraśca na hi vaktumalaṃ balaṃ ca |sā caṇḍikākhilajagatparipālanāyanāśāya cāśubhabhayasya matiṃ karotu ‖4‖
Whose matchless glory and strength not even the Lord Ananta (Viṣṇu), Brahmā and Hara (Śiva) are able to describe: may that Caṇḍikā set her mind on protecting the whole world and destroying the fear of evil.
WordMeans
prabhāvam atulammatchless glory
anantathe endless one: Viṣṇu
haraŚiva
na alamnot able
aśubha-bhayathe fear of evil
What the gods ask for. Not riches, not victory: they ask her to protect the world and destroy fear. Fear of evil is often worse than evil itself.
Even the trinity cannot describe her.

ŚLOKA 4.5 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

FORTUNE IN THE HOMES OF THE GOOD
yā śrīḥ svayaṃ sukṛtināṃ bhavaneṣvalakṣmīḥpāpātmanāṃ kṛtadhiyāṃ hṛdayeṣu buddhiḥ |śraddhā satāṃ kulajanaprabhavasya lajjātāṃ tvāṃ natāḥ sma paripālaya devi viśvam ‖5‖
You who are yourself Śrī (fortune) in the homes of the virtuous, and Alakṣmī (misfortune) in the homes of the wicked; intelligence in the hearts of the wise; faith in the good; and modesty in the well-born: to you we bow. O Devī, protect the universe.
WordMeans
śrī · alakṣmīfortune · misfortune
sukṛtinām bhavaneṣuin the homes of the virtuous
kṛta-dhiyām buddhiḥunderstanding in the wise
śraddhāfaith
lajjāmodesty, conscience
The most-quoted verse of the hymn. The same Goddess is fortune for one and misfortune for another: not by whim, but as the fruit of how they live. She is also the faith in good people and the conscience in the well-raised. She works from inside us.
She is fortune for the good, ruin for the wicked.

ŚLOKA 4.7 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

THE FIRST NATURE
hetuḥ samastajagatāṃ triguṇāpi doṣai-rna jñāyase hariharādibhirapyapārā |sarvāśrayākhilamidaṃ jagadaṃśabhūta-mavyākṛtā hi paramā prakṛtistvamādyā ‖7‖
You are the cause of all the worlds; though made of the three guṇas, you are not known even by Hari and Hara because of their limits; you are boundless. You are the refuge of all; this whole world is a part of you. You are the unmanifest, supreme, primal Prakṛti.
WordMeans
hetuḥthe cause
tri-guṇāmade of the three qualities
apārāboundless
aṃśa-bhūtamis a part (of you)
avyākṛtā prakṛtiḥunmanifest Nature
ādyāthe first
Philosophy in a hymn. In Sāṅkhya thought, Prakṛti is the unmanifest source from which the whole world unfolds through the three guṇas. The gods say: that is you. The whole world is only “a part” of her.
The whole world is only a part of her.

ŚLOKA 4.8 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

SVĀHĀ AND SVADHĀ
yasyāḥ samastasuratā samudīraṇenatṛptiṃ prayāti sakaleṣu makheṣu devi |svāhāsi vai pitṛgaṇasya ca tṛptihetu-ruccāryase tvamata eva janaiḥ svadhā ca ‖8‖
O Devī, by uttering you, all the gods are satisfied in every sacrifice: you are Svāhā. And you are the cause of the satisfaction of the hosts of ancestors: that is why people utter you as Svadhā.
WordMeans
samudīraṇauttering
tṛptisatisfaction
makhasacrifice
pitṛ-gaṇathe ancestors
svāhā · svadhāthe cries of offering to gods · to ancestors
Echo of Brahmā’s hymn. Brahmā began with “tvaṃ svāhā tvaṃ svadhā” (Part 6). The gods now explain why: every offering to gods and to ancestors reaches them through her. The words of ritual are her names.
Every offering travels through her.

ŚLOKAS 4.9–10 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

KNOWLEDGE THAT FREES
yā muktiheturavicintyamahāvratā tvaṃabhyasyase suniyatendriyatattvasāraiḥ |mokṣārthibhirmunibhirastasamastadoṣai-rvidyāsi sā bhagavatī paramā hi devi ‖9‖śabdātmikā suvimalargyajuṣāṃ nidhāna-mudgītharamyapadapāṭhavatāṃ ca sāmnām |devi trayī bhagavatī bhavabhāvanāyavārtāsi sarvajagatāṃ paramārtihantrī ‖10‖
You are the cause of liberation, of unthinkable great vows; you are studied by sages seeking freedom, who have mastered their senses and cast off every fault. You are the supreme knowledge, the blessed Devī. You are the soul of sound, the treasury of the pure Ṛk and Yajus hymns and of the Sāman, lovely with its sung words. O Devī, you are the three Vedas; you are livelihood for the world’s welfare; you destroy the deepest pain of all.
WordMeans
mukti-hetucause of liberation
mokṣa-arthibhiḥby those seeking freedom
vidyā paramāsupreme knowledge
śabda-ātmikāwhose self is sound
trayīthe three Vedas
vārtālivelihood: farming, trade
From the Vedas to the farm. She is the highest knowledge sought by sages, the three Vedas, and also vārtā, plain livelihood. Spiritual and practical life are both in her care.
She is the Vedas, and our daily bread.

ŚLOKA 4.11 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

MEDHĀ, DURGĀ, ŚRĪ, GAURĪ
medhā’si devi viditākhilaśāstrasārādurgāsi durgabhavasāgaranaurasaṅgā |śrīḥ kaiṭabhārihṛdayaikakṛtādhivāsāgaurī tvameva śaśimaulikṛtapratiṣṭhā ‖11‖
O Devī, you are Medhā, intelligence that knows the essence of all scriptures; you are Durgā, the boat that crosses the hard ocean of worldly life, without attachment; you are Śrī, who alone dwells in the heart of Kaiṭabha’s enemy (Viṣṇu); you indeed are Gaurī, established with the moon-crested one (Śiva).
WordMeans
medhāintelligence
durgā“hard to reach”: the one who carries us across
bhava-sāgara-nauboat over the sea of becoming
kaiṭabha-arienemy of Kaiṭabha: Viṣṇu (Part 6)
śaśi-maulimoon-crested: Śiva
One Goddess, every consort. She is Sarasvatī-like wisdom, Durgā the rescuer, Lakṣmī on Viṣṇu’s chest and Gaurī beside Śiva. The text is clear: these are not rivals but one Devī. Note “Durgā” explained as a boat across the durga, the hard-to-cross sea.
Wisdom, rescuer, Lakṣmī, Gaurī: all one Devī.

ŚLOKAS 4.12–13 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

HER SMILE, HER FROWN
īṣatsahāsamamalaṃ paripūrṇacandra-bimbānukāri kanakottamakāntikāntam |atyadbhutaṃ prahṛtamāttaruṣā tathāpivaktraṃ vilokya sahasā mahiṣāsureṇa ‖12‖dṛṣṭvā tu devi kupitaṃ bhrukuṭīkarāla-mudyacchaśāṅkasadṛśacchavi yanna sadyaḥ |prāṇān mumoca mahiṣastadatīva citraṃkairjīvyate hi kupitāntakadarśanena ‖13‖
It is most wonderful that Mahiṣāsura, seeing your face, gently smiling, spotless, like the full moon’s disc, lovely as the finest gold, still struck at it in anger. And more wonderful still, O Devī, that seeing your face angry, terrible with frowning brows, the colour of the rising moon, Mahiṣa did not give up his life at once. For who can live after seeing Death himself enraged?
WordMeans
īṣat-sa-hāsamgently smiling
pūrṇa-candra-bimbafull moon’s disc
bhrukuṭī-karālamterrible with frowns
udyat-śaśāṅkathe rising moon, red
kupita-antakaDeath enraged
Two wonders. The gods tease Mahiṣa: who attacks a face so lovely? And who survives a face so terrible? Both are her faces. The beauty and the terror are one being.
Her smile and her frown are both wonders.

ŚLOKA 4.15 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

WHEN SHE IS PLEASED
te sammatā janapadeṣu dhanāni teṣāṃteṣāṃ yaśāṃsi na ca sīdati dharmavargaḥ |dhanyāsta eva nibhṛtātmajabhṛtyadārāyeṣāṃ sadābhyudayadā bhavatī prasannā ‖15‖
Those to whom you, the giver of prosperity, are always gracious: they are honoured in the land, theirs is wealth, theirs is fame; their righteousness never fails; blessed are they, with devoted children, servants and wives.
WordMeans
sammatāḥhonoured
dhanāniwealth
yaśāṃsifame
dharma-vargathe range of right living
abhyudaya-dāgiver of prosperity
A family blessing. Many households chant this verse for the home: honour, wealth, good name, right living and a loyal family. Note that dharma is listed among the blessings: goodness itself is her gift.
Her grace shows as honour, wealth and a good home.

ŚLOKA 4.17 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

“DURGE SMṚTĀ”
durge smṛtā harasi bhītimaśeṣajantoḥsvasthaiḥ smṛtā matimatīva śubhāṃ dadāsi |dāridryaduḥkhabhayahāriṇi kā tvadanyāsarvopakārakaraṇāya sadārdracittā ‖17‖
O Durgā, remembered, you take away the fear of every creature; remembered by the untroubled, you give a most auspicious mind. Remover of poverty, pain and fear, who but you is always tender-hearted, ready to help all?
WordMeans
durge smṛtāO Durgā, when remembered
bhītifear
svasthaiḥby those at ease
dāridrya-duḥkha-bhaya-hāriṇiremover of poverty, pain and fear
ārdra-cittātender-hearted
Remember her in bad times and good. The verse has two halves: in danger, remembering her removes fear; in good times, it gives a good mind. It is one of the most chanted lines of the whole Devī Māhātmya.
Remembered in fear, she removes it; in peace, she blesses the mind.

ŚLOKAS 4.18–19 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

WHY SHE KILLS
ebhirhatairjagadupaiti sukhaṃ tathaitekurvantu nāma narakāya cirāya pāpam |saṅgrāmamṛtyumadhigamya divaṃ prayāntumatveti nūnamahitānvinihaṃsi devi ‖18‖dṛṣṭvaiva kiṃ na bhavatī prakaroti bhasmasarvāsurānariṣu yatprahiṇoṣi śastram |lokānprayāntu ripavo’pi hi śastrapūtāitthaṃ matirbhavati teṣvahiteṣu sādhvī ‖19‖
“When these are slain the world will be happy; let them not go on sinning for long and earn hell; let them die in battle and go to heaven.” Surely with this thought, O Devī, you slay the enemies. Could you not turn all the asuras to ashes by a mere look? Yet you hurl weapons at them, so that even enemies, purified by your weapons, may reach the higher worlds. Such is your kindly intent, even toward the hostile.
WordMeans
narakāya cirāyafor a long time in hell
saṅgrāma-mṛtyudeath in battle
bhasmaashes
śastra-pūtāḥpurified by weapons
sādhvī matiḥa kindly intent
Killing as grace. The gods explain her violence: she could burn the asuras with a glance, but she uses weapons so that dying by her hand purifies them. Even her enemies are her children.
Even her killing is kindness to the enemy.

ŚLOKAS 4.21–22 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

MERCY AND FIERCENESS TOGETHER
durvṛttavṛttaśamanaṃ tava devi śīlaṃrūpaṃ tathaitadavicintyamatulyamanyaiḥ |vīryaṃ ca hantṛ hṛtadevaparākramāṇāṃvairiṣvapi prakaṭitaiva dayā tvayettham ‖21‖kenopamā bhavatu te’sya parākramasyarūpaṃ ca śatrubhayakāryatihāri kutra |citte kṛpā samaraniṣṭhuratā ca dṛṣṭātvayyeva devi varade bhuvanatraye’pi ‖22‖
O Devī, your nature is to quell the conduct of the wicked; this form of yours is unthinkable and unequalled; your valour slays those who robbed the gods of their might; and thus you have shown mercy even to enemies. To what can this valour of yours be compared? Where is a form like yours, which strikes fear in foes yet is so charming? Mercy in the heart and harshness in battle are seen, O boon-giver, in you alone in the three worlds.
WordMeans
durvṛtta-śamanaquelling the wicked
atulyaunequalled
dayāmercy
citte kṛpācompassion in the heart
samara-niṣṭhuratāharshness in battle
varadeO giver of boons
The one-line summary of the Goddess. “Compassion in the heart, ruthlessness in battle”: in her alone are both seen. A mother protecting her child is both. This pair runs through the whole text.
Compassion in the heart, ruthlessness in battle.

ŚLOKAS 4.24–27 · THE ŚAKRĀDI STUTI

THE PRAYER FOR PROTECTION
śūlena pāhi no devi pāhi khaḍgena cāmbike |ghaṇṭāsvanena naḥ pāhi cāpajyāniḥsvanena ca ‖24‖prācyāṃ rakṣa pratīcyāṃ ca caṇḍike rakṣa dakṣiṇe |bhrāmaṇenātmaśūlasya uttarasyāṃ tatheśvari ‖25‖saumyāni yāni rūpāṇi trailokye vicaranti te |yāni cātyantaghorāṇi tai rakṣāsmāṃstathā bhuvam ‖26‖khaḍgaśūlagadādīni yāni cāstrāni te’mbike |karapallavasaṅgīni tairasmānrakṣa sarvataḥ ‖27‖
Protect us with your trident, O Devī; protect us with your sword, O Ambikā; protect us with the sound of your bell and the twang of your bowstring. O Caṇḍikā, guard us in the east and in the west; guard us in the south; and in the north, O Īśvarī, by the whirling of your trident. With your gentle forms that move in the three worlds, and with your exceedingly terrible ones, protect us and the earth. O Ambikā, with the sword, trident, mace and all the weapons that rest in your sprout-soft hands, protect us on every side.

The best-known lines. “Śūlena pāhi no devi” is chanted daily by many as a protection prayer. Note the change of metre: the long verses give way to short, strong lines, easy to remember. Gentle and terrible forms are both asked to protect.
“Śūlena pāhi no devi”: guard us on every side.

ŚLOKAS 4.34–37

THE GODS’ BOON
bhagavatyā kṛtaṃ sarvaṃ na kiñcidavaśiṣyate |yadayaṃ nihataḥ śatrurasmākaṃ mahiṣāsuraḥ ‖34‖yadi cāpi varo deyastvayāsmākaṃ maheśvari |saṃsmṛtā saṃsmṛtā tvaṃ no hiṃsethāḥ paramāpadaḥ ‖35‖yaśca martyaḥ stavairebhistvāṃ stoṣyatyamalānane |tasya vittarddhivibhavairdhanadārādisampadām ‖36‖vṛddhaye’smatprasannā tvaṃ bhavethāḥ sarvadāmbike ‖37‖
The gods said: “The Blessed One has done everything; nothing remains, since our enemy Mahiṣāsura is slain. Yet if a boon is to be given to us, O Great Ruler: whenever we remember you, again and again, destroy our worst dangers. And whatever mortal praises you with these hymns, O pure-faced one, may you, pleased with us, always be there for the growth of his wealth, prosperity and fortune, his riches, wife and blessings, O Ambikā.”

A boon for us. The gods ask nothing for themselves except help whenever they remember her. Then they ask for us: any human who chants this hymn. That is why people chant it to this day.
The gods’ boon is for everyone who chants this hymn.

THE BOON AND THE PROMISE

Ślokas 4.28–42
The story
Worship. Praised, she is honoured with flowers from Nandana, the garden of heaven, with perfumes and incense.
The offer. Gracious, she says to the bowing gods: “Choose, all of you, whatever you desire from me.”
The answer. “Nothing is left to do. But whenever we remember you, remove our dangers; and bless every mortal who praises you with these hymns.”
“Tathā!” “So be it,” says Bhadrakālī, and vanishes.
A promise. Medhas tells the king: “This is how she arose from the bodies of the gods. Hear now how she arose again, from the body of Gaurī, to slay Śumbha and Niśumbha.”

ZAPPY: She just vanishes?

PROF. BOLT: She comes when called and goes when the work is done. That's the boon: she comes back whenever we remember her. The next time, as Medhas says, she comes from Gaurī.

She comes when remembered: that is the boon.

PART 9 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 4 in five lines
Praise (4.2–4). The gods bow: she pervades the world by her own power; not even the trinity can describe her.
Who she is (4.5–11). Fortune in good homes and ruin in bad; primal Nature; Svāhā and Svadhā; the Vedas; Medhā, Durgā, Śrī and Gaurī.
Her two faces (4.12–23). Moon-like smile and terrible frown; even her killing purifies; compassion in the heart, ruthlessness in battle.
Protect us (4.24–27). “Śūlena pāhi no devi”: with trident, sword, bell and bow, in every direction, with gentle and terrible forms.
The boon (4.28–42). “Whenever remembered, remove our dangers.” She vanishes, to return from the body of Gaurī.

ZAPPY: What's next?

PROF. BOLT: Part 10: chapter 5, the third carita. The gods, oppressed by Śumbha and Niśumbha, go to the Himālaya and sing “yā devī sarvabhūteṣu”, the most famous hymn of the Goddess.

Part 9: remember her, and she comes. 🙏
