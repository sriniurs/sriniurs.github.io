NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 16 · CHAPTER 11
“Sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye” and the promise to return · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The whole hymn (ślokas 2–35) in full for chanting, then a page for each verse or group, and the Goddess’s boon: the forms in which she promises to return.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 15, Śumbha fell. The Goddess said “I alone exist”, the Mothers merged into her, and the whole world became healthy and clear.

Chapter 11 is the gods’ last and greatest hymn, the Nārāyaṇī stuti, with its refrain “nārāyaṇi namo’stu te” and its most famous verse, “sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye”. Then the Devī gives a boon: she will come again, in new forms, whenever the world is in trouble.

With the blessings of the Devī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Why is she called Nārāyaṇī here?

PROF. BOLT: Nārāyaṇa is Viṣṇu; Nārāyaṇī is his power, and the refuge of all beings. After the last battle, the gods praise every form they have seen, Brahmāṇī to Cāmuṇḍā, with one name.

ŚLOKAS 11.2–3 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

FACES LIKE OPEN LOTUSES
devyā hate tatra mahāsurendresendrāḥ surā vahnipurogamāstām |kātyāyanīṃ tuṣṭuvuriṣṭalābhādvikāśivaktrābjavikāśitāśāḥ ‖2‖devī prapannārtihare prasīdaprasīda mātarjagato’khilasya |prasīda viśveśvari pāhi viśvaṃtvamīśvarī devi carācarasya ‖3‖
When the great lord of asuras was slain there by the Devī, the gods with Indra, led by Agni, praised Kātyāyanī, their wish fulfilled, their faces shining like open lotuses, lighting up the quarters: “O Devī, remover of the suffering of those who take refuge in you, be gracious. Be gracious, Mother of the whole world. Be gracious, Ruler of the universe; protect the universe. You are the Ruler, O Devī, of all that moves and does not move.”
WordMeans
vahni-purogamāḥled by Agni, the fire god
kātyāyanīKātyāyanī: a name of Durgā
vikāsi-vaktra-abjafaces like opening lotuses
prapanna-ārti-hareremover of the pain of those who surrender
prasīdabe gracious!
The last great hymn. The Nārāyaṇī stuti is named after its refrain, “nārāyaṇi namo’stu te”. Fire, the god who carries every offering, leads the singing. The first word they repeat is prasīda: be gracious, be pleased.
The final hymn begins: “Prasīda, Mother of the world.”

ŚLOKAS 11.4–5 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

EARTH, WATER AND MĀYĀ
ādhārabhūtā jagatastvamekāmahīsvarūpeṇa yataḥ sthitāsi |apāṃ svarūpasthitayā tvayaita-dāpyāyate kṛtsnamalaṅghyavīrye ‖4‖tvaṃ vaiṣṇavīśaktiranantavīryāviśvasya bījaṃ paramāsi māyā |sammohitaṃ devi samastametattvaṃ vai prasannā bhuvi muktihetuḥ ‖5‖
You alone are the support of the world, for you abide in the form of the earth. By you, abiding in the form of the waters, this whole world is nourished, O you of unsurpassable valour. You are the power of Viṣṇu, of endless valour; you are the seed of the universe, the supreme Māyā. All this world, O Devī, is deluded by you; you, when pleased, are the cause of liberation on earth.
WordMeans
ādhāra-bhūtāthe support
mahī-svarūpain the form of the earth
āpyāyateis nourished
vaiṣṇavī śaktithe power of Viṣṇu
viśvasya bījamthe seed of the universe
mukti-hetuḥthe cause of liberation
The whole text in two verses. She holds the world as earth and feeds it as water. She is Māyā who deludes it, and, when pleased, the one who frees it. That was Medhas’s first answer to the king, back in chapter 1.
She binds the world as Māyā, and frees it when pleased.

ŚLOKAS 11.6–7 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

EVERY WOMAN IS HER FORM
vidyāḥ samastāstava devi bhedāḥstriyaḥ samastāḥ sakalā jagatsu |tvayaikayā pūritamambayaitatkā te stutiḥ stavyaparāparoktiḥ ‖6‖sarvabhūtā yadā devī bhuktimuktipradāyinī |tvaṃ stutā stutaye kā vā bhavantu paramoktayaḥ ‖7‖
All forms of knowledge are aspects of you, O Devī, and so are all women in all the worlds. By you alone, the Mother, this world is filled. What praise can there be for you, who are beyond the highest and lowest words of praise? When you are everything, the Devī who grants enjoyment and liberation, what words, however lofty, could serve to praise you?
WordMeans
vidyāḥ samastāḥall forms of knowledge
bhedāḥaspects, divisions
striyaḥ samastāḥall women
pūritamfilled
bhukti-mukti-pradāyinīgiver of enjoyment and liberation
“Striyaḥ samastāḥ sakalā jagatsu”. Every woman in every world is her form. This line is often quoted to explain why, in Śākta tradition, women and girls are honoured as the Devī herself (as in Kumārī pūjā during Navarātri).
Every woman is a form of the Devī.

ŚLOKAS 11.8–9 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

NĀRĀYAṆI NAMO’STU TE
sarvasya buddhirūpeṇa janasya hṛdi saṃsthite |svargāpavargade devi nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖8‖kalākāṣṭhādirūpeṇa pariṇāmapradāyini |viśvasyoparatau śakte nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖9‖
You who dwell in the heart of every creature as intelligence, giver of heaven and liberation, O Devī Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you. You who, in the form of moments, minutes and the other divisions of time, bring about change, and who are the power that ends the universe, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you.
WordMeans
buddhi-rūpeṇaas intelligence
svarga-apavarga-dagiver of heaven and liberation
kalā · kāṣṭhātiny units of time
pariṇāmachange, transformation
uparatithe ending
The refrain begins. From here on almost every verse ends “nārāyaṇi namo’stu te”. Nārāyaṇī is the feminine of Nārāyaṇa (Viṣṇu): she is the power behind him, and the ground (ayana) of all beings (nara).
Mind in the heart, time in the world: Nārāyaṇī, namo’stu te.

ŚLOKA 11.10 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

“SARVA MAṄGALA MĀṄGALYE”
sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye śive sarvārthasādhike |śaraṇye tryambake gauri nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖10‖
O auspiciousness of all that is auspicious, O Śivā, accomplisher of every aim, O refuge, O three-eyed Gaurī, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you.
WordMeans
sarva-maṅgala-māṅgalyāthe auspiciousness of all auspicious things
śivāthe auspicious one
sarva-artha-sādhikāaccomplisher of every aim
śaraṇyāthe refuge
tryambakāthree-eyed
The best-loved verse of the Devī. “Sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye śive sarvārthasādhike” is chanted at weddings, at the start of journeys, at the end of pūjās, all over India. If you learn one verse from the whole Devī Māhātmya, many would say: learn this one.
If you learn one verse, learn this one.

ŚLOKAS 11.11–12 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

SHE RUNS TO THE HELPLESS
sṛṣṭisthitivināśānāṃ śaktibhūte sanātani |guṇāśraye guṇamaye nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖11‖śaraṇāgatadīnārtaparitrāṇaparāyaṇe |sarvasyārtihare devi nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖12‖
You who are the power of creation, preservation and destruction, eternal one, abode of the guṇas and made of the guṇas, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you. You who are devoted to saving the downcast and distressed who come to you for refuge, remover of the suffering of all, O Devī Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you.
WordMeans
sṛṣṭi-sthiti-vināśacreation, preservation, destruction
sanātanīeternal
guṇa-āśraye guṇa-mayeabode of the guṇas, made of the guṇas
śaraṇāgata-dīna-ārtathe poor and suffering who seek refuge
paritrāṇa-parāyaṇādevoted to saving
Cosmic and close. One verse makes her the power of creation and dissolution; the next makes her the one who runs to save the poor and frightened. Both are chanted daily. The second is one of the most comforting lines in the text.
Power of the cosmos, rescuer of the helpless.

ŚLOKAS 11.13–16 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

THE MOTHERS PRAISED · I
haṃsayuktavimānasthe brahmāṇīrūpadhāriṇi |kauśāmbhaḥkṣarike devi nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖13‖triśūlacandrāhidhare mahāvṛṣabhavāhini |māheśvarīsvarūpeṇa nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖14‖mayūrakukkuṭavṛte mahāśaktidhare’naghe |kaumārīrūpasaṃsthāne nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖15‖śaṅkhacakragadāśārṅgagṛhītaparamāyudhe |prasīda vaiṣṇavīrūpe nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖16‖
You who ride in the swan-drawn chariot, taking the form of Brahmāṇī, sprinkling water with kuśa grass, O Devī Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you. You who hold the trident, the moon and the serpent, riding the great bull, in the form of Māheśvarī, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you. You who are attended by peacock and cock, holding the great spear, sinless one, in the form of Kaumārī, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you. You who hold the supreme weapons, conch, discus, mace and the bow Śārṅga, be gracious, in the form of Vaiṣṇavī, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you.
WordMeans
kauśa-ambhaḥ-kṣarikāsprinkling water with kuśa grass
candra-ahi-dharabearing moon and serpent
mayūra-kukkuṭapeacock and cock: Skanda’s birds
anaghāsinless
śārṅgaViṣṇu’s bow
The Mothers again, as one. In chapter 8 the Mothers came out of the gods; in chapter 10 they merged into her. Now the gods praise each Mother as a form of Nārāyaṇī. Many forms, one refrain.
Each Mother is a form of Nārāyaṇī.

ŚLOKAS 11.17–21 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

THE MOTHERS PRAISED · II
gṛhītogramahācakre daṃṣṭroddhṛtavasundhare |varāharūpiṇi śive nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖17‖nṛsiṃharūpeṇogreṇa hantuṃ daityān kṛtodyame |trailokyatrāṇasahite nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖18‖kirīṭini mahāvajre sahasranayanojjvale |vṛtraprāṇahare caindri nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖19‖śivadūtīsvarūpeṇa hatadaityamahābale |ghorarūpe mahārāve nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖20‖daṃṣṭrākarālavadane śiromālāvibhūṣaṇe |cāmuṇḍe muṇḍamathane nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖21‖
Boar-formed, lifting the earth on your tusk; Man-Lion, fierce to slay the daityas; Aindrī, crowned, thunderbolt in hand, thousand-eyed, slayer of Vṛtra; Śivadūtī, terrible, of great roar; Cāmuṇḍā, fanged, garlanded with heads, crusher of Muṇḍa: O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you.
WordMeans
daṃṣṭra-uddhṛta-vasundharāwho lifted the earth on her tusk
trailokya-trāṇaprotection of the three worlds
vṛtra-prāṇa-harātaker of Vṛtra’s life
mahā-rāvāof great roar
muṇḍa-mathanīcrusher of Muṇḍa
Even Cāmuṇḍā is Nārāyaṇī. The gentlest names and the most terrible all carry the same refrain. The garland of heads and the lotus of Lakṣmī are praised in the same breath.
Gentle or terrible, the same refrain.

ŚLOKAS 11.22–24 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

LAKṢMĪ, SARASVATĪ, DURGĀ
lakṣmi lajje mahāvidye śraddhe puṣṭi svadhe dhruve |mahārātri mahāmāye nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖22‖medhe sarasvati vare bhūti bābhravi tāmasi |niyate tvaṃ prasīdeśe nārāyaṇi namo’stu te ‖23‖sarvasvarūpe sarveśe sarvaśaktisamanvite |bhayebhyastrāhi no devi durge devi namo’stu te ‖24‖
Lakṣmī, Modesty, Great Knowledge, Faith, Nourishment, Svadhā, the Steadfast; Great Night, Great Māyā, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you. Medhā, Sarasvatī, the Best, Prosperity, the tawny one, the dark one, the Controller: be gracious, O Ruler, O Nārāyaṇī, salutations to you. You who are the form of all, Ruler of all, endowed with every power: save us from fears, O Devī; O Durgā, O Devī, salutations to you.
WordMeans
lakṣmī · lajjāfortune · modesty
mahā-rātri · mahā-māyāgreat night · great illusion
medhā · sarasvatīintelligence · the goddess of learning
bābhravī · tāmasīthe tawny one · the dark one
sarva-śakti-samanvitāendowed with every power
bhayebhyaḥ trāhisave us from fears
“Sarvasvarūpe sarveśe”. After naming Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī, the gods sum up: you are the form of all, Ruler of all, with every power. Verse 24 is among the most popular Durgā prayers: “bhayebhyas trāhi no devi”, save us from fears.
“Bhayebhyas trāhi no devi”: save us from fears.

ŚLOKAS 11.25–28 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

HER FACE, TRIDENT, BELL AND SWORD
etatte vadanaṃ saumyaṃ locanatrayabhūṣitam |pātu naḥ sarvabhūtebhyaḥ kātyāyani namo’stu te ‖25‖jvālākarālamatyugramaśeṣāsurasūdanam |triśūlaṃ pātu no bhīterbhadrakāli namo’stu te ‖26‖hinasti daityatejāṃsi svanenāpūrya yā jagat |sā ghaṇṭā pātu no devi pāpebhyo naḥ sutāniva ‖27‖asurāsṛgvasāpaṅkacarcitaste karojjvalaḥ |śubhāya khaḍgo bhavatu caṇḍike tvāṃ natā vayam ‖28‖
May this gentle face of yours, adorned with three eyes, protect us from all beings; O Kātyāyanī, salutations to you. Blazing, terrible, most fierce, destroyer of every asura: may your trident protect us from fear; O Bhadrakālī, salutations to you. The bell that fills the world with its sound and destroys the vigour of the daityas: O Devī, may it protect us from sins, as a mother protects her children. Smeared with the blood and fat of asuras, may the sword shining in your hand be for our good, O Caṇḍikā; we bow to you.
WordMeans
vadanam saumyamyour gentle face
locana-trayathree eyes
pātu naḥmay it protect us
ghaṇṭābell
sutān ivalike children
śubhāyafor our good
Her face first. Before any weapon, the gods ask her gentle face to protect them. Then the trident, the bell and the sword. The bell guards “from sins, as a mother guards her children”: one of the tenderest images in the hymn.
Her face, trident, bell and sword all protect her children.

ŚLOKA 11.29 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

PLEASED, SHE HEALS
rogānaśeṣānapahaṃsi tuṣṭāruṣṭā tu kāmān sakalānabhīṣṭān |tvāmāśritānāṃ na vipannarāṇāṃtvāmāśritā hyāśrayatāṃ prayānti ‖29‖
When pleased, you destroy all illnesses; when angered, all the desires one longs for. Those who take refuge in you meet no calamity; those who take refuge in you become a refuge for others.
WordMeans
rogān aśeṣānall illnesses
tuṣṭā · ruṣṭāpleased · angered
kāmān abhīṣṭānlonged-for desires
na vipatno calamity
āśrayatām prayāntibecome a refuge (for others)
A healing verse. “Rogān aśeṣān apahaṃsi tuṣṭā” is widely chanted for health. Its last line is beautiful: those who shelter in her become shelters for others. Refuge spreads.
Those who shelter in her become shelter for others.

ŚLOKAS 11.30–31 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

THE PIT OF “MINE”
etatkṛtaṃ yatkadanaṃ tvayādyadharmadviṣāṃ devi mahāsurāṇām |rūpairanekairbahudhātmamūrtiṃkṛtvāmbike tatprakaroti kā’nyā ‖30‖vidyāsu śāstreṣu vivekadīpe-ṣvādyeṣu vākyeṣu ca kā tvadanyā |mamatvagarte’timahāndhakārevibhrāmayatyetadatīva viśvam ‖31‖
This slaughter of the great asuras who hate dharma, done today by making your own form many, O Ambikā: what other could do it? In the sciences, the scriptures, the lamps of discernment and the first sayings, who is there but you? Yet you whirl this world round and round in the deep, dark pit of “mine”.
WordMeans
dharma-dviṣāmof the haters of dharma
bahudhā ātma-mūrtimmaking your own form many
viveka-dīpathe lamp of discernment
mamatva-gartathe pit of “mine”
Back to the start. The king and the merchant could not let go of “my” kingdom, “my” family (Part 5). The gods name that trap exactly, mamatva-garta. She spins us in it, and she is also the lamp of discernment that shows the way out.
She spins us in the pit of “mine”, and lights the way out.

ŚLOKAS 11.32–33 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

WHEREVER THERE IS DANGER
rakṣāṃsi yatrograviṣāśca nāgāyatrārayo dasyubalāni yatra |dāvānalo yatra tathābdhimadhyetatra sthitā tvaṃ paripāsi viśvam ‖32‖viśveśvari tvaṃ paripāsi viśvaṃviśvātmikā dhārayasīha viśvam |viśveśavandyā bhavatī bhavantiviśvāśrayā ye tvayi bhaktinamrāḥ ‖33‖
Where there are demons, where there are snakes of fierce venom, where there are enemies and bands of robbers, where there is forest fire, in the middle of the sea: there you stand and protect the world. O Ruler of the universe, you protect the universe; as its soul you uphold it; you are worshipped by the Lord of the universe; those who bow to you in devotion become the support of the universe.
WordMeans
ugra-viṣāḥ nāgāḥsnakes of fierce venom
dasyu-balānibands of robbers
dāva-analaforest fire
viśva-ātmikāsoul of the universe
viśva-āśrayāḥsupports of the universe
A travellers’ prayer. Forest, sea, robbers, fire: the dangers of every journey in old India. Verse 32 was chanted for safety on the road. And again the promise spreads: her devotees become supports of the world.
In every danger, she stands there.

ŚLOKAS 11.34–35 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

THE CLOSING PRAYER
devi prasīda paripālaya no’ribhīte-rnityaṃ yathāsuravadhādadhunaiva sadyaḥ |pāpāni sarvajagatāṃ praśamaṃ nayāśuutpātapākajanitāṃśca mahopasargān ‖34‖praṇatānāṃ prasīda tvaṃ devi viśvārtihāriṇi |trailokyavāsināmīḍye lokānāṃ varadā bhava ‖35‖
O Devī, be gracious; protect us always from fear of enemies, as you have just now done by slaying the asuras. Quickly bring to rest the sins of all the worlds and the great calamities born of the ripening of evil omens. O Devī, remover of the world’s suffering, be gracious to us who bow to you. O you worthy of praise by all who dwell in the three worlds, be the giver of boons to the worlds.
WordMeans
ari-bhītifear of enemies
pāpāni sarva-jagatāmthe sins of all the worlds
praśamam nayabring to rest
mahā-upasargagreat calamities
varadā bhavabe the giver of boons
For all the worlds. The gods’ last request is for everyone: calm the sins and calamities of all worlds. Many read this verse as a prayer for peace in times of war, famine and epidemic.
The last prayer is for all the worlds.

ŚLOKAS 11.37–42 · THE NĀRĀYAṆĪ STUTI

“I WILL BE BORN AGAIN”
varadāhaṃ suragaṇā varaṃ yanmanasecchatha |taṃ vṛṇudhvaṃ prayacchāmi jagatāmupakārakam ‖37‖sarvābādhāpraśamanaṃ trailokyasyākhileśvari |evameva tvayā kāryamasmadvairivināśanam ‖39‖vaivasvate’ntare prāpte aṣṭāviṃśatime yuge |śumbho niśumbhaścaivānyāvutpatsyete mahāsurau ‖41‖nandagopagṛhe jātā yaśodāgarbhasambhavā |tatastau nāśayiṣyāmi vindhyācalanivāsinī ‖42‖
The Devī said: “I give boons; choose one that helps the world.” The gods said: “Always calm the afflictions of the three worlds and destroy our enemies.” The Devī said: “In the twenty-eighth yuga of the Vaivasvata age, another Śumbha and Niśumbha will be born. Then, born in Nanda’s house from Yaśodā’s womb, dwelling on the Vindhyas, I will destroy them.”
WordMeans
vara-dāgiver of boons
sarva-ābādhā-praśamanacalming every affliction
vaivasvata antarathe age of Manu Vaivasvata, our own
nanda-gopa-gṛhein the house of Nanda the cowherd
vindhya-acala-nivāsinīdweller on the Vindhyas
A link to Kṛṣṇa. The baby girl born to Yaśodā on the night Kṛṣṇa was born, swapped with him and seized by Kaṃsa, slipped from his hands as a goddess: she is Yogamāyā, worshipped as Vindhyavāsinī. The Devī Māhātmya foretells her here.
She promises to come again: as Kṛṣṇa’s sister, Vindhyavāsinī.

ŚLOKAS 11.46–50

RAKTADANTIKĀ, ŚATĀKṢĪ, ŚĀKAMBHARĪ, DURGĀ
bhūyaśca śatavārṣikyāmanāvṛṣṭyāmanambhasi |munibhiḥ saṃsmṛtā bhūmau sambhaviṣyāmyayonijā ‖46‖tataḥ śatena netrāṇāṃ nirīkṣiṣyāmyahaṃ munīn |kīrtayiṣyanti manujāḥ śatākṣīmiti māṃ tataḥ ‖47‖tato’hamakhilaṃ lokamātmadehasamudbhavaiḥ |bhariṣyāmi surāḥ śākairāvṛṣṭeḥ prāṇadhārakaiḥ ‖48‖tatraiva ca vadhiṣyāmi durgamākhyaṃ mahāsuram |durgādevīti vikhyātaṃ tanme nāma bhaviṣyati ‖50‖
In a hundred-year drought, remembered by the sages, I will be born, not from a womb, and look on them with a hundred eyes: they will call me Śatākṣī. I will feed the world with life-giving greens from my own body until the rains come: I will be Śākambharī. There I will slay the asura Durgama, and be famed as Durgādevī. (Before that, red-toothed from devouring Vipracitti’s dānavas, I will be Raktadantikā.)
WordMeans
dāḍimī-kusumapomegranate flowers
rakta-dantikāthe red-toothed
an-āvṛṣṭidrought
a-yonijānot born of a womb
śata-akṣīthe hundred-eyed
śākavegetables, greens
śākambharīshe who sustains with greens
Goddess of the drought. Śatākṣī weeps with a hundred eyes for the suffering, and her tears become rain; Śākambharī feeds the world from her own body. Śākambharī is still worshipped, at Sakrai and Saharanpur in the north and as Banashankari in Karnataka.
In famine she weeps rain and feeds the world.

ŚLOKAS 11.51–55

“WHENEVER TROUBLE ARISES”
punaścāhaṃ yadā bhīmaṃ rūpaṃ kṛtvā himācale |rakṣāṃsi bhakṣayiṣyāmi munīnāṃ trāṇakāraṇāt ‖51‖tadā māṃ munayaḥ sarve stoṣyantyānamramūrtayaḥ |bhīmādevīti vikhyātaṃ tanme nāma bhaviṣyati ‖52‖yadā’ruṇākhyastrailokye mahābādhāṃ kariṣyati |tadā’haṃ bhrāmaraṃ rūpaṃ kṛtvāsaṅkhyeyaṣaṭpadam ‖53‖trailokyasya hitārthāya vadhiṣyāmi mahāsuram|bhrāmarīti ca māṃ lokāstadā stoṣyanti sarvataḥ ‖54‖itthaṃ yadā yadā bādhā dānavotthā bhaviṣyati |tadā tadāvatīryāhaṃ kariṣyāmyarisaṅkṣayam ‖55‖
On the Himālaya, in a terrible form, I will devour rākṣasas to save the sages; they will praise me as Bhīmādevī. When the asura Aruṇa afflicts the three worlds, I will become a swarm of countless bees and slay him; I will be praised as Bhrāmarī. Thus, whenever trouble arises from the dānavas, I will descend and destroy the enemies.
WordMeans
bhīmāthe terrible one
bhrāmaraa bee
asaṅkhyeya-ṣaṭpadacountless six-legged ones
yadā yadā … tadā tadāwhenever … then
avatīryadescending: avatāra
Her avatāra promise. “Yadā yadā bādhā … tadā tadā avatīryāham”: whenever trouble arises, I will descend. Compare Kṛṣṇa in the Gītā, “yadā yadā hi dharmasya…”. The Devī Māhātmya gives the Goddess her own promise of avatāras, age after age.
“Whenever trouble arises, I will descend.”

PART 16 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 11 in five lines
Prasīda (11.2–7). Led by Agni, the gods beg her grace: earth and water, Viṣṇu’s power, Māyā and liberation, every woman and every science.
Nārāyaṇī (11.8–12). Mind in the heart, time in the world; “sarvamaṅgalamāṅgalye”; power of the cosmos and rescuer of the helpless.
Every form (11.13–24). Each Mother praised as Nārāyaṇī; Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Durgā: “save us from fears”.
Protect us (11.25–35). Face, trident, bell and sword; pleased, she heals; she lights the way out of the pit of “mine”; calm the calamities of all worlds.
The promise (11.37–55). She will return as Vindhyavāsinī, Raktadantikā, Śatākṣī, Śākambharī, Durgā, Bhīmā and Bhrāmarī: “whenever trouble arises, I will descend.”
