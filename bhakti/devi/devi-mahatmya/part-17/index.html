DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 17 · CHAPTER 12
The Devī’s own promises · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The key ślokas of chapter 12 on their own pages: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 16, the gods sang the Nārāyaṇī stuti, and the Devī promised to return whenever trouble arises.

Chapter 12 is the phalaśruti: the Goddess herself tells what hearing and reading her story brings. It ends with the sage Medhas summing up for King Suratha: the eternal Devī who comes again and again.

With the blessings of the Devī,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is this the part that says why people read it?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly. Navarātri readings, daily readings at home, reading in times of illness: the reasons all come from this chapter, in the Devī's own words.

ŚLOKAS 12.2–3 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

WHOEVER PRAISES ME
ebhiḥ stavaiśca māṃ nityaṃ stoṣyate yaḥ samāhitaḥ |tasyāhaṃ sakalāṃ bādhāṃ śamayiṣyāmyasaṃśayam ‖2‖madhukaiṭabhanāśaṃ ca mahiṣāsuraghātanam |kīrtayiṣyanti ye tadvadvadhaṃ śumbhaniśumbhayoḥ ‖3‖
The Devī said: “Whoever, with a collected mind, praises me constantly with these hymns, I will without doubt quell every trouble of his. And those who sing of the slaying of Madhu and Kaiṭabha, the killing of Mahiṣāsura, and likewise the slaying of Śumbha and Niśumbha…”
WordMeans
samāhitaḥwith a collected mind
sakalām bādhāmevery trouble
śamayiṣyāmiI will quell
asaṃśayamwithout doubt
kīrtayiṣyantiwill sing of
The Goddess speaks for herself. After eleven chapters of being praised, the Devī now tells what reading her story does. Chapter 12 is called the phalaśruti, “the hearing of the fruits”. She names all three stories, the three caritas, as one.
The Devī herself promises to quell every trouble.

ŚLOKAS 12.4–6 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

THE EIGHTH, NINTH AND FOURTEENTH
aṣṭamyāṃ ca caturdaśyāṃ navamyāṃ caikacetasaḥ |śroṣyanti caiva ye bhaktyā mama māhātmyamuttamam ‖4‖na teṣāṃ duṣkṛtaṃ kiñcidduṣkṛtotthā na cāpadaḥ |bhaviṣyati na dāridryaṃ na caiveṣṭaviyojanam ‖5‖śatrubhyo na bhayaṃ tasya dasyuto vā na rājataḥ |na śastrānalatoyaughāt kadācit sambhaviṣyati ‖6‖
“…and those who, with one-pointed mind and devotion, hear this supreme glory of mine on the eighth, the fourteenth and the ninth days: to them no evil will come, nor calamities born of evil deeds, nor poverty, nor separation from loved ones. They will have no fear from enemies, from robbers or from kings, nor ever from weapons, fire or floods.”
WordMeans
aṣṭamī · navamī · caturdaśīthe 8th, 9th and 14th lunar days
eka-cetasaḥone-pointed
dāridryapoverty
iṣṭa-viyojanaseparation from loved ones
śastra-anala-toyaweapons, fire, water
Why Aṣṭamī and Navamī. These lunar days are already sacred to the Goddess; in Navarātri, Durgā Aṣṭamī and Mahānavamī are the high points. This verse is one reason the Devī Māhātmya is read on exactly those days.
Read on Aṣṭamī and Navamī, it protects from every fear.

ŚLOKAS 12.7–9 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

“I WILL NEVER LEAVE THAT PLACE”
tasmānmamaitanmāhātmyaṃ paṭhitavyaṃ samāhitaiḥ |śrotavyaṃ ca sadā bhaktyā paraṃ svastyayanaṃ mahat ‖7‖upasargānaśeṣāṃstu mahāmārīsamudbhavān |tathā trividhamutpātaṃ māhātmyaṃ śamayenmama ‖8‖yatraitatpaṭhyate samyaṅnityamāyatane mama |sadā na tadvimokṣyāmi sānnidhyaṃ tatra me sthitam ‖9‖
“So this glory of mine should be read with a collected mind, and always heard with devotion: it is the supreme, great way to well-being. My glory will quell all calamities arising from great epidemics, and the threefold portents. The place, my shrine, where this is properly read every day, I will never leave; my presence remains there.”
WordMeans
svasti-ayanathe way to well-being
mahā-mārīgreat epidemic
trividham utpātamthe threefold portents: on earth, in the sky, in the heavens
āyatanashrine, home
sānnidhyapresence
Her presence stays. The most loved promise of the chapter: where her story is read daily, she never leaves. That is why many homes and temples read the Devī Māhātmya every day. Note too the specific mention of epidemics (mahāmārī).
Where her story is read, she stays.

ŚLOKAS 12.11–13 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

IN AUTUMN, THE GREAT WORSHIP
jānatājānatā vāpi balipūjāṃ yathā kṛtām |pratīkṣiṣyāmyahaṃ prītyā vahnihomaṃ tathākṛtam ‖11‖śaratkāle mahāpūjā kriyate yā ca vārṣikī |tasyāṃ mamaitanmāhātmyaṃ śrutvā bhaktisamanvitaḥ ‖12‖sarvābādhāvinirmukto dhanadhānyasamanvitaḥ |manuṣyo matprasādena bhaviṣyati na saṃśayaḥ ‖13‖
“Whether done knowingly or unknowingly, I will accept with love the worship and offerings made, and the fire-offering too. And at the great annual worship performed in the autumn season, the person who hears this glory of mine with devotion will, by my grace, be freed from all troubles and blessed with wealth and grain; of this there is no doubt.”
WordMeans
jānatā ajānatāknowingly or unknowingly
prītyāwith love
śarat-kālaautumn
mahā-pūjā vārṣikīthe great annual worship
dhana-dhānyawealth and grain
Śarad Navarātri. The “great annual worship in autumn” is the root of Durgā Pūjā and the autumn Navarātri, when this text is read across India. And the kindest line: worship done without knowing the rules is accepted with love too.
Even worship done without knowing the rules is accepted with love.

ŚLOKAS 12.14–15 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

FEARLESSNESS
śrutvā mamaitanmāhātmyaṃ tathā cotpattayaḥ śubhāḥ |parākramaṃ ca yuddheṣu jāyate nirbhayaḥ pumān ‖14‖ripavaḥ saṅkṣayaṃ yānti kalyāṇaṃ copapadyate |nandate ca kulaṃ puṃsāṃ māhātmyaṃ mama śṛṇvatām ‖15‖
“Hearing this glory of mine, and my auspicious births, and my valour in battles, a person becomes fearless. Enemies perish, welfare comes, and the families of those who hear my glory rejoice.”
WordMeans
utpattayaḥ śubhāḥauspicious births: her appearances
parākramavalour
nirbhayaḥfearless
kalyāṇamwelfare
nandate kulamthe family rejoices
The real fruit. Of all the promises, this one explains the rest: the hearer becomes nirbhaya, fearless. The stories of her three victories are meant to change the reader’s heart, as they changed King Suratha’s.
The deepest fruit is fearlessness.

ŚLOKAS 12.16–18 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

BAD DREAMS AND QUARRELS
śāntikarmaṇi sarvatra tathā duḥsvapnadarśane |grahapīḍāsu cogrāsu māhātmyaṃ śṛṇuyānmama ‖16‖upasargāḥ śamaṃ yānti grahapīḍāśca dāruṇāḥ |duḥsvapnaṃ ca nṛbhirdṛṣṭaṃ susvapnamupajāyate ‖17‖bālagrahābhibhūtānāṃ bālānāṃ śāntikārakam |saṅghātabhede ca nṛṇāṃ maitrīkaraṇamuttamam ‖18‖
“In all rites for peace, when bad dreams are seen, and in severe troubles from the planets, one should hear my glory. Calamities are calmed, and the cruel troubles of the planets; the bad dream people have seen turns into a good dream. It brings peace to children seized by childhood ailments, and is the best means of making friendship among people when there is a rift.”
WordMeans
śānti-karmanrites for peace
duḥsvapnabad dream
graha-pīḍātrouble from planets
bāla-grahachildhood afflictions
saṅghāta-bhedaa rift among people
maitrī-karaṇammaking friendship
Ordinary life. These verses are about everyday worries: nightmares, sick children, quarrels in a family or a group. The Devī Māhātmya was always a household text as much as a temple one.
It turns bad dreams good, and quarrels into friendship.

ŚLOKAS 12.21–23 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

ONE READING = A YEAR OF WORSHIP
paśupuṣpārghyadhūpaiśca gandhadīpaistathottamaiḥ |viprāṇāṃ bhojanairhomaiḥ prokṣaṇīyairaharniśam ‖21‖anyaiśca vividhairbhogaiḥ pradānairvatsareṇa yā |prītirme kriyate sāsmin sakṛduccarite śrute ‖22‖śrutaṃ harati pāpāni tathārogyaṃ prayacchati |rakṣāṃ karoti bhūtebhyo janmanāṃ kīrtanaṃ mama ‖23‖
“The love I feel when worshipped for a whole year with animals, flowers, offerings, incense, perfumes and the finest lamps, with feeding of brāhmaṇas, with fire-offerings, with sprinklings day and night, and with many other enjoyments and gifts: that love is mine when this is recited or heard just once. Heard, the telling of my births takes away sins, gives health, and protects from evil beings.”
WordMeans
vatsareṇaover a year
sakṛtjust once
uccarite śruterecited or heard
ārogyamhealth
rakṣāprotection
A bold claim. A single heartfelt reading pleases her as much as a whole year of elaborate ritual. The text puts listening with devotion above expensive offerings.
One devoted reading pleases her like a year of rituals.

ŚLOKA 12.25 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

MAY THE HYMNS GIVE A GOOD MIND
yuṣmābhiḥ stutayo yāśca yāśca brahmarṣibhiḥ kṛtāḥ|brahmaṇā ca kṛtāstāstu prayacchantu śubhāṃ matim ‖25‖
“The hymns sung by you, the hymns made by the brahmarṣis, and those made by Brahmā: may they grant an auspicious mind.”
WordMeans
yuṣmābhiḥby you, the gods
stutayaḥhymns
brahma-ṛṣibhiḥby the great sages
śubhām matiman auspicious mind
The three hymns. She means Brahmā’s hymn (chapter 1, Part 6), the gods’ hymns (chapters 4, 5 and 11), and the sages’ praises. Their fruit is not things but śubhā mati, a good mind.
The hymns’ best gift is a good mind.

ŚLOKAS 12.26–28 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

IN EVERY DANGER
araṇye prāntare vāpi dāvāgniparivāritaḥ|dasyubhirvā vṛtaḥ śūnye gṛhīto vāpi śatrubhiḥ ‖26‖siṃhavyāghrānuyāto vā vane vā vanahastibhiḥ |rājñā kruddhena cājñapto vadhyo bandhagato’pi vā ‖27‖āghūrṇito vā vātena sthitaḥ pote mahārṇave |patatsu cāpi śastreṣu saṅgrāme bhṛśadāruṇe ‖28‖
“In a forest or a lonely place, surrounded by forest fire; surrounded by robbers in a deserted spot or seized by enemies; pursued by lions and tigers, or by wild elephants in the forest; condemned to death by an angry king, or in prison; tossed by the wind in a boat on the great ocean; or where weapons fall in the fiercest battle…”
WordMeans
dāva-agniforest fire
dasyurobbers
vana-hastinwild elephant
vadhyaḥ bandha-gataḥcondemned or in prison
pote mahā-arṇavein a boat on the great ocean
A list of real fears. These are the dangers of an ancient traveller, soldier or prisoner. The list builds up to one promise on the next page.
Fire, beasts, kings, storms, war: every danger is listed.

ŚLOKAS 12.29–30 · THE DEVĪ SPEAKS

“REMEMBER MY STORY”
sarvābādhāsu ghorāsu vedanābhyardito’pi vā ‖29‖smaran mamaitaccaritaṃ naro mucyeta saṅkaṭāt |mama prabhāvātsiṃhādyā dasyavo vairiṇastathā |dūrādeva palāyante smarataścaritaṃ mama ‖30‖
“…in all terrible troubles, or tormented by pain: remembering this story of mine, a person is freed from the crisis. By my power, lions and others, robbers and enemies flee far away from one who remembers my story.”
WordMeans
vedanā-abhyarditaḥtormented by pain
smaranremembering
mucyeta saṅkaṭātis freed from crisis
dūrāt eva palāyanteflee from afar
Remembering is enough. Not reading, not ritual: in a crisis, just remembering her story frees us. It is the same boon the gods received in chapter 4: “whenever we remember you, end our dangers.”
In a crisis, just remember her story.

ŚLOKAS 12.32–33

SHE VANISHES; HEAVEN IS RESTORED
ityuktvā sā bhagavatī caṇḍikā caṇḍavikramā|paśyatāṃ sarvadevānāṃ tatraivāntaradhīyata ‖32‖te’pi devā nirātaṅkāḥ svādhikārānyathā purā |yajñabhāgabhujaḥ sarve cakrurvinihatārayaḥ ‖33‖
The sage said: Having said this, the blessed Caṇḍikā of fierce valour vanished right there, as all the gods watched. And the gods, their enemies slain, free from fear, resumed their offices as before and all enjoyed their shares of sacrifice.
WordMeans
caṇḍa-vikramāof fierce valour
antaradhīyatavanished
nirātaṅkāḥfree from fear
sva-adhikārāntheir own offices
yajña-bhāgashares of sacrifice
Order restored. Everything taken by Śumbha in chapter 5 (the Sun’s office, the Moon’s, Kubera’s, the shares of sacrifice) returns to its rightful holder. The surviving asuras go down to Pātāla (12.34–35).
She vanishes, and everything returns to its place.

ŚLOKAS 12.36–37

ETERNAL, YET SHE COMES AGAIN
evaṃ bhagavatī devī sā nityāpi punaḥ punaḥ |sambhūya kurute bhūpa jagataḥ paripālanam ‖36‖tayaitanmohyate viśvaṃ saiva viśvaṃ prasūyate |sā yācitā ca vijñānaṃ tuṣṭā ṛddhiṃ prayacchati ‖37‖
Thus, O king, the blessed Devī, though eternal, comes into being again and again and protects the world. By her this universe is deluded; she gives birth to the universe; when prayed to, she grants wisdom, and when pleased, prosperity.
WordMeans
nityā apithough eternal
punaḥ punaḥ sambhūyacoming into being again and again
mohyateis deluded
prasūyateis born
vijñānam · ṛddhimwisdom · prosperity
Medhas sums up. Back in the frame, the sage turns to the king. She is eternal, so she was never born; yet she “comes into being again and again”. Her appearances are for the world, not because she needs them.
Eternal, yet she comes again and again.

ŚLOKAS 12.38–41

MAHĀMĀRĪ AND LAKṢMĪ
vyāptaṃ tayaitatsakalaṃ brahmāṇḍaṃ manujeśvara |mahādevyā mahākālī mahāmārīsvarūpayā ‖38‖saiva kāle mahāmārī saiva sṛṣṭirbhavatyajā |sthitiṃ karoti bhūtānāṃ saiva kāle sanātanī ‖39‖bhavakāle nṛṇāṃ saiva lakṣmīrvṛddhipradā gṛhe |saivābhāve tathā’lakṣmīrvināśāyopajāyate ‖40‖stutā sampūjitā puṣpairgandhadhūpādibhistathā |dadāti vittaṃ putrāṃśca matiṃ dharme tathā śubhām ‖41‖
This whole cosmic egg, O lord of men, is pervaded by her, the Great Devī, Mahākālī, in the form of Mahāmārī. She, in time, is the Great Death; she, the unborn, becomes creation; she, the eternal, sustains beings in time. In good times she is Lakṣmī in people’s homes, giving increase; and in times of loss she becomes Alakṣmī, for their ruin. Praised and worshipped with flowers, perfumes, incense and the rest, she gives wealth and children, and a mind set well on dharma.
WordMeans
brahmāṇḍathe cosmic egg, the universe
mahā-mārīthe great death, also great pestilence
ajāthe unborn
bhava-kālein good times
matim dharme śubhāma mind well set on dharma
All of it is her. Death and creation, prosperity and ruin, Mahākālī and Lakṣmī. Medhas does not soften it: the same Goddess is behind both. And her last gift in the list is not wealth, but a good mind set on dharma.
Death and birth, ruin and fortune: all are her.

PART 17 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 12 in five lines
Her promise (12.2–6). Whoever praises her and sings the three stories, especially on the 8th, 9th and 14th days, is free of misfortune and fear.
Her presence (12.7–13). Where it is read daily, she never leaves; epidemics are quelled; worship done even unknowingly is accepted; the autumn worship brings wealth and grain.
Everyday fruits (12.14–25). Fearlessness; bad dreams turned good; children at peace; quarrels healed; one reading worth a year of rituals; a good mind.
In every danger (12.26–30). Fire, robbers, beasts, angry kings, storms, battle: remembering her story frees us.
The sage sums up (12.31–41). She vanishes; heaven is restored. Eternal, she comes again and again: Mahāmārī and Lakṣmī, ruin and fortune, giver of a mind set on dharma.

PROF. BOLT: Part 18: chapter 13, the last. The king and the merchant go to the riverbank, make an image of the Goddess in clay and worship her for three years. She appears. One asks for his kingdom; the other for wisdom.
