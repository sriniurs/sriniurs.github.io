DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 14 · CHAPTER 9
The younger brother falls · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The story in comic panels, and the key ślokas of chapter 9 on their own pages: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 13, the Seven Mothers came out of the gods, and Cāmuṇḍā drank the blood of Raktabīja until he fell, bloodless.

Chapter 9 opens with King Suratha asking for more. Then the two tyrant brothers ride out themselves. This chapter belongs to the younger, Niśumbha.

With the blessings of the Devī,

ZAPPY: Two brothers. Why two?

PROF. BOLT: Some teachers read Śumbha and Niśumbha as ‘I’ and ‘mine’, ahaṃkāra and mamakāra. Mine falls first; I falls last. It's a reading, not the text, but it fits the story well.

NIŚUMBHA FALLS

Ślokas 9.1–41
The story
The king asks. “Tell me what Śumbha and the furious Niśumbha did next.”
Niśumbha’s weapons. He strikes her lion; she cuts his sword, shield, spear, trident, mace and axe, and fells him with arrows.
Śumbha rises. Eight-armed in his chariot, he fills the sky. Conch, bow, bell, lion and Kālī answer; she cries “Stop!” and the gods cry “Victory!” Her trident drops him in a faint.
Niśumbha again. With ten thousand arms and discuses; she cuts them all, pierces his heart, and beheads the man who steps out of it.
The army. The lion, Kālī, Śivadūtī and the Mothers finish the rest. Some flee. Only Śumbha remains.

ZAPPY: Why does a man step out of his heart?

PROF. BOLT: The Purāṇa doesn't explain. Many read it as the inner demon, the one hiding behind the outer one. Notice: she doesn't fight it. She laughs and finishes it in one stroke.

Pierce the heart; the hidden self steps out, and falls.

ŚLOKAS 9.2–3

THE KING WANTS MORE
vicitramidamākhyātaṃ bhagavan bhavatā mama |devyāścaritamāhātmyaṃ raktabījavadhāśritam ‖2‖bhūyaścecchāmyahaṃ śrotuṃ raktabīje nipātite |cakāra śumbho yatkarma niśumbhaścātikopanaḥ ‖3‖
The king said: “Wonderful is this that you have told me, venerable one, the glory of the Devī’s deeds in the slaying of Raktabīja. I wish to hear more: what did Śumbha do after Raktabīja fell, and the furious Niśumbha?”
WordMeans
rājā uvācathe king said: Suratha
vicitramwonderful
caritamdeeds
bhūyaḥmore
śrotum icchāmiI wish to hear
Back in the frame. We had nearly forgotten: all of this is being told to King Suratha in the forest (Part 5). Here he speaks, eager for more. The man who could not let go of his kingdom is now absorbed in the Goddess’s story. That itself is the start of his healing.
The listener is changing: he wants to hear more.

ŚLOKAS 9.5–8

THE BROTHERS CHARGE
cakāra kopamatulaṃ raktabīje nipātite |śumbhāsuro niśumbhaśca hateṣvanyeṣu cāhave ‖5‖hanyamānaṃ mahāsainyaṃ vilokyāmarṣamudvahan |abhyadhāvanniśumbho’tha mukhyayāsurasenayā ‖6‖tasyāgratastathā pṛṣṭhe pārśvayośca mahāsurāḥ |sandaṣṭauṣṭhapuṭāḥ kruddhā hantuṃ devīmupāyayuḥ ‖7‖ājagāma mahāvīryaḥ śumbho’pi svabalairvṛtaḥ |nihantuṃ caṇḍikāṃ kopātkṛtvā yuddhaṃ tu mātṛbhiḥ ‖8‖
The sage said: When Raktabīja and the others had fallen in battle, the asura Śumbha and Niśumbha were filled with boundless anger. Seeing his great army being slain, Niśumbha, burning with indignation, rushed forward with the chief asura forces. In front of him, behind him and on both sides, great asuras, biting their lips in fury, came to kill the Devī. Mighty Śumbha too came, surrounded by his own forces, to slay Caṇḍikā in his anger, after fighting the Mothers.
WordMeans
atulam kopamboundless anger
mukhyayā senayāwith the chief army
sandaṣṭa-oṣṭha-puṭāḥbiting their lips
svabalaiḥ vṛtaḥsurrounded by his own forces
The final two. Every general and champion has fallen. Now the two brothers who started it all ride out themselves. The younger, Niśumbha, goes first.
At last the tyrants themselves come to fight.

ŚLOKAS 9.11–13

NIŚUMBHA STRIKES THE LION
niśumbho niśitaṃ khaḍgaṃ carma cādāya suprabham |atāḍayanmūrdhni siṃhaṃ devyā vāhanamuttamam ‖11‖tāḍite vāhane devī kṣurapreṇāsimuttamam |niśumbhasyāśu ciccheda carma cāpyaṣṭacandrakam ‖12‖chinne carmaṇi khaḍge ca śaktiṃ cikṣepa so’suraḥ |tāmapyasya dvidhā cakre cakreṇābhimukhāgatām ‖13‖
Niśumbha took a sharp sword and a gleaming shield and struck the head of the lion, the Devī’s noble mount. When her mount was struck, the Devī at once cut Niśumbha’s fine sword with a razor-headed arrow, and his shield with its eight moons too. His shield and sword cut, the asura hurled a spear; that too, as it came at her, she split in two with her discus.
WordMeans
niśita khaḍgasharp sword
vāhanam uttamamthe noble mount
kṣurapraa razor-headed arrow
aṣṭa-candrakawith eight moons: a decorated shield
dvidhā cakresplit in two
Strike her lion, and see. Niśumbha goes for her mount first. That is the one thing that makes her answer at once. Weapon after weapon follows: sword, shield, spear, trident, mace, axe. Each is cut down (9.11–16).
Attack what she protects, and she answers at once.

ŚLOKAS 9.17–18

ŚUMBHA, EIGHT-ARMED
tasminnipatite bhūmau niśumbhe bhīmavikrame |bhrātaryatīva saṅkruddhaḥ prayayau hantumambikām ‖17‖sa rathasthastathātyuccairgṛhītaparamāyudhaiḥ |bhujairaṣṭābhiratulairvyāpyāśeṣaṃ babhau nabhaḥ ‖18‖
When his brother Niśumbha, of terrible valour, fell to the ground, Śumbha, in the greatest rage, went to kill Ambikā. Standing in his chariot, holding mighty weapons in eight matchless, upraised arms, he shone, filling the whole sky.
WordMeans
bhrātarihis brother
ati-saṅkruddhaḥin the greatest rage
ratha-sthaḥstanding in his chariot
bhujaiḥ aṣṭābhiḥwith eight arms
vyāpya nabhaḥfilling the sky
The tyrant’s cosmic pose. Niśumbha is stunned (9.16), and Śumbha rides out with eight arms filling the sky: a parody of the Goddess with her thousand arms in chapter 2. Power copied from the Divine, used for the ego.
Śumbha copies her greatness, without her goodness.

ŚLOKAS 9.19–22

CONCH, BOW, BELL, LION, KĀLĪ
tamāyāntaṃ samālokya devī śaṅkhamavādayat |jyāśabdaṃ cāpi dhanuṣaścakārātīva duḥsaham ‖19‖pūrayāmāsa kakubho nijaghaṇṭāsvanena ca |samastadaityasainyānāṃ tejovadhavidhāyinā ‖20‖tataḥ siṃho mahānādaistyājitebhamahāmadaiḥ |pūrayāmāsa gaganaṃ gāṃ tathaiva diśo daśa ‖21‖tataḥ kālī samutpatya gaganaṃ kṣmāmatāḍayat |karābhyāṃ tanninādena prāksvanāste tirohitāḥ ‖22‖
Seeing him coming, the Devī blew her conch and made her bowstring twang, unbearable to hear. She filled the directions with the sound of her bell, which destroys the vigour of all the daitya armies. Then the lion, with great roars that made the great elephants lose their fury, filled the sky, the earth and the ten directions. Then Kālī leapt into the sky and struck the earth with both hands; with that sound all the earlier sounds were drowned.
WordMeans
śaṅkham avādayatblew the conch
duḥsahamunbearable
tejo-vadhadestroying their vigour
ibha-mahā-madathe great fury of elephants
kṣmām atāḍayatstruck the earth
Sound again. As in chapter 8, battle begins with sound. The bell “destroys the vigour” of the enemy, an idea behind ringing the bell in pūjā. And Kālī, slapping the ground, drowns out everything.
Her sounds drain the enemy before the fight.

ŚLOKA 9.24

“STOP, WICKED ONE!”
durātmaṃstiṣṭha tiṣṭheti vyājahārāmbikā yadā |tadā jayetyabhihitaṃ devairākāśasaṃsthitaiḥ ‖24‖
When Ambikā called out, “Stop, stop, evil one!”, the gods standing in the sky cried out, “Victory!”
WordMeans
durātmanevil one
tiṣṭha tiṣṭhastop! stop!, stand and fight
jayavictory!
ākāśa-saṃsthitaiḥstanding in the sky
Two words each. She says “tiṣṭha”; the gods say “jaya”. In chapter 2 the asuras’ headless bodies shouted “tiṣṭha, tiṣṭha” at her. Now the word is hers, and the gods’ cheer answers it.
“Tiṣṭha!” she says; “Jaya!” the gods reply.

ŚLOKAS 9.28–31

TEN THOUSAND ARMS
tataḥ sā caṇḍikā kruddhā śūlenābhijaghāna tam |sa tadābhihato bhūmau mūrcchito nipapāta ha ‖28‖tato niśumbhaḥ samprāpya cetanāmāttakārmukaḥ |ājaghāna śarairdevīṃ kālīṃ kesariṇaṃ tathā ‖29‖punaśca kṛtvā bāhūnāmayutaṃ danujeśvaraḥ |cakrāyutena ditijaśchādayāmāsa caṇḍikām ‖30‖tato bhagavatī kruddhā durgā durgārtināśinī |ciccheda tāni cakrāṇi svaśaraiḥ sāyakāṃśca tān ‖31‖
Then Caṇḍikā, enraged, struck Śumbha with her trident; struck, he fell to the ground in a faint. Then Niśumbha, regaining his senses, seized his bow and struck the Devī, Kālī and the lion with arrows. Again, making ten thousand arms, the lord of the dānavas covered Caṇḍikā with ten thousand discuses. Then the blessed Durgā, destroyer of hardship, enraged, cut those discuses and arrows with her own arrows.
WordMeans
mūrcchitaḥfainted
cetanām samprāpyaregaining consciousness
bāhūnām ayutamten thousand arms
cakra-ayutaten thousand discuses
durgā durgārti-nāśinīDurgā, destroyer of hardship
The brothers take turns. Śumbha falls stunned; Niśumbha rises with ten thousand arms. Ego is like that: knock down one side, the other gets up bigger. The name used here, Durgā durgārtināśinī, is a favourite line in Durgā prayers.
Durgā, destroyer of hardship.

ŚLOKAS 9.34–36

A MAN FROM HIS HEART
śūlahastaṃ samāyāntaṃ niśumbhamamarārdanam |hṛdi vivyādha śūlena vegāviddhena caṇḍikā ‖34‖bhinnasya tasya śūlena hṛdayānniḥsṛto’paraḥ |mahābalo mahāvīryastiṣṭheti puruṣo vadan ‖35‖tasya niṣkrāmato devī prahasya svanavattataḥ |śiraściccheda khaḍgena tato’sāvapatadbhuvi ‖36‖
As Niśumbha, scourge of the gods, came at her with a trident in hand, Caṇḍikā pierced him in the heart with her swiftly hurled trident. From the heart of the pierced one came out another man, of great strength and valour, crying “Stop!” As he was coming out, the Devī laughed aloud and cut off his head with her sword. Then he fell to the ground.
WordMeans
hṛdi vivyādhapierced in the heart
niḥsṛtaḥ aparaḥanother came out
puruṣaḥa man, a person
prahasya svanavatlaughing aloud
apatat bhuvifell to the ground
Like Mahiṣa. In chapter 3 a man half emerged from the buffalo’s mouth. Here a man steps out of Niśumbha’s heart. Readers often see it as the ego’s last disguise: pierce the heart, and another self pops out, still shouting “Stop!” She laughs, and ends it.
Even the ego’s last disguise makes her laugh.

ŚLOKAS 9.37–41

THE MOTHERS FINISH THE ARMY
tataḥ siṃhaścakhādogradaṃṣṭrākṣuṇṇaśirodharān |asurāṃstāṃstathā kālī śivadūtī tathāparān ‖37‖kaumārīśaktinirbhinnāḥ kecinneśurmahāsurāḥ |brahmāṇīmantrapūtena toyenānye nirākṛtāḥ ‖38‖māheśvarītriśūlena bhinnāḥ petustathāpare |vārāhītuṇḍaghātena keciccūrṇīkṛtā bhuvi ‖39‖khaṇḍaṃ khaṇḍaṃ ca cakreṇa vaiṣṇavyā dānavāḥ kṛtāḥ |vajreṇa caindrīhastāgravimuktena tathāpare ‖40‖kecidvineśurasurāḥ kecinnaṣṭā mahāhavāt |bhakṣitāścāpare kālīśivadūtīmṛgādhipaiḥ ‖41‖
The lion devoured asuras whose necks its fangs had crushed; Kālī and Śivadūtī devoured others. Some fell to Kaumārī’s spear; others were driven off by Brahmāṇī’s mantra-purified water; others by Māheśvarī’s trident, Vārāhī’s snout, Vaiṣṇavī’s discus and Aindrī’s thunderbolt. Some perished, some fled the battle, and others were eaten by Kālī, Śivadūtī and the lion.
WordMeans
mantra-pūtena toyenawith water purified by mantra
tuṇḍa-ghātablows of the snout
khaṇḍaṃ khaṇḍamto pieces
naṣṭāḥ mahāhavātfled from the great battle
mṛga-adhipalord of beasts: the lion
Not all die. The last line is honest: some perished, but “some fled”. Brahmāṇī still uses only holy water. Only Śumbha now remains.
One brother left: Śumbha stands alone.

PART 14 ON ONE PAGE

Chapter 9 in five lines
The frame (9.1–3). King Suratha, absorbed now, asks what happened next.
Niśumbha (9.4–16). He strikes her lion; she cuts every weapon he lifts and fells him.
Śumbha (9.17–28). Eight-armed in his chariot; conch, bow, bell, lion and Kālī answer; “Stop!” “Victory!”; her trident drops him in a faint.
The heart (9.29–36). Niśumbha rises with ten thousand arms; she pierces his heart, laughs at the man who steps out, and beheads him.
The army (9.37–41). The lion, Kālī, Śivadūtī and the Mothers finish it. Only Śumbha remains.

PROF. BOLT: Part 15: chapter 10. Śumbha taunts her: “You fight with the help of others.” She answers: “I am alone in this world. Who else is there besides me?” And all the Mothers merge into her.
