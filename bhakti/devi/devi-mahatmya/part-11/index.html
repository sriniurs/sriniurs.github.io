Smoke-eyes and the syllable huṃ · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: The whole of chapter 6 in full for chanting, the story in comic panels, and the key ślokas on their own pages: the chant, a picture, what it says, the key words, one insight and the one thing to remember.

WHERE WE ARE

In Part 10, the gods sang “yā devī sarvabhūteṣu”. Kauśikī stepped out of Pārvatī, Śumbha proposed marriage, and she answered with her vow: “Whoever conquers me in battle shall be my husband.”

Chapter 6 is short and sharp. Śumbha sends his general Dhūmralocana, “smoke-eyed”, to drag her by the hair. It is the first of four battles of the third carita, each against a stronger enemy.

ZAPPY: Why does she let them keep attacking?

PROF. BOLT: It's a ladder. Each enemy is a harder one: Dhūmralocana, then Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa, then Raktabīja, then the brothers themselves. Watch how she answers each one.

SMOKE-EYES

Ślokas 6.1–24
The story
The report. The messenger tells Śumbha her answer. Furious, Śumbha orders Dhūmralocana: “Bring her by force, dragged by the hair. Kill anyone who protects her.”
The threat. With 60,000 asuras he reaches the snowy mountain: “Come to my master willingly, or I drag you by your hair.”
Her reply. “You are mighty, with an army; if you take me by force, what can I do?”
“Huṃ”. He charges, and with that one sound she burns him to ash. Her arrows and her lion destroy his whole army in a moment.
Next. Śumbha, lip trembling with rage, sends the great asuras Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa with a larger force.

ZAPPY: Sixty thousand soldiers, and the chapter is so short!

PROF. BOLT: That's the point. Brute force doesn't even make a good story against her. The real battle is still ahead.

Against her, brute force doesn’t last a chapter.

ŚLOKAS 6.2–5

“DRAG HER BY THE HAIR”
ityākarṇya vaco devyāḥ sa dūto’marṣapūritaḥ |samācaṣṭa samāgamya daityarājāya vistarāt ‖2‖tasya dūtasya tadvākyamākarṇyāsurarāṭ tataḥ |sakrodhaḥ prāha daityānāmadhipaṃ dhūmralocanam ‖3‖he dhūmralocanāśu tvaṃ svasainyaparivāritaḥ |tāmānaya balādduṣṭāṃ keśākarṣaṇavihvalām ‖4‖tatparitrāṇadaḥ kaścidyadi vottiṣṭhate paraḥ |sa hantavyo’maro vāpi yakṣo gandharva eva vā ‖5‖
Hearing the Devī’s words, the messenger, full of indignation, went back and told the king of the daityas everything in detail. Hearing his report, the asura king, in anger, said to Dhūmralocana, chief of the daityas: “Dhūmralocana, go quickly with your army and bring that wicked woman here by force, distraught, dragged by her hair. If anyone rises to protect her, be he god, yakṣa or gandharva, he must be killed.”
WordMeans
amarṣa-pūritaḥfull of indignation
dhūmra-locana“smoke-eyed”
balātby force
keśa-ākarṣaṇadragging by the hair
paritrāṇa-daa protector
The insult. Śumbha does not hear a challenge; he hears a woman refusing him. His answer is to send force: drag her by the hair. Note the name of his general: Dhūmralocana, “smoke-eyed”, whose sight is clouded.
Pride hears a “no” and reaches for force.

ŚLOKAS 6.7–9

SIXTY THOUSAND ASURAS
tenājñaptastataḥ śīghraṃ sa daityo dhūmralocanaḥ |vṛtaḥ ṣaṣṭyā sahasrāṇāmasurāṇāṃ drutaṃ yayau ‖7‖sa dṛṣṭvā tāṃ tato devīṃ tuhinācalasaṃsthitām |jagādoccaiḥ prayāhīti mūlaṃ śumbhaniśumbhayoḥ ‖8‖na cetprītyādya bhavatī madbhartāramupaiṣyati |tato balānnayāmyeṣa keśākarṣaṇavihvalām ‖9‖
So commanded, the daitya Dhūmralocana went swiftly, surrounded by sixty thousand asuras. Seeing the Devī standing on the snowy mountain, he shouted: “Come to Śumbha and Niśumbha! If you will not come to my master gladly today, I will take you by force, distraught, dragged by your hair.”
WordMeans
ṣaṣṭyā sahasrāṇāmwith sixty thousand
tuhina-acalathe snowy mountain
prayāhigo! come along!
prītyāwillingly
bhartāram(my) master
Willingly or by force. The same false choice every bully offers. Dhūmralocana repeats his master’s threat word for word: he has no thought of his own.
“Come willingly, or be dragged.”

ŚLOKA 6.11

“THEN WHAT CAN I DO?”
daityeśvareṇa prahito balavānbalasaṃvṛtaḥ |balānnayasi māmevaṃ tataḥ kiṃ te karomyaham ‖11‖
The Devī said: “Sent by the lord of the daityas, mighty yourself and surrounded by an army: if you take me by force like this, then what can I do to you?”
WordMeans
prahitaḥsent
balavān bala-saṃvṛtaḥmighty, and surrounded by might
kiṃ te karomi ahamwhat can I do to you?
Deadpan. She plays helpless: “You are so strong, with such an army, what can I possibly do?” It is irony, like her “little understanding” in chapter 5. The next verse shows exactly what she can do.
Her humility is a trap of irony.

ŚLOKA 6.13

“HUṂ”
ityuktaḥ so’bhyadhāvattāmasuro dhūmralocanaḥ |huṅkāreṇaiva taṃ bhasma sā cakārāmbikā tadā ‖13‖
Spoken to thus, the asura Dhūmralocana rushed at her; and Ambikā, with a mere “huṃ”, reduced him to ashes.
WordMeans
abhyadhāvatrushed at
huṃ-kāreṇa evawith just the sound “huṃ”
bhasma cakāramade into ashes
One syllable. No weapon, no lion: just “huṃ”. In mantra tradition huṃ is a powerful seed syllable of protection and destruction. The general whose eyes were clouded with smoke is himself turned to ash.
One syllable from her turns pride to ash.

ŚLOKAS 6.14–15

THE LION LEAPS
atha kruddhaṃ mahāsainyamasurāṇāṃ tathāmbikā |vavarṣa sāyakaistīkṣṇaistathā śaktiparaśvadhaiḥ ‖14‖tato dhutasaṭaḥ kopātkṛtvā nādaṃ subhairavam |papātāsurasenāyāṃ siṃho devyāḥ svavāhanaḥ ‖15‖
Then Ambikā rained sharp arrows, spears and axes on the great, enraged army of the asuras. Then the Devī’s own mount, the lion, shaking its mane in anger and giving a most terrible roar, fell upon the asura army.
WordMeans
sāyakaarrows
paraśvadhaaxes
dhuta-saṭaḥshaking its mane
su-bhairavammost terrible
sva-vāhanaher own mount
The lion again. As in chapter 3, her vāhana fights as a hero in his own right. Sixty thousand asuras, and she leaves most of them to the lion.
Her lion alone is more than an army.

ŚLOKAS 6.16–19

SIXTY THOUSAND IN A MOMENT
kāṃścitkaraprahāreṇa daityānāsyena cāparān |ākrāntyā cādhareṇānyān jaghāna sa mahāsurān ‖16‖keṣāñcitpāṭayāmāsa nakhaiḥ koṣṭhāni kesarī |tathā talaprahāreṇa śirāṃsi kṛtavānpṛthak ‖17‖vicchinnabāhuśirasaḥ kṛtāstena tathāpare |papau ca rudhiraṃ koṣṭhādanyeṣāṃ dhutakesaraḥ ‖18‖kṣaṇena tadbalaṃ sarvaṃ kṣayaṃ nītaṃ mahātmanā |tena kesariṇā devyā vāhanenātikopinā ‖19‖
Some daityas it killed with blows of its paw, others with its jaws, others by trampling them under its hind feet. The lion tore open the bellies of some with its claws and with a slap struck off the heads of others. Others it left without arms and heads; and shaking its mane, it drank the blood from the bellies of others. In a moment the whole army was destroyed by that great-souled, furious lion, the Devī’s mount.
WordMeans
kara-prahārablow of the paw
āsyamouth, jaws
nakhaclaws
kṣaṇenain a moment
mahātmangreat-souled
“Great-souled” lion. The text calls the lion mahātman, a word used for saints. The fierce details are the Purāṇa’s way of saying: what serves her is itself holy, and nothing stands against it.
Even her lion is called great-souled.

ŚLOKAS 6.20–24

ŚUMBHA SENDS CAṆḌA AND MUṆḌA
śrutvā tamasuraṃ devyā nihataṃ dhūmralocanam |balaṃ ca kṣayitaṃ kṛtsnaṃ devīkesariṇā tataḥ ‖20‖cukopa daityādhipatiḥ śumbhaḥ prasphuritādharaḥ |ājñāpayāmāsa ca tau caṇḍamuṇḍau mahāsurau ‖21‖he caṇḍa he muṇḍa balairbahubhiḥ parivāritau |tatra gacchata gatvā ca sā samānīyatāṃ laghu ‖22‖keśeṣvākṛṣya baddhvā vā yadi vaḥ saṃśayo yudhi |tadāśeṣāyudhaiḥ sarvairasurairvinihanyatām ‖23‖tasyāṃ hatāyāṃ duṣṭāyāṃ siṃhe ca vinipātite |śīghramāgamyatāṃ baddhvā gṛhītvā tāmathāmbikām ‖24‖
Hearing that the asura Dhūmralocana was slain by the Devī and his whole army destroyed by her lion, Śumbha, lord of the daityas, grew angry, his lip trembling, and commanded the two great asuras Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa: “Caṇḍa! Muṇḍa! Go there with large forces and bring her quickly, dragged by the hair or bound. If you are in doubt in the fight, then let all the asuras strike her with every weapon. When that wicked woman is wounded and the lion struck down, seize Ambikā, bind her and come back quickly.”
WordMeans
prasphurita-adharaḥhis lip trembling
caṇḍa · muṇḍatwo great asura generals
laghuquickly
baddhvāhaving bound her
saṃśayadoubt
Learning nothing. One general has become ash with a single sound; Śumbha’s answer is to send two bigger ones with bigger armies. Notice the new hedge: “if you are in doubt in the fight”. Fear is starting to creep in.
Pride doubles its bet instead of learning.
