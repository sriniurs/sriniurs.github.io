ARGALĀ STOTRAM

DEVĪ MĀHĀTMYA · PART 3 · THE BOLT
“Rūpaṃ dehi, jayaṃ dehi” · explained and illustrated
HOW THIS WORKS: All 25 verses for chanting, then what “argalā” means and the famous four requests. Verses 1, 2, 24 and 25 get a page each; the 21 refrain verses come four to six to a page, each with a picture and its meaning.

WHERE WE ARE

In Parts 1 and 2, the reader put on the Devī Kavacam, armour from crest to sole. Now comes the second limb: the Argalā Stotram.

An argalā is a bolt, the heavy bar across a door. This hymn lifts it. Its 25 verses praise the Goddess for her victories and ask her, again and again, for four things: rūpaṃ dehi, jayaṃ dehi, yaśo dehi, dviṣo jahi.

Twenty-one times the same line. By the end, nobody forgets it.

THE FOUR REQUESTS

rūpaṃ dehi · jayaṃ dehi · yaśo dehi · dviṣo jahi
RŪPAṂ DEHI“Give form.” Outer: beauty, a good presence. Inner: many teachers read rūpa as one’s true nature, the Self.
JAYAṂ DEHI“Give victory.” Outer: success in what we undertake. Inner: victory over oneself.
YAŚO DEHI“Give glory.” Outer: a good name. Inner: the honour of a life well lived.
DVIṢO JAHI“Destroy the enemies.” Outer: those who wish us harm. Inner: the six inner enemies, desire, anger, greed, delusion, pride and envy.

Three “give”s and one “destroy”. Dehi is the plainest Sanskrit word for “give”, the word a child says to its mother. The fourth line flips it: jahi, “strike down”. The outer meanings are the plain sense; the inner readings are those of later teachers.

ZAPPY: So which meaning is right?

PROF. BOLT: Both! Ask for the outer things honestly, like a child asking its mother, and let the inner meaning grow on you with each repetition.

VERSE 1

ELEVEN NAMES
jayantī maṅgalā kālībhadrakālī kapālinī |durgā kṣamā śivā dhātrīsvāhā svadhā namo’stu te ‖1‖
Jayantī, Maṅgalā, Kālī, Bhadrakālī, Kapālinī; Durgā, Kṣamā, Śivā, Dhātrī, Svāhā, Svadhā: salutations to you.
The Victorious, the Auspicious, the Dark One, the Gracious Kālī, the Skull-bearer; Durgā, Patience, the Kind One, the Nurse of all, and the offerings to gods and ancestors: we bow to you.
WordMeans
jayantī · maṅgalāthe victorious · the auspicious
kālī · bhadrakālīthe dark one · the gracious Kālī
kapālinībearer of skulls
kṣamā · dhātrīpatience, forgiveness · the nurse, sustainer
svāhā · svadhāthe cry of offering to gods · to ancestors
The most chanted line of the Saptaśatī. Many people who know nothing else of the text know this verse. Eleven names run from fierce (Kālī, Kapālinī) to gentle (Kṣamā, Dhātrī) and end with svāhā and svadhā: she is even the offering itself.
Fierce and gentle, even the offering is she.

VERSE 2

VICTORY TO YOU
jaya tvaṃ devi cāmuṇḍejaya bhūtārtihāriṇi |jaya sarvagate devikālarātri namo’stu te ‖2‖
Victory to you, O Devī Cāmuṇḍā! Victory to you who take away the pain of all beings! Victory to you, Devī, present everywhere! O Kālarātri, salutations to you.
Victory to you, fierce Cāmuṇḍā, who ends the suffering of every creature; victory to you who are everywhere; Dark Night of destruction, we bow.
WordMeans
jayavictory!
bhūta-ārti-hāriṇiremover of beings’ pain
sarva-gateO all-pervading one
kāla-rātrithe night of time, the dark night of dissolution
Three cheers. Three times jaya, “victory!”, like a crowd cheering a champion. Then from verse 3 the pattern changes: every verse names one of her deeds and ends with the same four requests.
Jaya, jaya, jaya: victory to the Mother.

VERSES 3–6

HER FOUR GREAT VICTORIES

3 · MADHU AND KAIṬABHA

madhukaiṭabhavidrāvi vidhātṛvarade namaḥ

You who routed Madhu and Kaiṭabha, and gave Brahmā his boon: salutations. (Chapter 1)

4 · MAHIṢĀSURA

mahiṣāsuranirṇāśi bhaktānāṃ sukhade namaḥ

Destroyer of the buffalo demon Mahiṣa, giver of joy to devotees: salutations. (Chapters 2–4)

5 · RAKTABĪJA, CAṆḌA, MUṆḌA

raktabījavadhe devi caṇḍamuṇḍavināśini

Slayer of Raktabīja, destroyer of Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa. (Chapters 7–8)

6 · ŚUMBHA AND NIŚUMBHA

śumbhasyaiva niśumbhasya dhūmrākṣasya ca mardini

Crusher of Śumbha, Niśumbha and Dhūmrākṣa. (Chapters 6, 9–10)

after each: rūpaṃ dehi jayaṃ dehi · yaśo dehi dviṣo jahi

The whole story in four lines. These verses list the Goddess’s victories in the order the Devī Māhātmya tells them. Saying them is like reading the book’s contents page before you begin.

Four battles, four victories: the whole story in brief.

VERSES 7–11

FOR THOSE WHO BOW TO YOU

7 · HER WORSHIPPED FEET

vanditāṅghriyuge devi sarvasaubhāgyadāyini

Goddess whose two feet are worshipped, giver of every good fortune.

8 · BEYOND THOUGHT

acintyarūpacarite sarvaśatruvināśini

You whose form and deeds are beyond imagining, destroyer of all enemies.

9 · FOR THOSE WHO BOW

natebhyaḥ sarvadā bhaktyā caṇḍike duritāpahe

O Caṇḍikā, remover of wrongs, for those who always bow to you with devotion…

10 · FOR THOSE WHO PRAISE

stuvadbhyo bhaktipūrvaṃ tvāṃ caṇḍike vyādhināśini

O Caṇḍikā, destroyer of illness, for those who praise you with devotion…

11 · FOR THOSE WHO WORSHIP

caṇḍike satataṃ ye tvām arcayantīha bhaktitaḥ

O Caṇḍikā, for those who worship you here always, with love…

after each: rūpaṃ dehi jayaṃ dehi · yaśo dehi dviṣo jahi

Who the requests are for. Verses 9–11 name the devotee: one who bows, one who praises, one who worships. The four requests that follow are asked for them, and for ourselves.

Bow, praise, worship: and ask.

VERSES 12–17

WHAT WE ASK FOR

12 · HEALTH AND HAPPINESS

dehi saubhāgyam ārogyaṃ dehi me paramaṃ sukham

Give me good fortune and health; give me the highest happiness.

13 · STRENGTH

vidhehi dviṣatāṃ nāśaṃ vidhehi balam uccakaiḥ

Bring about the end of those who hate; grant me great strength.

14 · WELL-BEING

vidhehi devi kalyāṇaṃ vidhehi paramāṃ śriyam

Grant welfare, O Devī; grant the highest prosperity.

15 · GODS AND DEMONS BOW

surāsuraśiroratna- nighṛṣṭacaraṇe’mbike

O Ambikā, whose feet are touched by the crown-jewels of gods and demons alike.

16 · LEARNED, FAMOUS, PROSPEROUS

vidyāvantaṃ yaśasvantaṃ lakṣmīvantaṃ janaṃ kuru

Make your devotee learned, renowned and prosperous.

17 · FOR ME, WHO BOWS

pracaṇḍadaityadarpaghne caṇḍike praṇatāya me

O Caṇḍikā, who crushes the pride of fierce demons, to me who bows before you…

after each: rūpaṃ dehi jayaṃ dehi · yaśo dehi dviṣo jahi

From “them” to “me”. Verse 12 turns personal: dehi me, “give me”. Health, strength, welfare, learning, fame. Verse 17 ends: “to me who bows”. Prayer has to become your own.

Health, strength, learning: ask her plainly.

VERSES 18–23

PRAISED BY ALL THE GODS

18 · PRAISED BY BRAHMĀ

caturbhuje caturvaktra- saṃstute parameśvari

Four-armed one, praised by the four-faced Brahmā, Supreme Goddess.

19 · PRAISED BY KṚṢṆA

kṛṣṇena saṃstute devi śaśvadbhaktyā sadāmbike

Goddess, Ambikā, praised by Kṛṣṇa with unending devotion.

20 · PRAISED BY ŚIVA

himācalasutānātha- saṃstute parameśvari

Supreme Goddess, praised by Śiva, lord of the Himālaya’s daughter.

21 · WORSHIPPED BY INDRA

indrāṇīpatisadbhāva- pūjite parameśvari

Supreme Goddess, worshipped with true devotion by Indra, Indrāṇī’s husband.

22 · MIGHTY ARMS

devi pracaṇḍadordaṇḍa- daityadarpavināśini

Goddess whose mighty arms destroy the pride of demons.

23 · JOY WITHOUT BOUND

devi bhaktajanoddāma- dattānandodaye’mbike

Ambikā, who gives boundless, ever-rising joy to her devotees.

after each: rūpaṃ dehi jayaṃ dehi · yaśo dehi dviṣo jahi

Even the gods are devotees. Brahmā the creator, Kṛṣṇa (Viṣṇu), Śiva and Indra all appear as her worshippers. In the Devī Māhātmya’s view, the great gods draw their power from her.

Creator, preserver, destroyer: all of them bow to her.

VERSE 24

A COMPANION FOR THE CROSSING
patnīṃ manoramāṃ dehimanovṛttānusāriṇīm |tāriṇīṃ durgasaṃsāra-sāgarasya kulodbhavām ‖24‖
Give me a delightful wife who follows the ways of my mind, who helps carry me across the hard ocean of worldly life, and who is born of a good family.
Give me a loving life-partner who understands me, and who helps me cross the difficult sea of life.
WordMeans
patnīṃ manoramāma delightful wife
mano-vṛtta-anusāriṇīmin tune with my mind
tāriṇīmone who helps carry across
durga-saṃsāra-sāgarathe hard-to-cross ocean of worldly life
kula-udbhavāmborn of a good family
A partner as a boat. The verse is written in the voice of a man of its time, but its picture is of a companion who helps tārayati, ferry, across life. Note durga again: the “hard to cross” sea. Many readers today take it as a prayer for a loving life-partner.
Give me a companion for the crossing.

VERSE 25

NOW OPEN THE BOOK
idaṃ stotraṃ paṭhitvā tumahāstotraṃ paṭhen naraḥ |sa tu saptaśatīsaṃkhyā-varam āpnoti sampadām ‖25‖
Having recited this hymn, one should recite the great hymn (the Saptaśatī). Such a person gains the best of riches, as many as its seven hundred verses.
Say this hymn first, then read the Saptaśatī. Every one of its 700 verses becomes a blessing.
WordMeans
idaṃ stotraṃ paṭhitvāhaving recited this hymn
mahā-stotramthe great hymn: the Saptaśatī
sapta-śatī-saṃkhyāthe count of seven hundred
varam … sampadāmthe best of riches
The bolt slides back. Argalā means the wooden bar that locks a door. This hymn lifts it. The last verse says plainly what comes next: the “great hymn”, the 700 verses of the Saptaśatī.
The bolt is lifted: now the door can open.

PART 3 ON ONE PAGE

The Argalā Stotram in five lines
Verses 1–2. Eleven names, Jayantī to Svadhā, and three cries of victory to Cāmuṇḍā.
Verses 3–6. Her four great victories: Madhu-Kaiṭabha, Mahiṣa, Raktabīja with Caṇḍa-Muṇḍa, Śumbha-Niśumbha.
Verses 7–17. For those who bow, praise and worship: give health, strength, welfare, learning and fame.
Verses 18–23. Brahmā, Kṛṣṇa, Śiva and Indra all praise her; she gives her devotees boundless joy.
Verses 24–25. A companion for life’s crossing; then, the bolt lifted, read the Saptaśatī.
