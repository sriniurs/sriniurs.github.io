MELUKŌṬE

CELUVANĀRĀYAṆA · YOGA NARASIṂHA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Rāmānuja’s hill of the beautiful Lord

FOREWORD

On a rocky hill north of the Kāverī, a great teacher once dug for white clay and found a god.

That teacher was Rāmānuja, the god was Tirunārāyaṇa, and the hill became Melukōṭe: a town of temples and tanks, of Sanskrit scholars, of a Sultan’s daughter who loved the Lord, and of a diamond crown that only comes out at night.

This guide tells its legends, its history and its festival.

srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is Melukōṭe a temple or a town?

PROF. BOLT: Both! A whole hill town of temples, tanks and scholars. Rāmānuja's second home.

WHERE IT IS

A temple town on the Yādava hills

The hills. Melukōṭe sits on the rocky Yādavagiri (Yadugiri) hills north of the Kāverī, the only town in Mandya district above 1,000 m. Its name means roughly “the high fort”.

Two temples. Celuvanārāyaṇa stands in the town at the foot of the hill; Yoga Narasiṃha crowns the summit.

Around it. The hills are a wildlife sanctuary, created in 1974 chiefly to protect wolves, with about 200 kinds of birds.

THE LORD OF MELUKŌṬE

Tirunārāyaṇa and Celuvanārāyaṇa

🪨 Tirunārāyaṇa

The stone image in the sanctum, the mūlavar: Nārāyaṇa of Tirunārāyaṇapura.

✨ Celuvanārāyaṇa

The metal festival image, “the handsome Nārāyaṇa”, also called Celuvapiḷḷe Rāya, Selva Piḷḷai and Sampatkumāra. Inscriptions call him Rāmapriya, “Rāma’s beloved”.

Also in the halls: images of Rāmānuja, the Āḻvārs and Yadugiri Ammanavaru, the Goddess of the hill.

THE LEGENDS · I

Rāma’s beloved image
For Brahmā. The temple’s story says Brahmā asked Viṣṇu for an image to worship. Viṣṇu gave him one, which Brahmā’s son Sanatkumāra installed at Melukōṭe.
A second, of metal. Brahmā later received a metal image too. It passed down the ages: worshipped by Rāma, then by Balarāma, then by the Yādava family.
Rāmapriya. So the festival image was called Rāmapriya, “dear to Rāma”, and the hills Yādavagiri, the hills of the Yādavas.
Lost. Over the centuries, the story goes, the image was lost, until Rāmānuja came.

THE LEGENDS · II

Rāmānuja finds the Lord
A dream. After Rāmānuja came to the Hoysaḷa country, Viṣṇu appeared to him in a dream and sent him to Melukōṭe to find holy white earth, the clay for the Śrīvaiṣṇava nāmam.
A discovery. Digging there, he found the clay and, with it, a buried image: the Lord Tirunārāyaṇa.
Installed. Tradition dates the installation, by the Pāñcarātra āgama, to the Bahudhānya year, 1099, in the month of Puṣya.
But older still. A Mysore Archaeological Department report, based on inscriptions, says the deity was already well known before Rāmānuja worshipped here in December 1098.

ZAPPY: So did Rāmānuja start the temple or not?

PROF. BOLT: Inscriptions say there was worship here already; tradition says he found the Lord and made it great. Both can be true!

THE LEGENDS · III

Bībī Nācciyār, the Sultan’s daughter
The doll. The festival image, the story goes, had been carried off to Delhi, where the Sultan’s daughter played with it as her doll and grew to love it.
“Celuva piḷḷe!” Rāmānuja went to Delhi and called out, “Come, my darling child!” The image ran to him of its own accord. From then he was Celuvapiḷḷe, Celuvanārāyaṇa.
The princess. The princess followed him to Melukōṭe and, in her devotion, became one with the Lord. She is honoured in the temple as Bībī Nācciyār.
A note on dates. Historians point out the story cannot be literal: the Delhi Sultanate began in the 13th century, long after Rāmānuja. Śrīraṅgam and Madurai have similar Tulukka Nācciyār stories.

ZAPPY: A Muslim princess honoured in a Vaiṣṇava temple?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, as the Lord's own devotee. History may doubt the details; the devotion it celebrates is the point.

RĀMĀNUJA AT MELUKŌṬE

The teacher’s second home
Why hereRāmānuja came into the Hoysaḷa country in the late 11th century, and became rājaguru, teacher to the Hoysaḷa court
How longAbout 12 years by most accounts (some say 14)
The templeKing Viṣṇuvardhana granted 5,000 gadyāṇas for the building, completed, tradition says, in 1104
The maṭhaThe Yadugiri Yatirāja maṭha was set up so that a sannyāsī would manage the temple; it does to this day
The Tamil VedaRāmānuja had the Āḻvārs’ hymns recited here, and Melukōṭe still follows the Teṅkalai tradition
OpennessTradition credits him with opening temple worship here to people of all communities on certain days

Two homes. Rāmānuja lived and died at Śrīraṅgam, and spent his years of exile at Melukōṭe. Śrīvaiṣṇavas count both among their holiest places.

YOGA NARASIṂHA

The Lord on the summit

Narasiṃha at peace. On the hilltop the Lord sits as Yoga Narasiṃha: the fierce man-lion of the Hiraṇyakaśipu story, calm now, seated in meditation with a band around his knees.

Prahlāda’s Lord. Tradition says that after Hiraṇyakaśipu’s death, his son Prahlāda worshipped Narasiṃha here as a śālagrāma, and installed the image.

A king’s crown. Kṛṣṇarāja Woḍeyar III gave a gold crown to the shrine on the hill. A large pond lies on the summit beside it.

THE THREE CROWNS

Jewels kept in a treasury
RājamuḍiA jewelled crown given by Rāja Woḍeyar (1578–1617)
KṛṣṇarājamuḍiGiven by Kṛṣṇarāja Woḍeyar III (from 1799)
VairamuḍiThe diamond crown (vajra-mukuṭa): the oldest and most precious, its origin unknown
The legendThe demon Virocana stole the crown from Nārāyaṇa in Vaikuṇṭha, and Garuḍa won it back; a blue gem is said to have fallen at Nācciyār Kōyil in Thanjavur
Kept whereAll the crowns stay in the government treasury at Mandya, and are brought to Melukōṭe under heavy guard for the festivals

ZAPPY: Garuḍa fought a demon for a crown?

PROF. BOLT: Of course, he's the Lord's own bodyguard! And now the police guard it instead.

THE VAIRAMUḌI BRAHMOTSAVA

A night in a diamond crown

When. A 13-day festival in Phālguna (March–April), from the Bharaṇī star to Hasta. Garuḍotsava is held a day before it begins.

The night. On the great night, under the Puṣya star, the Lord wears the Vairamuḍi only for the night procession on a golden Garuḍa, which goes on until dawn. Devotees believe the crown must not see daylight.

The crowds. Up to four lakh devotees come, from Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh. The festival also includes the Kalyāṇotsava, Nāgavalli, the great car festival and Gajendra Mokṣa.

Back to the vault. The next morning the crown is checked before officials and returned to the treasury.

THE TEMPLE TOWN

Kalyāṇi, gopura and maṭhas
KalyāṇiThe great stepped tank, ringed with pillared maṇḍapas: one of the most photographed temple tanks in Karnataka
Rāya GopuraA gateway of tall stone pillars from the Vijayanagara period on the hill, never completed into a tower
Raja WoḍeyarA small relief of the king, about 1.5 ft high, on a pillar of the hall; he granted the temple an estate in 1614
ShrinesOf Rāmānuja, the Āḻvārs and Yadugiri Ammanavaru
The 108Melukōṭe is counted among the 108 Abhimāna Kṣetras, the cherished places of Śrīvaiṣṇavism

On screen. The Kalyāṇi and the Rāya Gopura have appeared in many films, including the Hindi film Damini.

HISTORY

Nine hundred years on the hill
Before 1098 Inscriptions show worship of the Lord here already.
1098–99 Rāmānuja comes; the image is installed in Puṣya, Bahudhānya year.
1104 The temple completed with Viṣṇuvardhana’s grant.
Vijayanagara Renovations and the gopura works.
1614 Rāja Woḍeyar grants an estate; the Rājamuḍi.
1780s–90s Tipu Sultan: a decree on recitation, gold and silver vessels, and, it is believed, elephants and drums for the temple. The Mandyam Iyengar community also remembers a massacre on Naraka Caturdaśī.
1799 on Kṛṣṇarāja Woḍeyar III: the Kṛṣṇarājamuḍi and the gold crown for Narasiṃha.
1854 The Veda Vedānta Bodhinī Sanskrit College is founded.
1974 Melukōṭe Temple Wildlife Sanctuary.
1977–78 The Academy of Sanskrit Research opens: 11,000 manuscripts and 35,000 books.

For the full, two-sided story of Tipu and Melukōṭe, see Tipu Sultan and the Temples around Mysuru.

Sanskrit CollegeThe Veda Vedānta Bodhinī Sanskrit College, since 1854
