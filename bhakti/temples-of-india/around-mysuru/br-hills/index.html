BR HILLS · DAKṢIṆA TIRUPATI · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The Lord of the white hill

FOREWORD

Where the Western and Eastern Ghats reach out and touch, a pale cliff rises out of the forest: Biḷigiri, the white hill.

On top stands the Lord the forest people call family. The Soligas say he married their daughter, and to this day they carry him in his festival and make him new sandals for his night walks.

This guide tells the story of the Lord, the hill and its people.

WHERE IT IS

A bridge between two mountain ranges

The hills. The Biḷigiri Raṅgana hills rise in Chamarajanagar district, south-east of Mysuru. They form a bridge between the Western Ghats and the Eastern Ghats, so animals and plants of both ranges meet here.

The cliff. The temple stands on the hill above a pale granite cliff: the “white hill” that gives the place its name. The highest peak of the range, Kaṭṭari Beṭṭa, is about 1,800 m.

The forest. All around is the Biḷigiri Raṅganātha Svāmi Temple Wildlife Sanctuary, now the BRT Tiger Reserve: named after the temple itself.

THE LORD OF THE WHITE HILL

Raṅganātha who stands

🧍 Standing, not reclining

Unlike the Raṅgas of the Kāverī, who recline on Ādiśeṣa, the Lord here stands, like Veṅkaṭeśvara of Tirumala. The oldest record names him Tiru Veṅkaṭanātha.

🛕 The shrine

An east-facing Dravidian temple. Raṅganāyakī has her own shrine; there are images of Rāmānuja and other teachers, and pillars carved with Sarasvatī, Kṛṣṇa and Āñjaneya.

ZAPPY: Then why is he called Raṅganātha?

PROF. BOLT: There's a story: the name changed after a famous visitor. Turn the page!

WHITE HILL, MANY NAMES

Biḷigiri, Śvetādri, Bilikal
BiḷigiriKannada: “white hill”, for the pale cliff, or for the white mist that covers the hills much of the year
ŚvetādriThe same in Sanskrit: “the white mountain”
Bilikal“White rock”, the name in the 1667 copper plate
Tiru VeṅkaṭanāthaThe Lord’s name in that record
RaṅganāthaTradition says the name came after Tipu Sultan, visiting on a hunt, described it as a temple of Raṅganātha
Dakṣiṇa Tirupati“The southern Tirupati”, the name in the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa telling

On screen and in song. The Lord is so loved in Karnataka that a famous song of the 1971 Kannada film Śarapañjara calls out to him by name: “Biḷigiri Raṅgayya”.

THE LEGENDS · I

Vasiṣṭha, and Rāma on the hill
Vasiṣṭha’s penance. Tradition says the sage Vasiṣṭha, guru of the Sun dynasty, did penance on these hills to the Lord as Veṅkaṭeśvara. The Lord appeared and blessed him.
Rāma passes by. Another tradition says Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, searching for Sītā, came this way and worshipped the Lord of the white hill.
Siddappāji shows the way. In Soliga lore, it was their own forest god Siddappāji who showed Viṣṇu this cliff, after the Lord had slain a demon.

THE LEGENDS · II

The Lord who married a Soliga girl
An errand. The Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa telling says the Lord set out for Śvetādri, the white mountain, to fetch soap-nut powder for his wife’s oil bath.
A meeting. On the way he met a girl of the forest-dwelling Soliga people, fell in love, and stayed. The Soligas name her Kusumāle (also told as Pusumāle), daughter of Bomme Gowda, the first Soliga.
Family. Since then the Soligas call the Lord bhāva, “brother-in-law”, and serve in his festivals as family.
Two traditions meet. The story ties a forest people’s own lore to the great temple tradition: the Lord of the hill belongs to both.

ZAPPY: He went shopping for soap-nut and came back married?

PROF. BOLT: That's how the story goes! And it explains why he never went home: he's still here.

THE LORD’S SANDALS

Footwear that wears out

The giant pair. The temple keeps a huge pair of leather sandals, said to be the ones the Lord wears to walk his forest at night.

They wear out. Devotees say the sandals become worn even though they are kept enclosed, and they are replaced with new ones.

A Soliga gift. The Soligas offer the Lord a new pair of leather sandals, about 1 ft 9 in long, once every two years.

Golden footprint. Nearby is Honnameṭṭi, a stone said to hold the Lord’s footprint turned to stone.

ZAPPY: Who's walking around in them at night?

PROF. BOLT: Nobody has caught him at it. But the soles keep wearing down, and the Soligas keep making new ones!

THE SOLIGAS AND THEIR LORD

People of the bamboo
WhoThe Soligas, a forest people of these hills, whose lore traces their origin to Kārayya, a son of Male Mahadēśvara
FamilyThe Lord is their bhāva; Kusumāle is their daughter
The festivalDuring the annual festival the deity is carried by four Soliga youths
SandalsA new pair of leather sandals every two years
Their own godsSiddappāji, the forms of Mahadēśvara, the tiger Doḍḍanāyi, and sacred trees and stones across the hills

Doḍḍa Sampige. A giant champak tree in the forest, sacred to the Soligas. The story goes that the sage Paraśurāma received seeds from a bird; one grew into this tree, where he sat in penance, and his mother Reṇukā flowed to him as the river Bhārgavī.

HISTORY

From copper plate to tiger reserve
Legend Vasiṣṭha’s penance; the Lord marries Kusumāle.
1667 A copper plate of Mudduraju, son of Trimalarājanāyaka of Hadinadu, names the Lord Tiru Veṅkaṭanātha of Bilikal; Mudduraju endows the temple with a village.
18th c. Tipu Sultan visits on a hunt and calls it a Raṅganātha temple; the name sticks.
c. 1800 Dīvān Pūrṇayya of Mysuru also endows the temple: between them, two villages are given for its service.
1974 The hills, temple and all, become the BRT Wildlife Sanctuary; enlarged in 1987.
2011 Declared the BRT Tiger Reserve.

Hoysaḷa claim. Some accounts credit the Hoysaḷa king Viṣṇuvardhana with the temple. The oldest dated record we found is the copper plate of 1667, so we give that as the earliest firm date.

THE ANNUAL FESTIVAL

Carried by Soliga hands

Spring festival. The temple’s main festival falls in spring (sources say March or April), with the car festival drawing pilgrims from far and wide.

Four youths. The deity is carried by four Soliga young men: the family of the bride carrying the bridegroom.

Sandal offering. Every two years the Soligas bring their gift of new leather sandals for the Lord.
