MADHYA RAṄGA

JAGANMOHANA RAṄGANĀTHA · ŚIVANASAMUDRA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The middle Raṅga, between two waterfalls

FOREWORD

Downstream of Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa, the Kāverī reaches the edge of the plateau, splits around a rock, and roars down in two great falls. On that rock, between the thunder of the water, a small temple keeps a quiet Lord: Madhya Raṅga.

Here Raṅganātha is the youthful one, Jaganmohana, “enchanter of the world”, carved from a single black stone. Here, legend says, Indra came to wash away a terrible sin.

Smaller and less visited than the other two Raṅgas, it is still the heart of the Triraṅga: the middle that joins the first and the last.

WHERE IT IS

An island between two waterfalls

Below Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa, the Kāverī runs east until, at Śivanasamudra, she divides around a rocky island and plunges down the Deccan edge in two great falls: Bharacukki on the west branch, Gaganacukki on the east.

On the island stands the temple of Madhya Raṅga, the middle Raṅga, between the first at Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa and the last at Śrīraṅgam.

THE YOUTHFUL LORD

Jaganmohana, the enchanter of the world

🪨 One black stone

The image is carved from a single black śālagrāma stone, about 8 feet long by one account and 9 by another, resting on Ādiśeṣa.

✨ The youthful form

Of the three Raṅgas, devotees see the Lord here in his youth: Mohana Raṅga, “the charming Raṅga”.

Seven hoods or five? Some sources say the serpent here has seven hoods, unlike five at the other two Raṅgas. Others describe seven hoods at Ādi Raṅga too. We give both rather than pick one. The goddess near the feet is described as Lakṣmī, and by one source as Lakṣmī in the form of the river Kāverī.

THE LEGENDS · I

Indra and the sin of slaying Vṛtra
The demon. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa tells of Vṛtra, a mighty demon born from the sage Tvaṣṭṛ’s sacrifice, who terrified sages and gods alike.
The weapon. The sage Dadhīci gave up his body, and from his bones Viśvakarmā made the vajra, the thunderbolt. Riding Airāvata, Indra slew Vṛtra.
The stain. But the slaying left Indra with brahmahatyā, the grave sin of killing one born of a sage’s line.
The island. Temple tradition says Viṣṇu sent Indra to an island in the Kāverī, Takṣaka Kṣetra, to do penance; Viśvakarmā carved a Raṅganātha from a single stone for him to worship.
Release. After long penance and bathing in the Kāverī, Raṅganātha appeared and freed him. So the place became Madhya Raṅga, where worship is believed to wash away sin.

THE LEGENDS · II

The split rock, the falls and the name
The rock split. One telling continues Indra’s story: when the Kāverī rose and threatened his worship, Indra cleft the rock to turn the river aside. The two streams became Bharacukki and Gaganacukki, and the island is called Śilābhedana Kṣetra, “where the rock was split”.
The name. Another tells that Śiva and Pārvatī, passing through the sky, saw the Kāverī spread out around a great rock like an ocean. Śiva praised it, and Pārvatī named the place Śivanasamudra, “Śiva’s ocean”.
Seven sages. A third remembers the island as Saptarṣi Kṣetra, where the river split into seven streams, one before each of seven sages.
Tellings differ. These are temple and local traditions, and sources tell them differently.

ZAPPY: So who made the waterfalls, Indra or geology?

PROF. BOLT: Geology made the Deccan edge! The legends make it holy. Both can sit on the same rock.

HISTORY

What is known, and what is not
Origins Sources disagree. Deccan Herald dates the temple to the Cōḻa period; temple tradition links it to the early Western Gaṅgas. No founding inscription is cited by either.
Patrons Later work is credited to the Hoysaḷas, the Vijayanagara kings and the Woḍeyars of Mysore.
A carved history Deccan Herald notes a claim that the temple’s whole history is carved on a pillar in the Someśvara temple at Halasūru (Ulsoor), Bengaluru.
Quiet years A small, little-visited temple for long periods; Deccan Herald found some outer maṇḍapas neglected.
2021 Renovation of the temple was reported to begin in February 2021.

Small, but not lesser. Beside Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa’s royal history and Śrīraṅgam’s vast walls, Madhya Raṅga’s story is quiet and partly unrecorded. In the Triraṅga, it is still the indispensable middle.

ZAPPY: We don't know who built it?

PROF. BOLT: Not for certain. Some temples come with inscriptions; some come only with devotion. Here, the honest answer is 'Cōḻa or Gaṅga, and patrons since'.

THE TEMPLE

From gopuram to the Lord

🏯 The approach

A seven-tiered rājagopuram, a flagstaff and a lamp pillar, a four-pillared maṇḍapa, then two Garuḍa pillars, each crowned with a lamp, leading to a shrine of Garuḍa kneeling with folded hands.

🙏 Within

The sanctum of the reclining Lord; a separate shrine of the Goddess Raṅganāyakī; and a small utsava maṇḍapa for festival images.

Outside the walls. A tall four-pillared stone structure and a five-pillared maṇḍapa stand further along the same line; Deccan Herald reported them neglected and in need of care.

FESTIVALS

The Lord’s calendar

🧈 Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī

The greatest day: darśan through the Vaikuṇṭha door, and a rare alaṅkāra of fresh butter on the Lord.

🌅 Dhanurmāsa

Special early-morning worship through the month, the season of the Triraṅga vow.

☀️ Ratha Saptamī

The sun’s birthday (Sūrya Jayantī), kept as a major day of worship.

🎉 Brahmotsava

The annual festival: several days of rituals and processions on vāhanas.

Through the year. Śrī Rāma Navamī, Narasiṃha Jayantī, Kṛṣṇa Janmāṣṭamī, Dīpāvalī and Kārtika Dīpotsava are also kept. Dates follow the temple’s own calendar each year.

ZAPPY: A butter costume? Seriously?

PROF. BOLT: Seriously! Butter alaṅkāra is a loving old custom. For the youthful Lord, think of little Kṛṣṇa the butter thief, now wearing it.

PROF. BOLT: That's Śivanasamudra: Hari, Hara, the Mother, and the river's power, all on one rock.

THE MIDDLE OF THREE

Madhya Raṅga in the Triraṅga

The middle. In the Triraṅga vow, Madhya Raṅga comes second, after Ādi Raṅga at Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa and before Antya Raṅga at Śrīraṅgam.

First, middle, last. Some read the three as a life: beginning, middle and end. If so, the middle Raṅga is the youthful one, the Lord in the prime of life.

See the Triraṅga overview for all three together.
