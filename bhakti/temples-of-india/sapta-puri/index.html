SAPTAPURĪ

SEVEN CITIES OF LIBERATION · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Ayodhyā · Mathurā · Māyā · Kāśī · Kāñcī · Avantikā · Dvārakā

FOREWORD

One line of Sanskrit names seven cities and makes them a promise: saptaitā mokṣadāyikāḥ, “these seven give liberation”.

Rāma’s Ayodhyā, Kṛṣṇa’s Mathurā and Dvārakā, the Gaṅgā’s Haridvār, Śiva’s Kāśī and Ujjain, and in the South, Kāñcī, where Śiva, Viṣṇu and the Goddess share one city.

This guide takes the verse word by word, visits each city, and shows how the seven tie in with the Jyotirliṅgas and Śakti Pīṭhas.

THE VERSE

SEVEN CITIES THAT GIVE LIBERATION
ayodhyā mathurā māyākāśī kāñcī avantikā |purī dvārāvatī caivasaptaitā mokṣadāyikāḥ ‖
Ayodhyā, Mathurā, Māyā, Kāśī, Kāñcī, Avantikā, and the city of Dvārāvatī too: these seven are the givers of liberation.
Seven cities, named in one breath: from Rāma’s city to Kṛṣṇa’s, by way of the Gaṅgā, Śiva’s Kāśī, the southern Kāñcī and Ujjain.
WordMeans
māyāMāyāpurī: Haridvār
avantikāAvanti: Ujjain
purī dvārāvatī“the city Dvārāvatī”: Dvārakā (but see next page)
caiva“and also”
sapta etāḥthese seven
mokṣadāyikāḥgivers of liberation

Where it comes from. The verse appears in the Garuḍa Purāṇa (in its section on the dead), the Nārada Purāṇa, and in a slightly different order in the Kāśī Khaṇḍa of the Skanda Purāṇa. Some versions read jñeyāḥ, “should be known”, for caiva.

SEVEN OR EIGHT?

The little word “purī”

📖 Reading 1: seven

Purī simply means “city”: “the city Dvārāvatī”. The verse itself says sapta, seven, so this is the usual reading.

📖 Reading 2: Purī the city

Some take Purī as Jagannātha Purī in Odisha. Then there are eight names for seven places, so one list drops Māyā and keeps Purī.

ZAPPY: So is Jagannātha Purī one of the seven or not?

PROF. BOLT: Most lists say no: it's one of the four Dhāms instead. But you'll find books that slip it in.

THE SEVEN ON ONE MAP

Six in the North and West, one in the South
Three rivers hold four of them: Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Sarayū. Kāñcī stands alone in the South.

1 · AYODHYĀ

Rāma’s city on the Sarayū
NameAyodhyā, “the unconquerable”; also Sāketa
RiverThe Sarayū (Ghaghara)
StoryBirthplace and capital of Rāma; where the Rāmāyaṇa begins and ends
TemplesHanumān Gaṛhī, Kanak Bhavan, and more than a hundred others
TodayThe Rāma Janmabhūmi temple, consecrated in January 2024 after the Supreme Court’s 2019 judgment in the long dispute over the site
FestivalDīpāvalī, Rāma’s homecoming, with lamps along the Sarayū

ZAPPY: Is this the Ayodhyā of the Rāmāyaṇa?

PROF. BOLT: Tradition says yes, and has for many centuries. Archaeologists debate the early layers; devotees don't.

2 · MATHURĀ

Kṛṣṇa’s birthplace on the Yamunā
NameMathurā, “churned”; also Madhupurī
RiverOn the right bank of the Yamunā
StoryBirthplace of Kṛṣṇa in Kaṃsa’s prison; heart of Braj, with Vṛndāvana nearby
SitesThe Kṛṣṇa Janmasthān complex, Viśrām Ghāṭ and its evening ārati
HistoryA great centre of art under the Kuṣāṇas; the Janmasthān stands beside the Shāhī Īdgāh mosque, and the site’s history is before the courts
FestivalJanmāṣṭamī and Holī

ZAPPY: Why do people walk around the whole region?

PROF. BOLT: Braj parikramā: the land itself is Kṛṣṇa's playground. Mathurā is the gate.

3 · MĀYĀ · HARIDVĀR

Where the Gaṅgā leaves the hills
NameMāyāpurī, after the Goddess Māyā Devī; Haridvār (“gate of Hari”) or Haradvār (“gate of Hara”)
RiverThe Gaṅgā, just as she reaches the plains
SitesHar kī Pauṛī and the Brahmakuṇḍ; the Māyā Devī temple, counted among the Śakti Pīṭhas
StoryOne of the four places where drops of amṛta fell from the pot of nectar
FestivalsThe Kumbha Melā every twelve years; the evening Gaṅgā ārati every day

ZAPPY: Gate of Hari or gate of Hara?

PROF. BOLT: Both! Vaiṣṇavas say Hari, Śaivas say Hara. The Gaṅgā doesn't mind.

4 · KĀŚĪ

Śiva’s city that he never leaves
NameKāśī, “the shining”; Vārāṇasī (between the Varuṇā and Asi); Avimukta, “never released”
RiverThe Gaṅgā, flowing north here
LordŚiva as Viśvanātha, a jyotirliṅga; the Goddess as Viśālākṣī, a Śakti Pīṭha
BeliefWhoever dies in Kāśī is freed: Śiva himself whispers the saving mantra in the dying ear
SitesSome 80 ghāṭs, with Maṇikarṇikā where the cremation fires never go out

ZAPPY: Why is Kāśī the most famous of the seven?

PROF. BOLT: Because of that promise about dying. People still come to Kāśī to spend their last days.

5 · KĀÑCĪ

The one in the South
NameKāñcī, “the girdle”: the city as the earth’s waistband
RiversThe Vegavatī and Pālār
All threeŚiva (Ekāmbareśvara, the earth liṅga), Viṣṇu (Varadarāja and many Divya Deśams) and the Goddess Kāmākṣī, a Śakti Pīṭha
HalvesOld writers speak of Śiva Kāñcī and Viṣṇu Kāñcī; Jain temples stand across the river
HistoryCapital of the Pallavas; a great centre of Sanskrit, Tamil and Buddhist learning

Read more: Kāmākṣī of Kāñcīpuram.

ZAPPY: Why only one southern city in the list?

PROF. BOLT: Nobody knows. But Kāñcī carries three traditions at once, so perhaps it counts double.

6 · AVANTIKĀ · UJJAIN

The city of Mahākāla on the Kṣiprā
NameAvanti or Avantikā, Ujjayinī (“the victorious”); Ujjain today
RiverThe Kṣiprā
LordŚiva as Mahākāla, “Great Time”, a jyotirliṅga; the Goddess as Mahākālī, a Śakti Pīṭha
LearningThe old prime meridian of Indian astronomy ran through Ujjain; an 18th-century Jantar Mantar stands here
FestivalThe Siṃhastha Kumbha, when Jupiter is in Leo

ZAPPY: Indian astronomers measured from Ujjain?

PROF. BOLT: Like Greenwich, centuries earlier. Fitting for the city of the Lord of Time.

7 · DVĀRAKĀ

Kṛṣṇa’s city on the western sea
NameDvārakā or Dvārāvatī, “the city of gates”
WaterWhere the Gomatī meets the Arabian Sea
StoryThe city Kṛṣṇa built after leaving Mathurā; the Mahābhārata says the sea swallowed it after he left the world
TempleDvārakādhīśa (Jagat Mandir), with its great flag changed several times a day; Bet Dvārakā on an island nearby
AlsoOne of the four Dhāms; the Nāgeśvara jyotirliṅga is close by
Under the seaMarine archaeologists have studied submerged stone structures off the coast; what they are is debated

ZAPPY: A city under the sea? Like Atlantis?

PROF. BOLT: Divers have found stone structures off the coast. Whether they're Kṛṣṇa's city is another question.

WHY CITIES?

What “mokṣadāyikā” means

The idea. Hindu traditions teach that the self is reborn again and again until it is freed: mokṣa. Most paths to it are inner, through knowledge, devotion or right action.

A place that helps. The Purāṇas add something simpler: certain places are so holy that living, worshipping, or dying there carries one towards liberation. The Garuḍa Purāṇa gives this verse in its teaching about death.

Kāśī’s promise is the strongest: Śiva, the texts say, gives the dying in Kāśī the knowledge that frees them.

A modern reading. Some present-day teachers map the seven cities onto the seven cakras of yoga. This is a new interpretation, not found in the Purāṇas.

🏆 Three lists

Kāśī and Ujjain are in all three of the Saptapurī, the Jyotirliṅgas and the 18 Śakti Pīṭhas.

🧭 The four Dhāms

Badrīnāth, Dvārakā, Purī and Rāmeśvaram, at the four corners. Only Dvārakā is also a mokṣapurī.

ZAPPY: Which city wins the most lists?

PROF. BOLT: Kāśī and Ujjain, tie! Mokṣapurī, jyotirliṅga and Śakti Pīṭha, all at once.

THE RIVERS

Every city has its water
SarayūAyodhyā: where Rāma, the Rāmāyaṇa says, left the world
YamunāMathurā: Kṛṣṇa’s river
GaṅgāHaridvār and Kāśī: the same river, at the hills and on the plains
Vegavatī & PālārKāñcī: the Vegavatī of the mango-tree flood
KṣiprāUjjain: the Kumbha river of Mālwā
Gomatī & the seaDvārakā: river meets ocean

ZAPPY: Is it a coincidence they're all on water?

PROF. BOLT: Never. A holy city needs a holy bath. The tīrtha is the crossing place.

ON ONE PAGE

The whole guide in five lines
The list. Ayodhyā, Mathurā, Māyā, Kāśī, Kāñcī, Avantikā, Dvārakā: seven cities that give liberation.
The verse. In the Garuḍa, Nārada and Skanda Purāṇas; “purī” usually means “city”, not Jagannātha Purī.
The gods. Rāma, Kṛṣṇa twice, the Gaṅgā, Śiva twice, and Kāñcī with Śiva, Viṣṇu and Kāmākṣī.
The map. Six in the North and West, Kāñcī alone in the South; every one on a river or the sea.
The links. Kāśī and Ujjain are also jyotirliṅgas and Śakti Pīṭhas; Dvārakā is also a Dhām.

ZAPPY: One line to remember?

PROF. BOLT: Seven cities, one promise: come here, and the road home is shorter.
