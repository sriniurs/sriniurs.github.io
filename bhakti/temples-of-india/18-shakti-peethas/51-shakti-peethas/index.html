THE 51 PĪṬHAS

BEYOND THE EIGHTEEN · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Every seat of the Mother, region by region

FOREWORD

Śaṅkara’s hymn names eighteen great seats of the Goddess. But the older Tantric list, the Pīṭhanirṇaya, names fifty-one: each where a part of Satī’s body, or one of her ornaments, fell, each with its own Goddess and its own Bhairava.

Many of them are in Bengal; others are in Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nepal, Tibet and Laṅkā. Some are great temples; some are a stone under a tree; a few nobody can find any more.

This round-up walks the fifty-one region by region, marks which ones are also in the eighteen, and is honest about where the lists disagree.

HOW MANY PĪṬHAS?

4, 18, 51, 52 or 108
4The Ādi (first) Pīṭhas: Kāmākhyā, Vimalā, Tārā Tāriṇī, Dakṣiṇa Kālikā
18The Mahā Pīṭhas of the Aṣṭādaśa hymn credited to Śaṅkara: our main series
51The Pīṭhanirṇaya (Mahāpīṭhanirūpaṇa) of the Tantra Cūḍāmaṇi: each with body part, Śakti and Bhairava
52A Bengal tradition adds one more
108The Devī Bhāgavata names 108 seats of the Goddess

ZAPPY: Why so many different numbers?

PROF. BOLT: Different texts, different centuries, different regions. And the same Pīṭha is sometimes placed in two towns.

SHARED WITH THE EIGHTEEN

Where the two lists meet
SharedKāñcī, Ujjain, Jājpur, Kāmākhyā, Prayāga, Jvālāmukhī, Vārāṇasī: on both lists
Near-sharedŚrīśailam, Gayā, Drākṣārāmam, Kolhāpur, Māhur, Mysuru, Ālampur, Pīṭhāpuram, Śāradā and Laṅkā: in the eighteen, but missing or differently placed in common 51 lists
WhyThe eighteen is a southern, hymn-based list; the 51 is a Tantric, eastern list. They grew up apart
ResultTogether they make some sixty seats that pilgrims honour as Pīṭhas

ZAPPY: So the eighteen aren't just a subset of the 51?

PROF. BOLT: No. Overlapping circles, not one inside the other.

Counts. 4 Ādi, 18 Mahā, 51 in the Pīṭhanirṇaya, 52 in Bengal, 108 in the Devī Bhāgavata.
Bengal. The richest cluster: Kālīghāṭ, Bahulā, Aṭṭahāsa, Bakreśvar and more.

Where the facts come from

Lists: the 51-seat table follows the widely reproduced Pīṭhanirṇaya-based list (as in religion.fandom.com, “Shakti Peethas”, derived from an older Wikipedia version), checked against hinduscriptures.com and other lists. Wikipedia, “Shakti Peetha” (counts of 51, 52, 64, 108; the 18 Mahā list; the four primary seats; disputes over Laṅkā, Śāradā, Guhyeśvarī; Sircar on the Bengal 52). encyclopedia.pub and the Kālikā Purāṇa verse on the four Ādi Pīṭhas.

Caution: many entries are disputed. Body parts, Goddess names and Bhairavas vary between manuscripts and regional traditions; several sites (Pañcasāgara, Śrī Parvata, Karṇāṭa, Rāmagiri) are not securely located; some lists include Ambājī, Dantewāḍā or Panna. Treat the tables as a guide, not a final word. Maps are simplified. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
