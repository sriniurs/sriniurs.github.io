MĀDHAVEŚVARĪ

PRAYĀGA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The Goddess who vanished at the Saṅgam

FOREWORD

At Prayāga, where the pale Gaṅgā meets the dark Yamunā and the hidden Sarasvatī rises to join them, the Śakti Pīṭha hymn places Mādhaveśvarī, the Goddess beside Prayāga’s Viṣṇu, Veṇī Mādhava.

In her best-known shrine there is no image at all. Pilgrims touch and garland an empty red palanquin, because the Goddess here is Alopī: the one who disappeared. A second temple across town says the Pīṭha is really there.

This guide tells both stories, and the story of the confluence that makes Prayāga the king of pilgrim places.

Jai Alopī Mātā,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: If nothing is there, what do people see?

PROF. BOLT: Whatever their faith shows them. An empty seat is the easiest place to imagine the Mother.

ZAPPY: Two temples for one Pīṭha?

PROF. BOLT: Prayāga is that kind of place: everything here is a meeting. Even the claims meet.

WHERE IT IS

Where three rivers meet

Two colours. At the Saṅgam the pale, muddy Gaṅgā and the dark, clear Yamunā flow side by side before mixing. The Sarasvatī is said to join unseen, from below.

Both temples are a short way inland from the confluence.

ZAPPY: Why name her after Viṣṇu?

PROF. BOLT: Prayāga's guardian is Veṇī Mādhava. Śiva's wife here is called 'Mādhava's Goddess' by the hymn: everyone belongs to the Saṅgam.

THE PĪṬHA

The last to fall
The fingers. The Pīṭha-nirṇaya Tantra says the group of fingers (aṅguli-vṛnda) of the Goddess fell at Prayāga; she is Lalitā and her Bhairava is Bhava.
The last piece. Alopī Devī’s tradition adds that this was the last part to fall, and that it vanished at once: so she is Alopī, “the one who disappeared”, and the holiest of all.
Other tellings. One account names the ringlets of her hair instead. Sources differ.

ALOPĪ DEVĪ

Worshipping an empty doli
No imageThere is no statue and no stone: a wooden doli (palanquin), wrapped in red, is the Goddess
WhereIt hangs over a small pool, on a platform in the sanctum
TouchDevotees may touch it and make offerings directly
Rakṣā-sūtraPilgrims tie a red protective thread, believed to keep the Mother’s care with them while it stays tied
RitesFamilies bring children here for the muṇḍana, the first head-shaving
BeliefSome say the doli sways by itself when the temple is locked

ZAPPY: They worship an empty carriage?

PROF. BOLT: An empty seat says 'she was here, she is here, but you can't see her'. That's what Alopī means.

LEGEND · THE VANISHED BRIDE

A local story
The procession. A bridal party was carrying a new bride in her doli through the forest.
Robbers. Bandits attacked, killed the men and seized the wealth, then turned to the doli.
Empty. When they pulled back the curtain, the doli was empty. The bride had vanished.
A temple. The story spread; a shrine rose where it happened, and people worshipped the bride as Alopī Devī, the maiden Goddess who disappeared.

ZAPPY: Is that the 'real' origin?

PROF. BOLT: It's the local one. The Pīṭha story and the bride story both end the same way: she's gone, and the doli stays.

LALITĀ DEVĪ OF MĪRĀPUR

The rival claim
WhereMīrāpur, near the Yamunā bank in Prayāga
ClaimMany hold that this is the Prayāga Śakti Pīṭha, where Satī’s fingers fell, with the Goddess named Lalitā as in the Pīṭha-nirṇaya
ImageHere the Goddess has a form, unlike at Alopī Bāgh
Three placesOne tradition counts three Prayāga Pīṭha spots: Alopī, Mīrāpur and the Akṣayavaṭa

🔴 For Alopī

Long local devotion; the name Alopśaṅkarī; the story of the last, vanished part; most Aṣṭādaśa pilgrims go here.

🔵 For Lalitā

The Tantric list names Lalitā with the fingers; some writers say Prayāga has only one Pīṭha, and it is this.

ZAPPY: Which should a pilgrim visit?

PROF. BOLT: Most visit both. They're in the same city, and the Goddess isn't counting.

VEṆĪ MĀDHAVA

The Viṣṇu of Prayāga
WhoViṣṇu as Veṇī Mādhava, presiding deity of Prayāga; his temple is at Dārāgañj
Veṇī“Braid”: the three rivers braided together
Twelve MādhavasPrayāga tradition has twelve Mādhava shrines guarding the sacred zone
PilgrimsA visit to the Saṅgam is said to be complete only after darśana of Veṇī Mādhava
CaitanyaThe Bengali saint is said to have danced in ecstasy at his temple

ZAPPY: So the hymn's 'Mādhaveśvarī' points here?

PROF. BOLT: To his consort-Goddess at the Saṅgam. Prayāga is Mādhava's city; she is its queen.

THE TRIVEṆĪ SAṄGAM

King of tīrthas
Three riversThe Gaṅgā, the Yamunā, and the unseen Sarasvatī
TīrtharājaThe Purāṇas call Prayāga the king of all sacred places
SnānaA dip at the Saṅgam is held to wash away sins; ancestors’ rites and the shaving of heads are done here
Brahmā’s yajñaPrayāga is said to be where Brahmā performed the first sacrifice after creating the world
MāghaEvery year in the month of Māgha, pilgrims camp by the river: the Māgh Melā

THE KUMBHA

The pot of nectar
LegendWhen gods and demons churned the ocean, a pot (kumbha) of nectar emerged; in the struggle, drops fell at four places
Four placesPrayāga, Haridvāra, Ujjayinī and Nāsika
The MelāA great gathering is held at each by turns, timed by the positions of Jupiter and the Sun
Prayāga’sThe largest of all: the Kumbha, the Ardh Kumbha, and the Mahā Kumbha
AkhāṛāsOrders of ascetics lead the royal bathing processions
See also: Ujjain · Haridvāra (Māyā)

THE AKṢAYAVAṬA

The banyan that never dies
What“The undying banyan”: a sacred tree, now inside the fort by the Saṅgam
LegendAt the end of an age, when the world floods, the infant Viṣṇu floats on a leaf of this banyan
RāmaBy tradition, Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa rested under it on their way to exile
Pīṭha linkOne tradition counts it among Prayāga’s three Pīṭha spots
PātālapurīAn underground temple near it is part of the pilgrimage

ZAPPY: A tree that survives the end of the world?

PROF. BOLT: So the legend says. It's Prayāga's way of saying: this place was here before, and will be after.

HISTORY

From Prayāga to Prayagraj
Vedic Hymns praise the meeting of the white and dark rivers.
3rd c. BCE An Aśokan pillar, later inscribed by Samudragupta, now stands in the fort.
7th c. The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang describes a great gathering at the confluence under King Harṣa.
1575 Akbar founds a fort at the Saṅgam; the city becomes Illāhābād.
1800s Mahārānī Baizābāī Scindia restores the Saṅgam ghāṭs and temples.
2018 The city is officially renamed Prayagraj.

The temples’ age. The founding date of the Alopī Devī temple is not known; its roots are in tradition rather than records.
