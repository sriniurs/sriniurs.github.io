CIDAMBARAM

PAÑCABHŪTA STHALAS · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Naṭarāja: Śiva as space

FOREWORD

The last element is the one that holds all the others: space. At Cidambaram, the old forest of Tillai, Śiva is worshipped as space itself. In the sanctum he dances as Naṭarāja, the Lord of Dance, under a roof of gold. Beside him, behind a curtain, is the Secret of Cidambaram: an empty space hung with a garland of golden bilva leaves.

The sages Patañjali and Vyāghrapāda waited here to see his dance of bliss. The Tamil saints sang it, a Chola king gilded its roof, the lost Tēvāram was found in its locked room, and the poet Cēkkiḻār first recited the lives of the 63 saints in its hall. Viṣṇu reclines in the same courtyard.

This guide explains the dance, the secret and the temple, and the people of Tillai.

Om Naṭarājāya namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Worship an empty space?

PROF. BOLT: Space has no shape, so the highest form here is no form. The dance and the emptiness are the same God.

Part of the Pañcabhūta Sthalas, Śiva’s five temples of the five elements. Previous: Kāḷahastīśvara, Śrīkāḷahasti (air).

ZAPPY: Is Cidambaram the most important Śiva temple?

PROF. BOLT: For Tamil Śaivas, simply “the Temple” (Kōyil) means Cidambaram.

NAMES

Hall of consciousness
Cidambaramcit consciousness + ambara sky, space: “the space of awareness”
CiṟṟambalamTamil: “the little hall”, the sanctum
TillaiThe old name, from the tillai mangrove trees that grew here
Poṉṉambalam“The golden hall”
Kōyil“The Temple”, as Tamil Śaivas call it
Abhrasadas“Hall in the sky”: its Sanskrit name in the verse on liberation

ZAPPY: Consciousness and sky in one word?

PROF. BOLT: That's the whole idea. Inner space and outer space are the same space.

THE DANCE OF BLISS

Patañjali and Vyāghrapāda
Two sages. Patañjali, said to be Ādiśeṣa, the cosmic serpent, in human form, and Vyāghrapāda, “tiger-foot”, who asked for tiger’s claws to climb trees and pick unspoilt flowers for Śiva, longed to see Śiva’s dance.
The wait. They worshipped in the Tillai forest for ages.
The dance. On Tai Pūsam, in the month of Tai, Śiva danced the Ānanda Tāṇḍava, the dance of bliss, for them in the golden hall, and promised to dance there forever.

ZAPPY: Patañjali of the Yoga Sūtras?

PROF. BOLT: Tradition identifies the two. His Naṭarāja hymn without long vowels is still sung here.

THE NAṬARĀJA IMAGE

Every detail means something
Ḍamaru (drum)The first sound: creation
Agni (fire)Dissolution
Abhaya hand“Do not fear”: protection
Pointing handTo the raised foot: refuge and release
ApasmāraThe dwarf of forgetfulness crushed underfoot
Ring of fireThe cosmos, endlessly made and unmade
Five actsCreation, sustenance, dissolution, concealment and grace: the whole cosmos in one dance

THREE FORMS OF ŚIVA

Form, form-and-formless, formless
Rūpa · formNaṭarāja, the dancing image
Rūpārūpa · form and formlessThe crystal liṅga, Candramauḷīśvara, bathed several times a day
Arūpa · formlessThe empty space of the Rahasyam
RatnasabhāpatiA small emerald Naṭarāja, bathed once a day
One sanctumAll three in the Cit Sabhā

ZAPPY: Three Śivas in one room?

PROF. BOLT: One Śiva, shown three ways: shape, half-shape, no shape. You choose where your mind can rest.

THE SECRET OF CIDAMBARAM

Cidambara Rahasyam
WhereTo the right of Naṭarāja in the Cit Sabhā
What you seeA curtain is drawn aside during the pūjā: behind it, empty space and a hanging garland of golden bilva leaves
The curtainBlack outside for ignorance, red inside for knowledge
MeaningŚiva as pure space, without form: when ignorance is drawn aside, there is only the infinite
“Rahasyam”The saying “that’s the secret of Cidambaram” now means a hidden truth, in Tamil and beyond

ZAPPY: Nothing there?

PROF. BOLT: Nothing that is a thing. That's the secret.

THE GOLDEN ROOF

Poṉṉambalam
GildedBy the Chola king Parāntaka I (907–955), who took the title “the king who roofed the hall of Tillai with gold”; Āditya I is also credited
21,600 tilesSaid to equal the breaths a person takes in a day
72,000 nailsSaid to equal the nāḍīs, channels of the subtle body
Nine finialsThe nine kalaśas, for nine forms of energy
Our noteThe counts are the temple’s symbolic tradition; sources also give 26,000 or 22,600 tiles

ZAPPY: Did they really count breaths?

PROF. BOLT: 15 breaths a minute × 60 × 24 = 21,600. The roof is a lesson in arithmetic and yoga.

THE FIVE HALLS

Pañca Sabhā
Cit SabhāThe sanctum hall where Naṭarāja dances, the Ciṟṟambalam
Kanaka SabhāThe golden hall in front of it, where daily rites are performed
Nṛtta SabhāThe hall of the dance contest, carved like a chariot
Deva SabhāWhere the processional images are kept
Rāja SabhāThe thousand-pillared hall, where great festivals and gatherings are held

ZAPPY: Five halls for five elements?

PROF. BOLT: A neat parallel some teachers draw. The halls are also the dance stages of Śiva's life here.

THE DANCE CONTEST

Śiva and Kāḷī
The challengeKāḷī, the guardian of Tillai, challenged Śiva to a dance contest
The moveŚiva raised one leg straight up to his ear: the Ūrdhva Tāṇḍava
The endKāḷī would not do that pose and accepted defeat
Tillai KāḷīShe went to the edge of town, where her temple still stands; worshippers visit her too
WhereThe Nṛtta Sabhā carvings show the contest

GOVINDARĀJA PERUMĀḶ

Viṣṇu in the same courtyard
WhatA shrine of Viṣṇu reclining (Govindarāja), the Tillai Tiruccitrakūṭam, one of the 108 Divya Deśams
UniqueFrom one spot, devotees can see both Naṭarāja and Govindarāja
HistoryThe shrine had a troubled past: sources say a Chola king removed the image in the 12th century, and it was reinstated under Vijayanagara rule in the 16th
StoryViṣṇu is said to have watched the dance contest as witness, playing the drum for Śiva in some tellings

ZAPPY: Śiva and Viṣṇu side by side?

PROF. BOLT: Here more than anywhere. The past had quarrels; the present has both.

FOUR TOWERS AND 108 POSES

Dance in stone
Four towersSeven-tiered gopurams on four sides, built between the 12th and 16th centuries
108 karaṇasThe eastern and western towers carry carvings of the 108 dance poses of Bharata’s Nāṭyaśāstra, with their verses
DancersBharatanāṭyam dancers study these panels
ŚivagaṅgāThe temple tank
SizeThe temple covers about 16 hectares (40 acres) at the heart of the town

ZAPPY: A dance textbook carved on a tower?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly that. 108 poses, each labelled. Dancers still come to read it.

THE DĪKṢITARS

Priests of Tillai
WhoThe Poṭu Dīkṣitars, a hereditary community of priests; tradition says 3,000 came with Śiva from Kailāsa, the “Tillai Mūvāyiravar”
MarksThey wear the hair-knot at the front of the head; only married men serve in the rites
ManagementThey manage the temple as a community, not under the usual state endowments system
2014 rulingOn 6 January 2014 India’s Supreme Court recognised them as a religious denomination and set aside the Tamil Nadu government’s 2009 takeover of administration
SinceDisputes over rites and access have continued in the courts and in public debate; this guide reports them, it does not judge them

SAINTS OF TILLAI

Songs, a lost book and a fire
TēvāramAll three Tēvāram poets sang Tillai
The lost hymnsTradition says Rājarāja Chola and Nampi Āṇḍār Nampi found the forgotten Tēvāram palm leaves in a locked room of this temple, half eaten by ants, and arranged them as the Tirumuṟai
MāṇikkavācakarSang the Tiruvācakam and is said to have merged into Naṭarāja here
NandanārA saint from an outcaste community who longed to see Naṭarāja; the Periya Purāṇam says he passed through fire and entered the temple
CēkkiḻārFirst recited his Periya Purāṇam, the lives of the 63 Nāyaṉmārs, in the thousand-pillared hall

NAṬARĀJA IN THE WORLD

From Tillai to Geneva
Chola bronzesThe classic Naṭarāja bronze took its form in the Chola workshops around the 10th century
MuseumsChola Naṭarājas are treasured in museums around the world
CERNIn 2004 India gave a 2 m Naṭarāja statue to CERN, the particle physics laboratory in Geneva, as a symbol of the cosmic dance of matter
DanceBharatanāṭyam dancers bow to Naṭarāja before they begin
NāṭyāñjaliA dance festival held at the temple around Mahāśivarātri

ZAPPY: A Hindu god at a physics lab?

PROF. BOLT: A gift from India. Physicists liked the image of the dance of creation and destruction.

WHAT SPACE TEACHES

ZAPPY: What should I take from space?

PROF. BOLT: Make room. And remember that the whole universe is dancing.
