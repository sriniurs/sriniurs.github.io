KĀḶAHASTĪŚVARA

PAÑCABHŪTA STHALAS · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Śrīkāḷahasti: Śiva as air

FOREWORD

Air cannot be seen, only felt. At Śrīkāḷahasti, on the river Svarṇamukhī near Tirupati, Śiva is worshipped as air. In the sanctum there is no window to let a breeze in, yet a lamp beside the liṅga is said to flicker as if someone were breathing on it.

The town takes its name from three devotees: a spider (śrī), a snake (kāla) and an elephant (hasti). Here too a hunter named Tiṇṇaṉ saw blood flowing from the liṅga’s eye and gave his own eye to stop it, becoming the saint Kaṇṇappa. In 2010 the great tower raised by Kṛṣṇadevarāya in 1516 fell to the ground; a new one now stands.

This guide tells the stories of the breath, the three creatures and the hunter.

ZAPPY: A lamp that moves with no wind?

PROF. BOLT: That is what devotees see and believe. In the temple of air, the flame shows the breath of Śiva.

VĀYU’S PENANCE

How air came to the liṅga
The camphor liṅga. In the first age, Vāyu, god of the wind, did penance for thousands of years before a liṅga of camphor (karpūra).
The boons. Pleased, Śiva gave him three gifts: to be present everywhere, to be the life-breath of every creature, and to have the liṅga named after him.
The Vāyu liṅga. So it became the liṅga of air, to be worshipped by all the worlds.

ZAPPY: Camphor?

PROF. BOLT: Camphor vanishes into the air when it burns. A good liṅga for the god of wind.

THE SPIDER, THE SNAKE, THE ELEPHANT

Three devotees
The spider spun webs over the liṅga as a canopy against sun and dust; when a lamp burned the web, it tried to eat the flame to save the Lord.
The snake brought precious gems from the underworld and laid them on the liṅga.
The elephant bathed the liṅga with river water and leaves, washing away the gems as if they were dirt. The angry snake entered its trunk; the elephant beat its head against the liṅga and all three died together.
Liberation. Śiva freed all three and made them one with the liṅga.

THE VĀYU LIṄGA

What you see
ShapeTall, white, likened to an elephant’s trunk
MarksAt its base a spider; on the sides two elephant tusks; at the top a five-hooded snake
Not touchedBy temple custom, offerings are made without the priests laying hands on the main liṅga, many accounts say
LampsLamps on either side are said to flicker though the sanctum has no opening for wind
Self-manifestSvayambhū: not made by human hands, by tradition

ZAPPY: Is the flicker real?

PROF. BOLT: Devotees say they see it. The temple calls it Vāyu's breath. Believers see a sign; skeptics see air currents from crowds and lamps.

KAṆṆAPPA, THE HUNTER

The eyes he gave
A hunter’s worship. Tiṇṇaṉ, a young hunter, found the liṅga in the forest and loved it at once. He bathed it with water carried in his mouth, decked it with flowers from his hair and offered the best of his hunt.
The test. One day blood flowed from the liṅga’s right eye. Tiṇṇaṉ tried herbs, then dug out his own eye with an arrow and pressed it on the wound. The bleeding stopped.
The second eye. Then the left eye bled. So he would not lose the place blind, he set his foot on the spot and raised the arrow to his other eye.
“Stop, Kaṇṇappa!” Śiva’s hand caught his. Three times he cried “Niḷ Kaṇṇappa”. The hunter got back his sight and became Kaṇṇappa, “the one who gave his eye”.

WHY KAṆṆAPPA MATTERS

Love over rule
The other worshipperA priest, Śivagocarīyār, came daily and was horrified to find meat and mouth-water on the liṅga; Śiva let him hide and watch the hunter’s devotion
The lessonLove counts more than ritual correctness
NāyaṉmārKaṇṇappa is one of the 63 Tamil Śaiva saints; his story is in the Periya Purāṇam
ShrineA shrine of Kaṇṇappa stands on a hill above the temple
RetoldIn Telugu, Tamil and Kannada poems and many films

ZAPPY: A priest learning from a hunter?

PROF. BOLT: That's the point. The priest had rules; the hunter had love. Śiva chose love.

RĀHU–KETU AND ECLIPSES

A temple that stays open
Rāhu–KetuThe temple is famous for rites to ease the influence of the shadow planets Rāhu and Ketu
Sarpa doṣaPeople come for relief from “serpent affliction” in their horoscopes
EclipsesUnlike most temples, which shut during an eclipse, Śrīkāḷahasti is known for staying open
WhyTradition says the Lord here is beyond the planets: the 27 stars and 9 planets are on his kavaca
Our noteThese are beliefs and practices of the temple, described as they are held

ZAPPY: Do the rites work?

PROF. BOLT: That's faith, not science. What's certain is that thousands come for them every day.

THE TOWER THAT FELL

26 May 2010
BuiltBy Kṛṣṇadevarāya in 1516 to mark his victory over the Gajapatis of Odisha; about 37 m (120 ft)
The crackA thin crack noted about 25 years earlier grew into a deep fissure down its face
The fallAt night on 26 May 2010 the whole tower came down; the main shrine was not damaged
WhyThe Archaeology Department found its foundation was only about one and a half feet deep
AfterAn inquiry was ordered, and a new Rāja Gopuram has since been built

ZAPPY: Five hundred years on a foot and a half?

PROF. BOLT: Stone towers are heavier than they look. It lasted a long time on very little.

WHAT AIR TEACHES

The wisdom of breath
Unseen presenceAir is felt, not seen; so is the divine
Every breathVāyu’s boon: the life-breath in every creature is his
Love over ruleThe hunter’s devotion was worth more than the priest’s
