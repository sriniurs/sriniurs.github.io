EKĀMBAREŚVARA

PAÑCABHŪTA STHALAS · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Kāñcīpuram: Śiva as the earth

FOREWORD

The first of the five elements is the one under your feet. At Kāñcīpuram Śiva is worshipped as earth: a liṅga said to be shaped from sand by the Goddess herself, under a single mango tree. That tree gave him his name, Ekāmbareśvara, “Lord of the one mango tree”.

When the river Vegavatī flooded, Kāmākṣī threw her arms around the sand liṅga to save it, and the marks of her bangles are still pointed out today. Above the temple rises one of the tallest towers of South India, built by Kṛṣṇadevarāya in 1509, and inside its walls stands, unusually, a shrine of Viṣṇu.

This guide tells the stories, walks through the temple and explains why earth is the ground of all worship.

Om Namaḥ Śivāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Can a liṅga really be made of sand?

PROF. BOLT: That is the tradition here. It is why water is not poured over it as at other shrines.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
ElementEarth (Pṛthvī): the first of the Pañcabhūta Sthalas
Śiva asEkāmbareśvara or Ekāmbaranātha, “Lord of the one mango tree”
LiṅgaThe Pṛthvī liṅga, by tradition of sand; not bathed with water
GoddessKāmākṣī, worshipped as Ēlavārkuḻali; the great Kāmākṣī temple is nearby
TreeThe sacred mango (sthala vṛkṣa), with four branches for the four Vedas
SizeAbout 9 hectares (23 acres), five courtyards, a thousand-pillared hall
TowerThe southern Rāja Gopuram, 11 storeys, about 57–59 m, built by Kṛṣṇadevarāya from 1509
AlsoNilāttiṅgaḷ Tuṇḍam, a Divya Deśam of Viṣṇu, inside the temple

ZAPPY: The biggest temple in Kāñcī?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, the largest in a city of a thousand temples. And its tower is among the tallest in the South.

HIS NAMES

The one mango
Ekāmbareśvaraeka “one” + āmra “mango” + īśvara “Lord”
EkāmbaranāthaThe same name with nātha, “master”
TiruvēkampamThe Tamil name of the temple in the Tēvāram
Pṛthvī liṅga“Liṅga of earth”: his element
Ēlavārkuḻali“She of the fragrant tresses”: the Goddess here
KampāThe river of the story, today a small stream; also called Vegavatī in the tellings

ZAPPY: Why name a god after a tree?

PROF. BOLT: Because he appeared under it. The tree is the place where she prayed and he came.

THE DARKENED WORLD

Why Pārvatī came to earth
A game. On Kailāsa, Pārvatī playfully covered Śiva’s eyes with her hands.
Darkness. His eyes are the sun and moon: the whole world went dark, and all creatures suffered for what was, to the gods, a moment.
Penance. To make amends, Pārvatī came down to earth. At Kāñcī, under a single mango tree on the river bank, she shaped a liṅga from sand and began her tapas.

ZAPPY: All that for a little joke?

PROF. BOLT: A moment for gods is ages for us. She chose to make it right, the hard way.

THE FLOOD AND THE EMBRACE

Kāmākṣī holds on
The testŚiva sent the river, Kampā or Vegavatī, rushing in flood to test her
The embraceAfraid the sand liṅga would wash away, she threw her arms around it
The marksHer bangles and breasts left marks on the liṅga, still pointed out by the priests; the liṅga is kept covered
The rewardŚiva appeared as Ekāmbareśvara, and the two were married at this spot
NamesHe is called Taḻuvakkuḻainthār, “he who melted in her embrace”, in Tamil

ZAPPY: He sent the flood, then married her?

PROF. BOLT: Testing and grace are one act in these stories. She held on; he gave himself.

THE MANGO TREE

Sthala vṛkṣa
WhereIn the courtyard behind the sanctum, with the divine couple shown at its foot
Four branchesSaid to stand for the four Vedas: Ṛg, Yajus, Sāman and Atharvan
Four tastesEach branch is said to bear mangoes of a different taste
AgeClaimed at 2,500 to 3,500 years; reports say the old trunk has died and a shoot from it is tended as its successor
Meaning“Eka-āmra”: one tree, one God, many fruits

ZAPPY: Is the tree really 3,500 years old?

PROF. BOLT: That's the temple's claim. Botanists would doubt it; devotees treat the living shoot as the same tree.

THE EARTH LIṄGA

What makes this sanctum different
MaterialBy tradition, sand: the liṅga Pārvatī made
No waterBecause sand would dissolve, water abhiṣeka is not done on the main liṅga; oil and unguents are used instead
CoveredThe liṅga is kept under a protective covering
SomāskandaA panel of Śiva, Pārvatī and baby Skanda stands behind the main shrine
Earth’s lessonEarth is the patient element: it bears everything and is worshipped gently

ZAPPY: No water on a Śiva liṅga? Isn't that unusual?

PROF. BOLT: Very. Almost every Śiva temple pours water. Here earth is respected for what it is.

WHY NO SEPARATE GODDESS SHRINE?

One Goddess for the whole city
The ruleBy local tradition, the Śiva temples of Kāñcī have no separate sanctum for the Goddess
WhyKāmākṣī of the Kāmakoṭi Pīṭha is the one Goddess of the city, and every Śiva of Kāñcī is her consort
HereĒlavārkuḻali is shown with Ekāmbaranātha in the processional images
Śakti PīṭhaKāmākṣī’s temple is one of the 18 Śakti Pīṭhas
Read: Kāmākṣī, Kāñcī

A VIṢṆU SHRINE INSIDE

Nilāttiṅgaḷ Tuṇḍam Perumāḷ
WhatA shrine of Viṣṇu within the Ekāmbareśvara complex, counted among the 108 Divya Deśams sung by the Āḻvārs
NameNilāttiṅgaḷ Tuṇḍam, “piece of the moon”: Viṣṇu cooled here by the moon’s light
StoryViṣṇu, scorched while helping at the churning of the ocean, was soothed here by Śiva’s moon
MeaningŚiva and Viṣṇu under one roof, in a city famous for both

ZAPPY: A Divya Deśam inside a Śiva temple?

PROF. BOLT: One of the very few. Kāñcī likes to keep its two gods close.

INSIDE THE TEMPLE

A walk through the courtyards
Five courtyardsFive prākāras, one inside another, lead to the sanctum
GopuramsFour gateway towers; the southern Rāja Gopuram is the tallest
HallThe Āyirakkāl Maṇḍapam, a thousand-pillared hall
SahasraliṅgaA liṅga carved with 1,008 small liṅgas, in a corner shrine
TankŚivagaṅgā Tīrtham, the temple tank

THE RĀJA GOPURAM

Kṛṣṇadevarāya’s tower
BuilderKṛṣṇadevarāya of Vijayanagara, begun in 1509
HeightEleven storeys, about 57 m by one measure and 59 m by another; among the tallest gopurams in South India
AlsoVijayanagara kings added outer walls and the pillared hall before the sanctum
AchyutarāyaHis successor settled a land dispute between this temple and the Varadarāja (Viṣṇu) temple by dividing the lands equally

ZAPPY: A Vaiṣṇava–Śaiva land fight?

PROF. BOLT: The temple's history records it. The king split it down the middle.

A LONG HISTORY

Pallavas to the present
Legend Pārvatī’s sand liṅga under the mango tree
Before 600 A shrine here is believed to predate the Pallava stone temples; the earliest structure has not survived
7th c. The Tēvāram saints sing Tiruvēkampam
Pallavas Kāñcī is their capital; the city fills with temples
Cholas Rebuild and enlarge; inscriptions record grants
1509 Kṛṣṇadevarāya begins the Rāja Gopuram and outer walls
16th c. Achyutarāya divides lands between this temple and Varadarāja
Today Managed under Tamil Nadu’s endowments department; kumbhābhiṣekams renew it periodically

SAINTS AND SONGS

The Tēvāram and Sundarar’s eye
TēvāramAppar, Campantar and Cuntarar all sang this temple
SundararThe saint had lost his sight; tradition says he regained the sight of his left eye at Kāñcī by singing to Ekāmbaranātha
Pāṭal Peṟṟa TalamOne of the 275 temples praised in the Tēvāram
Later poetsKāñcī is praised in Tamil purāṇas such as the Kāñcip Purāṇam

ZAPPY: He got one eye back?

PROF. BOLT: One here, the other at Tiruvārūr, as the story goes. Kāñcī gave the first.

THE CITY OF A THOUSAND TEMPLES

Kāñcī around the temple
SaptapurīKāñcī is one of the seven cities of liberation
Two halvesŚiva Kāñcī (big Kāñcī) around Ekāmbareśvara; Viṣṇu Kāñcī (little Kāñcī) around Varadarāja
KāmākṣīThe Goddess of the city, a Śakti Pīṭha
KailāsanāthaAn early 8th-century Pallava stone temple of Śiva, older than Ekāmbareśvara’s present buildings
SilkKāñcīpuram silk sarees are woven here; the city’s weavers serve its temples

WHAT EARTH TEACHES

The wisdom of the ground
PatienceEarth bears everything, as Kāmākṣī endured her long tapas
Holding onIn the flood, she did not let go
GentlenessEven worship adjusts to what earth can bear: no water poured
UnityOne tree, four Vedas; one city, two gods

ZAPPY: What should I take from earth?

PROF. BOLT: Stand firm, bear weight, and hold on to what you love when the flood comes.
