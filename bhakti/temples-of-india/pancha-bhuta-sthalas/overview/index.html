PAÑCABHŪTA STHALAS

PAÑCABHŪTA STHALAS · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Śiva as earth, water, fire, air and space

FOREWORD

Everything you can touch is made of five things, say the old Indian thinkers: earth, water, fire, air and space. In the Tamil country and its northern edge, five great temples hold Śiva in each of these elements. Together they are the Pañcabhūta Sthalas, the five element shrines.

At Kāñcīpuram he is a liṅga of sand. At Tiruvānaikkāval a spring wells up around him. At Tiruvaṇṇāmalai the whole hill is his column of fire. At Śrīkāḷahasti a lamp flickers in a windless room. At Cidambaram there is only an empty space and a garland of golden leaves.

This guide explains the idea behind the five, shows where they are, and introduces each temple. Five detailed guides follow, one for each element.

ZAPPY: Five temples, one God?

PROF. BOLT: One Śiva, five ways to see him: in what you stand on, drink, cook with, breathe and live in.

ZAPPY: Four in Tamil Nadu and one in Andhra?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. Śrīkāḷahasti is just across the border, near Tirupati. All five sit in the old Tamil Śaiva world.

THE FIVE ELEMENTS

Pañca mahābhūta
The ideaAll matter is made of five “great elements” (mahābhūta): space, air, fire, water, earth
UpaniṣadThe Taittirīya Upaniṣad (2.1) gives the order of creation: from the Self came space, from space air, from air fire, from fire water, from water earth
SensesEach element has its quality: space–sound, air–touch, fire–form, water–taste, earth–smell
Sāṅkhya and ĀyurvedaThe same five build the body: bones and flesh are earth, blood water, digestion fire, breath air, the hollows space
The sthalasEach of the five temples makes one element the very body of Śiva

tasmād vā etasmād ātmana ākāśaḥ sambhūtaḥ, ākāśād vāyuḥ, vāyor agniḥ, agner āpaḥ, adbhyaḥ pṛthivī

“From this Self came space; from space, air; from air, fire; from fire, water; from water, earth.” Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1

ŚIVA’S EIGHT FORMS

Aṣṭamūrti
AṣṭamūrtiThe old Śaiva idea that Śiva has eight bodies: five elements, the sun, the moon and the sacrificer
KālidāsaThe opening verse of the Abhijñānaśākuntalam praises Śiva in these eight visible forms
ŚaṅkaraThe Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotram (v. 9) lists them: bhūr ambhāṃsy analo ’nilo ’mbaram aharnātho himāṃśuḥ pumān
The sthalasFive of the eight are given temples of their own

ZAPPY: So Śiva is not in the earth, he is the earth?

PROF. BOLT: That's the Aṣṭamūrti view. The ground is one of his bodies.

THE “STRAIGHT LINE” CLAIM

Three temples on one meridian
The claimPopular posts say the five temples, or three of them, lie on one exact line of longitude
What is trueKāḷahasti (≈79.70°E), Kāñcīpuram (≈79.70°E) and Cidambaram (≈79.69°E) are within about 1–2 km of the same meridian
What is notTiruvaṇṇāmalai (≈79.07°E) and Tiruvānaikkāval (≈78.71°E) lie well to the west
ExaggerationsClaims of “exactly 79°41′ E” or “built with satellite precision” go beyond the evidence
Our viewA striking coincidence for three of the five, worth noticing; no text claims it was planned

ZAPPY: So is it a mystery?

PROF. BOLT: Three temples line up closely. That's a fun fact, not proof of ancient satellites.

FIVE KINDS OF LIṄGA

What you actually see
Earth · KāñcīA liṅga said to be made of sand, the Pṛthvī liṅga; it is not bathed with water
Water · TiruvānaikkāvalThe Appu liṅga in a low sanctum where water seeps up from an underground spring
Fire · TiruvaṇṇāmalaiThe Agni liṅga; the hill itself is worshipped as Śiva’s column of fire
Air · KāḷahastiThe Vāyu liṅga: a lamp beside it is said to flicker though no breeze enters
Space · CidambaramThe Ākāśa liṅga: an empty space behind a curtain, the “Cidambara Rahasyam”

ZAPPY: A liṅga that is not there?

PROF. BOLT: At Cidambaram, yes. Space has no shape, so the sanctum shows nothing.

THE FIVE GODDESSES

Pārvatī in each
KāñcīpuramKāmākṣī (Ēlavārkuḻali): Kāmākṣī worshipped the sand liṅga under the mango tree and held it in the flood
TiruvānaikkāvalAkhilāṇḍeśvarī: she worshipped a liṅga made of water and became his student
TiruvaṇṇāmalaiUnnāmulai Ammaṉ (Apītakucāmbā): “she whose breast was not suckled”; she won half of Śiva’s body here as Ardhanārīśvara
ŚrīkāḷahastiJñānaprasūnāmbā: “mother of the flower of wisdom”
CidambaramŚivakāmasundarī: Śiva’s companion in the dance of bliss

ZAPPY: Is Pārvatī always the one doing tapas?

PROF. BOLT: In four of these stories, yes. She worships, he appears. The shrine marks the meeting.

THE TĒVĀRAM SAINTS

Sung by the Nāyaṉmārs
TēvāramHymns of three Tamil saints, Appar, Campantar (Sambandar) and Cuntarar (Sundarar), from the 7th–8th centuries
Pāṭal Peṟṟa TalamThe 275 temples “that received a song”: all five Pañcabhūta Sthalas are among them
MāṇikkavācakarHis Tiruvācakam is tied above all to Cidambaram
KaṇṇapparThe hunter-saint of Kāḷahasti is one of the 63 Nāyaṉmārs
Koccheṅkaṭ CōḻaṉThe king of the Tiruvānaikkāval legend is also a Nāyaṉmār

FIVE STHALAS AND TWELVE JYOTIRLIṄGAS

Two different lists
JyotirliṅgasTwelve “liṅgas of light” across India, named in a Sanskrit hymn; Rāmeśvaram is the only one in Tamil Nadu
PañcabhūtaFive element shrines of the Tamil Śaiva tradition; none of them is a Jyotirliṅga
Shared rootBoth lists rest on Śiva as endless light: the Liṅgodbhava story belongs to Aruṇācala and to the Jyotirliṅgas
KāñcīAlso one of the 18 Śakti Pīṭhas, as Kāmākṣī
CidambaramAlso counted among the five halls where Śiva dances (Pañca Sabhai)
Read: Kāmākṣī, Kāñcī

WHO BUILT THEM

Pallavas to Nāyakas
Legend Koccheṅkaṭ Cōḻaṉ, an early Chola king, is credited with Tiruvānaikkāval
7th–8th c. Pallava Kāñcī; the Tēvāram saints sing all five shrines
9th–10th c. Chola kings Āditya I and Parāntaka I gild the roof of Cidambaram
10th–13th c. Cholas expand all five; stone inscriptions record their gifts
1509–1529 Kṛṣṇadevarāya of Vijayanagara builds the tall gopurams of Kāñcī (1509) and Kāḷahasti (1516)
16th–17th c. Nāyaka rulers add halls and towers
2010 Kāḷahasti’s Rāja Gopuram collapses; a new one is built

WHAT THEY TEACH

The wisdom of the five
EarthPatience and steadiness; Kāmākṣī held on through the flood
WaterFlow and humility; the low doorway makes everyone bow
FireBurning away ego; Brahmā and Viṣṇu could not find its end
AirBreath and life; a hunter offered his own eyes
SpaceEmptiness and freedom; the highest form is no form

ZAPPY: Which one is the most important?

PROF. BOLT: Each tradition says its own. The idea is that you need all five, as the body does.

THE FIVE GUIDES

Read on
Earth · Ekāmbareśvara, Kāñcīpuram: the sand liṅga, the mango tree with four branches, Kāmākṣī’s embrace and a tower 59 m tall.
Water · Jambukeśvara, Tiruvānaikkāval: the spring in the sanctum, the spider and the elephant, the Chola king and the priest dressed as the Goddess.
Fire · Aruṇācaleśvara, Tiruvaṇṇāmalai: the endless column of fire, the hill that is Śiva, Kārttikai Dīpam, Girivalam and Ramaṇa Maharṣi.
Air · Kāḷahastīśvara, Śrīkāḷahasti: the flickering lamp, the spider, snake and elephant, Kaṇṇappa’s eyes and the fallen tower.
Space · Naṭarāja (Sabhānāyaka), Cidambaram: Naṭarāja’s dance, the secret of Cidambaram, the golden roof and the Dīkṣitars.
