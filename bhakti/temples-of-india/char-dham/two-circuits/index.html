THE FOUR ABODES

Srini presents

CHAR DHAM

TEMPLES OF INDIA · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Four corners of India, and four Himalayan valleys

FOREWORD

Say “Char Dham” to ten pilgrims and you may get two different answers. For some it is the great circle around India: Badrīnāth in the snows, Purī on the eastern sea, Rāmeśvaram at the southern tip and Dvārakā on the western shore.

For others it is four shrines in the Himalaya, at the heads of the rivers that become the Gaṅgā: Yamunotrī, Gaṅgotrī, Kedārnāth and Badrīnāth again.

This guide explains both: the abodes, the legends, the four ages and Śaṅkara’s four maṭhas, and where the two circles meet.

Jai Badrī Viśāl,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Two Char Dhams? Which one did my grandparents mean?

PROF. BOLT: Depends where they lived. In the south, the big circle. In the north, often the Himalayan four.

ZAPPY: So which one is the real Char Dham?

PROF. BOLT: Both are real. The big one circles India; the small one circles four Himalayan valleys. Badrīnāth anchors them both.

WHAT IS A DHĀM?

Abode, seat, home
DhāmaSanskrit: an abode, a dwelling, a seat of power; also light and splendour
CharHindi for “four”; in Sanskrit, catur-dhāma
BeliefA pilgrimage to all four is held to help one towards mokṣa, liberation
Purī, tīrtha, dhāmA purī is a holy city, a tīrtha a crossing-place, a dhām a place God lives

Four corners. The big circuit sets one abode near each edge of the land: Himalayan snow in the north, the Bay of Bengal in the east, the strait facing Laṅkā in the south and the Arabian Sea in the west. To complete it is, quite literally, to walk around India.

ZAPPY: Why four, and why at the edges?

PROF. BOLT: Four directions, one home of God in each. Draw a line through them and you've drawn the country.

FOUR AGES, FOUR ABODES

Satya to Kali
YugaDhāmWhy
SatyaBadrīnāthNara-Nārāyaṇa’s penance among the badrī (jujube) trees
TretāRāmeśvaramRāma installs a Śiva-liṅga here after the war with Rāvaṇa
DvāparaDvārakāKṛṣṇa leaves Mathurā and makes Dvārakā his capital
KaliPurīViṣṇu as Jagannātha, Lord of the World, for this age

Tradition links this scheme to Ādi Śaṅkara: each abode stands for one age of the world, so the circuit is a walk through time as well as space. Pilgrims often go in the order of the ages, ending at Purī in our own age.

ZAPPY: We live in the Kali age, so Purī is ours?

PROF. BOLT: That's the idea: Jagannātha is the Lord for now. The other three are the ages behind us.

BADRĪNĀTH

Abode of the North · Satya Yuga
WhereUttarakhand, high in the Garhwal Himalaya, on the Alakanandā river
DeityBadrīnārāyaṇa: Viṣṇu seated in meditation, in black stone
LegendViṣṇu did penance here as Nara-Nārāyaṇa; Lakṣmī became a badrī tree to shade him
ŚaṅkaraTradition says he recovered the image from the Nārada Kuṇḍa and re-established worship
PriestThe chief priest, the Rāval, is by custom a Nambūdiri brāhmaṇa from Kerala
SeasonOpen in summer; in winter the worship moves down to Jyotirmaṭh (Joshimath)
In both listsThe northern Dhām of the Bada Char Dham and the last stop of the Chota Char Dham

ZAPPY: A priest from Kerala at the top of the Himalayas?

PROF. BOLT: Śaṅkara's legacy: India's south looking after its north for over a thousand years.

RĀMEŚVARAM

Abode of the South · Tretā Yuga
WherePamban island, Tamil Nadu, facing Laṅkā across the sea
DeityRāmanāthasvāmī: Śiva as “Rāma’s Lord”, a Jyotirliṅga
LegendRāma worshipped Śiva here after the war, to cleanse the sin of killing Rāvaṇa
Famous forIts very long pillared corridors and the many tīrthas (wells) bathed in before darśan
The one Śiva DhāmThe only Śaiva abode of the four
Full guide: Rāmeśvaram, in the 12 Jyotirliṅgas series

ZAPPY: Why is a Viṣṇu circuit holding a Śiva temple?

PROF. BOLT: Because Viṣṇu, as Rāma, worshipped Śiva here. The four are a conversation, not a contest.

DVĀRAKĀ

Abode of the West · Dvāpara Yuga
WhereGujarat, on the Arabian Sea where the Gomatī meets the ocean
DeityDvārakādhīś: Kṛṣṇa as “King of Dvārakā”
LegendKṛṣṇa built his capital here after leaving Mathurā; after his time the sea swallowed the city
TempleThe Jagat Mandir, a tall five-storeyed spire with a great flag
MaṭhaSeat of the western Śaṅkara maṭha, the Śāradā Pīṭha
AlsoOne of the seven sacred cities, the Saptapurī
See also: The Saptapurī

ZAPPY: Is the old city really under the sea?

PROF. BOLT: Tradition says so, and divers have searched offshore. What they found is still debated.

PURĪ

Abode of the East · Kali Yuga
WhereOdisha, on the Bay of Bengal
DeitiesJagannātha with his brother Balabhadra and sister Subhadrā, carved in wood
Navakalevara“New body”: the wooden images are replaced in a secret rite, in certain years
Ratha YātrāThe chariot festival: the three ride huge chariots through the streets
TempleBegun in the 12th century by Anantavarman Coḍagaṅga of the Eastern Gaṅga dynasty
MaṭhaSeat of the eastern Śaṅkara maṭha, the Govardhana Maṭha

ZAPPY: God made of wood, renewed every few years?

PROF. BOLT: A God who takes a new body, just as we do. That's the Kali-age lesson Purī teaches.

ŚAṄKARA’S FOUR MAṬHAS

Four seats, four directions
DirectionMaṭhaVedaGreat sayingFirst head
NorthJyotirmaṭh (near Badrīnāth)Atharvaayam ātmā brahmaToṭaka
EastGovardhana, PurīṚgprajñānaṃ brahmaPadmapāda
SouthŚṛṅgeri, KarnatakaYajurahaṃ brahmāsmiSureśvara
WestŚāradā, DvārakāSāmatat tvam asiHastāmalaka

Matching the Dhāms. Three maṭhas sit at or beside a Dhām. The southern one is at Śṛṅgeri in Karnataka, not at Rāmeśvaram.

Dates. Tradition credits Śaṅkara with founding all four; historians debate how many go back to him, and when.

ZAPPY: Four Vedas, four sayings, four corners?

PROF. BOLT: Śaṅkara's map of India was also a map of the Vedas. Each corner guards one.

PAIRED SHRINES

A partner for each abode
Badrīnāthpaired with Kedārnāth
Rāmeśvarampaired with Raṅganātha (Śrīraṅgam)
Dvārakāpaired with Somanātha
Purīpaired with Liṅgarāja, Bhubaneswar

Some traditions pair each abode with a partner of the other god, so every Viṣṇu Dhām has a Śiva shrine nearby and Śiva’s Rāmeśvaram has Viṣṇu’s Śrīraṅgam. One version even lists the four Dhāms as all Viṣṇu (with Raṅganātha in place of Rāmeśvaram) and the partners as all Śiva.

Kedārnāth and Somanātha are Jyotirliṅgas: Kedārnāth · Somanātha

THE CHOTA CHAR DHAM

Four in the Himalaya

Four river sources. Each of the four stands near the head of a sacred river: Yamunā at Yamunotrī, Bhāgīrathī at Gaṅgotrī, Mandākinī at Kedārnāth and Alakanandā at Badrīnāth. The last three meet and at Devaprayāg become the Gaṅgā.

West to east. Pilgrims usually go in that order: Yamunotrī, Gaṅgotrī, Kedārnāth, then Badrīnāth.

HOW THE SMALL ONE GOT ITS NAME

From “Chota” to just “Char Dham”
ChotaHindi “small”: the Himalayan four were called the Chota Char Dham to set them apart from the all-India circuit
Since mid-20th c.As roads reached the hills, the Himalayan four became known simply as “the Char Dham”
Mix of traditionsTwo goddess shrines (Yamunā, Gaṅgā), one Śiva (Kedārnāth), one Viṣṇu (Badrīnāth)
SeasonAll four close in winter under snow; the deities’ worship moves to villages lower down, and returns in summer

ZAPPY: So when someone says 'I did the Char Dham'...

PROF. BOLT: Ask which one. Nine times in ten, they mean the Himalayan four.

YAMUNOTRĪ

Source of the Yamunā
RiverThe Yamunā, rising on the glacier above
GoddessYamunā, daughter of Sūrya the Sun and sister of Yama, god of death
BeliefA bath in her waters is said to spare one an untimely death
Sūrya KuṇḍaA hot spring beside the temple: pilgrims cook rice and potatoes in it as prasāda
Winter seatHer image goes down to the village of Kharsālī

ZAPPY: Cooking rice in a holy spring?

PROF. BOLT: Boiled by the Sun's daughter herself. It's the only temple where the stove is the river.

GAṄGOTRĪ

Where Gaṅgā came down
RiverThe Bhāgīrathī, which becomes the Gaṅgā; its glacier source is Gaumukha, “cow’s mouth”, higher up
LegendKing Bhagīratha did penance here to bring Gaṅgā down from heaven to free his ancestors; Śiva caught her in his hair
Bhagīratha ŚilāThe rock where he is said to have meditated
TempleBuilt by the Gorkha general Amar Singh Thapa, during Nepal’s rule of Garhwal in the early 19th century
Winter seatGaṅgā’s image goes down to Mukhbā

ZAPPY: Why is the river called Bhāgīrathī here?

PROF. BOLT: After Bhagīratha. We still say 'Bhagīratha effort' for a near-impossible task.

KEDĀRNĀTH

Śiva in the snows
RiverThe Mandākinī
DeityŚiva as Kedāreśvara, a Jyotirliṅga: the hump of the bull-form he took to hide from the Pāṇḍavas
Paired withBadrīnāth, in the Bada Char Dham pairs
Winter seatWorship moves to Ukhīmaṭh
Full guide: Kedārnāth, in the 12 Jyotirliṅgas series

ZAPPY: The Pāṇḍavas are here too?

PROF. BOLT: Seeking Śiva's forgiveness after the war. He dived into the earth; his hump stayed here.

ON ONE PAGE

The whole guide in five lines
Two sets. “Char Dham” means two different circuits of four.
The big one. Badrīnāth, Purī, Rāmeśvaram, Dvārakā: four corners of India, four yugas, credited to Śaṅkara.
The maṭhas. Four Śaṅkara seats at Jyotirmaṭh, Purī, Śṛṅgeri and Dvārakā guard four Vedas.
The small one. Yamunotrī, Gaṅgotrī, Kedārnāth, Badrīnāth: four river sources in Garhwal, open only in summer.
The hinge. Badrīnāth belongs to both.

ZAPPY: One line to remember?

PROF. BOLT: Walk the big circle and you've walked India; walk the small one and you've walked to where the Gaṅgā begins.
