PURĪ JAGANNĀTHA

CHAR DHAM · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The Lord of the World, in wood, by the sea

FOREWORD

On the sands of Odisha, God is carved in wood, with no hands to speak of and eyes too big to close. He lives with his brother and sister, eats from one of the largest kitchens in India, falls ill after his yearly bath, and once a year rides out on a chariot so huge that English borrowed his name for anything unstoppable.

This is Jagannātha, Lord of the World, and Purī is his abode: the eastern Dhām, the one for our own age.

This guide tells his legends, his temple, his festivals and his saints.

Jai Jagannātha,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is Jagannātha Kṛṣṇa or Viṣṇu?

PROF. BOLT: Both, and more. Vaiṣṇavas see Kṛṣṇa; others see Viṣṇu, Śiva-Śakti or the forest god of the Śabaras. He doesn't mind.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
DeitiesJagannātha, his elder brother Balabhadra, his sister Subhadrā, and Sudarśana
Made ofWood: neem logs called dāru brahma, replaced from time to time
WherePurī, on the Bay of Bengal, Odisha
TempleThe Baḍa Deuḷa, 12th century, Eastern Gaṅga kings, about 65 m tall
Char DhamThe eastern abode, for Kali Yuga
Famous forRatha Yātrā, the chariot festival; mahāprasāda from one of India’s biggest kitchens
GoddessVimalā, inside the complex; Lakṣmī has her own shrine
Śaṅkara maṭhaGovardhana Maṭha, the eastern seat

ZAPPY: Why does God have such huge round eyes here?

PROF. BOLT: So he never blinks. Jagannātha keeps watching the world, and the world can always meet his gaze.

WHERE IT IS

On the Bay of Bengal

The holy field. Purī stands on the sand by the sea. Its old names are Śrī Kṣetra, Puruṣottama Kṣetra and Nīlācala, “the blue hill”, for the low sandy rise the temple stands on.

The triangle. With the Sun temple of Koṇārka and Śiva’s Liṅgarāja at Bhubaneswar, Purī forms Odisha’s great temple triangle. Liṅgarāja is counted as Purī’s Śiva partner in the Char Dham pairs.

THE NAMES

Lord of the world
Jagannāthajagat “world” + nātha “lord”: Lord of the Universe
Jagā, JagabandhuOdia pet names: “Jaga”, “Friend of the world”
Kālia, Cakā Ākhi“The dark one”; “Round-eyes”
Puruṣottama“The Supreme Person”: an old name of the god and of Purī
Baḍa Deuḷa“The Great Temple”, as Odias call it
Baḍa Ṭhākura“Elder Lord”: Balabhadra

ZAPPY: English got the word 'juggernaut' from here?

PROF. BOLT: Yes: from the giant chariots that nothing could stop. The word travelled; the god stayed home.

THE LEGEND · NĪLA MĀDHAVA

The blue god in the forest
The secret god. In the forests lived the Śabara people. Their chief, Viśvāvasu, secretly worshipped a shining blue image of Viṣṇu, Nīla Mādhava, in a hidden cave.
The king’s search. King Indradyumna of Avanti heard of it and sent priests to find it. Only one, Vidyāpati, came close: he lived with the Śabaras and married Viśvāvasu’s daughter, Lalitā.
Blindfolded. At last the chief led his son-in-law to the god, blindfolded. A popular telling adds that Vidyāpati dropped mustard seeds along the way, so the path could be found again when they sprouted.
Gone. When the king came, Nīla Mādhava had vanished. A voice told him the Lord would come again, as a log of wood.

THE LOG AND THE CARPENTER

How the images were made
The dāru. A great log, the dāru brahma, washed up on the shore, as the voice had promised.
The old carpenter. No one could carve it, until an old craftsman (in many tellings Viśvakarmā, or Viṣṇu himself) offered to, on one condition: he must work behind closed doors for 21 days, undisturbed.
The door. When the sounds stopped, the queen grew anxious and the king opened the door early. The carpenter had vanished, leaving the images with stumps for arms and no feet.
As they are. A voice said: worship them just so. That is why Jagannātha looks as he does.

ZAPPY: So God is unfinished?

PROF. BOLT: Or finished in a way we didn't expect. He needs no hands to hold you, no feet to come to you.

THE FOUR ON THE JEWEL THRONE

Ratnavedī
BalabhadraThe elder brother, white, the tallest; Balarāma
SubhadrāThe sister, yellow, in the middle, smaller and without arms
JagannāthaDark, with round eyes and a wide smile; Kṛṣṇa, Viṣṇu
SudarśanaViṣṇu’s discus, as a tall wooden pillar
AlsoSmall metal images of Mādhava, Śrīdevī and Bhūdevī
A god with his family, brother and sister, rather than a consort.

THE BAḌA DEUḶA

The great temple
StyleKaliṅga rekha deuḷa: a curved tower on a five-fold (pañcaratha) plan, about 65 m tall
BuiltBegun under Anantavarman Coḍagaṅga (12th c.), completed or renewed under Anaṅgabhīma III
GatesFour: the main Siṃha Dvāra (Lion Gate) faces east
Aruṇa StambhaA pillar before the Lion Gate, brought from the Sun temple at Koṇārka in the 18th century
Patita PāvanaAn image of Jagannātha in the gate passage, visible from the road to all
WithinOver a hundred smaller shrines, among them Lakṣmī, Vimalā and Gaṇeśa

THE NĪLA CAKRA

The wheel on top
WhatA great wheel of eight metals (aṣṭadhātu) on the very top of the tower
FlagA fresh flag is tied to its mast every day by a servitor who climbs the tower
EkādaśīA lamp is lit up beside it on Ekādaśī
DarśanaSeeing the wheel from outside is held to be as good as seeing the Lord

ZAPPY: Climbing a 65-metre tower every day, by hand?

PROF. BOLT: Every single day, for centuries. The family that does it says the Lord holds the ladder.

THE KITCHEN

Mahāprasāda
KitchenOne of the largest temple kitchens in India, run by hereditary cooks
CookingIn clay pots on wood fires; pots are stacked, and tradition says the top one cooks first
OfferedTo Jagannātha, then to Vimalā: only then does it become mahāprasāda
Ānanda BāzārThe market inside the temple where mahāprasāda is sold and shared
No casteMahāprasāda is shared from one plate, whoever you are

ZAPPY: Why must it go to Vimalā first?

PROF. BOLT: Jagannātha's food becomes 'mahā' only after the goddess tastes it. Even here, she has the last word.

SNĀNA YĀTRĀ AND ANASARA

The bath and the fever
Snāna PūrṇimāOn the full moon of Jyeṣṭha, the deities are brought out and bathed with 108 pots of water from the Sunā Kuā, the golden well
AnasaraAfter the bath they “fall ill” and are hidden from view for about two weeks, given herbal medicines by their own servitors
Paṭṭi DiyaṁPainted cloth images are worshipped in their place
Nava YauvanaThey reappear “young again”, just before the chariot festival

ZAPPY: God catches a fever?

PROF. BOLT: He lives as a member of the family: bathed, fed, put to bed, nursed when ill. That's the sweetness of Purī.

RATHA YĀTRĀ

The chariot festival
WhenĀṣāḍha, in the monsoon
ChariotsNandighoṣa (Jagannātha), Tāladhvaja (Balabhadra), Darpadalana (Subhadrā): built new every year
Where toAlong the Grand Road (Baḍa Daṇḍa) to the Guṇḍicā temple, the “aunt’s house”
Chherā PahanrāThe Gajapati king sweeps the chariots with a golden broom: before God, a king is a sweeper
ReturnAfter about a week, the Bahuḍā Yātrā brings them home
Suna BeśaBack on the chariots at the gate, they are dressed in gold

NAVAKALEVARA

A new body
What“New body”: the wooden images are replaced with new ones carved from fresh neem
WhenIn a year with an extra (adhika) Āṣāḍha month: usually every 12 to 19 years
Finding the treesServitors search for neem trees with special signs: a conch or discus mark, a snake’s hole, no birds’ nests, near water and a Śiva shrine
The secretThe mysterious brahma padārtha, “divine substance”, is moved from old body to new by blindfolded servitors at night
The oldThe old images are buried in the Koili Vaikuṇṭha garden within the temple

ZAPPY: God dies and is reborn?

PROF. BOLT: The wood is laid to rest; the life inside moves on. Purī acts out the Gītā's line about changing clothes.

VIMALĀ

The goddess within
WhoVimalā, a form of Durgā, in her own shrine in the inner enclosure
Śakti PīṭhaCounted among the Śakti Pīṭhas: in one tradition the place where Satī’s feet fell
MahāprasādaFood offered to Jagannātha becomes mahāprasāda only after it is offered to her
Durgā PūjāHer great festival is in autumn, with special rites
BhairavaJagannātha himself is called her Bhairava, her guardian

ZAPPY: A Śakti Pīṭha inside a Viṣṇu temple?

PROF. BOLT: Purī keeps every tradition under one roof: Vaiṣṇava, Śākta, Śaiva, even tribal roots.

HISTORY

Kings, saints and invasions
Legend King Indradyumna builds the first temple.
12th c. Anantavarman Coḍagaṅga of the Eastern Gaṅgas begins the present temple; Jayadeva sings the Gītagovinda.
13th c. Anaṅgabhīma III dedicates his kingdom to Jagannātha, ruling as his deputy.
15th c. Kapilendra Deva declares Jagannātha the true king and himself the first servant.
1506 Guru Nānak visits Purī.
16th c. Caitanya lives his last years in Purī, lost in love for Jagannātha.
Through history The temple chronicle, the Mādalā Pāñji, records eighteen invasions; the images were hidden many times.
Today Run by the Shree Jagannath Temple Administration; the Gajapati still performs his rites.

WHO MAY ENTER

A rule and a debate

📜 The rule

Entry is for Hindus; Jains, Sikhs and Buddhists are also admitted, but other non-Hindus and foreigners are not. The temple says the rule is as old as the temple.

🗣️ The debate

Critics point to devout foreign-born Hindus turned away, and to the temple’s past bar on Dalits. Defenders say a living temple sets its own customs. The question returns to the news again and again.

For everyone. Patita Pāvana, “saviour of the fallen”, looks out from the gate passage so all can see him; the Nīla Cakra is visible across the town; and on Ratha Yātrā the Lord comes out into the street for all.

ZAPPY: So someone who can't go in still gets darśana?

PROF. BOLT: Once a year, Jagannātha goes out to them. That's the whole point of the chariots.
