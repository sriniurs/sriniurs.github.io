YAMUNOTRĪ

CHAR DHAM · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The Sun’s daughter, Death’s sister

FOREWORD

High in the western Garhwal Himalaya, a glacier melts into a dark, fast stream. That stream is a goddess: Yamunā, daughter of the Sun, twin sister of Yama, the god of death.

At her small temple, pilgrims cook rice in a spring hot enough to boil it, and pray that her brother will be gentle with them. Every Bhāī Dūj, the festival she is said to have begun, she leaves for the winter to stay with her other brother, Śani, in the village below.

This guide tells her family story, her shrine and her springs.

ZAPPY: Is this the same Yamunā that flows through Delhi and Āgrā?

PROF. BOLT: The very same, just born. Here she's a mountain stream; by Prayāga she's a great river meeting the Gaṅgā.

ZAPPY: A river goddess who is Death's sister?

PROF. BOLT: And the Sun's daughter. Bathe in her, they say, and her brother will be kind to you.

WHERE IT IS

The westernmost Dhām

Far west of the four. Yamunotrī sits at the head of the Yamunā valley, on the western flank of the Bandarpūñch massif. Pilgrims usually start the Himalayan Char Dham here and go east.

The real source. The river is born a little higher, on the Champāsar glacier below Kalinda Parvata. The way up is so hard that worship is done at the temple below.

THE SUN’S CHILDREN

Yamunā, Yama and Śani
The parents. Sūrya, the Sun, married Saṃjñā. She could not bear his blazing light and could not meet his eyes.
The twins. Their children were Yama, who became lord of the dead, and his twin sister Yamī, who became the river Yamunā.
The shadow. When Saṃjñā left, her shadow Chāyā took her place and bore Śani (Saturn). So Śani is Yamunā’s half-brother.
Kālindī. The mountain at the source is Kalinda, a name of the Sun; so Yamunā is also Kālindī, “daughter of Kalinda”.

YAMA DVITĪYĀ

The brother who came to dinner
The visit. Yamunā longed for her brother to visit. At last, on the second day after Dīpāvalī, Yama came. She marked his brow, fed him, and asked a boon.
The boon. “Any brother who visits his sister on this day, and any who bathes in my waters, need not fear you.” Yama agreed.
Bhāī Dūj. So began Yama Dvitīyā, Bhāī Dūj or Bhāū Bīj, when sisters across India mark their brothers’ foreheads.
At Yamunotrī. Bhāī Dūj is also the day Yamunotrī closes for winter.

ZAPPY: So Bhāī Dūj started here?

PROF. BOLT: With these two siblings, by the legend. Every tilaka on that day echoes Yamunā's.

KĀLINDĪ AND KṚṢṆA

The river of Vraja
Kṛṣṇa’s riverFar downstream, the Yamunā flows past Mathurā and Vṛndāvana: the river of Kṛṣṇa’s childhood
KāliyaWhere Kṛṣṇa danced on the serpent Kāliya and drove him from her waters
A queenIn the Bhāgavata, Kālindī did penance by the river and became one of Kṛṣṇa’s eight chief queens
PuṣṭimārgaVallabhācārya’s Yamunāṣṭaka praises her as the one who brings souls to Kṛṣṇa
PrayāgaAt the end she meets the Gaṅgā and the hidden Sarasvatī at Prayāga

ZAPPY: Why is the Yamunā dark?

PROF. BOLT: Her mother couldn't face the Sun's glare; the daughter carries the shadow. Kṛṣṇa, the dark one, loved her for it.

THE SAGE ASITA

Gaṅgā came to him
His hermitage. The sage Asita is said to have lived here, bathing every day in both the Yamunā and the Gaṅgā.
Too old to walk. When age stopped him crossing the mountains to the Gaṅgā, a stream of the Gaṅgā is said to have appeared beside the Yamunā for him.

ZAPPY: A river came to an old man?

PROF. BOLT: A holy one did. Faith that strong moves water.

THE TEMPLE

Rebuilt by snow and flood
ImageA black stone image of Yamunā; a silver image too; Gaṅgā is worshipped beside her
BuilderDisputed: a ruler of Tehri Garhwal (Sudarśan Śāh, 1839, or Pratāp Śāh), and/or Mahārānī Guleriā of Jaipur
DamagedTwice destroyed by snow and flood in the 19th century and rebuilt
PriestsFrom Kharsālī, the village below

ZAPPY: Nobody knows who built it?

PROF. BOLT: Several people did, one after another. In the Himalaya, every temple is a rebuild.

SŪRYA KUṆḌA AND THE DIVYA ŚILĀ

Cooking in the Sun’s spring
Sūrya KuṇḍaThe hottest spring, named after Yamunā’s father: water close to boiling
PrasādaRice and potatoes tied in muslin are dipped in it and cooked, then offered and taken home
Gaurī KuṇḍaA milder spring where pilgrims bathe
Divya ŚilāA rock pillar near Sūrya Kuṇḍa, worshipped first, before the goddess
OthersSome accounts list five kuṇḍas: Sūrya, Tapta, Yamunā, Viṣṇu and Brahma

KHARSĀLĪ

Winter with her brother
VillageKharsālī, below Yamunotrī: home of her priests
ŚaniA temple of Śani, held to be Yamunā’s brother, stands here
ClosingOn Bhāī Dūj her image is carried down from Yamunotrī to Kharsālī
OpeningAround Akṣaya Tṛtīyā in spring, she is carried back up
EscortTradition has Śani’s palanquin escorting his sister each way

ZAPPY: She spends winter at her brother's?

PROF. BOLT: On Bhāī Dūj, of all days. Even the calendar is a family story here.

WHY PILGRIMS COME

The first of four
No untimely deathA bath in the Yamunā is said to keep Yama away until one’s time
First stopThe Himalayan Char Dham traditionally begins here, west to east
A sister’s blessingBrothers and sisters come together, especially near Bhāī Dūj
PurificationHer waters are held to cleanse sin; even the sight of her is blessed
