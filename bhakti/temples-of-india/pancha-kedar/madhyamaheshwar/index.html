MADHYAMAHEŚVAR

PAÑCA KEDĀR · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The navel of the bull

FOREWORD

Madhyamaheśvar, “the lord in the middle”, holds the bull’s navel. It sits in a green high valley with the four summits of Chaukhamba rising behind it.

Its liṅga is navel-shaped, its water is considered especially holy, and like Kedārnāth it is served by South Indian priests. This guide tells its story.

ZAPPY: Why the middle?

PROF. BOLT: The navel is the body's centre. This is the centre of the five.

Madhyamaheśvar is the quietest of the major Kedārs: a stone temple in a broad, green high valley, with the snow peaks of Chaukhamba behind it. Its name means the great lord of the middle.

Like Kedārnāth, it is served by priests from the South Indian Vīraśaiva tradition, and in winter its image goes down to the same temple at Ukhimath.

THE NAVEL

The middle of Śiva
LegendThe bull’s navel and middle rose here
LiṅgaShaped like a navel
WaterWater from the shrine is considered so holy that a few drops are enough for purification
CentreThe navel as the centre of life: the cord that once fed us

The navel, nābhi, is rich in meaning in Indian thought. Brahmā is born from a lotus growing from Viṣṇu’s navel; the navel is the centre of the body and the site of the cord that fed us before birth. Śiva’s navel here makes the shrine a centre of the five.

The tradition that a few drops of the shrine’s water are enough for purification gives it a special place among pilgrims.

BŪḌHĀ MADHYAMAHEŚVAR

The old shrine above
AboveA small shrine on a meadow above the temple
LakeA small lake that reflects Chaukhamba
OldIts name means “old Madhyamaheśvar”
TraditionPilgrims climb to it after darśan below

Above the temple, a short climb leads to Būḍhā Madhyamaheśvar, a tiny shrine on a meadow by a small lake. On still mornings the lake mirrors Chaukhamba’s snows.

Many pilgrims consider this view one of the finest rewards of the Pañca Kedār.

SOUTH INDIAN PRIESTS

From Karnataka again
PriestsJaṅgama priests from the south, as at Kedārnāth
WinterThe image joins Kedārnāth at Omkāreśvar, Ukhimath
RākeśvarīA goddess honoured as Madhyamaheśvar’s consort at the Ukhimath end
FairA festival when the ḍolī returns in spring

The ḍolī of Madhyamaheśvar travels between the temple and Ukhimath each season, stopping at villages that hold festivals for it. At Ukhimath it shares the Omkāreśvar temple with Kedārnāth’s image.

Local tradition honours the goddess Rākeśvarī at Ukhimath as Madhyamaheśvar’s consort, and a fair marks the god’s return to the mountains.

THE VALLEY

A green bowl

ChaukhambaIts four summits rise behind the temple
WildlifePart of a rich Himalayan forest region

The path to the temple passes through villages and forests that are among the most beautiful in Garhwal. The valley’s isolation has kept it quiet compared with Kedārnāth.

Chaukhamba, “four pillars”, a massif of four summits, dominates the skyline.

WHAT MADHYAMAHEŚVAR TEACHES

The centre
CentreKeep a centre in life
PurityA few holy drops are enough
Middle wayBalance between extremes

Madhyamaheśvar, the middle, suggests balance: a centre held steady between extremes. Its holy water adds a gentle lesson: purity needs only a little, given rightly.
