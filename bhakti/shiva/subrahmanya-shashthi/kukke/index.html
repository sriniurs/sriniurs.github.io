KUKKE SUBRAHMAṆYA

SUBRAHMAṆYA ṢAṢṬHĪ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The serpent lord on the Kumāradhārā

FOREWORD

Deep in the forests of the Western Ghats, where the Kumāradhārā river comes down from Kumāra Parvata, Śiva’s son is worshipped not as a warrior with a spear but as the lord of serpents.

Here, the story goes, the great serpent Vāsuki hid in a cave from Garuḍa, and Subrahmaṇya promised to stay with him forever. So at Kukke the god and the serpent are worshipped together, and families from all over India come for the rites of the nāgas.

This part tells the stories, walks through the shrine, and explains the festival that makes Kukke the heart of Subrahmaṇya Ṣaṣṭhī.

Om Śrī Subrahmaṇyāya Namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A god worshipped as a snake?

PROF. BOLT: As the snake’s protector, and in the snake. At Kukke the two cannot be separated.

ZAPPY: Why is everyone here for snakes?

PROF. BOLT: Because the serpent king himself lives here, under the god's protection. Who better to ask?

ITS NAMES

Kukke, Subrahmaṇya, Subbappa
Kukke SubrahmaṇyaThe temple’s common name; “Kukke” is tied to the old Kukke liṅga worshipped here
SubrahmaṇyaAlso the name of the village around the temple
Kukke Śrī Subrahmaṇya SvāmiThe full temple name
Subbappa, SubbarāyaAffectionate Kannaḍa and Tuḷu names for the god
Annadāta Subbappa“Subbappa the giver of food”: the temple is known for feeding pilgrims
Ahipeśvara“Lord of serpents”: an old name of the Kukke liṅga

VĀSUKI AND GARUḌA

The serpent who hid in a cave
The enemy. Garuḍa, the eagle who carries Viṣṇu, was the old enemy of the serpents and hunted them to eat.
The hiding place. Vāsuki, king of serpents and a great devotee of Śiva, hid in a cave called Biladvāra near the Kumāradhārā, deep in prayer.
The fight. In one telling Garuḍa found him and attacked, until the sage Kaśyapa, father of both, asked him to spare Śiva’s devotee.
Śiva’s answer. Vāsuki prayed to Śiva for safety. Śiva sent him to his son: “Subrahmaṇya will protect you.”
The promise. Subrahmaṇya came, blessed Vāsuki and promised to stay here with him forever. So worship of Vāsuki here is worship of Subrahmaṇya.

ZAPPY: Isn't the peacock also a snake-eater?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, and still it is the snake who gets refuge. That's the point: the god protects even his mount's prey.

THE WEDDING AND THE RIVER

Kumāra Parvata and the Kumāradhārā
After the war. In this tradition, after killing Tāraka and Śūrapadma, Skanda came with Gaṇeśa to the peak now called Kumāra Parvata.
The wedding. Indra gave him Devasenā on Mārgaśīrṣa śuddha Ṣaṣṭhī, with the gods present.
The river. Waters of all the sacred rivers brought for the wedding flowed down as the Kumāradhārā, “the stream of Kumāra”.
The vel washed. Another telling says he washed his vel in this river after the war.
The bath. So pilgrims bathe in the Kumāradhārā before entering the temple.

THE SANCTUM

God above, serpents below
TopSubrahmaṇya, seated, the lord of the shrine
MiddleVāsuki, king of serpents, whom he protects
BaseŚeṣa (Ādiśeṣa), the serpent Viṣṇu rests on
Garuḍa pillarA silver-covered pillar stands between the sanctum and the entrance; tradition says it shields devotees from the fiery breath of Vāsuki

ZAPPY: Garuḍa guards the people from the serpent here?

PROF. BOLT: Neatly balanced. Garuḍa can't touch Vāsuki, and Vāsuki's breath can't touch you.

ĀDI SUBRAHMAṆYA AND BILADVĀRA

The anthill and the cave
Ādi SubrahmaṇyaA short walk north of the main gopura: a separate shrine where an anthill (valmīka) in the sanctum is worshipped every day
Why “Ādi”“The first”: held to be the original seat of the serpent god
Biladvāra“The door of the hole”: the cave where Vāsuki is said to have hidden and prayed
Kāśikaṭṭe GaṇapatiAn old Gaṇeśa shrine nearby, said in tradition to be set up by Nārada
Darpaṇa tīrthaA stream behind the temple, flowing into the Kumāradhārā

THE KUKKE LIṄGA AND ŚAṄKARA

A liṅga in Skanda’s home
Kukke liṅgaBefore the present image, worship centred on the Kukke liṅga, also called Ahipeśvara, “lord of serpents”
ŚaṅkaraThe biographies of Ādi Śaṅkara record his visit here
The hymnIn the Subrahmaṇya Bhujaṅgam, attributed to Śaṅkara, a verse praises the god as the “Kukke liṅga”
The metreBhujaṅga, “the serpent”: a gliding metre, fitting for a serpent shrine
Two lineagesA Śṛṅgeri maṭha and the Chandramauḷīśvara shrine stand in the precincts; the Subrahmaṇya Maṭha of the Mādhva tradition is beside the temple

ZAPPY: A Śiva liṅga in Subrahmaṇya's temple?

PROF. BOLT: Father and son share the house. The son's name was first written on the father's stone.

THE NĀGA RITES

Why families come to Kukke
Sarpa saṃskāraA two-day rite, like last rites for a serpent, done by those who believe they harmed a snake, in this life or another
Āśleṣā baliA rite on the day of the Āśleṣā star, sacred to the serpents
Nāga pratiṣṭhāInstalling a stone serpent image, often after a vow
Sought forChildren, health, marriage, and relief from sarpa doṣa, the trouble believed to follow harm to serpents
TraditionKept by the temple’s priests; details and rules are given by the temple

ZAPPY: Do these rites really work?

PROF. BOLT: That's for the believer. What's certain is how old the trust in them is along this coast.

CAMPĀ ṢAṢṬHĪ AT KUKKE

The great chariot
WhenMārgaśīrṣa śuddha Ṣaṣṭhī, the day of the wedding: Subrahmaṇya Ṣaṣṭhī
FestivalA run of days with processions, ending in the Brahmaratha, the great chariot, pulled by devotees
Other daysKiru Ṣaṣṭhī, the “small Ṣaṣṭhī”; Nāgara Pañcamī; Nāgārādhane
CrowdsThe busiest days of the year at Kukke
FeedingPilgrims are fed by the temple, true to the name Annadāta Subbappa

HISTORY

Priests, maṭhas and the State
Paraśurāma kṣetraCounted among the seven holy places of the coast said to be set up by Paraśurāma
StyleBuilt in the Karnataka coastal style: sloping tiled roofs, wood, granite
StewardsThe Koojugodu Kattemane family long served as chief administrators, with close ties to Śṛṅgeri
PriestsPriesthood passed between Brahmin communities in the 19th century; the Śrī Subrahmaṇya Sabhā was founded in 1908
TodayRun by the Karnataka Endowment Department; among the state’s richest temples by income

ZAPPY: Why so many groups in one temple's story?

PROF. BOLT: Big temples gather many hands. The god stays the same; the stewards change.
