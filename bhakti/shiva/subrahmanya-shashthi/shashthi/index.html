THE ṢAṢṬHĪ STORY

SUBRAHMAṆYA ṢAṢṬHĪ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
The war of Skanda, and the day we remember it

FOREWORD

Skanda was born for a war. On the sixth day of the bright fortnight, the tradition says, he finished it: the demon king was struck down, and from his body came the peacock Skanda rides and the rooster on his flag.

That sixth day, Ṣaṣṭhī, became his day. Tamil Nadu keeps Skanda Ṣaṣṭhī in Aippasi with six days of fasting and the great Sūrasaṃhāram on the beach at Tiruccendūr. A month later, Karnataka and the Telugu lands keep Subrahmaṇya Ṣaṣṭhī in Mārgaśīrṣa, with chariots at Kukke and offerings at the cobra’s anthill.

This part tells the war, explains the two Ṣaṣṭhīs, and shows how the day is kept.

Vetrivel Murugaṉukku Arōharā,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Two Ṣaṣṭhīs for one god?

PROF. BOLT: Two regions, two months, one victory. Tamil Nadu remembers the war; Karnataka remembers the wedding and the serpent.

ZAPPY: Is it the same day every year?

PROF. BOLT: Same lunar day, different calendar date. Check your pañcāṅga each year.

WHICH ṢAṢṬHĪ IS WHICH?

Three names, three months
Monthly ṢaṣṭhīEvery bright Ṣaṣṭhī: a small fast for Skanda, all year round
Skanda Ṣaṣṭhī (Kanda Ṣaṣṭi)Aippasi (Oct–Nov): Tamil Nadu, Sri Lanka, Malaysia. Six days; the war on day six, the wedding on day seven
Subrahmaṇya ṢaṣṭhīMārgaśīrṣa (Nov–Dec): Karnataka and the Telugu states; drik pañcāṅgas call it “the next Ṣaṣṭhī after Sūrasaṃhāram”
Campā Ṣaṣṭhī, MaharashtraThe same Mārgaśīrṣa day ends the six-day festival of Khaṇḍobā at Jejuri, a form of Śiva
Puṣya ṢaṣṭhīThe month after: Ghāṭi Subrahmaṇya’s chariot festival
Why “Campā”?Often said to be for the campaka flower, dear to Skanda; explanations differ

ZAPPY: So Kukke Ṣaṣṭhī and Jejuri's Campā Ṣaṣṭhī fall on one day?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. Two sons of the same family, you could say: Śiva's son at Kukke, Śiva himself as Khaṇḍobā at Jejuri.

WHY THE SIXTH DAY?

Sixes everywhere
Six mothersNursed by the six Kṛttikās
Six facesṢaṇmukha: one face per mother
Six days of warIn the Tamil telling the battle with Śūrapadma lasts six days
Six syllablesŚa-ra-va-ṇa-bha-va
Six abodesThe Āṟupaṭai Vīṭu of Tamil Nadu
Six enemiesA common teaching: the six inner foes (ṣaḍripu) of desire, anger, greed, delusion, pride and envy, which Skanda’s vel destroys

ZAPPY: Is the six enemies idea in the Purāṇa?

PROF. BOLT: It's a teaching built on the story, very popular in Ṣaṣṭhī sermons. Read it as a meaning, not a fact.

THE GENERAL OF THE GODS

A boy takes command
Grown in days. The child of the reeds grew in six days into a young warrior.
Made general. The gods made him Devasenāpati, commander of their army. Each gave a gift: Pārvatī the vel, others weapons, a banner and a mount.
The march. With the gods behind him, Kumāra marched against Tāraka’s city.
The brothers. In the Tamil telling, Śūrapadma ruled with his brothers Siṃhamukha, lion-faced, and Tāraka, elephant-faced.
The messenger. Skanda first sent his commander Vīrabāhu to offer peace. Śūrapadma refused.

TĀRAKA AND THE KRAUÑCA HILL

The first victory
The battle. Tāraka fought with every trick of illusion. He hid behind the mountain Krauñca, which his ally guarded.
The spear. Skanda hurled his vel. It pierced the Krauñca mountain right through, and killed Tāraka.
Krauñca. Old texts remember Skanda as Krauñcabhettā, “the piercer of Krauñca”. In some tellings Krauñca is a demon ally of Tāraka.
The boon kept. Brahmā’s boon held: only Śiva’s son could kill Tāraka, and he did.
In the south. The Tamil Kanda Purāṇam goes on: Siṃhamukha falls next, and then the king, Śūrapadma.

ŚŪRAPADMA AND THE MANGO TREE

Pride split in two
Six days. The war raged for six days. One by one, Śūrapadma’s sons and brothers fell.
Shape-shifting. On the last day the demon king took one form after another. At last he became a huge mango tree standing in the sea.
The vel strikes. Skanda hurled his vel and split the tree in two.
Mercy. Even then he did not destroy him. One half became the peacock he rides, the other the rooster on his banner.
The meaning. The enemy is not killed but turned into a servant. Many read it as the ego, split and tamed, carrying the god.

ZAPPY: He spared the worst demon?

PROF. BOLT: He spared what was left after the pride was gone. That's the Ṣaṣṭhī lesson.

SŪRASAṂHĀRAM

The war re-enacted on the beach
WhereTiruccendūr, Murugan’s seashore abode, where he camped for the war
WhenEvening of Skanda Ṣaṣṭhī, the sixth day of the Aippasi festival
WhatMurugan’s image meets effigies of the demon, who keeps changing heads; finally the tree and the peacock
Next dayTirukkalyāṇam, his wedding with Devasenā
FastMany devotees fast for all six days, some on one meal, some on milk and fruit; others keep it on Ṣaṣṭhī alone
ElsewhereKept in Murugan temples across Tamil Nadu, Sri Lanka, Malaysia and Singapore

THE WEDDING AT KUMĀRA PARVATA

Karnataka’s Ṣaṣṭhī
After the war. In the Karnataka tradition, after victory Skanda came with his brother Gaṇeśa to Kumāra Parvata in the Western Ghats.
Indra’s gift. There Indra gave him his daughter Devasenā in marriage.
The day. The wedding took place on Mārgaśīrṣa śuddha Ṣaṣṭhī. That is Karnataka’s Subrahmaṇya Ṣaṣṭhī.
The river. Holy waters brought from every river for the wedding flowed together as the Kumāradhārā, the river at Kukke.
The serpent. Nearby, the serpent king Vāsuki was praying in a cave. Skanda stayed to protect him. That story is Part 3.
Visit it: Kukke Subrahmaṇya (Part 3)

HOW KARNATAKA KEEPS IT

At home and at the anthill
FastMany fast till the pūjā, or keep one meal
The anthillMilk, turmeric, flowers and a lamp offered at the cobra’s anthill or at nāga stones under a peepal tree
FeedingIn many homes a young bachelor boy is fed and honoured as the boy-god Subrahmaṇya
TemplesChariot festivals at Kukke Subrahmaṇya and Subrahmaṇya shrines across the state
MysuruṢaṣṭhī is kept at Mysuru’s Subrahmaṇya temples, such as the one at Bannimaṇṭap
Snake vowsA day to fulfil vows made for children, health and freedom from “sarpa doṣa”

ZAPPY: Do I need a special mantra?

PROF. BOLT: His name is enough. Initiated mantras come from a guru; this guide doesn't print them.

Simplest of all: say his name six times, once for each face. Meet him first in Part 1.

WHAT ṢAṢṬHĪ TEACHES

The war inside
The demonŚūrapadma is pride: “I am the doer, I own everything”
The velClear knowledge, which pierces illusion
The split treeThe ego cut in two, not wiped out: its energy turned to service
Six daysSix inner enemies, overcome one day at a time
The weddingVictory is followed by union: power joined to grace

ZAPPY: So the fast is a little war of my own?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly. Six days of saying no to the six enemies, then the sweet on day seven.
