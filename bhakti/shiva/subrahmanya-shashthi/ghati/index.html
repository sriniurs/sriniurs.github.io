GHĀṬI SUBRAHMAṆYA

SUBRAHMAṆYA ṢAṢṬHĪ · A REFERENCE GUIDE
Two gods in one stone, near Bengaluru

FOREWORD

An hour north of Bengaluru, in the dry hills of Doḍḍaballāpura, stands a temple with one of the strangest images in Karnataka. Facing east is Subrahmaṇya, as a seven-hooded cobra. Facing west, on the back, is Lakṣmī Narasiṃha. You see the cobra directly, and Narasiṃha in a mirror.

The story is the same theme as Kukke, told another way: the son of Śiva took the form of a serpent, and Viṣṇu himself, as Narasiṃha, guarded him from Garuḍa.

This last part visits Ghāṭi, its legends, its forest of nāga stones and its chariot festival a month after Subrahmaṇya Ṣaṣṭhī.

Om Śrī Subrahmaṇyāya Namaḥ,

ZAPPY: Śiva’s son and Viṣṇu in one stone?

PROF. BOLT: Back to back, protecting each other. Ghāṭi settles every Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava quarrel without a word.

ZAPPY: Why a mirror?

PROF. BOLT: Because the two gods face opposite ways. The mirror lets you see both at once without walking round.

THE NAME

Pot, hill or demon?
Ghaṭa sarpaA popular explanation: the cobra’s spread hood looks like a pot (ghaṭa); the god lived here as a “pot-hooded serpent”
GhāṭAnother: “ghāṭ”, the hill country where the temple stands
GhaṭikāsuraA third: the demon Ghaṭikāsura, said to have been killed here
Full nameŚrī Ghāṭi Subrahmaṇya Svāmi Kṣetra
AlsoCounted among seven svayaṃvyakta (self-revealed) shrines of Subrahmaṇya

ZAPPY: Which one is right?

PROF. BOLT: Traditions differ. All three tell you something about the place: a serpent, a hill and a victory.

THE SERPENT’S PENANCE

Why the god became a cobra
Penance. Tradition says Subrahmaṇya came to these hills and did penance in the form of a serpent.
Why? Tellings differ. In one, it was before his war with Tāraka. In another, it was to atone after the killing in that war.
Ghaṭikāsura. A local story adds a demon, Ghaṭikāsura, whom the god destroyed here.
The serpent clan. While he was here as a serpent, he learnt that the nāgas around him lived in fear of Garuḍa.
Next. He turned to Viṣṇu for help. That is the next page.

NARASIṂHA GUARDS THE NĀGAS

Viṣṇu stands behind
A request. Subrahmaṇya asked Viṣṇu to keep his mount Garuḍa from harming the serpents.
Lakṣmī Narasiṃha. Viṣṇu came with Lakṣmī as Lakṣmī Narasiṃha and stood guard over him.
Protection. Garuḍa could not touch the serpents here; Narasiṃha gave them abhaya, freedom from fear.
Two in one. Sages, one telling names Mārkaṇḍeya, prayed that both forms stay here together. So the image shows the cobra facing east and Narasiṃha facing west.
Same theme. At Kukke the son protects the serpent from Garuḍa; at Ghāṭi Viṣṇu himself holds his own eagle back.
Compare: Kukke Subrahmaṇya, where Vāsuki hides from Garuḍa

THE IMAGE AND THE MIRROR

How you see both gods
East faceSubrahmaṇya as a seven-hooded cobra: the main darśan
West faceLakṣmī Narasiṃha, on the back of the same image
The mirrorA large mirror in the sanctum shows Narasiṃha to devotees standing before Subrahmaṇya
SvayambhūBoth are held to have risen from the earth, not carved by hands
Sources differMost say one stone; some describe two images back to back

THE FOREST OF NĀGA STONES

Vows for children
Nāga stonesThousands of stone serpent images stand near the temple, set up by devotees
HarakeA vow (harake in Kannaḍa): couples pray here for children and return to fulfil it
Nāga pratiṣṭhāInstalling a nāga stone is a common way of fulfilling a vow
KetuThe temple is also a centre of worship for Ketu, the shadow planet linked with serpents
Sarpa doṣaAs at Kukke, devotees come for relief from troubles believed to follow harm to snakes

HISTORY

The Ghorpaḍes of Sandūr
Recorded historyMore than 600 years, by temple records
Ghorpaḍe rulersFirst developed by the Ghorpaḍes, rulers of the small state of Sandūr, who governed parts of Ballāri
StyleA Dravidian-style temple with a gopura
PriestsTradition says one family has served as priests for generations
TodayA major pilgrim centre of the Bengaluru North district

ZAPPY: Sandūr is far from Bengaluru, isn't it?

PROF. BOLT: Over 300 km. Kings built for gods wherever their faith took them, not only at home.

THE CHARIOT AND THE CATTLE FAIR

Puṣya Ṣaṣṭhī at Ghāṭi
BrahmarathotsavaThe great chariot festival on Puṣya śuddha Ṣaṣṭhī, one lunar month after Subrahmaṇya Ṣaṣṭhī
Cattle fairHeld around the festival; one of the best known in South India, with farmers from Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala and Maharashtra
Subrahmaṇya ṢaṣṭhīAlso kept with special worship
Narasiṃha JayantīThe other great festival, for the god on the west face
KārttikaKārttika Pūrṇimā draws crowds too

ZAPPY: A cattle fair at a temple?

PROF. BOLT: The old way. The god's festival was the farmers' market; bullocks were bought under his blessing.

KUKKE AND GHĀṬI

Two serpent shrines side by side
RegionKukke: rainforest of the west coast. Ghāṭi: dry hills near Bengaluru
FormKukke: Subrahmaṇya above Vāsuki and Śeṣa. Ghāṭi: Subrahmaṇya himself as a seven-hooded cobra
ProtectorKukke: Subrahmaṇya protects Vāsuki from Garuḍa. Ghāṭi: Narasiṃha protects the nāgas
PartnerKukke: Ādi Subrahmaṇya’s anthill. Ghāṭi: Lakṣmī Narasiṃha in the mirror
ChariotKukke: Mārgaśīrṣa Ṣaṣṭhī. Ghāṭi: Puṣya Ṣaṣṭhī, a month later
SharedNāga vows, sarpa doṣa, Ṣaṣṭhī, and the god of Karnataka’s serpents
