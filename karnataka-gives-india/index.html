ABOUT 12 MINUTES

01

More than Bengaluru

Say “Karnataka” and most people think of one thing: Bengaluru. Tech parks, startups, traffic.

But look closely at an ordinary Indian day, and Karnataka turns up everywhere. In the coffee you drink in the morning. In the silk saree at a wedding. In the gold in a temple. In the flag on Independence Day, the ink on your finger on election day, and the satellites over your head.

It starts with size. Karnataka covers 191,791 km², about 5.8% of India, making it the sixth-largest state. [1] Inside that space are four UNESCO World Heritage listings: Hampi (1986), Pattadakal (1987), the Western Ghats (2012), and since 2023 the Hoysala temples of Belur, Halebidu and Somanathapura. [2] Somanathapura is just outside Mysuru.

ZappyFour World Heritage listings? I’ve only been to Hampi.

Prof. BoltThen you have three trips to plan. Let’s go through the list: fifteen things India gets from Karnataka.

02

Gold and iron from under the ground

1 · India’s own gold. Almost every gram of gold mined in India comes from one place: the Hutti Gold Mine in Raichur district, India’s only working primary gold mine, run by a Karnataka government company. [3] The Ministry of Mines puts Karnataka’s share at 92.4% of India’s gold production in 2025–26, and some reports go as high as 99%. [4] [5]

The famous Kolar Gold Fields, the KGF of the films, are in Karnataka too. They were once among the deepest mines in the world and closed in 2001. [6]

2 · India’s magnetite. Iron ore comes in two main kinds. For one of them, magnetite, Karnataka holds about 72–73% of India’s deposits, according to the Indian Bureau of Mines. [7] [8]

Karnataka’s share, under the ground
Primary gold mined · 92%+→Magnetite iron ore · 73%
Hutti is small next to India’s appetite for gold, but it is the only working primary gold mine the country has, and it’s in Karnataka.
03

Your morning coffee, your wedding silk

3 · Coffee. Karnataka grows about 70% of India’s coffee. [9] The heart of it is three hill districts. For 2025–26, Coffee Board figures put Kodagu (Coorg) at 1.30 lakh tonnes, Chikkamagaluru at 1.04 lakh and Hassan at about 45,000, out of India’s 4.03 lakh tonnes. [10] And most of India’s coffee goes abroad: nearly 70% is exported to 128 countries. [11]

4 · Silk. Karnataka is India’s leading silk state, with about 40% of the country’s raw silk by one Deccan Herald count. [12] For mulberry silk, the kind woven into Mysore silk sarees, its share is about 42%. [13] The cocoon market in Ramanagara is described as the largest in Asia. [14]

ZappySo my filter coffee and my sister’s wedding saree probably came from the same state?

Prof. BoltVery likely. Karnataka dresses India and wakes it up.

04

Two gifts from Mysuru

5 · Sandalwood. Mysore sandalwood is one of the most famous fragrances in the world. The Mysore government set up a sandalwood oil distillery in 1916–17, and in 2006 “Mysore Sandalwood Oil” won a Geographical Indication (GI) tag, so the name can only be used for the real thing. [15] The story has a lovely twist: when the First World War stopped sandalwood exports, the state was left with piles of wood, and turning it into oil and soap is how Mysore Sandal soap began. [16] Today the tree is being planted again: Karnataka Soaps and Detergents has tied up with 670 farmers to grow sandalwood on 3,586 acres. [17]

6 · The ink on your finger. Every Indian who has ever voted carries a little Mysuru on their finger. The purple ink comes from Mysore Paints and Varnish Ltd, a Karnataka government company founded in 1937 by the Maharaja of Mysore. [18] It has supplied the Election Commission since 1962 and exports the ink to more than 25 countries, including Canada, Ghana, Nigeria, Malaysia and South Africa. [19]

A Maharaja’s sandalwood factory and a Maharaja’s lac factory. Both are over a century old in spirit, and both still reach every corner of India.
05

Two national records from Hubballi

North Karnataka’s twin city has two claims to fame that the whole country shares.

7 · The national flag

The Karnataka Khadi Gramodyoga Samyukta Sangha at Bengeri, Hubballi is the only unit in India certified by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) to make the national flag. It won that certification in 2006. [20] The Tricolour hoisted at the Red Fort comes from here, and it is made only from hand-spun, hand-woven khadi. [21]

8 · The world’s longest railway platform

Platform 8 at Shree Siddharoodha Swamiji station in Hubballi is 1,507 m long, about one and a half kilometres. Guinness World Records recognised it in March 2023. [22]

ZappyThe flag at the Red Fort is from Hubballi?

Prof. BoltEvery Independence Day. Next 15 August, look at the flag and think of a khadi unit in Bengeri.

06

The waterfall that lit up India

9 · Hydroelectric power. In 1902, the old Mysore state did something remarkable. It built a hydroelectric station at the Shivanasamudra falls on the Kaveri and sent the power 92 miles to the Kolar Gold Fields. [23]

Then came the city. The plan was to divert 300–400 horsepower of spare power to light Bengaluru, and the Maharaja approved it on 30 May 1904. On 5 August 1905, more than 100 electric street lamps came on near K.R. Market, replacing kerosene lamps that three men had to clean and light every evening. [24] By the end of 1905 there were 1,395 street lamps in the city. [23] Bengaluru became one of Asia’s earliest electrically lit cities. [25] Two of the original 30-foot lamp posts still stand at the BBMP head office. [26]

10 · Engineers’ Day. The man behind the next great Kaveri project was Sir M. Visvesvaraya. As chief engineer he built the Krishna Raja Sagara (KRS) dam near Mysuru, constructed between 1911 and 1931. [27] He went on to become Diwan of Mysore and won the Bharat Ratna in 1955. [28] His birthday, 15 September, is celebrated as Engineers’ Day in India, and in Sri Lanka and Tanzania too. [27]

From a waterfall to a national holiday for engineers
1902 · Shivanasamudra power to KGF→1905 · Bengaluru’s street lights→1911–31 · KRS dam→15 Sept · Engineers’ Day

ZappySo the same river that gives Mysuru its water also lit up Bengaluru 120 years ago?

Prof. BoltThe same Kaveri. Karnataka has been turning water into progress for a long time.

07

From satellites to software

The engineering spirit of Visvesvaraya’s time never left. Today it builds things that fly.

11 · India’s space agency

ISRO’s headquarters are at Antariksh Bhavan in Bengaluru. [29] The U R Rao Satellite Centre in Bengaluru is ISRO’s lead centre for designing and building satellites, including communication, navigation and interplanetary missions, and its teams have built around 127 of them. [30]

12 · Planes and helicopters for the forces

According to the state’s Aerospace and Defence Policy, 67% of all aircraft and helicopters made for India’s defence services are built in Karnataka, and 25% of India’s aircraft and spacecraft industry is based here. [31]

13 · Software for the world

Karnataka is India’s top exporter of software and services: about $173 billion in 2025–26, a 41% share by the state’s count. [32] Central government data for 2024–25 puts the share at 35%, more than the next three states combined. [33]

14 · Startups

As of 30 June 2026, the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade had recognised 24,026 startups from Karnataka, second only to Maharashtra. [34]

The satellites over your head, the aircraft our forces fly and the apps on your phone often start in the same city.
08

The wild side, and the full list

15 · India’s elephants and tigers. Karnataka has the most wild elephants of any state: an estimated 6,395 in its 2023 census, with Bandipur (1,116) and Nagarahole (831) leading. [35] It also has India’s second-largest tiger population, 563 in the 2022 national count, after Madhya Pradesh. [36]

ZappyI came in thinking Karnataka meant Bengaluru traffic.

Prof. BoltAnd now? From a gold mine in Raichur to a flag unit in Hubballi to a satellite centre in Bengaluru, it’s a whole state that India relies on, every single day.
