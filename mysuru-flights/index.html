Why Can’t
Mysuru Fly
Anywhere?

A palace city with an airport older than Independence, a runway 1,740 metres long, and flights to just two cities. Now IndiGo is shopping for a plane that sits between the one Mysuru gets and the one Mysuru wants.

ABOUT 8 MINUTES

01

A palace city with flights to two cities

Try booking a direct flight from Mysuru to Mumbai, Delhi or Goa. You can’t.

Mysuru’s airport at Mandakalli has scheduled flights to just two cities, Hyderabad and Chennai, on small ATR turboprops flown by IndiGo and Alliance Air. Flights to Goa, Kochi, Belagavi and Hubballi were started at different times and later withdrawn for low demand. [1] When a second daily Hyderabad flight was added in May 2026, the MP came to flag it off. [2]

The airport is not new. The Princely State of Mysore built it in 1940. [3] It was rebuilt for about ₹82 crore and reopened in 2010. [4] Since then the same story has played out again and again. Kingfisher arrived in October 2010 and did well during Dasara, but occupancy fell afterwards and it pulled out in November 2011. [5] SpiceJet came in January 2013 and left in October 2014. [6]

So the real question is not “why no flights?” It is “why do flights keep arriving and then leaving?” There are four answers: a runway, a highway, a subsidy and a plane that doesn’t exist in India yet.

ZappySo Mysuru has had an airport since 1940 and still can’t fly to Mumbai?

Prof. BoltIt had an airport before it had a Chief Minister. Patience is a local tradition.

02

The 1,740-metre problem

Mysuru’s runway measures 1,740 metres by 30 metres. [7] That one number decides which planes can use it.

Every airliner has a take-off field length: the runway it needs at full weight, with a safety margin in case an engine fails at the worst moment. An ATR 72-600, the 70-odd-seat propeller plane Mysuru sees today, needs about 1,315 m at maximum weight at sea level. [8] That is comfortable at Mandakalli.

The jets most Indians fly, the Airbus A320 and Boeing 737, carry about 180 people and need more room. The expansion plan therefore lengthens the runway: to 2,300 m in a first phase and 2,750 m in a second, according to February 2026 reports. [9] One report the same month described 2,750 m as the first phase. [10] The target has changed more than once over the years.

There is an unusual complication. The longer runway would cross NH-766 to Nanjangud, so the plan puts the highway in a tunnel of about 500 m under the runway. Its project report was finalised in April 2026. [12] The Airports Authority of India says the highway authority should bear the extra cost, as agreed in 2023. [11] As of now, who pays for the tunnel is one of the slowest parts of the project.

Mysuru is stuck between two sizes of plane: the 70-seater fits the runway, and the 180-seater doesn’t.
03

Enter the in-between plane

On 4 November 2026, Reuters reported that IndiGo is in early talks to buy 100 Embraer regional jets, with options for about 50 more. Deliveries would likely begin in 2030 or later. [13] Neither company has confirmed it. IndiGo denied talks with Embraer less than three months earlier, [14] and one report says it is also talking to ATR about next-generation turboprops. [15] So for now this is a negotiation, not an order.

Why would India’s biggest airline want a Brazilian plane? Look at the seat counts. Embraer’s E-Jets sit right in Mysuru’s gap: the E175 seats up to 88, the E190-E2 up to 114 and the E195-E2 up to 146. [16] IndiGo flies 44 ATRs and hundreds of Airbus jets, and nothing in between. [17]

Here is a number most news reports skipped. Embraer gives the E190-E2’s take-off field length as 1,615 m at maximum weight, and just 1,150 m with a full cabin on a 500-nautical-mile trip. [18] Mumbai is about 460 nautical miles from Mysuru as the crow flies (our calculation).

That is not a promise. Those figures assume a sea-level airport on a mild day. Mysuru sits 731 m above sea level, [3] and thin, warm air lengthens every take-off run. A runway’s strength and safety areas matter too, and nobody has said these jets are meant for Mysuru. But on paper, the in-between plane fits Mandakalli far more easily than an A320 does.

ZappySo we might get a Mumbai flight without waiting for the tunnel?

Prof. BoltMight. In aviation, ‘might’ is usually followed by ‘in 2030’.

04

Who actually flies from Mysuru?

Here is the surprise: when Mysuru got flights, people used them. In 2019, under the government’s UDAN regional-flights scheme, Alliance Air started Mysuru flights to Kochi, Goa and Hyderabad. Within months all three were running more than 80% full. [19] TruJet’s Chennai flight was reported at 95%. [20]

The airport’s explanation was revealing. Many passengers were not going to Goa or Hyderabad at all. They flew there to catch connecting flights to cities Mysuru had no direct link to. [19] The small airport was working as an on-ramp to the big network.

That fits the Kingfisher story too. Its flights did well during Dasara, when visitors pour in, and emptied once the festival ended. [5] An airline does not plan for the best ten days of the year. It needs a plane that is full on an ordinary Tuesday in February.

WHAT WORKED

Flights to hubs like Hyderabad and Chennai, where you can change to almost anywhere. 80–95% full in 2019.

WHAT DIDN’T

The Mysuru–Bengaluru flight: only 35–40% full the same year.[19] Why it flopped is the next story.

05

The 150 km rival

A Mysuru–Bengaluru flight has been tried at least four times: for a few days in 2015, as a 6 am service in 2016–17, during Dasara 2018, and as a five-day-a-week UDAN flight in 2019. [21] [22] The 2016–17 attempt failed partly because of its awkward early timing. [22] The 2019 one ran only 35–40% full. [19]

The reason is simple arithmetic. A one-hour flight needs check-in, security and a cab at both ends. Bengaluru’s airport is about 170 km from Mysuru by road, [23] on the far northern side of the city. For most people, driving all the way is just as quick and far more flexible.

Then the road got faster. The 118 km Bengaluru–Mysuru expressway opened in March 2023, and officials said it would cut the trip from about three hours to about 75 minutes. [24] Bengaluru’s airport has hundreds of departures to everywhere. For a family heading to Delhi or Dubai, the expressway is the real Mysuru airport.

06

Why a new plane type is a big bet

If E-Jets fit India so neatly, why has no big Indian airline bought them? Because for an airline, every new aircraft type is like opening a second company.

Pilots are licensed to fly a particular type. An A320 captain cannot simply walk across the apron and fly an Embraer: they need separate training and a separate rating. Engineers need new certifications, and the airline needs a new warehouse of spare parts, new simulators and new manuals. An airline with one or two types can move crews and planes around freely. A third type makes the whole operation less flexible.

That is the hurdle aviation analysts point to: persuading Indian airlines to take on another type, with the investment in crews, maintenance and spares it brings. [25] It is also why Star Air, a small regional airline, is the only Indian commercial operator flying Embraer jets today. [26]

So why is IndiGo still talking? Scale. It plans to grow from about 440 aircraft to around 600 by 2030. [17] Much of a new type’s set-up cost (simulators, training staff, a basic stock of spares) is needed whether you fly 10 planes or 100, so the cost per plane falls as the fleet grows. A 100-jet order is big enough to be worth the bother.

Rough rule of thumb: propellers are cheaper on short hops; jets win once a trip gets long, because they cruise faster and higher. An ATR is ideal for Mysuru–Chennai. Mysuru–Delhi in one is a long afternoon.
07

Made-in-India Embraers?

There is an Indian factory angle. In January 2026, Embraer and Adani Defence & Aerospace signed an agreement on regional aircraft. On 21 February 2026, in New Delhi, they exchanged an enhanced version to set up a final assembly line for the E175 in India, under the government’s Regional Transport Aircraft programme. [27] [28]

A final assembly line is where a plane’s big ready-made sections (wings, fuselage, engines) are joined, wired and tested. It is not the same as building every part in India. The partners also talk about developing local suppliers, maintenance and pilot training around it. [28]

The catch is orders. Embraer’s chief executive said in March that the Indian line depended on orders for at least 200 aircraft, with India-assembled jets possible from 2028 if orders came by the end of this year. [17] As analysts covering the plan note, whether India gets the line depends mainly on firm orders. [29] That is why a 100-jet IndiGo deal matters beyond Mysuru. And the plane in that pact, the 88-seat E175, is the smallest of the family, the one most suited to towns like ours.

IndiGo’s order and India’s jet factory are two halves of the same puzzle. Neither is confirmed yet.
08

So what would actually bring flights?

Remember those 2019 UDAN flights that were 80% full? They came with a subsidy. Under UDAN, the government pays airlines a viability gap fund to keep about half the seats at capped fares, and originally that support lasted three years. [30]

The national results show what happens when it ends. A government audit found that of 774 routes awarded in the first three rounds, only 54 kept flying beyond the three-year subsidy, about 7%. [31] Mysuru’s lost Goa, Kochi, Belagavi and Hubballi flights fit that national pattern.

The government has now changed the rules. Under Modified UDAN, announced in 2026, support lasts five years: full support for the first two, then tapering. [32] It also shifts funding from a levy on airfares to the government budget. [30] A slower taper gives routes more time to find regular flyers before the support stops.

Three things to watch

The 7-acre strip and the tunnel

Until the land is handed over and the tunnel cost is settled, no A320 can land at Mysuru.

IndiGo’s plane choice

An Embraer order would bring a jet that could, on paper, use a shorter runway. A turboprop order would not change much for Mysuru.

Modified UDAN bids

New subsidised routes are the quickest way Mysuru could get Mumbai or Goa back.

Put it together and the answer to “why can’t Mysuru fly anywhere?” is not a lack of people. Flights to hubs filled up. The real limits are a runway built for a 1940s city, a road that got too good, and a plane size missing from India’s fleet. Two of those three could change by the end of this decade.
