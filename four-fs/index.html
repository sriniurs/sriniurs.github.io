Survive the 4 F's

A comic about getting through tough money times in the made-up city of Techapura. A curious young learner asks, and a wise mentor with 33 years of money experience answers: inflation, recession fears, loans, gold, property and a portfolio you can sleep with.

EP 1

Why ₹500 buys so little now

Meanwhile… A senior minister warns that four F's are under pressure worldwide: fuel, food, finance and fertiliser.

Zappy₹500 used to fill my grocery bag. Now it's half empty. Will prices ever come back down?

Prof. BoltAlmost never. About a hundred years ago, one rupee bought around 11 grams of silver. Today those 11 grams cost a few thousand rupees. That gap is inflation.

Real inflation test Skip onion prices. The government controls milk, petrol and vegetables because people protest. Look at a hotel bill instead.

8 annas→₹30

Idli-sambar when Prof. Bolt was a kid vs. a self-service hotel today. Kids back then even sang a little rhyme about the 8-anna idli.

Prof. BoltThis round isn't made at home. It's crude oil, the dollar and wars abroad. That's why the warning came from the foreign affairs side, not the finance side. Nobody here controls it.

Most people keep 2–3 months of expenses as an emergency fund. This time, keep 5–6 months, or more if you can.

EP 2

The real leak: lifestyle inflation

ZappySo should I stop eating out?

Prof. BoltYou can step down: 5-star → 3-star → self-service → roadside → the cheap public canteen. After that there's nowhere left. You still have to eat. Cutting has a limit.

Every 5 years… nobody buys the same car or phone again. We upgrade.

Small hatchback→Bigger car
₹3,000 phone→₹40,000 phone

Same need, bigger bill. That's lifestyle inflation, and it's the part you control.

FOCUS!

You can't control food prices. You can control your upgrades and grow your income. Put your energy into earning more.

At that pace a ₹10 lakh treatment today could cost about ₹20 lakh in five years.

Prof. BoltSome good news. The government is watching hospitals now. They must show landed cost vs MRP for medicines and syringes, and cut bed charges. Hospital shares fell because of it. I expect medical inflation to slow to around 10%.

ZappyI'm young and I go to the gym. Can I skip health insurance?

Prof. BoltInsurers are like you at the fruit stall. They want the good apples. Fall sick first and you get rejected, charged a huge premium, or handed pages of conditions. Buy early.

Can't afford a full policy? At least take a top-up or critical-illness cover. After 45 it's a must. Even if it feels like money dropped into a temple donation box, take it.

Don't rely only on your company's policy. Lose the job and you lose the cover, and companies keep changing the terms.

EP 4

One job lost, ten wallets hit

Meet Murali. He loses his job to AI. Watch what happens next.

Murali's job gone→ Cab driver loses a daily rider→ Auto driver loses the school run (Dad drops the kid now)→ Bar loses its Saturday regular→ Movie hall: weekly visit becomes monthly
DOMINO!

That's how a recession spreads. In trade it works the same way. If my goods can't be shipped out, I cut production, then salaries, then people.

EP 5

How the big crash happened

A big foreign economy, years ago. Business is slow, so home-loan rates are cut step by step from about 10% to 2%.

Cheap money! People buy land left, right and centre. Buy a ₹1 crore plot, pledge it for a ₹60 lakh loan, buy another plot. Prices balloon.

ZappySounds like Techapura land a decade ago, when every plot doubled in a year or two!

Then… Brightstone Bank, over a century old, has lent heavily to these buyers.

Borrowers stop paying. Depositors ask for their money, get told "next month", and panic. Bad news spreads fast, lenders pull out, and the bank collapses.

CRASH!

Our market felt it too

21,000→~8,000

The stock index fell over 50% within a year. ₹1 crore in shares became about ₹48 lakh.

Recessions come once in a while. Build a portfolio that survives them.

EP 6

When it looks too easy, step back

A four-year boom. The stock index goes from about 3,000 to 21,000, roughly 7x. Pick any share and it doubles in months.

A new company launches its IPO. A big, respected brokerage calls it a 100x stock. The price jumps from about ₹40 to ₹700.

PLOT TWIST!

Six months later people discover the "company" was just a man selling juice at a railway station. He pocketed the IPO money.

Two lessons. One: when everything feels rosy, zoom out and look again. Two: even experts and big research firms get it wrong. "They said buy, so I'll make money" never holds.

EP 7

The great market shake-out

Pandemic lockdown. Work from home, business TV on all day, trading apps, finfluencers online.

Millions of new investors pour money into the same shares. The index runs from about 28,000 to 86,000, nearly 3x.

The water bottle lesson

Water bottles run short, so a ₹10 bottle sells for ₹50. Traders see it and ship in thousands of bottles. Suddenly there's too much supply and too few buyers. Price crashes. Shares work the same way.

Now more trading accounts are closing than opening. Online-broker shares are down. The loud finfluencers have gone quiet.

Prof. BoltWhen a tiger attacks a herd, it catches the slowest deer, and the herd gets faster. When weak hands sell, shares move to strong long-term holders. That's actually healthy.

Notice who still shows up when markets are down. Those are the voices you can trust.

EP 8

Build a full thali, not just rice

Prof. BoltRice and sambar alone isn't a meal. You want chapati, palya, buttermilk and papad too. Your money needs the same variety.

That's why some people make money even when the economy, the market or their companies are struggling: their whole portfolio is balanced.

EP 9

Salary slip before horoscope

Marriage talk today. Families used to match horoscopes. Now they ask for the salary slip, and Prof. Bolt thinks that's fair.

Talk about these before the wedding:

  1. Does each person have an income?
  2. Will that income last? AI is making even 25–30 year olds outdated, and companies eye 45+ staff who can be swapped for cheaper juniors.
  3. How much debt does each person have?
  4. Own house or not? Any bad habits?

ZappyIf I buy a flat on loan first, I'll find a match faster, right?

Prof. BoltFaster match, yes. Also a faster trip into the EMI trap. You'll have to decide which freedom you give up.

EP 10

Loan rules of thumb

Rule 1 Keep one full year of EMIs saved, in case the job disappears.

Rule 2 All EMIs together (home + car + personal) stay under 30% of income. Earn ₹1 lakh a month? Max ₹30,000 in EMIs.

Rule 3 Only home loans and education loans are OK. Not phones, TVs, fridges or holidays.

"Zero-cost EMI" usually hides charges and interest. Do the maths at home and it often works out to 15–16% a year. The finance company isn't your grandfather doing you a favour.

Prof. BoltBreak these rules and the house is in your name, but the bank is the real owner. On the 1st of every month, it rings your bell.

"Borrow and eat ghee" That old saying has become true for the middle class. Our elders taught us to save first and spend later. That habit is fading.

Plan big costs instead of borrowing: write each one down with its year. A car that costs ₹8 lakh today may cost about ₹16 lakh in six years. Start a separate SIP for it now.

Have the right amount of money at the right time. Underline that.

EP 11

Will Techapura property fall?

Prof. BoltPretend I'm a foreign company. Twenty years ago Techapura was my only choice. Now four rival cities compete for me, and one offers land almost free. Techapura's weather isn't what it was, and there are local tensions. Why pay ₹100 crore here?

Fewer new companies→AI eats jobs→Talent moves out→ PGs in the tech suburbs emptying→Landlords lose rent→Fewer new flats bought

More likely to drop: the IT belt along the Lakeside Tech Corridor.

Likely to hold: old Techapura that doesn't depend on IT, like the Temple Street and Old Market neighbourhoods.

EP 12

Our state vs the rest

Our state's weak spot Almost the whole state runs on Techapura IT. Other towns never got big industry.

A neighbouring state spreads its bets: cars in one city, textiles in another, engineering in a third. Another neighbour is building an AI hub and growing two port cities.

Once-lagging states are waking up

  • One state lets you buy industrial land online, like online shopping, without meeting an official.
  • Another has cut crime sharply, so factories are arriving.
  • Others are slowly improving.

Prof. Bolt's safety test: if a woman can ride a scooter alone at 9:30 pm, that place is advancing.

How to read the economy

Tax collections show the past. Factory electricity use shows the future. It rose about 12% in two recent months. GDP growth is around 7%, among the fastest of big economies.

GDP isn't your wallet. A billionaire's gain lifts GDP without reaching ordinary people.

Trust your eyes: crowded airports, metros, malls and hospitals, record temple donations, sold-out pilgrimage travel, a rock band filling a 1.2 lakh seat stadium.

EP 13

So, is a recession coming?

Prof. BoltI don't think so. Recessions usually start from problems inside a country. Our problems are outside: costly crude, and imports from our biggest supplier about 17% dearer because the rupee fell against its currency.

Interest rates may keep rising in steps over the next year. Home-loan EMIs go up quickly. Banks raise FD rates much more slowly.

Recession or not, get ready so it can't hurt you: savings, investments, and loans you can carry.

ZappyMy cousin graduates next year. No savings, maybe an education loan. What should she do?

Prof. BoltKeep upskilling. With AI, what you study can be outdated in 2–3 years. Start part-time work or learning skills now, so companies chase you. And try to become independent, so you aren't waiting on anyone for a job.

EP 14

Gold sleeps like a giant

ZZZ…

No investment pays every year. Gold has had six-year stretches with almost no returns, more than once. Like the sleeping giant of old stories, it naps for years, then pays out all at once.

Prof. BoltBy my reading, gold is napping for the next year or two. Short-term returns may disappoint. Over 5–10 years, it can still do well.

Silver: the busy metal

  • Used in EVs, solar panels and medicine, not just kitchen vessels.
  • Recycling loses metal: 100 g of gold comes back as 99.9 g, silver as 97–98 g.
  • Harder to find, more demand.

A carmaker's big battery announcement sent silver flying from about ₹80,000 to ₹3 lakh a kg. Then the carmaker shelved its plans. Speculators got caught.

Fun fact: long ago aluminium was pricier than gold, until people learnt to make it cheaply. Nobody knows what science will do to silver.

If you pick only one, silver looks better. Holding both is best. Buy based on your time horizon, not on hype.

EP 15

Digital gold, gold receipts or gold funds?

Prof. BoltAsk one question first. If someone cheats me, can I complain to the central bank or the market regulator? If neither will take responsibility, don't go there, whatever the returns.

  • App-based digital gold: the regulator says it's not their responsibility. Avoid.
  • Government gold bonds: new issues have stopped.
  • Electronic gold receipts: trade on the stock exchange. Buy with as little as ₹10 or ₹100, sell anytime. Prof. Bolt's pick.
  • Gold mutual funds: higher charges, less useful than before.

Gold's superpower: land depends on the area, shares depend on the company, but gold is the same gold in any country on earth.

Gold may not give the highest returns, but it lets you sleep at night.

EP 16

A recession-proof portfolio

ZappyMy gold SIP dropped and I didn't care. My equity fund dropped and I panicked. Why?

Prof. BoltNewspapers scream "bloodbath" when stocks fall. They never write that about gold or property, because jewellers and builders buy their ads. It's psychology.

Are shares cheap now? Think of the index like buying land.

At the last peak the index was around 26,000, and each "unit" earned about 1,040. Now it's near 22,000 but earnings grew to about 1,175. You get more land for a lower price.

But past performance isn't guaranteed. AI is a sword hanging over parts of the economy, so pick sectors.

Where the country is strong

  • Textiles: the neighbouring country that used to lead is struggling with internal trouble and power costs.
  • Defence: a grenade costs about ₹4,000 from one foreign supplier, ₹2,000 from another, and ₹260 made at home. Drones too.
  • Two economies: the AI/IT one and the manufacturing one. The countryside is weak this year, since poor rains hit fertiliser firms.

Beyond local shares

  • International: keep some money abroad, however patriotic you are. A special finance zone lets you start from about $500.
  • REITs: own slices of tech parks and malls and earn their rent. Over 5 years Prof. Bolt quotes about 12.4% vs the index's 10.4%, with far lower risk.
  • Specialised investment funds: unlike mutual funds, they can move fully to cash or bet on falls. They fell less in a war-scare dip and rose more in the rebound.

The AI picks-and-shovels idea

Nobody knows which AI company wins. But all of them need power, cables, transmission lines and lots of water to cool data centres. Those suppliers earn either way. Look at funds covering them.

Recession-proof isn't exciting. It's a mix of gold, silver, REITs/InvITs, equity mutual funds, specialised funds and international funds that lets you sleep whatever happens.

EP 17

Fearless, not foolish

An old saying The first quality of a wise person is fearlessness.

Recklessness: there's a tiger on the path, let me walk past and see what happens.

Fearlessness: there's a tiger, so I go carrying a gun.

Prof. BoltIn investing, the gun is knowledge. No gun? Walk with someone who has one: a good advisor. And if you crash a lorry on your first drive, don't blame the lorry. Calling the market gambling is the same mistake. Learn to drive.

EP 18

Should I study abroad?

ZappyEveryone's taking ₹50 lakh loans for a master's abroad. Should I?

Prof. BoltCheck where the opportunity really is. One popular region is weakening after losing its cheap energy supply; only one country there is holding up. Another has put up so many barriers that you may face humiliation, and a job isn't guaranteed.

Big loan + no sure job + rising interest rates = trouble repaying.

Unless you only want bragging rights, put the same money into a startup with friends or your family business here. You may do far better.

EP 19

What if fuel hits $120?

Prof. BoltEvery government thinks about the next election. With big polls coming, it will absorb the oil shock instead of passing it straight to the public. The country has about 75 days of reserves, stretchable to around 9 months with curbs, plus a friendly supplier nation.

The indirect hit Fewer freebies and higher taxes, mostly on the rich.

Earn ₹1 crore→18% GST→35% income tax→~₹60 lakh left

That's why more ultra-rich people moved to low-tax countries last year.

People adapt: about half of new two-wheeler registrations are now electric. Fossil fuel use will fall slowly.

No need for doomsday predictions about fuel.

EP 20

Credit card: a double-edged sword

KA-CHING!

Pay the full bill on time and it's the most useful tool around. Prof. Bolt pays his business taxes by card, and the reward points flew his family of four abroad and back in premium class, free. Lounges, concierge booking, airport assistance, premium events.

OUCH!

Pay only the minimum and nothing is more dangerous. Interest can reach 42% a year.

Test yourself: use one card for 6–12 months. Always paid in full and on time? Keep using it. If not, stop.

EP 21

Techapura's next chapter

The bright side: Techapura is quietly becoming a finance capital. Finance firms are opening offices and fintechs are building research centres.

Startup culture is strong. In one startup neighbourhood, nearly every house has one: home nurses, maids, even window cleaning for tall buildings.

95of100

startups fail. About five succeed, and one or two become huge.

If the government backs founders with support and cheaper loans, Techapura stops depending on IT alone and grows again.

EP 22

How much do I need to retire?

The 3% rule Some rich countries use 4%, because seniors there get health support. Here you pay hospital bills yourself, so plan to withdraw only 3% a year, raised for inflation.

₹1 lakh/month spend→₹12 lakh/year→≈ ₹4 crore needed

ZappyWhat if I quit at 42 to start my own thing?

Prof. BoltA startup takes 3–5 years to work. At ₹1 lakh a month, that's about ₹60 lakh for living costs alone, before inflation, and separate from the startup money.

At 40–45 you're the "sandwich generation": kids not earning yet, parents no longer earning. Risk is dangerous then.

Most people say "later" at 25, "too many responsibilities" at 35, and panic at 45. Start planning early so you have a backup.

EP 23

The 3 A's, 3 C's and 5 brothers

Before any loan: the 3 A's

  1. Asset: will it grow my money or remove a real worry?
  2. Affordable: my budget is a ₹3 crore home. Don't buy ₹6 crore just because the bank offers.
  3. Aligned: does it bring a real life goal closer, by years?

Borrowing isn't shameful. Prof. Bolt took a home loan even though he had the cash, because he knew he could repay.

Before any stock: the 3 C's

  1. Character: is the owner honest? Any jail time or criminal cases?
  2. Capability: can they run the company if it grows 10x?
  3. Cost / sustainability: will the money keep coming? Is there a barrier to copying them? Rangmahal Paints ruled for 20 years until a giant conglomerate entered. Ten friends can't build a food-delivery giant or a telecom network overnight.

Or check the five hero brothers of legend

The Honest OneEthics. Clean owner, clean books.
The Strong OneStrength. It sets prices; rivals follow.
The ArcherFocus. One or two businesses, not a hundred.
The ManagerA strong team below the owner.
The SeerVision. Great telecom bosses already talk about 7G.
THE END

Fridge-door cheat sheet

  • Emergency fund: 5–6 months of expenses.
  • Control lifestyle upgrades; grow your income.
  • Buy health insurance early, at least top-up or critical-illness cover. A must after 45.
  • All EMIs under 30% of income.
  • Keep a year of EMIs saved.
  • Borrow only for a home or education.
  • Pay credit cards in full, every time.
  • Diversify like a thali: equity, gold, silver, REITs, specialised funds, international.
  • Only invest where a regulator has your back.
  • When it all looks easy, step back.
  • Retire on ~3% withdrawals: yearly spend × 33.
