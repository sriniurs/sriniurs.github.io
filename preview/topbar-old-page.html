Will a Robot Operate on My Knee?

"Robotic knee replacement" is on hospital hoardings across India. Zappy's grandmother needs a new knee, and the family is confused. Is a machine doing the surgery? Is it better? Is it worth the extra money? Prof. Bolt opens the operation theatre doors.

PART 1: THE BIG WORRYWho is really holding the knife?

EP 1

"A robot will operate on Ajji?!"

ZappyThe hospital said "robotic knee replacement". Ajji is scared a machine will cut her knee while the doctor watches TV!

Prof. BoltHa! No. In these operations the surgeon stays in control. The robot is a very precise helper, like a ruler and a guard rail for the saw.

First, what is a knee replacement?

When arthritis wears out the knee, the surgeon removes thin slices of damaged bone from the thigh bone and shin bone and fits metal and plastic parts in their place.

Where exactly each slice is cut decides how the new knee lines up, moves and feels.

WAIT, REALLY?

Even with good surgery, a large Canadian study found about 1 in 5 knee-replacement patients were not satisfied a year later.

That's the problem robots were built to solve: make every cut more exact, and hope the knee feels better.

EP 2

34 years ago this week

7 November 1992, Sacramento, USA

A robot called ROBODOC helped replace the hip of a 64-year-old man. It was the world's first surgery in which a robot did part of the work on a person.

WHIRR...

It drilled a channel in the thigh bone to fit the implant snugly. The whole operation took nearly 6 hours, about double the usual time.

The robot is now in the Smithsonian museum in Washington. Its name? Inspired by the film RoboCop.

ZappySo robots in joint surgery are older than me!

Prof. BoltMuch older. What's new is how common, faster and smaller they've become, and how loudly hospitals talk about them.

PART 2: INSIDE THE OPERATION THEATREWhat the robot does, step by step

EP 3

Step 1: a map of your knee

Two ways to make the map

  • 📷 CT-based: a CT scan before surgery builds a 3D model of your bones.
  • 🖊️ Imageless: no CT. During surgery, the surgeon touches points on the bone with a tracked pointer, and the computer builds a live model.

ZappyHow does the robot know where the knee is during surgery?

Prof. BoltSmall markers are fixed to the bones, and a camera tracks them, like GPS for the knee. If the leg moves, the map moves with it.

EP 4

Step 2: the surgeon makes the plan

On the screen, the surgeon chooses the implant size, the angle of every cut, and how tight or loose the knee should be when bent and straight.

The computer shows the result in numbers, down to a millimetre or a degree, before a single cut is made.

CLICK!

Not happy? The surgeon changes the plan right there, tests the knee's movement again, and only then approves.

"Balancing" means making the ligaments, the knee's natural ropes, equally snug on both sides.

EP 5

Step 3: the cut, and the "invisible wall"

Three kinds of robot help

TypeWho moves the tool?The robot's job
Hand-heldSurgeonThe cutting tip switches off or pulls back outside the plan
Robotic armSurgeon guides the armAn "invisible wall" stops the saw going past the planned line
AutomaticRobot, after the surgeon approvesCuts the approved plan; the surgeon watches and can stop it

ZappyAn invisible wall for a saw? That sounds like a video game!

Prof. BoltPretty much. The surgeon pushes, and the robot simply refuses to go where the plan says no.

EP 6

What the surgeon still does

Still 100% human

  • Decides whether you need surgery at all.
  • Opens the knee and protects nerves, vessels and ligaments.
  • Makes the plan and every judgement call.
  • Fits the implants, checks the knee, closes the wound.
  • Handles the unexpected, and takes the responsibility.
BIG POINT

The robot doesn't decide what the best knee is. It helps carry out the surgeon's decision more exactly.

A good plan made exact is great. A poor plan made exact is still a poor plan.

PART 3: ROBOT vs HANDSWhat the best studies actually found

EP 7

Round 1: accuracy

An Indian trial of 100 patients (both knees operated)

Robot
82%
Hands
64%

Share of knees lined up within 3 degrees of the target, as reported in a 2026 Indian orthopaedic journal review.

ROUND 1: ROBOT WINS

Study after study agrees: robots cut more precisely and produce fewer "badly aligned" knees.

EP 8

Round 2: how the knee feels

RACER-Knee, UK (The Lancet, August 2026)

The world's largest trial of its kind: 339 patients, 10 hospitals, 33 surgeons. Patients didn't know which surgery they got, and neither did the people scoring them.

"Do you forget your knee is artificial?" score, after 1 year

Robot
49.2
Hands
50.2

Higher is better. The difference is too small to matter.

ROUND 2: A DRAW

Just as safe (16 serious problems in each group), but robotic surgery took about 10 minutes longer and cost more, with no better knee at one year.

EP 9

Round 3: does it last longer?

The UK National Joint Registry (The BMJ, 30 Sept 2026)

Researchers compared 6.97 lakh knee replacements, about 22,000 of them robotic, followed for 2.5 years on average.

Result: no difference in how many needed a repeat operation.

BUT...

Two years is early; knees are meant to last 15–20. And for partial knee replacements, an Australian registry study found one robot-assisted type needed fewer repeat operations than the average hand-done one (though no fewer than the best hand-done design).

ROUND 3: TOO EARLY TO CALL

Proven: robots are more precise. Not yet proven: that this means a better or longer-lasting knee for most people.

THE DEEP DIVEThe research behind the hoardings

EP 10

Why hospitals advertise it

The machine is expensive

In the UK, a robot costs about £1 million to bring in, plus £1,000–2,500 extra per operation (The BMJ).

A hospital that buys one needs patients to use it. So the hoardings, "first in the district" press releases and ads.

What patients pay in India (one Delhi hospital's own figures, Sept 2026)

ZappySo the ad is half medicine, half marketing?

Prof. BoltA real tool, sold loudly. Read the ad as "we have this tool", not "you'll get a better knee".

EP 11

India by the numbers

~290

knee robots installed across India by 2024, from Korean, American, British-owned and Indian makers.

India's own knee robot, MISSO, launched in 2024.

2.5–3.5 lakh

joint replacements a year in India. Only about 5–10% are robotic, a panel of surgeons said in May 2026.

ZOOM!

Robots have moved beyond metros to cities like Shivamogga (2024) and Mangaluru, where one big hospital did its first in July 2026.

EP 12

The insurance catch

In 2019, insurance regulator IRDAI made insurers cover 12 "modern treatments", including robotic surgery.

BUT...

Insurers were allowed to set sub-limits (a cap for that treatment inside your total cover). One public insurer's rule: up to 10% of your sum insured, maximum ₹1 lakh, unless you buy an add-on.

What that means

With ₹5 lakh cover, a sub-limit like that may pay only ₹50,000 towards a robotic surgery. Surgeons told a May 2026 panel that patients often learn this only at admission.

Check your own policy's "modern treatment" clause. Rules differ by insurer and plan.

EP 13

The Karnataka angle: free, in a government hospital

Hubballi, June 2026

KMCRI, the government medical college hospital in Hubballi, did its first robot-assisted knee replacement on a 55-year-old woman. It says it is the first government hospital in Karnataka to offer this.

₹0

She paid nothing, under the Ayushman Bharat–Arogya Karnataka scheme. In a private hospital, the same surgery costs about ₹3–3.5 lakh, the hospital said.

ZappySo robots aren't only for the rich?

Prof. BoltNot any more. And if costs keep falling, the question will shift from "can I afford it?" to "does my knee need it?".

EP 14

Prof. Bolt's verdict (opinion)

GOOD NEWS

Don't fear the robot. It's safe, it's precise, and the surgeon is in charge. Neither choice is risky.

REALITY CHECK

Don't pay just for the word "robotic". The best evidence so far shows the same knee at one year. The surgeon's skill, your fitness and your rehab matter more.

MY TAKE

Choose the surgeon first, then the tool. A robot in experienced hands is a fine choice. A great surgeon without one is just as fine.

EP 15

So what should Zappy do?

5 questions to ask Ajji's surgeon

  • How many knee replacements do you do in a year, and how many with this robot?
  • Why do you suggest robotic surgery for her knee?
  • What will it cost with and without the robot, in writing?
  • Which implant will you use, and why?
  • If the robot has a problem mid-surgery, what happens?

And at home

  • Read the "modern treatment" sub-limit in the insurance policy.
  • Start knee-strengthening exercises before surgery if the doctor allows.
  • Plan for rehab: physiotherapy decides much of the result.

ZappySo I'll tell Ajji: the robot is the surgeon's ruler, not the surgeon.

Prof. BoltPerfect. And on Dhanvantari Jayanti, that's a fine thing to tell her.
