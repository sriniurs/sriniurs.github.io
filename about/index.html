How a story gets made

  1. A real question. Something people are actually arguing about: a festival date, a price, a forward, a new treatment, a local mystery.
  2. Primary sources first. Research papers, clinical guidelines, government data and official statements come before opinion pieces.
  3. Myth, fact or not known yet. Each claim is sorted into one of the three, and the story says which.
  4. Sources at the end. Every story lists what it relied on, so you can read further.
  5. Corrections in the open. If a story is wrong, it gets fixed, and the fix is noted on the page.

Health stories

Stories about bodies, bones and medicines explain what the evidence says, for general understanding. They aren't advice for your situation. Take your questions to a doctor who knows you.
