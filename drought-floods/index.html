217 of Karnataka’s 240 taluks are drought-hit, while Bengaluru’s underpasses fill up after a single evening shower. Both are true. Here is how.

ABOUT 14 MINUTES

01

217 of 240 taluks

This year’s southwest monsoon, June to September, left Karnataka badly short of rain.

The state got about 551 mm against a normal of roughly 852 mm, a shortfall of 35%. [1] [2] The state government called it the second-lowest southwest monsoon since 1956, and the state’s disaster monitoring centre called it the weakest since 1971. [2] [3] The records use different baselines, but they agree on one thing: this was a very weak season.

Drought was declared in stages as officials checked crops and ground conditions. It started with 101 taluks, then 23 more, then 53, which made 177. Another 40 on 2 October brought the total to 217 of 240. Only 23 taluks are outside the list, and they include coastal ones like Udupi, which is normally one of the wettest parts of India. [3] [4] [5]

The state has sought ₹7,142.64 crore from the Centre in two memoranda, and estimates crop damage on about 47 lakh hectares. [1] [6] Even Bengaluru, which stays mostly outside farm statistics, had its second-driest July since 1918: just 23 mm. [7]

The 2026 monsoon, in three steps
June–Sept · 551 mm→35% short→217 of 240 taluks

ZappyEven Udupi? It rains there for months!

Prof. BoltRains there usually. Drought is measured against what a place normally gets, not against Bengaluru.

02

The last time was 1876

In a debate in the Assembly on 22 September, Deputy Chief Minister G. Parameshwara called this a “Super El Niño-driven drought”. He said the rainfall deficit was the worst in more than 150 years and comparable to 1876. [8]

That date carries a lot of weight. The Great Famine of 1876–78 hit the Madras and Bombay presidencies and the princely states of Mysore and Hyderabad. [9] In Mysore state, most of the irrigation tanks had dried up by the end of 1875. [10] The 1881 census counted 41.82 lakh people, 8.74 lakh fewer than in 1871, and Dewan C. Rungacharlu told the Dasara Representative Assembly that the real loss, counting normal growth, was nearer a fifth of the population. [11] About 5.6 lakh cattle were lost, the state ran up a debt of ₹80 lakh, and recovery took close to two decades. [10]

Why bring up 1876 in 2026? Because the cause was similar. Scientists who rebuilt India’s drought record back to 1870 found the 1876 drought came from a strong El Niño together with an unhelpful phase of the Indian Ocean Dipole. [12] Officials have told the state government that this year’s El Niño could be one of the strongest on record. [13]

1876

Rain failed. No large reservoirs, no ration system, relief too little and too late. A drought became a famine.

2026

Rain failed again. But reservoirs, ration shops, crop insurance and a drought manual are built to stop a drought from becoming a famine.

The comparison is about the rain, not the outcome. What changed between 1876 and now is everything people built around the rain.
03

Then why is Bengaluru flooding?

On 5 October, Bengaluru city got 42.6 mm of rain in 24 hours. Underpasses and roads went under water, and trees came down. [14] Five days later the city observatory logged 67.1 mm overnight, flooding areas like Rainbow Layout and Anekal. [15]

Is that a lot? For a whole month, not really. October is Bengaluru’s second-rainiest month and averages about 186 mm. [16] But one night’s 67 mm is more than a third of the whole month’s normal, coming down in a few hours.

This is the heart of the puzzle. Drought is counted over a season. Floods happen in an hour. A state can be 35% short over four months and still get downpours that no drain can carry. Officials and studies keep pointing to the same reasons Bengaluru can’t carry that water away: encroached and blocked drains, garbage, and fast building over the low ground where water used to collect. [17]

ZappySo the sky isn’t short of water. The ground just can’t take it when it comes?

Prof. BoltExactly. Rain falling on the wrong day, in the wrong place, at the wrong speed is still rain. A drought year just has less of the right kind.

04

How a taluk becomes “drought-hit”

Nobody can simply announce a drought. India uses a Manual for Drought Management, and a taluk has to pass a set of tests, in order, before it can be listed. [18] Karnataka’s minister put it plainly: the state cannot declare taluks arbitrarily; they must meet the criteria. [5]

Test 1 · Did the rain fail?

The mandatory first check: how far rainfall fell below normal (or a statistical score called SPI), and whether there was a long dry spell. [18]

Test 2 · Did it hurt?

Four kinds of impact are looked at: crops sown and their condition, satellite readings of how green fields are, soil moisture, and water in reservoirs and the ground. The state picks three of the four. [18] [19]

Severe or moderate?

If all three chosen impacts are severe, the drought is severe. If two of three are moderate or severe, it is moderate. [19]

Test 3 · Go and see

Officials survey sample fields. In Koppal, they checked 37 villages and found more than half the crop lost in 99% of the plots. [22] Karnataka used a “Crop Loss GT” app that records geo-tagged photos on the spot. [21]

That is why the list grew in waves, from 101 taluks on 27 August to 217 by 2 October: each round waited for the field checks. [21] [4] The rules want the monsoon declaration done by 31 October, and a declaration lasts six months. [20] [21]

ZappySo “drought” is a certificate, not just a feeling?

Prof. BoltYes. And the certificate decides relief money, crop insurance and loan breaks. That is why every taluk wants to be counted.

05

El Niño, explained with a bathtub

Picture the Pacific Ocean as a long bathtub. Normally, steady winds push warm water to the western end, near Indonesia. The warm water heats the air above it, the air rises, and that circulation helps pull moist monsoon winds over India.

In an El Niño year, those winds weaken and the warm water sloshes back east, towards South America. The rising air moves with it, and the circulation that feeds our monsoon weakens. [24] Not every El Niño brings drought, but roughly 43% of them have been followed by drought in India. [24] Even a weak one in 2009 cut India’s rain to 78.2% of normal. [27]

This one is not weak. In its 8 October update, the US weather agency NOAA put the chance of it reaching historic strength in October–December at 83%. Its next update is due today, 12 November. [25] One small note: “super El Niño” is a popular name. NOAA itself calls the strongest events “very strong”. [26]

El Niño doesn’t take India’s rain away. It moves the engine that delivers it a few thousand kilometres east.
06

Koppal vs Bengaluru, same season

Karnataka is not one rain zone. In early August, the Cauvery catchment and the Western Ghats had floods and landslides, while farmers in Koppal and Bagalkot watched standing crops dry up. [28] Here is how two places looked by October.

KOPPAL

45% short on rain; Gangavathi taluk 71%. All seven taluks drought-hit. Crop loss about ₹2,174 crore. 109 gram panchayats may run short of drinking water in summer. [22]

BENGALURU

A dry July, then sharp showers: 42.6 mm on 5 October and 67.1 mm overnight on 10–11 October, with flooded roads and underpasses. [7] [14] [15]

In Koppal, groundwater has dropped by 1.5 to 4 metres, there is fodder for about 30 weeks, and officials worry about people leaving villages for work. The job-guarantee scheme gave 12.69 lakh days of work between July and September. [23] Crop insurance sign-ups jumped 158% this year, to more than 1.56 lakh farmers. [22]

One district was short of water to grow food. The other got water faster than it could drain. Neither had a normal year.

ZappyCouldn’t Bengaluru’s extra water just… go to Koppal?

Prof. BoltRain doesn’t follow a map. What we can do is catch it where it falls, which brings us to Mysuru.

07

Mysuru’s water next summer

Mysuru drinks from the Cauvery, mainly through the KRS dam, which sends the city about 249 MLD (million litres a day). [32] Officials estimate drinking water needs at about 150 cusec a day for Mysuru and 300 for Bengaluru. [33]

The dam had a scary start to the year and a partial rescue:

KRS dam level, 2026 (full = 124.80 ft)
15 June · 81.34 ft (last year: 109)→3 Aug · 102.09 ft (last year: ~122)→7 Sept · 106.84 ft

Rain in Kodagu and Wayanad, the catchments, lifted the dams in August, and Kabini nearly filled. [29] [30] [31] But it didn’t rain enough on the farms below the dams in Mandya and Mysuru. [35] Water also has to be shared with Tamil Nadu under the Cauvery orders. [31]

How far can the city go? Officials have said water can be drawn from KRS until the level falls to about 70 ft. [32] But summer drains it quickly: this April the level fell 8 ft in 15 days, and officials talked of rotational supply if the monsoon was late. [34] We have not found an official outlook for summer 2027 yet; the levels after the October–November rains will decide it.

ZappySo Mysuru is safe?

Prof. BoltSafer than Koppal, yes. Safe enough to waste? No. The dam is a savings account, and next summer is a long month.

08

What actually helps

The same fix works for both problems: catch rain where it falls. Water stored or sent into the ground doesn’t flood the street, and it is still there in April.

Bengaluru already has the rule. Rainwater harvesting is mandatory for new buildings on 30x40 ft sites and older ones on 60x40 ft or larger, with a penalty of 50%, then 100%, of the water bill. [36] Yet BWSSB data cited by Deccan Herald showed only about 1.8 lakh of 19 lakh eligible properties had set it up. [37]

At home

Clean the roof and sump before the next rain, check the overflow pipe, and lead roof water to a sump or a recharge pit.

In the apartment

Ask the association whether the rainwater system actually works. Many exist on paper only.

In the street

Report blocked drains before a heavy-rain alert, not during it.

This summer

Fix leaks now, keep a few days of storage, and follow the city’s supply schedule if rotation starts.

For the next few weeks, the India Meteorological Department expects below-normal rain over most of Karnataka in November. [38] So the water we have now is roughly the water we take into summer.

ZappySo the lesson of 1876 is… fix the sump?

Prof. BoltHa! Partly. In 1876 the tanks went dry and there was no plan B. Today we have dams, rules and forecasts. The plan only works if we use it.

A drought year and a flood day are the same problem: rain arriving when we are not ready for it.
