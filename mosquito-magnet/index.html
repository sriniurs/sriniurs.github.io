Why Do Mosquitoes Bite You and Not the Person Next to You?

Every family has one: the person who comes home from an evening walk with eleven bites while everyone else has none. It is not sweet blood. Scientists have worked out what the mosquito is following, and most of it is coming from your skin.

ABOUT 7 MINUTES

01

One sofa, two people, eleven bites

Two people sit on the same sofa on the same veranda. One goes to bed scratching. The other wakes up without a mark.

It is not your imagination. In a long study at Rockefeller University in New York, the most attractive volunteer drew mosquitoes about 100 times more strongly than the least attractive one. [1] [2]

First, a correction to the villain. Only the female mosquito bites. She needs the protein in blood to make her eggs. For everyday energy, males and females both drink plant nectar and juices, like tiny bees. [3]

So the real question is not “why do mosquitoes like me?” It is: how does one hungry mother-to-be, flying in the dark with poor eyesight, pick one ankle out of a roomful?

ZappyA hundred times? So somewhere there is a person the mosquitoes just ignore.

Prof. BoltYes. And they are usually sitting right next to you, looking smug.

02

Step one: your breath gives you away

Every time you breathe out, you send a puff of carbon dioxide (CO₂) downwind. Humans cannot smell it. Mosquitoes can, with special sensors on two short feelers beside the mouth, and they pick up a breath plume from tens of metres away. [4]

Breath alone only says “something alive is upwind.” What it does next is clever. In a 2022 experiment at the University of Washington, mosquitoes ignored coloured dots in a wind tunnel. After a whiff of CO₂, they flew straight at dots that were red, orange, black or cyan, and still ignored green, blue, purple and white. [5]

Here is the surprise: human skin of every shade, dark or fair, gives off a strong red-orange signal to a mosquito’s eye. Your breath switches her vision on, and your skin is what she sees. [5]

Breath also explains some of the unfairness. A bigger body breathes out more. In a study in The Gambia, pregnant women drew twice as many malaria mosquitoes as women who were not pregnant, and those late in pregnancy breathed out about 21% more air. [6]

03

Step two: the smell nobody else notices

Fresh sweat has almost no smell. Your body odour is made by the bacteria living on your skin, which feed on sebum, the oily film our skin makes, and turn it into smelly chemicals. [7]

To find out which chemicals matter, the Rockefeller team asked 64 volunteers to wear nylon stockings on their forearms for six hours. Pieces of stocking were then put in front of hungry mosquitoes, two at a time, like a tournament. [1]

One volunteer, known only as Subject 33, won almost everything: four times more attractive than the runner-up. The winners all had much higher levels of carboxylic acids on their skin. These are fatty acids whose smell has been compared to cheese or dirty socks. [2] And the effect did not wear off. People who were magnets stayed magnets when tested again over months and years. [7]

Which bacteria you carry seems to matter too. A Dutch study collected skin smell from people’s feet and found that people with more bacteria, but fewer kinds of bacteria, were more attractive to malaria mosquitoes. [8]

ZappySo the mosquito is choosing me by my socks?

Prof. BoltBy what lives on you. Your socks are only the evidence.

04

Step three: the last few centimetres are heat

Close up, a mosquito can feel the warm air rising off your skin, but only within about 10 centimetres. [9]

In 2024, scientists in California showed she has one more sense for the middle distance. Mosquitoes detect infrared, the invisible heat that radiates from warm skin, using sensors at the tip of each antenna. On its own, infrared did little. Added to breath and skin smell, it doubled how hard the mosquitoes searched. [9]

One useful detail from the same study: loose clothing let through less infrared than tight clothing. [9]

How a mosquito finds you, from far to near
Breath (CO₂) · tens of metres→Sight · red-orange skin→Smell · skin acids & bacteria→Heat · the final approach→Bite

Each cue on its own is weak; together they are very hard to fool. Sources: [4] [5] [7] [9]

05

Sweet blood, O group and that one beer

Every family has a theory. Here is how the popular ones stand up:

The theoryWhat a study foundHow much to trust it
“I have sweet blood”The Rockefeller scientists say most such theories, including blood sugar, have little credible data behind them. [7]No good evidence
“Mosquitoes love O group”A 2004 Japanese test with 64 landing trials found O group drew more Asian tiger mosquitoes, but the difference was clear only against group A. [10]Weak: one small study, one species
“Drinking makes it worse”In Burkina Faso, men who drank a litre of local beer became more attractive to malaria mosquitoes; water had no effect. It was not extra breath or body heat. [11]Real in one study; the cause is unknown
“It runs in the family”Identical twins were much more alike in attractiveness than non-identical twins. [12]Promising pilot study

The twin study, from London in 2015, estimated that about 62% of the difference between people in how attractive they were came from their genes, most likely because genes shape body odour. [12]

Then the Rockefeller team tried to break the system. They bred mosquitoes with key smell sensors knocked out. Those mosquitoes still preferred the magnets. Her nose has backups for its backups. [7]

One more twist. The itchy bump is not the bite itself; it is your immune system reacting to the mosquito’s saliva. [13] So two people bitten equally can look very different the next morning. Some of the “never bitten” may simply be bitten but not bothered. That is our observation, not a study result.

06

The mosquito that keeps office hours

In India, the main carrier of dengue is Aedes aegypti, a small black mosquito with white stripes on its legs. [14] Unlike the night-time mosquito that whines in your ear, the dengue mosquito is active during the day. [15] It breeds in clean, still water close to people: a flower-pot saucer, a coconut shell, a water drum, a tyre on the terrace. That is why health officials ask people to cover, empty and clean stored water every week. [15]

Karnataka knows what happens when it is not. By September 2024 the state had recorded more than 25,000 dengue cases and 12 deaths, and the government declared it an epidemic. Bengaluru had over 11,000 cases; Mysuru, Mandya and Hassan also had many. [16]

Scientists are now fighting mosquitoes with mosquitoes. At the ICMR’s Vector Control Research Centre in Puducherry, researchers working with Monash University in Australia have bred a local “Puducherry strain” of Aedes aegypti carrying Wolbachia, a common bacterium that stops dengue virus from multiplying inside the insect. [14] [17] Releasing them into neighbourhoods still needs government approvals. [17] And since July 2026, India has also approved its first dengue vaccine, for people aged 4 to 60. [18]

ZappySo the dangerous one bites at lunchtime, not at night?

Prof. BoltDaytime, and often indoors. She is the only one in the house who keeps office hours.

07

What actually keeps her off

If the mosquito follows several signals at once, the only reliable fix is to block the last step: the landing. The best-known test of repellents, published in a leading medical journal in 2002, put volunteers’ arms into cages of hungry mosquitoes and timed how long each product gave complete protection. [19]

Short bars have a minimum width so their labels fit. Wristbands gave no protection. Products sold in India today differ; picaridin, now widely used, was not in this test. Source: [19]

Today, health agencies list DEET, picaridin, IR3535, oil of lemon eucalyptus and PMD as repellents that work on skin. If you use sunscreen too, put sunscreen on first and repellent second. [20]

What does not work? The little electronic gadgets that claim to scare mosquitoes away with a high-pitched buzz. A Cochrane review, which pools the best available studies, looked at ten field trials. None found any difference in how many mosquitoes landed on people with or without the device. [21]

And the colour study suggests one free trick. Since mosquitoes primed by breath head for red, orange and black, a light-coloured, loose shirt is probably a better choice for an evening walk than a dark, tight one. That last step is our inference from the studies, not something they tested on people. [5] [9]

08

Not sweeter, just louder

To us, a crowded veranda is a group of friends. To a mosquito it is a set of signals: plumes of breath, patches of red-orange skin, clouds of smell and glowing heat. Each person sends out a slightly different mix, and one of them is always turned up louder.

That person is not sweeter. They breathe a little more, carry a little more of the wrong skin acids, host a slightly different crowd of bacteria and, if the twins are right, partly inherited all of it. The mosquito is not picky about taste. She is simply following the strongest signal in the room.

Which also means the person next to you is not lucky. They are just quieter.

ZappySo I’m not sweet. I’m loud.

Prof. BoltChemically loud. How loud you are at dinner is a separate study.
