Will a Robot Take My Job? 5 Robot Myths, Checked

Zappy watched one robot video on Instagram and now thinks his job is gone in five years. Prof. Bolt says: before you panic, count the robots. The real numbers are far more surprising than the videos.

PART 1: ROBOTS 101What "physical AI" actually means

EP 1

The video that scared Zappy

ZappyI saw a robot doing kung fu and folding towels! Robots will take all our jobs in five years!

Prof. BoltVideos are edited. Factories are not. Let's first understand what this new robot actually is.

Chatbot vs robot

ChatGPT-type AI lives inside a screen. It works with words and pictures.

Physical AI is AI with a body. It has to see a real room, judge a real object and move real hands without breaking anything.

A robot in three parts

👁️ Eyes: cameras and sensors→🧠 Brain: AI that decides→🦾 Hands: motors that move

The hands are the hard part. That's where most robots still fail.

EP 2

It can win at chess. It can't fold a saree.

The strange rule of robots

In 1988, robot scientist Hans Moravec noticed something odd: computers could match adults on intelligence tests and board games, but couldn't match the skills of a one-year-old.

It's now called Moravec's paradox. What is hard for us is easy for machines, and what is easy for us is hard for them.

WAIT, REALLY?

A computer beat the world chess champion in 1997. Almost 30 years later, folding a soft, crumpled towel is still a big test for robots.

Cloth changes shape at every touch. An egg cracks if you squeeze 10% too hard. Your hands solve this without thinking.

ZappySo my Amma folding a saree in five seconds is "advanced technology"?

Prof. BoltMillions of years of it. Keep that in mind for every myth that follows.

PART 2: 5 MYTHS VS FACTSWhat the numbers actually say

EP 3

Myth 1: "Indian factories are already full of robots"

Every car plant and factory in India is filling up with robots. The humans are already on their way out.

India installed almost 10,500 new factory robots in 2025, up 15%, and is now 6th in the world. Sounds big. China installed 354,000 in the same year, about 34 times more.

VERDICT: MYTH

Korea has roughly 50 times more robots per factory worker than India. India's robots are growing fast, but from a very small base, and mostly in car plants.

EP 4

Myth 2: "Robots teach themselves"

Robots just download a brain and learn everything on their own. No humans needed.

Robots learn by watching human hands, thousands of hours of them. Much of that video is being shot in India.

In Tamil Nadu, workers strap cameras to their heads and film themselves folding towels, making coffee and chopping vegetables. One Chennai worker told AFP she earns about ₹250 an hour doing this for a data company.

PLOT TWIST

In a Karur textile factory, NPR found a worker filming her own job for robot training. Nobody had told her what the footage was for.

VERDICT: MYTH

Today's robots are students, and Indian hands are among their teachers. India now has workers training the very machines that might one day do their jobs.

EP 5

Myth 3: "A robot maid is science fiction"

Robot servants at home? That's only in films. Nobody can actually buy one.

You can pre-order one today. The NEO home robot from 1X costs $20,000 (about ₹19 lakh) or $499 a month (about ₹48,000). China's Unitree sells a small humanoid from $4,900 (about ₹4.7 lakh).

THE CATCH

For harder chores, NEO uses "Expert Mode": a company employee wearing a VR headset remotely sees through its cameras and drives the robot inside your home. In one widely seen newspaper test, the chores shown were done by remote control.

VERDICT: HALF MYTH

The robot is real and on sale. A robot that cleans your whole house on its own is not here yet. Today, there is often a human behind the robot.

Rupee figures: our arithmetic at about ₹96 a dollar (Sept 2026), before import duties.

EP 6

Myth 4: "Machines always kill jobs"

When the ATM came, bank tellers vanished. Robots will do the same to us.

In the US, the opposite happened. Bank tellers grew from about 2.5 lakh in 1970 to about 5 lakh, even as ATMs spread everywhere.

ATM does the cash→Each branch needs ~⅓ fewer tellers→Branches get cheaper→~40% more branches

The teller's job changed: less cash counting, more loans and customer advice.

VERDICT: HALF MYTH

Machines take over tasks more often than whole jobs. But it isn't always happy: tractors really did shrink farm jobs. Some jobs change, some vanish, new ones appear.

Economist James Bessen's research, as described by MIT's David Autor.

EP 7

Myth 5: "India only buys robots from abroad"

All the robots are made in China, Japan or America. India just imports them.

Indian companies are building robot brains and bodies. Noida's Addverb unveiled Elixis-W, a made-in-India wheeled humanoid for warehouses, in 2026, and is targeting 3,000 humanoids in factories by 2030.

And many of the others are from Bengaluru (more in EP 11).

VERDICT: MYTH

India is still small in robots, but it's no longer just a buyer. It's a builder, and a big supplier of the human data robots learn from.

How we got India's number

India had 52,570 factory robots at work in 2024 (IFR). Registered factories employed about 2.1 crore people in 2024-25 (Annual Survey of Industries).

52,570 ÷ 2.1 crore × 10,000 = about 25.

EVEN LOWER

Add the roughly 3.8 crore people in small, unregistered workshops and it falls to about 9.

ZappyBut India's installations are growing fast, right?

Prof. BoltVery fast: about 27% a year from 2020 to 2025. Car plants buy the most. Fast growth from a small base still leaves a small number.

EP 9

Why robots arrive slowly in India

Reason 1: people cost less than robots

A ₹19 lakh robot has to replace a lot of wages before it pays off. In India, that takes far longer than in Korea or Germany, where workers are scarce and expensive.

Reason 2: power and money

Robots need steady electricity and big upfront investment. Economists told NPR that AI robots are likely to stay more expensive than human workers in India for some time.

Reason 3: most work is informal

About 3 in 4 non-farm workers are in informal businesses (PLFS 2025): small shops, workshops, tailoring units. These rarely buy robots.

"Training workers, retraining workers, absorbing them into new occupations... Do we have the policy agility to do that? I doubt it."
Amit Basole, Azim Premji University, to NPR (Oct 2026)

EP 11

Karnataka builds the brains

Where India's factory jobs are

Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh and Haryana have about 56% of registered factory jobs (ASI 2024-25). Karnataka isn't in the top five.

But Bengaluru is where many Indian robots get their brains.

ZappySo the robot's brain may be from Bengaluru and its training video from Karur?

Prof. BoltExactly. India's first robot jobs are about building and teaching robots, not being replaced by them.

EP 12

Prof. Bolt's verdict (opinion)

RELAX

Not in five years, not for most of us. India has very few robots, people cost less than machines, and most work happens in small, messy places robots hate.

BUT WATCH

Repetitive factory and warehouse tasks will change first, especially in car plants. And Indian workers are already filming their own jobs to teach robots.

MY TAKE

Robots replace tasks; people who learn replace people who don't. The worker who can run, fix or train the robot will be the last one let go.

EP 13

So what should Zappy do?

Look at your own job

  • List your daily tasks. Which are the same every time?
  • Those are what a machine will take first. Plan for them.
  • The messy, people-facing parts are your safest skills. Grow them.

Learn next to the machine

  • Operating, maintaining and repairing robots: these jobs grow as robots spread.
  • Short ITI and polytechnic courses in automation and electricals are cheap and practical.
  • Asked to wear a camera at work? Ask what the video is for, and who is paying.

ZappySo I don't fight the robot. I learn to be its boss?

Prof. BoltOr its teacher, its mechanic or its trainer. Someone has to fold the saree first.
