EP 1

The cap clock is ticking

Deadline

15 Nov 2026

NPPA's knee implant ceiling prices run "till 15 November 2026 or until further orders". That's the one-year extension in notification S.O. 5190(E) of Nov 2025 (Medical Dialogues, 17 Nov 2025).

Still pending

A separate, possibly higher price category for cementless knees has been on hold since NPPA's committee meeting of 11 Feb 2026. Experts and companies were to be heard "at the next meeting".

We found no public decision as of 2 Nov.

ZappySir, a patient booked a TKA for 20 November. Will the implant cost change?

Prof. BoltNobody knows yet. That's why it's this week's editorial.

→ For your practice: for surgeries planned after 15 Nov, don't promise a fixed implant price until NPPA's next notification is out. Keep MRP-labelled invoices with every case file.

EP 4

Barcelona surgeon, Bengaluru patient

Late October

At the EACTS meeting in Barcelona, Dr Sudhir Srivastava used the Indian-made SSi Mantra robot to repair a heart defect (ASD) by telesurgery. The patient was at Sri Madhusudan Sai Institute, Bengaluru.

~12,000 km

The distance between surgeon and patient. As of 23 Oct, SSi had done 205 telesurgeries. Four more were done live at the first SRS India conference, Delhi (company release, 26 Oct).

ZappyRemote knee replacements next?

Prof. BoltNot yet. SSi is soft-tissue only. But remote mentoring for a district surgeon's first robotic knees is easy to imagine.

→ For your practice: telesurgery depends on network delay, a backup team and legal clarity, not just the robot. If a hospital invites you to a tele-proctored list, ask who carries the medico-legal liability first.

EP 5

That swollen knee probably isn't a tumour

Indian J Pathol Oncol, Sep 2026

Arundhathi Institute, Hyderabad. 10 years of knee and shoulder synovial biopsies: 6,204 (after excluding common infective and inflammatory cases).

~1.6%

were tumours or tumour-like lesions, and only 4 were malignant: synovial sarcoma, chondrosarcoma, malignant TGCT and a lung metastasis.

Commonest finds

  • Synovial chondromatosis: 50 cases
  • TGCT (old name PVNS): 25 cases
  • Haemangioma 7, lipoma 4

Retrospective, single centre (Medical Dialogues, 2 Nov).

→ For your practice: rare, but not zero. The authors' advice: send every excised synovial mass for histopathology, and think beyond TB and RA when an effusion or locking is unexplained.

Prof. BoltThe robot has become a marketing tool. The good question is how many cases a month it needs to pay for itself.

EP 7

What the cap actually did

Knee system (2017)Avg MRP beforeCeiling priceCut
Cobalt-chromium (most used)₹1,58,324₹54,72065%
Titanium / oxidised zirconium₹2,49,251₹76,60069%
High-flex₹1,81,728₹56,49069%
Revision₹2,76,869₹1,13,95059%

PIB, 16 Aug 2017 (S.O. 2668(E)), plus GST. Government estimate: ~₹1,500 crore a year saved, at 1–1.5 lakh knee procedures a year then.

How the money flowed

Factory / import price→Distributor→Hospital→Patient pays MRP

NPPA said the big money was in trade margins along that chain, which it called "unethical profiteering".

Prof. BoltBefore 2017, a ₹1.5 lakh knee often had far less than that in metal inside. The cap cut out the middlemen's share, not just the company's.

→ For your practice: patients still remember the ₹54,720 headline. Itemise your package so they can see the implant at or below MRP. It builds trust.

EP 8

Nine years of "temporary"

Aug 2017: cap notified→Renewed yearly→Sep 2025: 2-month extension→Nov 2025: 1-year extension→15 Nov 2026: ?

What industry wants (Nov 2025)

  • A 10% price hike, which manufacturers say the rules allow
  • Adjustment for exchange rates, freight, wages and inflation
  • A separate, freer category for "innovative" implants

OC Academy summary of NPPA's 2025 review.

The cementless twist

Companies want cementless knees priced separately. In Feb 2026, NPPA's committee deferred the decision. Meril withdrew its own request, saying its 3D-printed uncemented knee already sells under the cap.

→ For your practice: if cementless knees get a higher price band, expect more marketing of cementless implants for younger patients. Decide your indications on evidence before the sales reps arrive.

EP 9

What the stent cap taught us

Feb 2017, cardiology

₹29,600

Ceiling for drug-eluting stents. Six months before the knee cap.

What companies did

  • Abbott, Medtronic and Boston Scientific asked to withdraw premium stents (Apr 2017).
  • NPPA refused and ordered 6 months' continued supply.
  • Abbott later stopped Absorb worldwide over low sales (Medical Dialogues).

What hospitals might do (Scroll, Aug 2017)

Malini Aisola (All India Drug Action Network): the cap "will be nullified if hospitals further hike their procedure costs".

Dr Shailendra Patil (Wockhardt, Navi Mumbai): price limits mean people "will not be encouraged to innovate".

→ For your practice: in 2017 the newest premium products were the first to be threatened with withdrawal, while basic ones stayed. If supply gets tight after 15 Nov, keep a back-up implant system you're comfortable with.

EP 10

Three ways 15 November can go

ScenarioPatientsHospitals & surgeonsIndustry
A. Extend as isImplant cost stays the sameNo change; package rates holdSqueezed margins; slower premium launches
B. Revise (small hike + cementless/innovation band)A few thousand rupees more for some kneesNeed to re-quote packages; more implant choiceSome relief; reason to launch new designs
C. Lapse (back to trade-margin rules)Risk of pre-2017-style MRPsPatients and media will scrutinise pricingPricing freedom, subject to margin rules

The middle-path idea

Device makers' body AiMeD (8 Oct 2026, on consumables) suggests capping trade markups at 2–6 times the purchase price, with one national design instead of state-by-state caps. Coordinator Rajiv Nath: "After Maharashtra and Karnataka, the spotlight is now on UP."

Karnataka angle

Karnataka is one of the states AiMeD named in the trade-margin debate. Government schemes (PM-JAY, Arogya Karnataka) pay fixed package rates, so any rise in implant prices squeezes hospitals in those schemes first.

EP 11

Prof. Bolt's take OPINION

Prof. BoltA complete lapse is unlikely. No government wants headlines saying "knee prices double". My bet is another extension, maybe with a small inflation increase, and a separate cementless band within a year. The real fight is over trade margins, not factory prices. Surgeons should be visibly on the patient's side of that fight.

ZappySo we wait for the notification?

Prof. BoltWe wait. But we prepare, too.

What should we do?

  • 1. Check NPPA's website on and after 15 Nov. Don't quote implant prices for later surgeries until it's out.
  • 2. Show the implant as a separate line in packages, at or below MRP, and keep the invoices.
  • 3. Through KOA or IOA, send real Indian data (cementless outcomes, revision rates, OT costs) to the NPPA consultation. If we don't speak, only companies will.
