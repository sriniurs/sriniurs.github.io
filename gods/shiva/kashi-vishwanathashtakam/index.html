FOREWORD

Kāśī, Vārāṇasī, Banaras: one of the oldest living cities in the world, on the curve of the Gaṅgā, with the golden spire of the Viśvanātha temple at its heart. For thousands of years people have come here to pray, to study, and at the end of their lives, to be set free.

The Kāśī Viśvanāthāṣṭakam sings of the city's Lord. Each verse paints Him differently (the Gaṅgā in His hair, the moon on His crown, a lion to the elephants of sin, light and sound and the Self itself) and each ends with the same gentle instruction to the mind: vārāṇasī-purapatiṃ bhaja viśvanātham, "worship Viśvanātha, Lord of Vārāṇasī". Its eighth verse tells us where to find Him: in the lotus of our own heart.

May every reader find their own Kāśī there.

THE STORY · THE KING WHO SENT ŚIVA AWAY

King Divodāsa, from the Kāśī Khaṇḍa of the Skanda Purāṇa
The Purāṇic story in 5 frames
A strange condition. When drought ruined the earth, Brahmā asked the virtuous King Divodāsa to rule. He agreed on one condition: the gods must leave Kāśī, so he alone would rule there. Even Śiva left his beloved city for Mount Mandara.
A perfect king. Divodāsa ruled so justly that no fault could be found. Śiva, longing for home, sent messengers to find a flaw and end his rule.
No one came back. The yoginīs came, then the Sun, then Brahmā, who performed ten horse sacrifices at a ghat still called Daśāśvamedha. Then Gaṇeśa. Each fell in love with Kāśī and stayed.
The king lets go. At last Viṣṇu came as a wise teacher and gently turned the king's mind to detachment. Divodāsa installed a liṅga, gave up his kingdom, and rose to Śiva's world.
Śiva returns. Śiva came home to Kāśī, never to leave again. That is why the city is called Avimukta, "never abandoned".

The king

Divodāsa, too good to fault.

The ghat

Daśāśvamedha, Brahmā's ten sacrifices.

The city

Avimukta, never abandoned.

ZAPPY: Everyone who came to Kāśī stayed?

PROF. BOLT: That's the Purāṇa's point. Even gods can't leave once they've felt Kāśī. And the one who loved it most came back for good.

Kāśī is the city Śiva could not stay away from.

ABOUT THE VIŚVANĀTHĀṢṬAKAM

A hymn to the Lord of the oldest city

✍️ The hymn

Eight verses and a phalaśruti, traditionally attributed to Vyāsa (some attribute it to Ādi Śaṅkara), in the flowing vasantatilakā metre.

🛕 The temple

Kāśī Viśvanātha, one of the twelve jyotirliṅgas, on the western bank of the Gaṅgā in Vārāṇasī.

The refrain. Every verse ends vārāṇasī-purapatiṃ bhaja viśvanātham, "worship Viśvanātha, Lord of the city of Vārāṇasī": a gentle command to one's own mind.

The shape. Verses 1–4 paint Śiva's form; verse 5 shows His power over sin, demons and death; verses 6–7 His nature, the Self and love without clinging; verse 8 tells us how to find Him: in the heart.

The temple's story. Destroyed and rebuilt several times, the present temple was built by Ahilyābāī Holkar of Indore in 1780; Mahārājā Ranjit Singh gave gold for its spires; the Kāśī Viśvanātha corridor opened in 2021 joins the temple to the Gaṅgā.

VERSE 1 · GOING DEEPER

GAṄGĀ IN HIS HAIR, GAURĪ AT HIS SIDE

The refrain. Every verse ends vārāṇasī-purapatiṃ bhaja viśvanātham: "worship Viśvanātha, Lord of the city of Vārāṇasī". Bhaja is a command to one's own mind: worship! The hymn is a gentle self-instruction, repeated eight times.

Lord of the universe, Lord of one city. Viśvanātha means "Lord of the whole universe", yet the hymn calls Him purapati, Lord of one city. Tradition says Kāśī is Śiva's own city, which he never leaves: avimukta, "never abandoned".

Gaurī and Nārāyaṇa. Gaurī is always at his left side, hinting at Ardhanārīśvara, Śiva who is half Pārvatī. And he is nārāyaṇa-priya, dear to Viṣṇu. Kāśī has great Viṣṇu shrines too, like Bindu Mādhava: one city, one divine family.

💡 Ardhanārīśvara

Śiva as half-woman: Pārvatī forms his left half, the union of Śiva and Śakti.

💡 Avimukta

"Never abandoned": an ancient name for Kāśī, the place Śiva never leaves.

Tell your own mind what to do, kindly and firmly: "worship the Lord". The mind listens to the voice it hears most.

ZAPPY: 'Bhaja viśvanātham': who's telling whom?

PROF. BOLT: The poet is telling his own mind: worship Viśvanātha! Every verse ends with that nudge.

Every verse ends with a nudge to the mind: bhaja viśvanātham, worship the Lord of the universe.

VERSE 2 · GOING DEEPER

BEYOND WORDS, YET WITH HIS BELOVED

Beyond words. Vācām agocara: beyond the reach of speech. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad says of Brahman: "from which words turn back, with the mind, not reaching it". The hymn opens its second verse with that great idea.

Served by the gods. Vāgīśa-viṣṇu-sura-sevita-pāda-pīṭha: his footstool is served by Brahmā (the lord of speech, who can't describe him), by Viṣṇu and by all the gods.

And a householder. Vāmena vigraha-vareṇa kalatravantam: with the lovely left half of his form, he has a wife. The Absolute beyond words is also Pārvatī's husband. Both are true at once.

💡 Taittirīya Upaniṣad

yato vāco nivartante aprāpya manasā saha: "from which words turn back, with the mind, not reaching it" (2.4).

💡 Vāgīśa

"Lord of speech": Brahmā, husband of Sarasvatī, goddess of speech and learning.

Some things can't be said, only lived. Don't let words shrink what is bigger than them.

ZAPPY: Beyond words, but He has a wife?

PROF. BOLT: Both. The Absolute no word can reach, and the loving husband on Kailāsa. The hymn won't let us shrink Him into either one.

Beyond words, yet a householder with Pārvatī: never just one thing.

VERSE 3 · GOING DEEPER

LORD OF ALL BEINGS

Bhūtādhipa. Lord of all beings: the bhūtas, which means both "living beings" and "spirits". Everything that exists answers to Viśvanātha.

Noose and goad. Pāśa and aṅkuśa are the tools for catching and steering an elephant. The mind is often pictured as a wild elephant; the Lord holds the means to tame it.

Two open hands. Abhaya, the raised palm that says "do not fear", and vara, the lowered palm that gives. Every temple image of a deity shows these two gestures; the hymn names them.

💡 Abhaya mudrā

The raised right hand, palm out: "do not fear", on countless temple images.

💡 Aṅkuśa

The elephant goad, also held by Gaṇeśa: guidance for the wild mind.

Discipline and kindness go together. Steer the mind firmly, and don't be afraid.

ZAPPY: A noose and a goad... and blessings?

PROF. BOLT: The noose and goad tame the wild mind, like an elephant. The open hands say: don't be afraid, here is your boon.

Tools to tame the mind, and hands that say don't be afraid.

VERSE 4 · GOING DEEPER

MOON CROWN, FIRE EYE

Cool and fire. The moon on his crown is śītāṃśu, "cool-rayed"; the eye on his forehead is fire. Calm on top, power within: a picture of the mastered mind.

The five arrows. Kāma, god of desire, is pañcabāṇa, "five-arrowed": his flower-arrows (lotus, aśoka, mango blossom, jasmine and blue water-lily) are said to strike the five senses. When Kāma shot at Śiva, the third eye burnt him.

Serpent earrings. Nāgādhipāracita-karṇapūra: earrings made from the king of serpents. What frightens us is just jewellery to Him.

💡 Pañcabāṇa

Kāma's five flower-arrows; his bow is sugarcane, its string a row of bees.

💡 Śītāṃśu

"Cool-rayed": the moon, whose coolness balances the third eye's fire.

Stay cool on top, and keep a fire inside that burns away what shouldn't grow.

ZAPPY: Five arrows?

PROF. BOLT: Kāma's five flower-arrows strike the five senses. The third eye's fire burnt them all.

Cool moon above, burning eye below: a calm that can burn desire away.

VERSE 5 · GOING DEEPER

LION, EAGLE, WILDFIRE

The lion. Pañcānana means "lion", and also "five-faced", Śiva of the five faces (our Pañca-Brahma Mantras article). Sins are pictured as mad elephants (matta-mataṅgaja); no elephant, however mad, stands before a lion.

The eagle. Nāgāntaka, "ender of serpents", is Garuḍa, Viṣṇu's eagle. Demon chiefs are pictured as snakes; Śiva is the eagle that swoops on them.

The wildfire. Dāvānala: a forest fire, sweeping through the dense jungle (aṭavī) of death, grief and old age. Frightening, but forests renew after fire.

💡 Pañcānana

Means both "lion" and "five-faced": a favourite pun of Śaiva poets.

💡 Garuḍa

Viṣṇu's eagle-mount, eternal enemy of serpents: the image is borrowed with affection.

No problem is too big for grace. The mad elephant always meets its lion.

ZAPPY: Three animals in one verse?

PROF. BOLT: Three pictures: sins are mad elephants, He's the lion; demons are snakes, He's the eagle; death and grief are a jungle, He's the wildfire.

Lion, eagle, wildfire: whatever overwhelms us, He overwhelms.

VERSE 6 · GOING DEEPER

LIGHT, SOUND AND THE SELF

The Vedānta verse. Here the hymn goes deepest: advitīya, one without a second; ānandakanda, the root of bliss; ātmarūpa, the very form of the Self. The Lord of Kāśī is the Self of all.

Sound. Nādātmaka, "whose self is sound". Tradition says that when Naṭarāja ended his dance, his ḍamaru sounded fourteen times, giving the fourteen Māheśvara Sūtras on which Pāṇini's Sanskrit grammar rests. Om itself is Śiva as sound.

Both at once. Saguṇa-nirguṇa, with and without qualities; sakala-niṣkala, with and without parts. The liṅga is the classic example: a form that points to the formless.

💡 Māheśvara Sūtras

The fourteen sound-formulas at the start of Pāṇini's grammar, said to come from Śiva's ḍamaru.

💡 Sakala · Niṣkala

In Śaiva thought, the liṅga is both: a form that is formless.

Don't argue form versus formless. Use the form to reach the formless.

ZAPPY: With qualities and without? Both again?

PROF. BOLT: The form we worship and the formless it points to. The same Śiva, seen from two angles.

Form and formless, sound and silence: He is the Self itself.

VERSE 7 · GOING DEEPER

FREE OF PASSION, FULL OF LOVE

Rāga and anurāga. Rāga is grasping passion; anurāga is love. Śiva has none of the first and all of the second. Love without clinging is the ideal of every spiritual path.

Ascetic and householder. Vairāgya-śānti-nilaya, the home of detachment and peace, and in the same line girijā-sahāya, Pārvatī's companion. The supreme yogi is also the devoted husband.

Beautiful with poison. Garalābhirāma: made beautiful by poison. The hālāhala he drank to save the world left his throat blue; the poet calls it his loveliest ornament.

💡 Vairāgya

Detachment: not coldness, but freedom from needing things to be a certain way.

💡 Girijā

"Born of the mountain": Pārvatī, daughter of Himavān.

Love people fully, and hold them lightly.

ZAPPY: Without passion, but full of love?

PROF. BOLT: Love without clinging. He cares for everyone and grasps at nothing.

Detached, yet loving: care without clinging.

VERSE 8 · GOING DEEPER

HOW TO FIND HIM: IN THE HEART

A practical verse. After seven verses of praise comes a how-to: āśāṃ vihāya (give up craving), parasya nindāṃ parihṛtya (avoid speaking ill of others), pāpe ratiṃ sunivārya (turn the mind from its taste for wrong), manaḥ samādhau (settle the mind in meditation).

Gossip. It's striking that "don't speak ill of others" is one of only four steps. The poet clearly thought gossip was one of the biggest obstacles to finding God.

The inner Kāśī. Hṛt-kamala-madhya-gata: the Lord sits in the centre of the heart-lotus. The Chāndogya Upaniṣad speaks of a small lotus-dwelling in the heart that holds the whole universe. Kāśī is not only a city on the Gaṅgā; it's in every heart.

💡 Antar-Kāśī

Yogic texts speak of an inner Kāśī, located in the heart or between the eyebrows.

💡 Chāndogya 8.1

"In this city of Brahman (the body) is a small lotus-dwelling; within it, a small space": the whole universe is there.

Before looking for God far away, tidy the heart: less craving, less gossip. He's already there.

ZAPPY: So this verse is instructions?

PROF. BOLT: Four steps: drop craving, stop gossip, turn from wrong, sit still. Then find Viśvanātha in your own heart.

Drop craving, stop gossip, turn from wrong, sit still: Kāśī is in the heart.

PHALAŚRUTI · GOING DEEPER

THE FRUIT OF CHANTING

Four gifts and one goal. Vidyā (learning), śrī (prosperity), vipula-saukhya (great happiness), ananta-kīrti (endless fame): a good life. Then mokṣa, liberation, when the body falls away.

The Kāśī promise. Tradition holds that anyone who dies in Kāśī is liberated, because Śiva himself whispers the tāraka, the "saving" mantra, into the ear of the dying. Many have travelled there to spend their last days.

Carried anywhere. The hymn's promise is the same, without the journey: chant in praise of the Lord of Kāśī, and Kāśī's gift comes to you.

💡 Maṇikarṇikā

Kāśī's most sacred cremation ghat, where the fires are said never to go out.

💡 The temple today

The present Viśvanātha temple was built by Ahilyābāī Holkar (1780); Mahārājā Ranjit Singh gave the gold for its spire.

Live well: learn, prosper, enjoy. And keep freedom as the last goal.

ZAPPY: Mokṣa when the body falls: like dying in Kāśī?

PROF. BOLT: That's the Kāśī promise. The hymn carries it to wherever you chant.

Learning, wealth, joy, fame, and at the end, mokṣa.

THE WHOLE HYMN ON ONE PAGE

Worship Viśvanātha, Lord of Vārāṇasī
Verses 1–4: His form. Gaṅgā in His hair, Gaurī at His side; beyond words, yet a husband; noose, goad, trident, "do not fear"; moon crown, fire eye, serpent earrings.
Verse 5: His power. A lion to sin, an eagle to demons, a wildfire to death, grief and old age.
Verses 6–7: His nature. Light, sound and the Self, with and beyond form; free of passion, full of love.
Verse 8: the way. Drop craving, stop gossip, turn from wrong, sit still, and find Him in the heart.

🌊 Gaṅgā

🦁 Lion

🤐 No gossip

Guard your speech.

🪷 Heart

Kāśī is within.
