CATARACT

Why Does Everything Look Cloudy Even with My Glasses?

Why a cloudy lens can blur vision, what an eye examination checks and when surgery can help.

THE CLOUDY LENSGLARE AND NIGHT DRIVINGWHEN TO GET HELP

About 8 minutes to read · Patient education

01

The new glasses that still looked foggy

Shanta cleans her glasses twice before sitting down to read the newspaper. The letters are still hazy. That evening, on the way home, oncoming headlights seem to spread across the windscreen. Her glasses helped when she first got them, and she wonders whether she simply needs a stronger pair.

A cataract is clouding of the natural lens inside the eye. Glasses can adjust how light focuses. If the lens inside the eye has become cloudy, some light is scattered or blocked before it reaches the retina. That can leave the picture hazy or washed out even when the glasses are right. A new prescription can sometimes help in the early stages. [1][2][3]

Shanta’s eye clinician asks which tasks have changed. She mentions reading, recognising faces in dim rooms and seeing the road beside bright headlights. Those details help explain what the eye chart alone might miss. An examination can show whether a cataract accounts for what she’s noticing. [3][4][5]

02

What does the lens inside the eye do?

The lens sits behind the coloured part of the eye, called the iris. It helps focus light on the retina, the light-sensitive layer at the back. The retina sends visual signals to the brain. For a clear picture, light needs both a path through the eye and good focus when it arrives. [1][6]

As the lens changes with age, proteins in it can clump together and create areas of clouding. Imagine a camera lens that is partly misted inside. Adjusting the focus can help some parts of the picture, but light passing through the cloudy area can still scatter. Where the clouding sits and how dense it is can affect the way you see. [1][3][7]

Cataracts often develop gradually and can affect the two eyes differently. Shanta notices that one eye seems duller when she covers the other. The clinician can test each eye separately to measure that difference and look for the cause. [1][4][7]

03

Why are headlights harder to handle at night?

Scattered light from a bright source can spread across the picture you’re trying to see. A headlight may have a halo around it. A pedestrian or the edge of a lane can be harder to distinguish beside that glare. Some people also notice that colours look faded or that they need more light for reading. [1][2][3]

An eye chart measures how clearly you can recognise letters under test conditions. It may give an incomplete picture of how you do with low contrast, glare or dim light. Tell the clinician which situations are difficult. Shanta describes both the headlines that look washed out and the road markings she struggles to see after dark. [4][5][7]

If you can’t see clearly enough to drive safely, stop driving and discuss it with your eye clinician. While waiting for assessment or treatment, brighter task lighting or an updated prescription can help some activities. The adjustments depend on which problems you’re having. [1][4][8]

04

Why is it happening now?

Ageing is a common reason for cataracts. Diabetes, smoking, prolonged ultraviolet exposure, an eye injury and some steroid medicines can increase the chance of developing one. Mention your medical conditions and medicines at the visit. If a prescribed medicine may contribute, talk to the prescriber before making any changes. [1][7][9]

Cloudy vision has other possible causes. Conditions affecting the cornea, retina or optic nerve can also change what you see. Some can occur alongside a cataract. That matters because removing the cloudy lens can improve the light reaching the retina, while other eye disease may still limit the final result. [4][5][7]

Shanta asks why her clinician wants to look at the back of her eye when the clouding is in the lens. The examination helps check whether there’s another reason for the blur and gives a better idea of what treatment could achieve. [4][5]

THE DEEP DIVEWhat the eye examination shows and how surgery can help
05

How does an eye examination confirm a cataract?

The clinician checks how well each eye sees and whether adjusting the glasses prescription helps. A microscope with a narrow beam of light, called a slit lamp, lets the clinician see the lens and the clouding. Drops may be used to widen the pupil so the examiner can look at the lens and retina more clearly. [1][5][7][10]

The drops can temporarily make bright light uncomfortable and close-up tasks blurry. If your appointment includes dilation, ask the clinic whether you should arrange a lift home. Shanta brings her current glasses and tells the examiner which prescription she uses for reading. [5][10]

If surgery is being planned, the team usually measures the eye’s length and the curvature of the cornea. Those measurements help choose the power of the artificial lens. They’re part of planning how the eye will focus afterwards. Tell the team if you’ve had previous eye surgery, including laser treatment to correct your prescription. [4][5]

The decision about surgery takes account of the examination, the likely benefit, the risks and what you want to do more comfortably. It doesn’t have to turn on one line of an eye chart. Shanta’s clinician asks whether the glare has changed where and when she feels comfortable going out. [4][5]

06

When is cataract surgery worth considering?

If the cataract is mild and you can do the things that matter to you, it may be reasonable to monitor it and make practical adjustments. When vision starts limiting reading, work, safe mobility or driving, talk about whether the expected benefits of surgery outweigh the risks for you. Ask how other eye conditions might affect the result. [1][4][5]

During surgery, the cloudy natural lens is removed and usually replaced with a clear artificial lens. Many people see better afterwards. In one randomised study of older women, earlier first-eye surgery improved visual function and reduced the overall rate of falls over a year. The study did not show a clear reduction in the chance of a first fall. The result describes the group studied. [1][11][12]

Ask what improvement to expect for the activity that concerns you most. Shanta asks whether surgery is likely to help with glare and whether she will still need glasses to read or see at a distance. [4][11]

She also asks what happens if she decides to wait. A useful plan includes how vision will be reviewed and which changes should bring the appointment forward. If both eyes have cataracts, the clinician can discuss the likely benefit and timing for each eye. A second operation is a separate decision based on how that eye affects daily life. [4][5]

07

What happens after the cloudy lens is replaced?

An artificial lens has a chosen focus. A monofocal lens is usually set for one distance, so you may still need glasses for other distances. Before surgery, tell the team about the work you do close up and what you want to see clearly across a room or outdoors. Ask what choices are suitable for your eyes. [4][5][11]

After surgery, you’ll receive instructions about eye drops, follow-up and when to resume everyday activities. Recovery differs from person to person. Ask whom to call if vision worsens or pain increases. If vision becomes hazy again months or years later, the thin capsule behind the implant can sometimes be the reason. An eye clinician can check for this and discuss laser treatment if appropriate. [11][13][14]

Shanta writes down the aftercare number before she leaves the clinic. She also asks when her vision will be ready for a new glasses check and what needs to happen before she can drive again. [8][13]

08

When does a change in vision need urgent help?

Age-related cataract commonly causes a gradual change. Book an eye examination if blur, glare or difficulty reading is creeping up on you. A sudden change, severe pain or symptoms after surgery need a different response. [1][2][13]

Get emergency eye care immediately for sudden loss of vision, a dark curtain or shadow, a sudden shower of new floaters, or new flashes of light. Severe eye pain also needs emergency assessment. These symptoms can have causes that need prompt treatment. [13][15][16]

After cataract surgery, contact your surgical team or an emergency eye service right away for worsening vision, increasing pain, redness or discharge. If pain is severe or vision is worsening quickly, seek emergency care immediately. [13][17]

Shanta still brings her glasses to the follow-up. She also brings a short list of the things she wants to see more clearly, starting with the road at dusk and the newspaper at breakfast.

Get persistent cloudiness or glare checked. Tell the eye clinician what has become harder, and seek emergency care for a sudden change in vision. [4][5][16]

KEEP

The fridge-door cheat sheet

Why can my glasses still look foggy?

Glasses help focus light. A cloudy natural lens inside the eye can scatter it. [1][2][3]

When should I ask about surgery?

Talk to an eye clinician if blur or glare is getting in the way of reading, work, driving or getting around safely. [1][4][5]

When should I get help right away?

Seek emergency eye care for sudden vision loss, a new curtain or shadow, or severe eye pain. After surgery, report worsening vision or pain right away. [13][15][16][17]

Important patient-education notice

General information only. Use this article to discuss vision changes, eye examination and treatment with your clinician. Ask them before changing any medicine. For severe symptoms, call your local emergency number immediately.
