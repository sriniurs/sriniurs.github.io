SHOULDER INJURIES AFTER A FALL

Why Can’t I Lift My Arm After a Fall?

What a fall can injure, what an X-ray can show and when to get urgent help.

BONE, JOINT OR TENDONTHE FIRST X-RAYWHEN TO GET HELP

About 8 minutes to read · Patient education

01

The step he missed

Suresh is carrying a bag of vegetables up to his front door when his sandal catches the edge of a step. He lands on his right side. When he gets up, he can wiggle his fingers and bend his elbow, but he can barely lift his upper arm away from his body. He tucks it against his chest and asks his neighbour to take him for medical assessment that day.

After a fall, the arm may be hard to lift because a bone has broken, the shoulder has come out of its socket, or a tendon that helps raise the arm has torn. Pain can also make the muscles reluctant to work, even when the bone and joint are intact. It takes an examination and often an X-ray to start telling these possibilities apart. [1][2][3]

02

What happens if a bone breaks?

The upper arm bone is called the humerus. Its rounded top forms the ball of the shoulder joint. A break near that end is called a proximal humerus fracture. A fall directly onto the shoulder can also break the collarbone. Both injuries can make it painful to move the arm, and bruising or swelling may become more noticeable later. [2][5]

The clinician looks at where it hurts, how you landed and whether the shoulder looks different. An X-ray can show which bone is broken and whether the pieces have shifted. If a break is still suspected after an unclear X-ray, a CT scan or MRI may reveal a fracture that was hard to see. Your clinician will decide whether another scan would help. [2][4]

Suresh remembers that his shoulder struck the step and that he noticed the loss of movement immediately. Tell the clinician if instead you landed on an outstretched hand, felt the arm twist or heard a snap. Bring any information you have about a previous fracture or shoulder operation. [2][3]

03

What if the shoulder has come out of its socket?

The shoulder can dislocate when the ball of the upper arm bone slips out of the socket on the shoulder blade. It can cause severe pain, and the shoulder may look squarer or differently shaped than usual. Some people hold the arm still because any attempt to move it hurts. A break can occur at the same time, and nearby nerves or blood vessels can be affected. [2][5]

Go to an emergency department if you think the shoulder is dislocated. Keep it supported in the position you found it and leave it for trained clinicians to put back in place. Pulling on the arm or trying to force a position can make an injury worse. Tell staff if the shoulder seemed to pop out and then slip back in. It can still need an assessment. [2][5][8]

ZappyWhat does the clinician do to put it back?
Prof. BoltThe team gives pain relief and may give medicine to help you relax before guiding the joint back. They check the arm and confirm the position afterwards. [2][5]
04

Could a torn tendon stop me lifting my arm?

The rotator cuff is a group of muscles and tendons that helps steady the shoulder and turn and lift the arm. A sudden load, such as catching yourself with an outstretched hand, can tear one or more of these tendons. A tear may cause pain and weakness. You may find you can’t lift the arm as far as you did just before the fall. [3][6]

Pain also makes it harder to tell how much strength you’ve lost. The clinician may ask how you used the arm before the injury and reassess movement once it is safe and the pain is better controlled. If your arm remains unusually weak, tell the team even if you are less sore than you were at first. [2][3][6]

A suspected new cuff tear after a fall needs prompt assessment, especially when you suddenly lose the ability to lift the arm. If the X-ray doesn’t explain that weakness, an ultrasound or MRI may help answer whether a tendon is torn. The findings help decide whether a shoulder specialist should review you and how soon. [3][4][6]

Some cuff tears develop gradually and are found on scans after an injury. Tell the team if you already had shoulder pain or difficulty reaching before you fell. A scan finding can mean different things in someone who has lost strength suddenly and someone whose arm has been weak for months. The clinician makes sense of it alongside the examination. [3][4][6]

THE DEEP DIVEFrom the first X-ray to treatment and follow-up
05

What will the team check first?

They will ask what happened, where it hurts and whether the arm felt weak or numb straight away. They may look at the shoulder and arm, feel the pulse at your wrist, and check what you can feel over the shoulder and in the hand. Report any change in feeling, colour or temperature, including a hand that feels colder than the other. [2][5][7]

X-rays are usually the first imaging test after an acute shoulder injury. They can show a dislocation or many fractures. A CT scan can help show the shape of a complex break. Ultrasound and MRI can show tendons, which an ordinary X-ray cannot show clearly. The team chooses the test based on what it needs to find out. [2][4]

Pain may limit what the team can safely check at the first visit. Before you leave, ask whether they expect to reassess movement or strength, and who to contact if the arm still won’t lift. Knowing the follow-up plan is especially useful if you will be seen by a different team next time. [2][4][6]

When Suresh’s X-rays show a break near the top of the upper arm bone, the team discusses how the pieces line up and when they want to see him again. He asks which movements are allowed while he waits. [2]

06

Will I need an operation?

The treatment depends on what was injured. Many upper arm fractures near the shoulder can be treated with a sling and follow-up. Some need an operation depending on the fracture pattern and whether the pieces can heal in a useful position. After a dislocation, the team checks the joint after putting it back and advises you on support and rehabilitation. [2][5]

The PROFHER trial enrolled 250 people with certain displaced fractures near the top of the upper arm bone. The study found no meaningful difference in average shoulder function over two years between surgery and nonsurgical care. It studied a specific type of fracture in people for whom either treatment could be considered. Your fracture pattern and examination can lead to a different decision. [9]

For a new rotator cuff tear, the decision may depend on which tendon is torn, how much strength you have lost and what you need the arm to do. A shoulder specialist can explain when repair may help and what rehabilitation would involve. [3][6]

07

How do I look after the arm while it heals?

Suresh leaves with a sling and a follow-up appointment. He asks the team when to wear the sling, whether to sleep in it and which joints he can move. The timing depends on the injury and treatment. Ask for written instructions if you’re unsure when you can take it off, begin moving the shoulder or lift anything with that arm. [2][5][8]

Plan around one or two jobs that will be awkward at first. Suresh puts a loose shirt within easy reach and asks someone else to carry the shopping. At the next visit he wants to know when he can get back to pouring tea and when it’s safe to take the scooter out. Tell your care team what you need to return to so they can discuss it with you. [5][8]

If the team expects movement to improve but you still can’t lift the arm, contact them for a review. Tell them if the weakness has changed, the pain is worsening or you have new tingling. Bring any report or discharge note from the first visit, and describe what the arm could do before and after the fall. [1][4][6][8]

08

When do I need urgent or emergency help?

If you can barely lift your arm after a fall, get urgent medical assessment that day. Support the arm with a folded towel or sling in a comfortable position while arranging care. Keep it still and avoid testing how high it will go. You can use a cold pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes while waiting, if the skin has normal feeling. [1][5][7][8]

Go to emergency care now if the shoulder looks out of place, the hand is newly cold, pale or numb, there is a bone through the skin, or you have heavy bleeding. Call your local emergency number if you can’t get there safely. Leave any suspected dislocation for a clinician to put back. [1][2][5][7]

Prof. BoltGet emergency help. New numbness, a cold hand or a change in colour needs assessment, even after the first visit. [2][7]
