AC JOINT INJURY

Why Is There a Bump on Top of My Shoulder After a Fall?

Why the outer collarbone can stand out after a fall, what an X-ray can show and how the shoulder may recover.

THE COLLARBONE BUMPTHE AC JOINTGETTING BACK TO ACTIVITY

About 8 minutes to read · Patient education

01

The bump under the backpack strap

Nikhil falls off his bicycle and lands on the point of his left shoulder. He can bend his elbow, but lifting his backpack is painful. When he takes off his shirt, he sees a small rise at the outer end of his collarbone. The backpack strap had been pressing right on it. He asks a friend to take him for an assessment that day.

One possible cause of a new bump here is an injury to the acromioclavicular joint, or AC joint. This is the small joint where the collarbone meets the top of the shoulder blade. A fall onto the shoulder can stretch or tear the ligaments that hold those bones together. Swelling can add to the bump, and if the bones shift relative to one another, the end of the collarbone may stand out. [1][2]

02

Which two bones meet at the bump?

Run a finger along your uninjured collarbone towards the outside of the shoulder. Its far end meets a flat bony part of the shoulder blade called the acromion. That meeting point is the AC joint. Ligaments are strong bands of tissue that connect bones. Some wrap around the joint. Others run from the underside of the collarbone to another part of the shoulder blade and help support the connection. [1][4]

During a fall onto the point of the shoulder, the force can drive the shoulder blade down relative to the collarbone. If the supporting ligaments tear, the shoulder blade and arm can sit lower. In the mirror, the outer end of the collarbone can then look as if it has risen. [1]

The familiar ball-and-socket joint sits lower down. A clinician may call an AC injury a shoulder separation. If you hear that phrase, ask which joint was injured. The advice on recovery depends on where the injury is and what else was hurt. [1][2][4]

03

Why does reaching across my chest hurt?

The AC joint moves as the shoulder blade moves. Bringing the arm across your chest or reaching overhead can bring on pain at the top of the shoulder after an AC injury. Pressure from a bag strap or from lying on that side can also be uncomfortable. Show the clinician where you feel it and which normal activities bring it on. [1][2][4]

Nikhil can hold his phone with his elbow by his side, but reaching for the opposite sleeve hurts. He tells the clinician it feels tender in one spot, right under the strap. That helps them decide where to examine him. A clinician still needs to consider a collarbone fracture and other injuries that can follow the same fall. [1][2]

04

What can the examination and X-ray show?

The clinician will ask where you landed and how much you could move the arm afterwards. They can look for swelling or a change in shape, check the skin over the bump, and feel along the collarbone and AC joint. Tell them about numbness, weakness or a hand that feels colder than usual. Let them know straight away if you have chest pain or difficulty breathing after the fall. [2][3][5]

X-rays are usually the first imaging test after an acute shoulder injury. They can help show a fracture or a shift in the alignment of the AC joint. A mild ligament sprain can look normal on an X-ray, so the clinician combines the images with the examination. They may request another view if the first images don’t answer their question. [1][2][6]

Nikhil asks what his X-ray shows and what the clinician found by examining him. The team explains that his pain is coming from the AC joint. Before he leaves, he asks when to expect improvement and who to call if the pain gets worse. [2][4]

If you were seen straight after the fall, keep the first report or discharge advice for your review. Tell the next clinician if the bump became clearer as swelling settled, or if you still find it difficult to lift the arm. The first images and the change since then can both help guide the follow-up. [2][4][6]

THE DEEP DIVEWhat the injury grade means and how treatment is chosen
05

What does the grade mean?

You may hear the injury described by a number. In a type I injury, the ligaments around the AC joint are sprained and the bones stay aligned. In type II, the ligaments around the joint are more damaged and the bones may shift a little. In type III, both main sets of supporting ligaments are torn and the joint is more noticeably out of alignment. Further types describe more severe or different directions of displacement. [1]

The number helps the clinician describe what was injured and discuss treatment. The appearance of the bump can be one clue, but swelling, the direction the bones have moved and the arm’s position all affect what you see. The grade comes from the clinical assessment and imaging. [1][6]

A mild sprain can be quite sore even when the shoulder looks almost the same as it did before the fall. With a more obvious separation, the pain can improve while the shape stays different. At a review, describe the change in movement and comfort since the injury. That information helps the clinician judge how recovery is going. [1][4]

06

What helps the shoulder recover?

Many AC injuries improve with nonsurgical care. The team may give you a sling for comfort and advise when to start taking it off. A cold pack wrapped in a towel can help with discomfort early on. Ask a pharmacist or clinician what pain relief is suitable for you. Follow the movement plan for your own injury, especially if the fall also caused a fracture. [1][2][4]

As pain settles, the plan may include gentle movement followed by strengthening. Ask which movements to start with and what to do if the pain increases. Getting a shirt on, reaching the kitchen shelf and carrying a loaded bag need different amounts of movement and strength. Use those tasks to explain your progress at a review. [2][4]

Nikhil moves his backpack strap off the sore shoulder and asks someone else to carry it initially. Later he wants to get back on the bicycle. He asks his clinician when he can safely steer, brake and bear weight on the handlebars, and when it is reasonable to risk another fall. [2][4]

If you’re returning to a physical job or contact sport, tell the team what you actually do. Carrying light items at waist height and lifting a heavy load onto a shelf put different demands on the shoulder. Ask whether you can return in stages. [1][2][4]

07

When might surgery be discussed?

Mild sprains are usually managed without surgery. With more severe displacement, or pain and difficulty using the shoulder that persist despite rehabilitation, a specialist may discuss an operation to restore stability. The decision takes into account the direction of displacement, the condition of the skin, other injuries and what the person needs to do at work or in sport. [1][2][4]

A 2023 randomised trial compared surgical and nonsurgical care for acute type III AC injuries. The researchers found no improvement in shoulder function from surgery over the study period, and the surgical group had more complications and further operations. Those findings apply to the type III injuries studied. Ask which type you have and what benefit an operation would be expected to bring in your case. [7]

The bump may still be there after the shoulder feels better. What matters at follow-up is how it feels and works as well as how it looks. Tell the clinician if you still can’t carry everyday items, sleep comfortably or get back to the activity that matters to you. [1][4]

If surgery comes up, ask which problem it is meant to address and what the recovery would mean for your usual routine. For Nikhil, borrowing a different bag is easy. Being unable to steer and brake comfortably matters much more. Those priorities help him and his clinician discuss the options. [1][4]

08

When should I get help faster?

Arrange urgent assessment for a new bump after a fall, especially if the pain is severe or you can barely lift the arm. Support the arm comfortably while you wait. Leave the bump alone and let the clinician examine it. If you’ve already been seen, contact the treating team if pain or movement is getting worse or you’re unsure about the next steps. [2][3][4]

Get emergency care if bone is through the skin, there is heavy bleeding, or the hand becomes cold, pale or numb. Get emergency help for chest pain or trouble breathing after the fall. Call your local emergency number if you can’t get to emergency care safely. [2][5][8]
