SHOULDER PAIN AT NIGHT

Why Does My Shoulder Wake Me Up at Night?

Why turning in bed can wake a sore shoulder, how to support it with a pillow and when to get it checked.

THE NIGHT ACHEPILLOW SUPPORTWHEN TO GET HELP

About 8 minutes to read · Patient education

01

The turn that woke him up

Ravi falls asleep on his back. Sometime after midnight he rolls onto his right side and wakes with an ache running from his shoulder into his upper arm. He turns over and the ache settles enough for him to doze again. The following morning, he feels it when he reaches across to pull on his seat belt. After three nights like this, he starts wondering what happens to his shoulder while he sleeps.

A sore shoulder can wake you when you roll onto it or when your arm settles in a position that brings on the ache. Pain at night can occur with several shoulder problems, including rotator cuff–related pain and frozen shoulder. The way it behaves when you move, and what you notice during the day, can help narrow down the cause. [1][2][3]

02

What changes when I lie down?

On your sore side, the outside of your shoulder presses into the bed. On the other side, your top arm may drop forward if it has nothing to rest on. Either position can be uncomfortable. Ravi notices that the ache settles quicker if he rests his forearm on a pillow when he turns away from the sore side. [3]

Pay attention to the first minute after you change position. Does the ache ease when you take weight off the shoulder or support the elbow? Does it stay the same however you lie? This can be useful information for an appointment. You can jot it down the next morning from memory. [3][8]

People sometimes wonder if years of side sleeping caused the problem. One study compared usual sleep positions with shoulder symptoms and examination findings in workers at a single point in time. It could only look for associations. People may already have changed their sleep habits because the shoulder hurt. Use position changes to look for comfort and discuss the rest of your symptoms with a clinician. [3][4]

03

Which daytime clues go with the night ache?

The rotator cuff is a group of muscles and tendons that helps move and steady the shoulder. Cuff-related pain can show up when you lift a kettle or reach for a shirt on a hanger. It may also hurt when you lower the arm or lie down at night. The ache often sits at the front or outside of the shoulder and can travel into the upper arm. [1][5]

With frozen shoulder, a gradually shrinking range of movement is an important clue. You may struggle to tuck in a shirt, turn the arm to put on a jacket or get your hand behind your back. Night pain can be prominent, especially when the shoulder is painful and becoming stiff. A clinician can check how far the arm moves both on its own and with gentle support. [2][5]

Pain that travels farther down the arm with tingling or numbness may lead the clinician to check your neck and nerves. Mention if turning your head changes those symptoms. If the tingling persists or the arm becomes numb, seek urgent advice. [1][6][8]

An ache centred right at the top of the shoulder may have a different source, such as the small joint where the collarbone meets the shoulder blade. Show the clinician where you feel it with one finger if you can. If the ache spreads or is hard to pinpoint, describe the area it covers. [5][6]

ZappyWhat if my shoulder is sore at night and stiff when I get dressed?
Prof. BoltDescribe both. The movement you’ve lost can help guide the examination. [2][5]
THE DEEP DIVEPillow support, daytime clues and when to get help
04

How can I support the arm on my side?

Try lying on the more comfortable side and placing a pillow in front of your chest. Rest the sore arm on it, including the elbow and forearm. The pillow should fill the gap comfortably so you can let the arm rest. Move it nearer or farther away until the shoulder feels relaxed. If you roll back onto the sore side, a pillow behind your back may help you stay more comfortably on your side. [3][8]

Ravi tries hugging a spare pillow. The first one is so firm that it pushes his elbow up and he keeps shrugging. He swaps it for a softer pillow and can relax his arm. The setup that feels comfortable may change as symptoms settle. [3]

If you’re recovering from an operation or fracture, follow your treating team’s instructions about overnight arm support and sling use. Ask them how to position the arm if their instructions are difficult to follow in your bed. [6][9]

05

What if I prefer to sleep on my back?

Try a small pillow or folded cushion under the elbow and forearm on the sore side. Let the arm rest where the shoulder feels easy. Adjust the height so it supports the arm without making you shrug. Some people find this more comfortable than side sleeping. Others do better on their more comfortable side. [3]

If you wake again, try an adjustment you can make without repeatedly testing the shoulder. Ravi keeps the spare pillow within easy reach of his good arm. When he rolls over, he can move the pillow into place and settle down again. [3]

If you’re trying different setups, start with one small change. Use an ordinary pillow for a night and see whether you settle more easily. If it feels awkward, change its height or try the other position. The test is whether you’re comfortable enough to rest. [3][8]

If you use a wrapped warm or cold pack for comfort, try it while awake and remove it before you settle to sleep. NHS guidance suggests using a wrapped pack for up to 20 minutes at a time. A pharmacist can help you choose suitable pain relief, taking your other medicines and health conditions into account. [6]

ZappyShould I buy a special shoulder pillow?
Prof. BoltTry a pillow you already have. See whether it supports the elbow and forearm comfortably when you relax. [3]
06

What should I tell the clinician about my nights?

Tell them how often you wake, whether you wake on the sore side, and whether a change of position eases the ache. Share the daytime picture too. It helps to know which tasks bring on pain, whether movement has narrowed, and whether the arm feels weaker since an injury. Mention swelling, fever, night sweats or weight loss you didn’t intend. [5][6][8]

A clinician may check how you lift and turn the arm, how far it moves with help, and how much strength it has. The history and examination guide the next step. For typical suspected rotator cuff tendinopathy, care can usually begin without scans. A significant injury, unusual symptoms, or difficulty that persists despite care may change what tests are useful. [1][5][6]

At his appointment Ravi says, “I wake when I roll onto the right shoulder. Holding the arm on a pillow lets me settle, but pulling the seat belt still hurts.” That gives the clinician both a sleep problem and a movement to discuss at follow-up. [3][5]

A few lines are enough. For example, “Monday, woke twice. Turning onto my back helped. Tuesday, woke once and the ache stayed after I moved.” Include when it started and any recent change in work, exercise or activity. Bring the note even if your shoulder feels better on the day of the visit. [5][8]

07

What can help beyond changing pillows?

Pillows are a way to make the night more comfortable while you address what’s happening with the shoulder. During the day, keep up gentle movement you can manage and temporarily change activities that repeatedly aggravate it. If the problem fits rotator cuff tendinopathy, a physiotherapist may suggest gradually building movement and strength. Care for a stiff frozen shoulder may instead start with easing pain and finding a manageable range of motion. [2][3][5][6]

Choose something you can check again in a week. It might be how often you wake or how easily you reach the seat belt. If sleep keeps breaking up, pain is getting worse or taking a turn you haven’t noticed before, arrange a review. Seek an assessment if the shoulder is very hard to move or hasn’t started to improve after about two weeks. [5][6]

Ravi changes where his arm rests and keeps a short note of his nights. He also asks about the reach that still hurts during the day. His goal is to sleep more comfortably and get back to putting on his seat belt without having to stop halfway. [3][5]

08

When should I get help sooner?

Tell a clinician promptly if the pain is becoming constant and changing position brings little relief, particularly if you also have night sweats, unexplained weight loss or feel generally unwell. The combination matters more than the time on the clock. [6][8]

Get urgent medical help for sudden severe shoulder pain, pain after an injury, a shoulder that looks misshapen, an arm you suddenly can’t move, persistent numbness, or a hot or badly swollen shoulder, especially with fever. [6][8]

Call your local emergency number if shoulder or arm discomfort comes with chest pressure, breathlessness, a cold sweat or feeling faint. These can be signs of a heart attack, even when they begin at night. [7]

Why does it hurt more in bed?

Pressure on the sore side or an unsupported arm can make a sensitive shoulder ache. The daytime symptoms help a clinician work out the cause. [1][2][3]

What can I try tonight?

Lie on the more comfortable side with a pillow supporting the sore arm, or try lying on your back with a pillow under the elbow and forearm. [3]

When do I need help quickly?

Get urgent help for sudden severe pain, pain after an injury, or a hot swollen shoulder with fever. Call emergency services for possible heart attack symptoms. [6][7]

Important patient-education notice

General information only. Speak to a clinician if shoulder pain keeps interrupting sleep or is getting worse. Get urgent help for sudden severe pain, pain after injury, an arm you cannot move or a hot, swollen shoulder with fever. Call your local emergency number if shoulder or arm discomfort comes with chest pressure, breathlessness, a cold sweat or faintness. [6][7][8]
