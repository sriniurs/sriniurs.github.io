Shoulder

Ten illustrated answers about shoulder pain, stiffness and injuries, from a painful reach to shoulder arthritis.

  1. Pain With Overhead ReachingWhy Does My Shoulder Hurt When I Reach Overhead?Why a shirt on the clothesline can make your shoulder ache, what a clinician checks and how to build back to reaching overhead.
  2. Frozen ShoulderWhy Can’t I Reach Behind My Back Anymore?Why a stiff shoulder makes dressing harder, what the clinician checks and what may help you move more comfortably.
  3. Night-Time Shoulder PainWhy Does My Shoulder Wake Me Up at Night?Why turning in bed can wake a sore shoulder, how to support it with a pillow and when to get it checked.
  4. Shoulder Injury After a FallWhy Can’t I Lift My Arm After a Fall?What a fall can injure, what an X-ray can show and when to get urgent help.
  5. Shoulder InstabilityWhy Does My Shoulder Keep Slipping Out?Why the shoulder can give way after a dislocation, what helps it feel steadier and what to do if it comes out again.
  6. AC Joint InjuryWhy Is There a Bump on Top of My Shoulder After a Fall?Why the outer collarbone can stand out after a fall, what an X-ray can show and how the shoulder may recover.
  7. Calcific TendinopathyHow Can a Tiny Calcium Deposit Cause So Much Shoulder Pain?Why a calcium spot in a tendon can cause a sudden flare, what a scan can show and how treatment is chosen.
  8. Shoulder ClickingWhy Does My Shoulder Click When I Move It?What a painless click can mean, why a painful catch deserves attention and when an examination or scan can help.
  9. Referred Shoulder PainCan a Problem in My Neck Feel Like Shoulder Pain?How a neck problem can send symptoms into the shoulder or arm and what a clinician checks to find the source.
  10. Shoulder ArthritisWhy Does My Shoulder Grind and Feel Stiff?How arthritis can change shoulder movement, what an X-ray can show and when replacement may be discussed.

FOR PATIENT EDUCATION ONLY

These articles are intended to help you better understand your symptoms and the care advised after consultation and treatment by your doctor. They are not a guide to self-diagnosis or self-treatment.

Follow your treating specialist’s advice. Do not start or change medicines, exercises or other treatments based on these articles.
