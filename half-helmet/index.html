Why a Half Helmet Is Almost No Helmet

Karnataka plans to ban the cap-style helmet that sits on top of so many heads. Here is what a helmet really does in the split second of a fall, and why the half one so often isn’t enough.

The ban, explainedA crash, in slow motionReal helmet or fake?

ABOUT 8 MINUTES

01

The helmet that’s mostly for the police

You have seen it at every signal. A shiny plastic bowl perched on top of the head, the strap swinging free, the ears and face out in the breeze.

It is light, it is cheap, it doesn’t crush your hair, and it keeps the traffic constable from waving you over. What it does in a crash is another matter. That is why the state now wants it gone.

On 16 November, Karnataka’s Transport Minister announced a state-wide ban on half helmets, with action against the units that make them. The same package raises the fine for riding on footpaths from ₹1,000 to ₹5,000, and ₹10,000 or seizure of the vehicle for a repeat offence. [1] The government wants it in force before the end of 2026. [2] Even helmet makers are on board: their national association says the crackdown should reach factories and shops, not just riders. [3]

Why do so many people wear the half one in the first place? The World Health Organization (WHO) lists the usual reasons worldwide: good helmets that cost too much, weak enforcement, and hot weather. It adds that a helmet that is not properly fastened also raises the risk of death and injury. [4]

Here is the number that should worry every rider in Bengaluru. In 2023, 369 riders died in the city while wearing a helmet, up 21% in two years. The city traffic police linked this to riders wearing non-standard helmets, or not fastening them, just to avoid a fine. [5]

ZappyBut mine has never fallen off.

Prof. BoltBecause you’ve never fallen off. The helmet only gets tested once.

02

A fall, in slow motion

Picture a rider tipping over at an ordinary city speed. The head hits the road and stops. But the brain inside it is soft and floating, and for a split second it keeps moving, until it hits the inside of the skull. That second collision does the damage.

A helmet can’t stop the fall. Its whole job is to make the stop slower, by giving the head a few extra centimetres to slow down in. A real helmet has two layers for that:

THE HARD SHELL

Spreads one sharp blow over a wide area, so no single spot of the skull takes it all, and slides on the road instead of catching.

THE THICK FOAM

Usually expanded polystyrene, like stiff thermocol. It crushes on impact, using up the energy. A crushed helmet has done its job and must be replaced.

How much difference can a few centimetres make? Here is a rough sum. Take a head hitting the road at 20 km/h. If it stops within about half a centimetre, it feels around 300 times its own weight. Give it 3 cm of foam to sink into, and that falls to about 50 times. [our arithmetic]

Same fall, different stopping distance
≈0.5 cm stop

≈3 cm of foam
≈50 g

g = times the force of gravity. A simplified estimate, not a lab result. See the evidence file.

That is why the WHO says a safe, good-quality helmet cuts the risk of death in a crash more than six times, and the risk of brain injury by up to 74%. [6] The key words are safe and quality. A thin bowl with a strip of sponge inside has very little to crush, and a half helmet covers only the crown, leaving the face and jaw bare. In real crashes, between roughly 1 in 6 and 1 in 3 blows land on the chin area, depending on the study. [7] [8] [9]

The foam is the helmet. The shell is its raincoat, and the strap is what keeps both on your head.
03

Three helmets, three different heads

The Indian helmet standard, IS 4151, covers helmets with or without a lower face cover. [24] So the law has never said “full-face or nothing”. What it says is that the helmet must pass the tests. That leaves three shapes on our roads:

TypeWhat it coversThe catch
Full-faceSkull, ears, jaw and chin, with a visor in frontHeavier and warmer; you lift the visor to talk
Open-faceSkull, back of the head and ears; face left openNothing between your jaw and the road
Half (“cap”)The crown, roughly where a cap sitsEars, temples, back of the head and face all bare

The difference matters because of where heads actually land. Most falls pitch the rider forward. That is why crash studies keep finding so many blows on the front of the helmet and the chin, and why the chin bar is not decoration. [8] [9]

The half helmet sits exactly where blows are least likely to fall: the very top of the head. It protects you best in the one crash you are least likely to have.

ZappySo my helmet guards the part of my head that never touches the road.

Prof. BoltVery bravely, too.

04

The ISI sticker that lies

Since 1 June 2021, every two-wheeler helmet sold in India has had to carry the ISI mark under IS 4151. [10] Making, importing or selling one without a valid licence, or with a fake ISI mark, is prohibited. [11] The Motor Vehicles Act also says your headgear must meet BIS standards, and that the strap must be fastened. [15]

The trouble is that a sticker costs almost nothing. In one 2024 raid on a helmet unit, BIS officials seized helmets carrying an expired licence number, along with 23,600 ISI-marked holograms ready to be stuck on. [17]

Here is the check that takes thirty seconds at the shop. A genuine ISI mark carries a licence number next to it, written as CM/L followed by digits. [16] Type that number into the free BIS Care app and it shows the maker’s name and address, and whether the licence is valid. [16] [18]

The 30-second shop check
Find the ISI mark→Find the CM/L number beside it→Check it on BIS Care→Maker’s name matches the box
No CM/L number, or a number the app can’t find? You are holding a hat with a sticker on it.

05

54,568

India has about 21 crore two-wheelers on the road. [19] In 2023, two-wheeler riders made up 45% of all road deaths: 77,539 people. [20]

The government’s own accident report counts 54,568 people killed that year who were not wearing a helmet. About 72% were riders and 28% pillion passengers. [12] That works out to roughly 150 a day. [our arithmetic]

The second bar is the national count of people killed without a helmet, which includes a small number on other vehicle types. Sources: [12] [20]

One honest gap: these numbers split riders into “helmet” and “no helmet”. India does not publish how many of the helmeted dead were in a half helmet, a fake one, or one with the strap undone. The closest we have is Bengaluru’s 369 helmeted deaths in 2023, which the city police blamed on exactly those habits. [5]

ZappySo we know how many forgot the helmet, but not how many brought the wrong one.

Prof. BoltExactly. The half helmet hides inside the “wore a helmet” column.

06

“But it’s hot, and I can’t hear”

These are the two real reasons people reach for the cap, so they deserve a fair hearing.

The heat is real. The WHO itself lists hot weather as one of the main reasons riders skip helmets worldwide. [4] A full-face helmet in a May traffic jam is not pleasant. The middle path is a certified open-face helmet, which lets air reach your face and still covers the back and sides of the head. [24]

The hearing worry is mostly a myth. A US government study put 50 riders on a test route with no helmet, a partial helmet and a full helmet. It found no difference in how well they heard traffic signals, and only a slight narrowing of side vision. [21] What drowned out sound was wind noise at speed, helmet or not. [21]

Side vision matters less than people think anyway. A large US study of 900 crashes found that more than three-quarters of the hazards came from within 45° of straight ahead, where every helmet lets you see. [22]

ZappyWhat if I just ride slowly in the heat?

Prof. BoltGood. Remember our sum was for 20 km/h. Slow is still fast enough.

07

The strap, and the expiry date

The best helmet in the shop is useless if it flies off before your head lands. Sled tests by a Dutch road-safety institute found that with the chin strap left open, there was an 80–100% chance of the helmet coming off in an impact. [13] That is also why the law counts an unfastened strap as riding without a helmet: ₹1,000, and a possible three-month licence disqualification. [15] [23]

Two quick fit tests from US road-safety guidance: open your mouth in a big yawn, and the helmet should pull down on your head. And it should not rock back more than about two finger-widths above your eyebrows. [14]

REPLACE IT, NO ARGUMENT

After any crash or hard drop where it hit something. The foam crushes once; it can look perfect and still be spent. [26]

REPLACE IT, MAYBE

After about five years. Makers such as Shoei recommend it, but some testers argue stored foam holds up for decades. [25] [26]

08

What the ban will fix, and what it won’t

The ban’s strongest point is that it goes after the shop, not just the rider. If the cap disappears from roadside stalls, the cheapest “helmet for the police” goes with it. Helmet makers support that approach, saying checks only on riders would not be enough. [3]

But three of the habits behind Bengaluru’s 369 deaths are untouched by banning a shape: the open strap, the fake ISI sticker, and the non-standard helmet that happens to be full-face. The police report blamed non-standard helmets and open straps, not the cap’s shape alone. [5] Those need the same checks that exist today, actually enforced.

The rules are not final yet. One question to watch: whether a properly certified open-face helmet, which IS 4151 allows, will count as a “half helmet” in Karnataka’s rule. [2] [24] Riders on a tight budget will also be watching the price of the cheapest certified helmet; cost is one of the WHO’s reasons people go without. [4]

The plain truth underneath all of it: the helmet is tested once, on the worst day of your life. Whatever you wear, wear one that would pass.

ZappyFine. My cap goes in the cupboard.

Prof. BoltGood. It can finally do the job it was built for: looking like a helmet.
