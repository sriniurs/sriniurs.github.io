How Does Mysuru's Zoo Feed Its Animals?

More than 1,500 animals, 128 diets and a ₹7.86-crore food bill. How Mysuru’s zoo buys, checks and portions every meal, why the lions fast on Tuesdays, and who really pays.

1,505 ANIMALS128 SPECIES₹2 LAKH A DAY

ABOUT 8 MINUTES

01

A kitchen for 1,505 mouths

A giraffe, a gorilla, a king cobra and a flock of parakeets walk into a restaurant. Each wants something completely different, at a different time, in a different amount. Every single day.

That is daily life at Sri Chamarajendra Zoological Gardens, the Mysuru zoo. On 31 March 2025 it housed 1,505 animals of 128 species: 763 mammals, 647 birds and 95 reptiles. [1]

In 2024–25 the zoo spent ₹7.86 crore on animal food and fodder. [1] That’s about ₹2.15 lakh a day (our arithmetic). Each species has its own diet chart. [2]

Zappy₹2 lakh a day? My grocery bill feels small now.

Prof. BoltYour family doesn’t include an elephant, a crocodile and 600 birds. Yet.

Feeding a zoo is less like cooking and more like running a hospital kitchen with 128 different patients.
02

Five kinds of food, and how the meat gets in

The zoo buys five broad kinds of food: meat (including fish and poultry), dry and green fodder, grains, and fruits and vegetables, plus bread, milk and eggs. Suppliers are chosen through e-tenders. [2] Dry items such as grains, pulses and oil cakes come in once every 15 days. [2]

Meat gets the strictest checks. It is bought fresh every day from a licensed slaughterhouse. The animals are first certified by a city corporation vet, and the meat is checked again by the zoo’s vet before it is fed. Samples are sent to a government lab in Bengaluru from time to time. [2]

Licensed slaughterhouse→City vet certifies→Zoo vet checks again→Weighed and portioned per animal

Portions are weighed for each animal, by its body weight. [2] The rest varies with the season: primates get juicy fruit in summer and dry fruit in winter. [2] In hot months, the apes get tender coconuts twice a day, bears get ice blocks, and elephants get banana plants and sugarcane. [1]

Nothing in a zoo animal’s bowl is “whatever was available”. It is tendered, checked and weighed.
03

Why the lions don’t eat on Tuesdays

At Mysuru zoo, the big cats skip a meal once a week: carnivores fast every Tuesday. [1] [2]

Other animals keep their own calendars. Crocodiles fast once every 10 days and snakes once every 10 to 15 days. Herbivores, primates and birds never fast. [1]

Animal groupMain foodFasting
Tigers, lionsBeef and chickenEvery Tuesday [1]
CrocodilesFish and beefOnce every 10 days [1]
SnakesRats, mice, chicken, rabbits, even other snakesEvery 10–15 days [1]
Elephants, giraffesVegetables, grains, concentrates, roughageNever [1]
Gorillas, chimpsVegetables, fruit, eggs, milkNever [1]

Why fast at all? Hyderabad’s zoo gives its animals a controlled diet one day a week to prevent obesity and the illnesses that come with eating heavily every day. [3]

ZappySo the lions are on intermittent fasting?

Prof. BoltThey were doing it long before it became a fashion. Gyms only caught up later.

A day without dinner isn’t cruelty. For a big cat, it’s closer to its normal routine.
04

The zoo grows some of its own food

Not everything comes by truck. In 2020, during the COVID lockdown, the zoo started growing its own forage crops such as Napier grass, stopped buying them from outside, and expected to save at least ₹25 lakh a year. [4]

At its rescue and rehabilitation centre in Kurghalli, the zoo grows about 25 grafted fruit trees organically, to feed lion-tailed macaques and gibbons. [1]

Some diners need food that is still alive. The zoo breeds mealworms and superworms for insect-eaters; one cycle takes about 45–50 days. Snakes are given rodents sized to their length and age. [2]

Part of the zoo is a farm: grass, fruit trees and worm boxes, all grown to feed the animals.

05

Who pays the bill?

You do, at the gate. In 2024–25 the zoo earned a record ₹44.01 crore. [1] [5] Of that, ₹35.52 crore, about 81% (our arithmetic), came from zoo gate tickets. [1] The zoo doesn’t receive government grants. It runs on gate collections, donations and adoption fees. [6]

Food and fodder were about 18% of the zoo’s ₹42.53 crore spending that year (our arithmetic). [1] Twenty years ago the zoo spent ₹85 lakh on food out of ₹3.45 crore in revenue. [7]

YearRevenueFood and fodder
2004–05₹3.45 crore₹85 lakh [7]
2024–25₹44.01 crore₹7.86 crore [1]

Crowds swing the money. In 2020–21, during COVID, visitors fell to 7.43 lakh and revenue to ₹14.60 crore. [5] The district then led a public campaign that raised about ₹4 crore. [8] During the 11 days of Dasara 2025 alone, the zoo took in ₹1.91 crore at the gate. [9]

Every zoo ticket is partly a meal ticket for the animals.
06

Adopt an animal: what it really pays for

The zoo’s animal adoption scheme started in October 2001. [10] The idea is simple: you pay for an animal’s food for a year (or another period), and you get a certificate and recognition. [11] [12]

Fees depend on the animal. After a fee revision, adopting an elephant cost ₹3 lakh a year, up from ₹1.75 lakh. [13] A tiger has been adopted for ₹1 lakh, and a lovebird for ₹1,000. [14] Karnataka’s zoos have since revised fees again, sorting animals into 13 groups based on maintenance costs. [15]

It is still popular. On 13 November 2026, a company adopted two Asiatic elephants for a year. [16] But adoptions and donations brought in about ₹68 lakh in 2024–25. [1] That covers roughly 9% of the food bill (our arithmetic). The scheme really mattered after the pandemic, when it helped the zoo through a serious money crisis. [15]

ZappySo if I adopt a lovebird, I’m feeding it for a year?

Prof. BoltYour contribution goes towards it, yes. The tickets are still doing most of the work.

Adoption is a lovely gesture and a real safety net, but the ticket counter pays most of the food bill.
07

The year the menu changed

Diets can change because of law, not just science. When Karnataka’s cow-slaughter ban became law in 2021, beef shops in Mysuru closed and the zoo replaced beef with chicken for its big cats. At the time, the zoo needed about 350 kg of beef a day. [17]

A newspaper report afterwards claimed the cats had become less active on the new diet. [18] The zoo’s 2024–25 report lists beef and chicken for its tigers and lions. [1] The reports don’t say which kind of beef this is, so we can’t tell you how the menu changed back.

Even a lion’s dinner can be changed by a law passed in Bengaluru.
08

Where the leftovers go

Whatever goes in has to come out. The zoo turns animal dung into vermicompost and sells it to farmers and nurseries. [1]

Between April 2024 and March 2025, it sold 98,085 kg of compost at ₹15 a kg, earning about ₹14.71 lakh. [19] That’s small next to the food bill, but it closes the loop: farm produce feeds the animals, and the animals’ dung feeds the farms.

Farms grow fodder and fruit→Animals eat→Dung becomes compost→Compost goes back to farms
At the zoo, even the poop is part of the budget.

OPINION: the ticket is the conservation

This section is our opinion, not reporting.

WHAT WORKS

A zoo that pays its own food bill from tickets, with checked meat and diet charts for every species, is something Mysuru should be proud of.

WHAT’S FRAGILE

Relying on the gate means one bad year, like COVID, hits the animals’ budget directly. A reserve fund for food would be sensible.

WHAT WOULD HELP

Publish a simple “menu of the month” and the adoption fees on one clear page. Visitors who understand the costs are more likely to adopt.

✓

So what should Zappy do?

Don’t feed the animals. Their meals are weighed and vet-checked. A biscuit from you upsets all of that.

Check the weekly holiday before you go. And if you’re hoping to see the big cats eat, don’t go on a Tuesday.

Consider adopting, even small. A bird or a small animal can cost a few thousand rupees, and it’s a great present for a child. Check the current fee list.

Buy the compost. If you garden, the zoo’s vermicompost is cheap and closes the loop.

Visit in the cooler months. Animals, and people, are more active in winter.
