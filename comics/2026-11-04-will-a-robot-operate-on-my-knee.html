WILL A ROBOT
OPERATE ON MY KNEE?

Ajji has been advised a knee replacement. The hospital says “robotic”. The family hears “machine”. Zappy has questions—and Prof. Bolt is taking them into the operation theatre.

THE HUMAN MACHINEROBOTICSKNEE REPLACEMENT
PART 1 · WHO HOLDS THE CONTROLS?First, remove the biggest misunderstanding.
EP 1

“The robot is doing the operation?!”

ZappyAjji thinks a robot will cut her bones while the surgeon stands in the corner sipping coffee!
Prof. BoltNot quite. The surgeon plans and performs the operation. The robot is a precision tool—not an independent surgeon.

Think of it this way: the surgeon is the pilot. The robotic system is advanced navigation plus a controlled instrument. Workflows differ between platforms.

EP 2

What does the robot actually do?

ZappyDoes it see the worn-out part and decide how to fix it?
Prof. BoltThe surgeon decides the plan. Depending on the system, scans or live bone landmarks create a 3D map. The surgeon checks the plan and can adjust it.

THE ROBOTIC WORKFLOW

1Map the bone
2Plan implant position
3Check gaps & balance
4Guide bone cuts

Some systems use a pre-operative CT scan; others are image-free. Trackers and registration help the system locate the bones and instruments.

PART 2 · PRECISION IS NOT PERFECTIONWhat the evidence says—and what it does not say.
EP 3

A digital fence around the saw

ZappySo it stops the saw wandering off course?
Prof. BoltIn many semi-active systems, it provides a boundary or haptic resistance around the planned cut. It helps the surgeon stay within the plan—but the surgeon remains responsible.

Real technical advantage: robotic systems generally improve accuracy and consistency of certain measurements and implant-alignment targets.

EP 4

Does precision mean a better-feeling knee?

ZappyIf the cuts are more accurate, surely Ajji will walk better and have no pain?
Prof. BoltThat is the leap we must not make. A better alignment measurement does not automatically guarantee less pain, faster recovery or a happier patient.

THE EVIDENCE SNAPSHOT

WHAT ROBOTS DO BETTER

More accurate execution of planned alignment and fewer alignment outliers in many studies.

WHAT IS NOT PROVEN CONSISTENTLY

A meaningful advantage in pain, function, satisfaction, range of motion or long-term implant survival for every patient.

A 2025 meta-analysis of 21 RCTs (2,692 patients) found fewer mechanical-alignment outliers with robotics, but no significant difference in WOMAC or Oxford Knee Scores at assessed follow-up points.

EP 5

Will I recover faster? Will it cost more?

ZappyCan Ajji walk in half the time because a robot did the surgery?
Prof. BoltDon't buy that promise. Recovery depends on pain control, muscle strength, pre-op fitness, swelling, confidence and rehabilitation—not just the cutting tool.

MYTH: Robotic means painless, scarless and instant recovery.

FACT: It is still major knee replacement surgery. The implant, wound, rehab and recovery responsibilities remain.

Cost question: Equipment, planning, disposables and hospital workflows can add cost. Ask for the actual package difference and what it includes. Extra cost is not a guarantee of a better outcome.

PART 3 · THE FAMILY'S DECISIONGood questions beat glossy advertisements.
EP 6

Five questions Ajji should ask

  1. Why robotics in my case? What problem does it help solve?
  2. Which system? CT-based or image-free, and what does that mean for me?
  3. How experienced is my surgeon? How many cases with this system?
  4. What outcomes do you track? Pain, function, complications and revisions—not just X-ray alignment.
  5. What is the extra cost? What is included? Would conventional surgery also be reasonable?
ZappySo conventional knee replacement is old-fashioned?
Prof. BoltAbsolutely not. Conventional TKA is well-established, with decades of evidence. Skilled surgeons can achieve excellent results without a robot.

Remember: surgeon experience, sound technique, implant choice, infection prevention and rehabilitation matter enormously—robot or no robot.

DEEP DIVE · WHAT DOES NEWER EVIDENCE SAY?

A 2026 GRADE-assessed meta-analysis pooled 24 randomised trials involving 3,425 patients. It found no statistically significant difference in main functional scores (WOMAC, functional Knee Society Score and Oxford Knee Score), pain, satisfaction, range of motion or length of stay. Robotic cases took longer on average—about 22 minutes in the pooled analysis—but variation between studies was very high. The authors rated certainty low to very low, so the exact size of any effect remains uncertain.

A separate 2025 RCT meta-analysis of 21 trials (2,692 patients) found fewer mechanical-alignment outliers with robotic assistance, while patient-reported scores were broadly similar. In short: precision gains are clearer than patient-perceived gains.

Platforms differ, alignment philosophies differ, surgeons have learning curves, and many studies have limited follow-up. Long-term revision and cost-effectiveness need stronger evidence.

PROF. BOLT'S VERDICT · OPINION

Robotic knee replacement is a valuable precision tool—not a magic upgrade. It can help a surgeon execute a carefully chosen plan more consistently. But patients should not be promised a pain-free knee, faster recovery or a longer-lasting implant simply because a robot is involved.

Choose the surgeon, the plan and the care pathway—not the machine name on the billboard. If the surgeon can explain why robotics adds value in your particular knee, and the extra cost is acceptable, it may be reasonable. Conventional surgery remains a sound choice in experienced hands.

THE TAKE-HOME

What should Zappy tell Ajji?

“Ajji, the robot won't replace your surgeon. It helps the surgeon measure and guide the operation. Let's ask why it is useful for your knee, what it costs, and what results the surgeon gets. Then we'll decide together.”

The best knee replacement is not the one with the fanciest machine. It is the one that is appropriate for the patient and performed well—with careful planning, safe surgery and committed rehabilitation.

FRIDGE-DOOR CHEAT SHEET

WHO OPERATES?
The surgeon. The robot assists; it does not independently perform the replacement.
WHAT IMPROVES?
Accuracy and consistency of planned cuts and alignment measurements.
NOT GUARANTEED
Less pain, faster recovery, better satisfaction or longer implant life.
WHAT STILL MATTERS?
Surgeon skill, implant choice, infection prevention, patient factors and rehab.
ASK BEFORE YOU PAY
Why robotics? Which system? Surgeon experience? Extra cost?
BOTTOM LINE
Technology should serve good surgery—not substitute for it.
