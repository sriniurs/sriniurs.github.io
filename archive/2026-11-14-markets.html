What moved the market?

FACT + INTERPRETATION

Early in the week, softer crude oil and renewed overseas buying helped support Indian equities. Reuters reported that Nifty reached an eight-month closing high on Monday. But Friday brought a different pressure: rising oil, a firmer US-rate outlook and a weaker rupee. That combination can challenge Indian importers and market valuations.

On Wednesday, NSE-based market data showed PSU banks (+2.94%) and telecom (+2.38%) among the day's stronger sector indices; IT (-0.53%) was weaker. These are Wednesday snapshots, not weekly sector rankings. India VIX was 17.56 on 11 Nov, a measure of expected volatility rather than market direction. Friday's VIX and a complete weekly sector-return series were not reliably verified, so no weekly winners/losers ranking is asserted.

Mutual funds: the money changes lanes

FACT · AMFI OCTOBER 2026

₹83.39 lakh crore

Industry net assets under management at 31 October, up from ₹82.64 lakh crore at September-end. Monthly SIP contributions were ₹32,160 crore, compared with ₹32,490 crore in September. Open-ended equity funds received ₹32,993.64 crore net inflows, down 13.9% month-on-month. Debt-oriented funds received approximately ₹1.13 lakh crore net inflows, reversing September outflows.

Zappy: If SIP collections dipped, are investors abandoning mutual funds?
Prof. Bolt: No. A 1% monthly change in contributions is not the same as everyone redeeming. Contributions, net flows, folios and AUM each answer a different question.

The other categories matter too

FACT · OCTOBER Arbitrage funds drew about ₹3,929 crore, while balanced advantage funds saw approximately ₹853 crore outflow. Gold ETFs drew ₹3,474 crore and silver ETFs ₹3,092 crore. Overseas fund-of-funds had net outflows of about ₹370 crore. These flows reveal where money moved, not what will outperform.

INVESTOR LENS Large-cap categories tend to hold established businesses but still carry equity risk. Mid/small-cap categories can be more volatile and liquidity-sensitive. Flexi-cap managers can change market-cap exposure; multi-cap funds follow allocation rules. Sectoral/thematic funds concentrate a particular story. Gold/silver exposure responds to commodities and currency; debt funds face interest-rate and credit risks. Balanced advantage funds dynamically adjust equity; arbitrage funds pursue cash–futures spreads rather than conventional equity upside. ELSS carries a statutory lock-in for qualifying investments, but tax benefits depend on the applicable regime.

Source: AMFI category-flow reporting.

Returns: the missing comparison

DATA LIMITATION

A trustworthy short-term, one-year and three-year category-return comparison needs a dated, consistently constructed universe: direct or regular plans, growth options, equal-weighted or AUM-weighted averages, and treatment of new and merged schemes. This week's accessible data establishes flows, not a uniform return series. Therefore no category return averages, top-returning category, weakest-returning category or within-category dispersion percentages are invented.

For a future reproducible comparison, use AMFI historical NAV files with a fixed eligible-scheme universe and equal-weighted point-to-point returns; compare median and 10th–90th percentile dispersion for each horizon. This would be a methodology, not a calculation completed in this issue.

Fund takeaway: Follow the category's job in your portfolio—not last month's popularity. A large inflow can coexist with weak future returns; a temporary outflow does not prove a category is unsuitable.

PART 03

Why oil, the dollar and Nifty travel together

Three moving parts, one Indian investor

Zappy: Why does a barrel of oil abroad affect my Indian investments?
Prof. Bolt: Because India buys large amounts of energy from overseas. Oil is often priced in dollars. When oil rises and the rupee weakens, the same barrel can become more expensive in rupees.

FACT · 13 NOV Reuters reported the rupee at approximately ₹94.24 per US dollar on Friday, with rising oil and expectations of a more hawkish US Federal Reserve pressuring the currency. Its report also noted the 10-year Indian government bond yield near 7.31%. These are dated market observations, not permanent assumptions.

Source: Reuters, 13 Nov 2026.

The ₹ example

ILLUSTRATIVE ASSUMPTION

Imagine an importer buys 1,000 barrels at US$90 each. At ₹90 per dollar, the rupee bill is ₹81,00,000. If oil rises to US$100 and the rupee weakens to ₹94, the bill becomes ₹94,00,000. That's ₹13,00,000 more, or 16.05%, even though the dollar oil price increased only 11.11%.

ScenarioCost for 1,000 barrels
$90 × ₹90₹81.0 lakh
$100 × ₹94₹94.0 lakh
Difference+₹13.0 lakh

This simplified calculation excludes freight, hedges, taxes, refining margins and contract terms. It illustrates a mechanism, not an actual company expense or forecast.

WHOOSH!

How it reaches the stock market

EXPLANATORY CHAIN Higher landed energy costs may pressure inflation and margins → inflation concerns may influence bond yields and interest-rate expectations → financing costs and valuations can change → the Nifty's sector composition determines how much the index reacts. Some exporters may benefit from a weaker rupee, while import-dependent businesses may struggle. There is no one-direction rule for every company.

Global investors also compare India with US yields. When US rates look more attractive, foreign capital can move; when India's earnings prospects improve, the opposite can happen. Neither foreign buying nor selling alone determines tomorrow's market.

Captain Nifty: Three gauges, not one: oil price, rupee exchange rate and bond yields. Watch the combination.

What history teaches—and what it doesn't

DATED CONTEXT In the week ending 13 November 2026, the sequence itself illustrated both sides: early relief from oil and foreign inflows, followed by Friday pressure from oil, US rates and the rupee. The evidence establishes a plausible transmission mechanism; it does not prove how much of the index's move was caused by any one factor.

PRACTICAL INTERPRETATION For a long-horizon investor, the response is not to predict every currency tick. It is to understand why portfolios diversified across categories may behave differently, why imported inflation matters, and why a strong market week can still finish nervously.

The fridge-door line: Oil is the bill. The dollar is the exchange rate. Interest rates are the price of money. Nifty is the scoreboard—not the whole game.
