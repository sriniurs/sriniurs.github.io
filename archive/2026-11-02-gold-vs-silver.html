ZappyWhy would a central bank want gold instead of dollars?

Prof. BoltAfter 2022, countries saw that dollar reserves can be frozen in a dispute. Gold in your own vault can't be switched off by anyone else. That one lesson has kept gold's floor high.

EP 6

Why silver fell off a cliff

Jan 2026: frenzy, record ₹4.2 lakh→US exchange raises margin rules→31 Jan crash: silver ETFs fall up to 20% in a day→Oil spike + rate-hike fears + strong dollar→Oct: below ₹2 lakh

Oil and rates

Higher oil means higher inflation, which means higher interest rates. A metal that pays no interest looks less attractive when deposits pay more.

Factories using less

Solar makers are cutting the silver in each panel to save costs. The Silver Institute expects solar's silver use to drop sharply in 2026.

Silver fell about 17% in a single month to 23 October, against about 2% for gold over three months. That is what "higher risk" means in rupees.

EP 9

Silver's supply puzzle

Silver Institute outlook for 2026

  • 6th deficit year in a row: demand above supply by about 46 million ounces.
  • Mine output up only ~1%, to about 820 million ounces.
  • Factory demand down ~3%, to about 640 million ounces.
  • Coin and bar buying up ~20%, to about 227 million ounces.

Why supply can't just rise

Most silver comes as a by-product of copper, zinc and lead mines. Miners don't dig more just because silver is pricey; they follow copper.

Solar: friend and foe

Solar is a big user of silver, but makers are "thrifting", using less per panel, and some are testing copper. More solar panels no longer means proportionally more silver.

The long-term case for silver (shortage) is real. The short-term risk (factories cutting use, speculators leaving) is also real. That's why it swings so violently.

Why the brakes went on

Precious metals were over 5% of India's imports in FY26, adding to a $333 bn trade deficit and pressure on the rupee. From 16 October, several fund houses paused new money into gold and silver fund-of-funds.

Prof. BoltEvery gram we buy is paid for in dollars. When the whole country buys at once, the rupee feels it. That's why governments like gold bonds and hate gold imports.

Does buying on Dhanteras actually pay?

Over the long run (our arithmetic)

~13%a year

Gold from Dhanteras 2010 (₹19,704) to Dhanteras 2025 (₹1,21,164). Better than most fixed deposits.

Over the next week

In 2025, silver hit ₹1.85 lakh/kg on 17 October and fell to ₹1.50 lakh by 24 October, down ~19% within a week of Dhanteras, once festive demand faded.

Over the next year

Gold bought on Dhanteras 2025 was up about 27% by late August 2026. Silver bought then would be up only modestly today, after a wild ride.

Dhanteras is a good day for tradition and a random day for timing. The return comes from holding for years, not from the date you bought.

WHO WINS, WHENProf. Bolt's verdict, and what to do

EP 12

Prof. Bolt's verdict (opinion)

SAFE!

Gold wins if… wars drag on, the rupee keeps falling, central banks keep buying and the world gets scarier. It's the insurance policy.

ZOOM!

Silver wins if… the economy recovers, rates fall, solar and electronics boom and the shortage bites. It's the high-beta bet.

OUCH!

Both lose if… peace breaks out, oil collapses, the dollar soars and interest rates stay high for years.

Prof. BoltMy view: it's not gold versus silver. Gold is the core, silver is the spice. A typical mix many advisers use is 10–15% of savings in precious metals, mostly gold, with a smaller silver slice for those who can stomach 50% falls.

EP 13

So what should Zappy do?

For the festival

  • Buy a small coin or something you'll wear. Enjoy it.
  • Check the BIS hallmark and the bill: purity, weight, making charges, GST.
  • Compare buy-back rates before you buy.

For investing

  • Use ETFs on the stock exchange, not jewellery or app gold.
  • Buy in steps over months, not in one festive go.
  • Don't buy silver just because it "fell 50%". It can fall further.

ZappySo: small gold coin for Mom, an ETF for me, and no panic?

Prof. BoltExactly. Tradition from the shop, investment from the exchange, patience from yourself.
