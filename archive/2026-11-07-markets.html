FACT + CONTEXT

Why buyers came back

Three forces appeared together: softer crude oil eased India's import and inflation worries; a better earnings tone, especially in large index constituents, helped sentiment; and foreign buying returned. Reuters described the week as India's strongest index advance since February 2021. Financials rose about 10.4% over the week, while all 16 major sector groups gained. Even the weakest broad group was positive: IT was among the laggards in relative terms.

Friday cash-market figures reported by market trackers: FII net buying ₹13,750 crore, DII net selling ₹5,969 crore. Treat these as cash-market session figures, not the same thing as the monthly foreign-portfolio totals or mutual-fund inflows. Reuters weekly market report · Friday cash-market summary.

FACT · VOLATILITY

The unusual companion: rising fear gauge

India VIX ended around 17.47 in the NSE-close-based summaries, up about 12% on Friday. One derivatives tracker quoted 17.58; this is a vendor/timestamp discrepancy, not a reason to pretend the reading is exact across sources. A rising VIX alongside a rising market signals more expensive expected volatility, not a guaranteed fall.

CATEGORY LENS

A category is a job description, not a trophy

Large-cap: typically more established businesses; still equity risk. Mid-cap and small-cap: potential growth but sharper drawdowns, liquidity and valuation risk. Flexi-cap: manager decides the cap mix; multi-cap: mandated allocations across cap sizes create a different risk profile. ELSS: tax-linked equity with lock-in. Sector/thematic: concentrated bets that can lead for one year and lag the next.

Balanced advantage: dynamic equity/debt positioning, with widely different hedging methods. Arbitrage: equity cash–futures spreads rather than ordinary equity appreciation. Gold/silver: commodity exposure with currency and price risks. International: overseas markets plus currency and access constraints. Debt: interest-rate, credit and liquidity risks, not one uniform safety bucket.

Prof. Bolt: Before comparing two categories, ask what risk each is being paid to carry.

OPINION · PRACTICAL

How a long-term investor should read the dashboard

Record SIP collections show persistence of contributions, not immunity from a correction. Heavy small-cap subscriptions may signal investor enthusiasm but cannot tell us whether those shares are cheap. Debt outflows at quarter-end often reflect corporate cash management, not a nationwide rejection of debt investing.

Takeaway: match category to goal and time horizon; compare valuation, concentration, liquidity and costs before recent returns. Avoid treating a monthly inflow league table as a performance ranking.

PART 3 · The ₹10,000 illusion

Why money entering a fund is not the same as money earned by investors.

THE QUESTION

Three numbers that look alike

Zappy: A fund category gets ₹9,000 crore in one month. Does that mean investors made ₹9,000 crore?

Prof. Bolt: No. New subscriptions are cash entering the pool; investment returns are changes in what that pool owns.

FACT AMFI's monthly flow data record subscriptions minus redemptions for a category. AUM is the value of assets at a date. Return measures the change in NAV per unit over time, adjusted appropriately for distributions.

Imagine a bucket: new water poured in is flow; water currently inside is AUM; the rise or fall in the value of each litre is the return. Mixing them up leads to bad headlines and worse decisions.

WORKED EXAMPLE · ESTIMATE

A ₹10,000 SIP during a wild market

Suppose a hypothetical equity fund's NAV is ₹100 in month one, ₹80 in month two and ₹100 in month three. A ₹10,000 SIP buys 100, 125 and 100 units respectively. Total investment: ₹30,000. Units owned: 325. Final value at NAV ₹100: ₹32,500, or ₹2,500 above invested amount.

ESTIMATE This simplified illustration ignores fees, taxes, exit loads and exact cash-flow dates; ₹2,500/₹30,000 = 8.33% is a simple holding-period gain on contributions, not an annualised XIRR. A SIP does not guarantee profit: if the final NAV were ₹70, these same 325 units would be worth only ₹22,750.

FACT + HISTORY

Why September's numbers tell two stories

In September 2026, investors contributed a record ₹32,490 crore through SIPs while equity categories recorded ₹38,307 crore of net inflows. At the same time, industry AUM fell to ₹82.64 lakh crore. The apparent paradox disappears when we remember that fund flows, asset prices and withdrawals from other categories all affect total AUM.

The longer historical lesson: in every market cycle, investor behaviour and returns diverge. A record inflow can occur near a market high, a market low, or in the middle. It is a measure of behaviour, not a timing signal. AMFI monthly note archive.

OPINION · VERDICT

The discipline worth keeping

Prof. Bolt's verdict: follow the process, not the crowd's cash register. A strong market week is a reason to revisit your risk exposure, not to rewrite your long-term goal. A weak month is a reason to check liquidity needs, not automatically to stop a suitable SIP.

Fridge-door line: “Money going into a category tells you what investors are doing. NAV tells you what investments are doing. Neither predicts tomorrow.”

Three things to remember: ₹32,490 crore SIP contributions are gross monthly flows; ₹38,307 crore equity inflows are net category flows; ₹82.64 lakh crore AUM is the month-end stock of assets. They are not interchangeable.
