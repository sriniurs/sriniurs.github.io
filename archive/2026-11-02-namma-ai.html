Namma Bengaluru, Namma AI

A comic about how Karnataka went from a city of science labs and pensioners to the world's back office, and now into the AI age. A curious young learner asks, and a wise mentor who has watched it since the 1980s answers: where the money came from, where it is now, and where it may go.

THE PASTHow Bengaluru got its brain

EP 1

Science city before software

ZappyBengaluru was just a sleepy garden city before Infosys, no?

Prof. BoltSleepy, yes. Empty-headed, no. The Mysuru kings and Sir M. Visvesvaraya planted the seeds a century ago: dams, power, engineering colleges. IISc came in 1909.

The PSU era After Independence, the government parked its high-tech bets here.

IISc+HAL+BEL+ISRO+ITI, BHEL

Thousands of engineers, a cool climate and cheap land. "Pensioners' paradise" was also a quiet talent pool.

Talent came first, the money followed. Every boom since then has been built on that same pile of engineers.

EP 2

1985: a dish on the roof

1983 Infosys shifts base from Pune to Bengaluru. Wipro is already moving from vegetable oil to computers.

1985 Texas Instruments opens a design centre and beams code to the US by satellite dish. Phone lines were too slow.

BEEP-BEEP!

Prof. BoltPeople laughed. Who sends software through the sky? But the logic was simple: an American engineer cost ten times an Indian one, and the work didn't need a passport.

1 dish→1 industry
EP 3

The outsourcing gold rush

1990s India opens up in 1991. Software parks, Electronic City and the ITPL tech park in Whitefield fill up.

1997 Karnataka writes India's first state IT policy.

Y2K The world panics about the year 2000 bug. Bengaluru fixes the code and gets the trust.

ZappySo that's how my uncle went from a scooter to a villa in 15 years.

Prof. BoltThat's the model: sell hours of engineers. More clients, more hires, more salaries, more flats in Whitefield. It worked for 30 years.

US client→Hire 1,000 freshers→PGs, cabs, darshinis→Real-estate boom

Remember this chain. "Sell engineer hours" is exactly what AI now does cheaper.

EP 6

Back office → brain office

GCC = Global Capability Centre. A foreign company's own in-house tech and research arm in India. Not a vendor. The company itself.

900+GCCs

Counts vary by report (875 to 1,100+), but every list puts Bengaluru first in the world. About 35% of India's GCC workforce sits in Karnataka.

ZappyWhy does this matter? A job is a job.

Prof. BoltIn my time, Bengaluru did the work Americans designed. Now banks, chipmakers and retailers build their AI models here. Owner's work, not tenant's work.

State GCC policy target: 500 more centres, past 1,000 by 2029, with 3.5 lakh new jobs. Some incentives only for centres outside Bengaluru.

EP 7

Then AI walked into the office

CUT!

India's biggest IT firm cut about 12,000 jobs in FY26, mostly middle and senior managers. Its chairman says AI agents may one day equal its human headcount.

IT stocks fell sharply through 2025–26. Fresher offers shrank, and most of what's left asks for AI skills.

ZappyBut the same news says AI hiring is booming in Bengaluru!

Prof. BoltBoth are true. Companies are still hiring. They just need fewer people per project. The old pyramid had 100 freshers under 10 managers. The new one has 20 sharp people and a lot of AI.

The domino, again Remember the EP 3 chain? Run it backwards.

Fewer fresher hires→Empty PGs in Marathahalli→Darshini sells fewer idlis→Cab driver loses rides→Flat sales slow

Bengaluru's economy is not just techies. It is everyone who feeds, drives and houses techies.

THE FUTUREThe state's plan, and Prof. Bolt's guesses

EP 9

The government's big bets

IT Policy 2025–30

₹4.09L cr→₹11.5L cr

Software export target by 2030, nearly triple. IT's share of the state economy to rise from 26% to 36%.

The AI kit (announced 2026)

  • AI University: India's first government-led one, 100 acres in Bengaluru, regional campuses in Mysuru, Mangaluru, Hubballi-Dharwad, Belagavi, Kalaburagi.
  • Two green data centres: one coastal, one near Bengaluru.
  • Responsible AI policy: drafted by a committee led by Kris Gopalakrishnan.
  • Bengaluru Tech Summit 2026: 17–19 Nov, theme "AI & Beyond".

Startups beyond Bengaluru

Startup Policy 2025–30 targets 25,000 new startups, with at least 10,000 outside Bengaluru. Deep-tech grants of up to ₹1 crore for AI, chips, quantum and robotics.

The direction is right: push the work to tier-2 cities, and from services to products.

EP 10

AI is thirsty

GLUG GLUG

Data centres eat electricity and drink water for cooling. Bengaluru ran dry in the summer of 2024 and still gets its water pumped uphill from the Kaveri.

ZappySo the coastal data centre isn't random.

Prof. BoltNo. Sea air, sea water and space. Mangaluru and Udupi could gain. The traffic jam on Outer Ring Road is also an AI problem: talent wastes 2 hours a day in it.

Picks and shovels Whoever wins the AI race, someone must supply:

Power+Water+Land+Cables+Chips

Karnataka's real AI test is infrastructure, not talent.

~$51,000→~$53,000

FY25 to FY26. Each worker now brings in about 4% more. That's what "AI productivity" looks like on a balance sheet.

AI's own slice

$10–12 bn

Nasscom's estimate of AI-led revenue in FY26, about 3–4% of the industry. Over 20 lakh people were trained in AI skills during the year.

Prof. BoltFor 30 years, Bengaluru's rule was simple: 10% more business meant roughly 10% more people. That link is breaking. Revenue now grows nearly three times faster than jobs. Every PG owner, cab driver and flat builder in the city should notice that number.

Bengaluru isn't losing companies. It's at risk of losing the next expansion. That's how decline starts: slowly, at the margin.

EP 13

The traffic tax

TomTom Traffic Index 2025

36 minper 10 km

Two minutes worse than 2024. Rush-hour average speed: about 14 km/h, slower than a bicycle.

What 168 hours means (our arithmetic)

168 hours lost=7 full days=~21 eight-hour workdays

A month of working time, every year, for every rush-hour commuter.

HONK!

Only Mexico City ranked worse in the world. In 15 minutes of evening rush, a Bengaluru driver covers just 2.5 km.

Prof. BoltEconomists call this a hidden tax. Nobody pays it at a counter, but every company pays it in tired staff, late meetings and people quitting for remote jobs. It's Bengaluru's biggest competitive weakness, and AI can't fix it.

Each Finance Commission sets how the Centre's tax pool is shared among states for five years.

Why it fell

The formula gives more to poorer states. Because Bengaluru makes Karnataka rich per head, the state's share was cut by about 23% in 2020. The state estimates it lost over ₹80,000 crore in that period.

The 16th Commission raised it to 4.13% from April 2026; the state says it still loses ₹10,000–15,000 crore a year compared with the old share.

The infrastructure ask

₹1.15 lakh cr

What Karnataka asked the 16th Finance Commission for, for Bengaluru's infrastructure alone.

This is the core tension: the city that earns the money needs huge investment in roads, metro and water, but the national formula is built to send money to poorer states. Both goals are fair; they pull against each other.

EP 16

Bengaluru 2035: three endings

BOOM!

The good ending. GCCs move from support to building AI products. AI University and tier-2 cities fill with startups. Mysuru and Hubballi become the new Whitefield. Karnataka exports AI tools, not engineer hours.

HMM…

The likely ending. Bengaluru stays the AI capital, richer but smaller per head. Top earners do brilliantly, mid-level coders get squeezed, and the north still lags. GDP up, jobs flat.

CRASH!

The bad ending. AI swallows services jobs faster than products appear. Water and traffic push GCCs to Hyderabad and Chennai. PGs empty, IT-belt property drops, state taxes shrink.

Prof. BoltThese are my guesses, not forecasts. But I've seen this city reinvent itself three times: PSUs, outsourcing, GCCs. Every time, the people who learned the new thing early were fine. The ones who said "my company will look after me" were not.

EP 17

So what should Zappy do?

AI + something beats AI alone.

  • AI + banking
  • AI + farming (Karnataka is a coffee, ragi and silk state)
  • AI + medicine and hospitals
  • AI + aerospace and chips

Pure coders compete with the machine. Domain people use the machine.

ZappyShould I leave Bengaluru?

Prof. BoltNot yet. The best AI jobs in India are still here. But don't stretch for a ₹1.5 crore flat in the IT belt on one tech salary. Keep your EMIs light so a layoff is a setback, not a disaster.

Watch the jobs, not the headlines. If GCCs keep hiring in tier-2 cities and AI University graduates get placed, the good ending is coming.

THE END

Fridge-door cheat sheet

Past

  • IISc 1909, then HAL, BEL, ISRO: talent came first.
  • 1985 satellite dish → outsourcing.
  • 1997: India's first state IT policy.

Present

  • ₹33 lakh crore economy, 8% growth.
  • Income per head ~2x India's.
  • No.1 software exporter, world's GCC capital.
  • AI = fewer people per project. Freshers hit first.
  • Bengaluru rich, the north still poor.

Deep dive

  • Tech revenue +6.1%, jobs +2.3%: growth no longer means hiring.
  • Bengaluru leads leasing (13.1 m sq ft in H1) but rents +11.5%.
  • 168 hours a year lost in traffic ≈ 21 workdays.
  • Tax share: 4.71% → 3.64% → 4.13%.
  • 2030 export target needs ~23% growth a year vs 6% today.

Future

  • Target: triple software exports by 2030.
  • AI University, green data centres, 10,000 startups outside Bengaluru.
  • Biggest risks: water, power, traffic.
  • Your move: AI + a domain, and light EMIs.
