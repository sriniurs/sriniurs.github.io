India’s CNAP rule took effect on 1 November for 4G-and-newer mobile networks. But the name on your screen is a clue about the SIM record—not a guarantee about the person, the call or its safety.

Future Is HereCNAP4G & 5GCaller Safety

The phone rings. The number isn’t saved. A name may now appear too. That is CNAP — Calling Name Presentation, a network-level caller-name service. The Department of Telecommunications ordered operators to introduce it from 1 November 2026 on 4G-and-newer wireless networks. Important distinction: an order’s start date is not proof that every operator, handset and cross-network call is already displaying names perfectly.

PART 1 · THE NAME ON THE SCREENFrom an unknown number to a name you can inspect
EP 01

“Unknown number” gets a makeover

ZAPPY“So an unsaved number calls me… and my phone may show the caller’s name too?”
PROF. BOLT“Exactly. It’s called CNAP — Calling Name Presentation. For an unsaved domestic call on a supported network, it can show the name in the originating operator’s subscriber data record, beside the number. That is the registered subscriber name—not necessarily the person holding the phone.”
The important bit: it is the operator’s subscriber record—not a crowd-sourced nickname, and not a live face-check of the person speaking.
EP 02

It’s a network service, not another app

ZAPPY“Does it rummage through my contacts or need a special download?”
PROF. BOLT“No separate caller-ID app is required for the network service. The originating operator maintains a number-to-name record. The receiving network gets the caller-name information and presents it during the incoming call.”

PART 2 · THE PROMISE & THE CATCHHelpful caller identification is not the same as fraud-proof calling
EP 04

Why did India want this?

ZAPPY“Is this mainly to stop those fake bank and parcel calls?”
PROF. BOLT“The aim is to give people a better clue before answering an unknown call. TRAI’s recommendation was intended to help subscribers make an informed decision and reduce harassment from unknown or spam callers. But CNAP is caller identification—not a proven cure for fraud. The latest reporting has not produced evidence that it eliminates scams.”
Remember: A displayed name tells you what is recorded against a connection. It does not prove the caller’s authority, honesty or reason for calling.
EP 05

CNAP versus Truecaller: different sources

ZAPPY“Then is Truecaller finished?”
PROF. BOLT“Not necessarily. They solve related problems using different information sources. One is based on telecom subscriber records; the other is an app-based caller identification service with features such as community labels and spam reporting.”

CNAP adds a network-record name. It does not automatically add a spam score, community warning or call-blocking feature.
EP 06

A genuine name can still be a scam

ZAPPY“If the registered name looks genuine, can the caller still lie?”
PROF. BOLT“Of course. Imagine a person calling from their own properly registered SIM and claiming to be from your bank. CNAP can show the SIM’s recorded name; it cannot verify the caller’s job, authority or story. The rules require caller-ID protection against tampering, but that still isn’t a trust certificate.”
Red flag: “Your account will be blocked in 10 minutes—tell me the OTP now.” Hang up. Contact the bank using the number on its official app, card or website.
PART 3 · THE DETAILS THAT MATTERWhat the official directions say — and what they leave open
EP 07

Who gets CNAP first—and is it live everywhere?

ZAPPY“The date was 1 November. So every 4G phone in India must show a name now?”
PROF. BOLT“The DoT instructed all access-service providers to introduce CNAP across service areas from 1 November 2026 on wireless networks using 4G and newer technologies. It says 2G, 3G and landline networks will be considered separately.”
Reality check · 5 November: The start date is official. But reporting published on 2 November found no public confirmation of a uniform nationwide switch-on; cross-operator display had not been independently verified. So the name may not appear on every call yet. A missing name alone does not prove your handset is faulty.
EP 08

Privacy: what the rule does—and doesn’t—say

ZAPPY“Does the receiving network keep a permanent copy of every caller name it looks up?”
PROF. BOLT“The DoT requires a security-by-design approach: the query-response mechanism itself must prevent the calling-name identity from being retained after the incoming call is completed. That is a specific safeguard—not a promise that every ordinary telecom record or subscriber database disappears.”
And what about opting out? The 13 October 2026 circular says operators must provide CNAP to each user without a separate request. It expressly says names of subscribers using CLIR will not be presented. It does not spell out a separate routine opt-out process for ordinary subscribers.
EP 09

What if the name is wrong?

ZAPPY“My father bought the SIM, but I use it. Will his name appear?”
PROF. BOLT“It can. CNAP reads the subscriber record, not a live identity check. A family or employer connection may therefore show the registered subscriber’s name rather than the person holding the handset.”
How to complain: The DoT requires every operator to receive, record, maintain and redress CNAP grievances through its grievance mechanism in a time-bound manner. Start with the operator that holds the number’s subscriber record. Keep the number, date, time and a screenshot if useful.
Important fine print: The circular does not specify a fixed number of days or a separate CNAP helpline. Use your operator’s official complaint route and ask for a complaint/reference number.
EP 10

Can a business show its brand name?

ZAPPY“Will a delivery company’s call show a random director’s name?”
PROF. BOLT“The DoT allows business or bulk-connection holders to seek a preferred name instead of the subscriber-record name. It must be either a trademark registered in the user’s name or the user’s trade name registered on the GST portal—and the operator must verify the documents first. It is not a free-for-all nickname field.”
A verified business name can help customers recognise legitimate organisations — but it still does not authenticate the individual caller.
THE DEEP DIVE · FOUR THINGS TO KNOWThe mechanics, the exceptions and the limits
EP 11

The two-network handshake

1 · Originating operator: keeps the subscriber-name record mapped to the caller’s number.

2 · Receiving operator: retrieves the calling-name identity for presentation to the person receiving the call.

3 · Privacy safeguard: the query-response mechanism must not retain that calling-name identity after the incoming call is completed.
ZAPPY“So my operator asks the caller’s operator for the name?”
PROF. BOLT“That is the basic architecture. The originating network owns the subscriber record; the terminating network retrieves it for the incoming call. Both sides must have the network plumbing working properly.”
EP 12

Why saved contacts still win

Display priority: your phone’s own saved contact name comes first. If the number is not saved, the operator’s recorded name is used for domestic calls. For an unsaved international call, the display is “International Call”.
ZAPPY“So if I saved the plumber as ‘Super Plumber’, that name stays?”
PROF. BOLT“Yes. CNAP does not replace the name you assigned in your own contacts.”
EP 13

Why might no name appear?

Possible reasons:
• The number is saved, so your own contact name takes priority.
• It is an unsaved international call, shown as “International Call”.
• The caller uses CLIR, so the calling name is not presented.
• The call uses 2G/3G or a landline, which is outside the initial 1 November scope.
• Operator rollout, cross-network delivery or handset support may not yet be uniform.
ZAPPY“So a blank name doesn’t automatically mean a scam—or a broken phone?”
PROF. BOLT“Correct. First check the type of call and network. The rollout instruction is clear; universal real-world display is a separate question.”
EP 14

What CNAP cannot promise

MYTH: “A name on the screen means the caller is genuine.”
FACT: It shows the name in the operator’s subscriber record under the DoT rules. It does not verify the person speaking, their job, their bank authority or their intention.
MYTH: “CNAP will block spam and identify every internet call.”
FACT: CNAP is a caller-name presentation feature. The circular does not promise spam scoring, automatic blocking or identification of every WhatsApp/internet call.
NAME ≠ TRUST
EP 15

Prof. Bolt’s verdict

THE GOOD NEWSA network-supplied subscriber name can make some unfamiliar domestic calls easier to assess—without a separate caller-ID app or sign-up.
WATCH OUTThe record may name a parent or employer, the rollout may not be uniform yet, and the complaint rule gives no published fixed deadline. A name is not a fraud shield.
MY TAKE · OPINIONCNAP is a useful upgrade to India’s caller-identification system. But its real value will depend on accurate records, dependable inter-operator delivery and a grievance process that actually corrects mistakes. Treat the name as one extra clue—not permission to trust.
EP 16

So what should Zappy do?

ZAPPY“I’ll look at the name—but what should I do if it looks wrong?”
PROF. BOLT“Use it as a clue. Then verify the story independently.”
✓ If your own number shows the wrong subscriber name, contact your operator through its official customer-care/grievance channel and ask how to correct the subscriber record.
✓ If the SIM is registered to a parent or employer, remember that their name may appear; ask the operator about the proper ownership/KYC update process.
✓ If a business wants a preferred display name, check the trademark/GST eligibility rules with the operator.
✓ Never share OTPs, UPI PINs, passwords or card details with an incoming caller—even if a familiar name appears.
✓ For banks, hospitals, couriers or government offices, end the call and contact the organisation using a number you independently trust.
RING ≠ OBEY!
