Trin Trin Goes Silent: Why Mysuru Lost Its Green Bicycles

On 1 November, India's first city-wide public bicycle service stopped after nine years. The same week, a Mysuru cycle track was dug up for road works. A curious young learner who rode Trin Trin to college asks what went wrong. A wise mentor who has watched many good ideas come and go explains.

WHAT HAPPENEDThe last ride

EP 1

The bell rings for the last time

31 October 2026

Last day

MyByk Trin Trin, run by Greenpedia Bike Share Pvt Ltd, stopped after 31 October. Its docking stations are already being taken down across the city.

Why, officially

Company staff told Star of Mysore it "could no longer bear the continuing operational losses", even though people liked it. The city manager called it a temporary suspension.

ZappyI rode those green cycles to Manasagangotri! If people liked it, how did it lose money?

Prof. BoltBeing liked and paying the bills are two different things, Zappy. Let's start at the beginning.

EP 2

2017: India goes first

Launched 4 June 2017

India's first city-wide public bicycle sharing system, planned by Karnataka's Directorate of Urban Land Transport (DULT) and Mysuru City Corporation (MCC).

Who paid to start it

~₹20 crore

Funded partly by a World Bank-managed Global Environment Facility (GEF) grant, with MCC and the state.

How it worked

Sign up on the app→Unlock a cycle at any dock→Ride→Drop it at the dock nearest you

450 cycles across 48–52 docks at launch: Palace, railway station, bus stand, Kukkarahalli Lake and more.

EP 4

Same week: a cycle track disappears

Vishwamanava Double Road

The cycle track was dug up for "white-topping", which means laying a concrete layer over the road. Residents called it taxpayers' money down the drain.

Mysuru's cycle tracks

8.72 km

Built by MCC. By mid-2025, vendors had removed the bollards on parts of it, and two-wheelers were parking on it (Down To Earth).

CRUNCH!

MCC Commissioner M.K. Savitha says the track will be rebuilt after the road work is done.

WHY IT MATTERSWhat really went wrong

EP 5

The money leak

Few cycles, few docks→Each cycle used only ~2 times a day→Low fees + theft and repairs→Trucks to move cycles between docks→Costs beat income

The price puzzle

The first ride was free in 2017. When a ₹5 fee came in from December 2017, users fell by half, the then Deputy Commissioner said. Free 30-minute rides were brought back.

By 2025 (Down To Earth)

  • ₹30 for the first hour, then ₹10 an hour
  • ₹99 a day, ₹499 a week
  • ₹1,699 a month

The vandal problem

Star of Mysore reported rising theft, stripped parts and abandoned cycles as a "serious threat" to the service.

Prof. BoltBike-share is like a bus network. If the next dock is too far, people don't trust it. If they don't trust it, they don't use it. Then each cycle earns too little.

The plan that never came

Phase 2 was meant to double the fleet to 1,000 cycles and 100 docks. In 2025, the fleet was still about 500.

EP 7

India's bike-share graveyard

Ofo (2018)

The Chinese giant launched in 7 Indian cities in January 2018 and shut its India operations by July, despite raising $866 million that March.

Mobike, Zoomcar PEDL

Mobike put 3,000 cycles in Pune in 2018, then its India head left. Zoomcar's PEDL cycles shut in 2019.

Bengaluru

About three years after the public scheme launched, Deccan Herald said bicycles were "extinct" from its roads. Yulu switched from cycles to electric scooters in 2019.

Trin Trin outlived almost all of them. Nine years is a long run in India's bike-share world.

ITDP Bikeshare Planning Guide, UNC and Tucson feasibility study. This share is called "farebox recovery".

The running cost (ITDP)

Roughly US$900–3,500 per cycle per year for repairs, staff, software and trucks to move cycles between docks.

The rule

ITDP says most publicly funded dock-based systems don't cover their running costs from fees alone, just like most bus and metro systems.

Prof. BoltNobody asks a city bus to make a profit. Somehow we expected a cycle to. (Opinion)

EP 9

The Indian maths, worked out

Pune's 2017 plan (WRI India)

  • First-year running cost: ₹12–17 crore
  • First-year fare income: ~₹4 crore
  • Gap per cycle: ₹27,000+ in year 1, about ₹16,000 by year 7

Apply that to Trin Trin (our rough estimate)

~500 cycles×₹16,000–27,000 gap=₹80 lakh–1.35 crore a year

Using Pune's numbers as a stand-in; Trin Trin's own accounts aren't public.

Put in context

That yearly gap is less than 7% of the ~₹20 crore Mysuru spent to start the system (our arithmetic).

Building it was the expensive part. Keeping it alive was the cheap part nobody budgeted for.

Most vehicles are two-wheelers. Bars not to scale for cycles.

Short trips everywhere

About 35% of vehicle trips in India are under 5 km, and most bike-share rides are about 2 km (WRI India). That's exactly cycle distance.

The bus picture

Mysuru has 438 diesel city buses, with 160 electric buses planned under the PM e-Bus Sewa scheme. Shared cycles were meant to be the last link from bus stop to door.

EP 11

What the world's winners did differently

Dense networks

Copenhagen has 13 times Mysuru's cycles per sq km, so each cycle is used about 7 times a day, not 2.

Safe lanes first

A city commissioner told Down To Earth in 2025 that cycle infrastructure must come first. Mysuru has under 9 km of tracks, and some are blocked or dug up.

Steady public money

ITDP says gaps are usually filled by city funds, sponsors and advertising, often money raised from parking and car charges.

ZappySo Trin Trin didn't fail because Mysureans hate cycling?

Prof. BoltNo. 20,000 people signed up. It failed because it stayed small, had few safe lanes and was asked to pay for itself.

WHAT NEXTProf. Bolt's three endings, and Zappy's plan

EP 12

Three endings for Trin Trin (opinion)

TRIN!

Comeback: MCC finds a new operator with steady yearly support, adds e-cycles and more docks near bus stops, and rebuilds the tracks properly.

HMM.

Private only: the city lets app-based e-scooter or e-cycle firms take over, the way Bengaluru did. Convenient, but pricier and less "public".

SILENCE.

Gone for good: the docks rust, the ₹20 crore start-up money is written off, and cycling stays a weekend hobby.

Prof. BoltMy bet: a smaller e-cycle comeback within a year or two, if the city treats it like a bus service, not a business. The temporary tag gives some hope.

EP 13

So what should Zappy do?

For your commute

  • For trips under 5 km, a cycle costs almost nothing to run.
  • Pair a cycle with the city bus for longer trips.
  • Lock it well; theft is a real problem.

As a citizen

  • Ask your ward councillor or MCC to rebuild the cycle tracks properly, as promised.
  • Report vendors or parking on cycle lanes.
  • If a new bike-share comes, use it and give feedback in the first months.

ZappySo I buy an old cycle, and I nag MCC about the lanes?

Prof. BoltExactly. Cities build what people ask for loudly.
