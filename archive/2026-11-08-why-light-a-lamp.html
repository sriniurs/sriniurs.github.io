Why Light a Lamp on the Darkest Night?

A diya at the door. A kingdom coming home. A goddess at midnight. And a new year in a ledger. How did one festival collect so many stories?

The Curious FilesDeepavaliMany traditionsOne flame
TODAY IS SPECIAL! Sunday, 8 November 2026 is Deepavali / Diwali in India. This edition explores why different communities light up the same season.

PART 1: ONE SMALL FLAMEThe question at the doorstep

EP 1

A city without a switch

Imagine every street light off, but a single earthen lamp glowing at your doorstep. Why does that tiny flame feel bigger than a thousand bulbs?

ZappyIs Deepavali simply the birthday of fireworks?
Prof. BoltNo! The name points to rows of lamps. Different communities tell different stories about why they shine.
A lamp is a symbol—not one universal origin.
EP 2

The name hides the clue

Deepavali comes from words meaning a row of lamps. Diwali is a common shorter form. The festival is celebrated across India, but its stories and rituals differ by region.

ZappySo there is no single script everyone follows?
Prof. BoltExactly. Think of it as a grand anthology, not one chapter.
EP 3

North: the homecoming

A widely told North Indian tradition links the lights to Rama returning to Ayodhya after exile. Homes are lit to welcome him.

ZappyWas that the same story everywhere?
Prof. BoltNo. It is one beloved interpretation among several.
EP 4

South: the dawn of victory

In parts of southern India, Naraka Chaturdashi and Krishna’s defeat of Narakasura are central. The early oil bath and fresh clothes matter as much as evening lamps.

ZappySo some families celebrate before sunrise?
Prof. BoltYes. Festival time can begin at dawn, not just after sunset.

PART 2: MANY INDIASStories across communities

EP 5

West: a fresh ledger

For many traders and Gujarati families, the festival season also marks a new accounting year, with prayers for prosperity and the opening of new books.

ZappyDoes lighting a lamp mean hoping for wealth?
Prof. BoltSometimes—but gratitude, generosity and a fresh beginning are just as important.
EP 6

East: the midnight goddess

In Bengal and some eastern traditions, Kali Puja shares the new-moon night with Diwali. The imagery, worship and meaning are distinct.

ZappySame date, different deity?
Prof. BoltOften yes. India’s calendar holds more than one story at a time.
EP 7

Beyond one religion

Jain traditions associate this season with Mahavira’s nirvana. Sikh communities mark Bandi Chhor Divas, remembering Guru Hargobind’s release. Some Buddhist communities have their own observances.

ZappyThen one flame can carry several histories?
Prof. BoltPrecisely. Similar lights do not erase different beliefs.
EP 8

The darkest-night paradox

The main Lakshmi Puja night falls around the new moon, when the moon is absent or barely visible. That is why the contrast of a lamp against darkness is so striking.

ZappyA tiny flame matters more when the sky is dark?
Prof. BoltAnd that is why the metaphor travels so well.

PART 3: THE CALENDARWhy dates and rituals differ

EP 9

Why the date moves

Deepavali follows a lunisolar calendar rather than a fixed Gregorian date. Tithis depend on the angular relationship between Sun and Moon.

ZappySo the festival can shift by weeks from year to year?
Prof. BoltYes. An extra lunar month in some years can move it substantially.
TICK... TITHI... TOCK!
EP 10

The five-day puzzle

Dhanteras, Naraka Chaturdashi, Lakshmi Puja, Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj are often grouped together, but regional calendars and observances vary.

ZappyWhy does my cousin celebrate on a different day?
Prof. BoltBecause the relevant ritual, sunrise, tithi and local custom may differ.

DEEP DIVEFour things worth checking

EP 11

DEEP DIVE: the 2026 date

India Post’s 2026 holiday calendar lists Deepavali on Sunday, 8 November. Many panchang calculations place the new-moon tithi from late morning 8 November into 9 November.

ZappyShould we copy Delhi’s puja time in Mysuru?
Prof. BoltNo. Exact muhurat depends on location and tradition; consult your local panchang.
EP 12

DEEP DIVE: why earthen lamps?

Traditional diyas are simple vessels of clay holding oil or ghee and a cotton wick. Their materials, repeated lighting and household placement make the ritual accessible and visible.

ZappyIs there a scientific reason a lamp feels calming?
Prof. BoltA warm, small light can shape atmosphere, but spiritual meanings are cultural—not laboratory findings.
EP 13

DEEP DIVE: the air question

Fireworks add bursts of particulate pollution and noise. Local rules vary; smoke can especially trouble people with respiratory illness, young children and older adults.

ZappyDoes celebration have to mean louder fireworks?
Prof. BoltNot at all. Lamps, food, music and shared time can carry the festival without the smoke.
Watch-out: follow local fire safety rules and restrictions. Never place diyas near flammable materials.
EP 14

DEEP DIVE: the many meanings

The same festival season is associated with Rama, Krishna, Lakshmi, Kali, Mahavira and Sikh history in different communities. Describing all of them as one identical origin would flatten living traditions.

ZappySo the best answer is not 'which story is true for everyone'?
Prof. BoltRight. Ask which community and which observance you are learning about.

PART 4: THE TAKEAWAYWhat the light leaves behind

EP 15

The flame you take home

Light a lamp safely on a stable, non-flammable surface. Keep it away from curtains and children; never leave it unattended. Then share a meal or kindness with someone.

ZappySo what should I remember when the lamps go out?
Prof. BoltThat the festival is not only about light you can see. It is also about what you choose to illuminate for others.

PROF. BOLT'S OPINIONThree-panel verdict

MY OPINION: THE BEST PART

The beauty of Deepavali is not that everyone tells the same story. It is that many stories can share a season of light.

WHAT I WOULD AVOID

Reducing every regional and religious tradition to a single neat explanation—or equating celebration with noise and smoke.

THE VERDICT

Keep the lamp. Keep the curiosity. Make room for another family’s story.

SO WHAT SHOULD ZAPPY DO?A simple celebration checklist

Ask an elder which Deepavali story your family grew up with. Ask a friend from another region how their home celebrates. Light a diya safely, share a sweet, and discover how many ways India understands light.
