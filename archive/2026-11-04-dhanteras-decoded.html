Dhanteras Decoded: A Broom, a Lamp and 7 Gold Myths

Dhanteras falls on Friday, 6 November. Before the big shopping day, Zappy has questions. Why does India buy a broom? Why does one lamp face south? And is that shiny gold coin really a good deal? Prof. Bolt has the old stories and the new bill.

PART 1: THE STORIESWhy we shop on this one day

EP 1

Wait, it's Friday?

ZappyLast year Dhanteras was in October. This year everyone says November. Which is it?

Prof. BoltThe festival follows the moon, not the calendar on your wall. This year it is Friday, 6 November.

Why so late this year?

2026 has an extra lunar month (called Adhik Maas). So Dhanteras and Diwali come about three weeks later than in 2025, when Dhanteras was on 18 October.

"Dhanteras" means the 13th day (teras) of wealth (dhan). It falls on the 13th day of the waning moon.

The five days, 2026

  • 🪔 Fri 6 Nov: Dhanteras
  • 🛁 Sat 7 Nov: Naraka Chaturdashi
  • ✨ Sun 8 Nov: Deepavali, Lakshmi Puja
  • 🐄 Mon 9 Nov: Govardhan Puja / Bali Padyami
  • 👫 Tue 10 Nov: Bhai Dooj

Next year it moves again: Dhanteras 2027 falls on 27 October.

EP 2

The prince, the snake and the shining door

The story, as it is told

King Hima's 16-year-old son was fated to die of a snakebite on the fourth night after his wedding.

That night his clever bride piled her gold and jewels at the door and lit lamps everywhere. She kept him awake with songs and stories.

DAZZLE!

Yama, the god of death, came as a serpent. The glitter of lamps and gold blinded him. He sat on the heap of jewels, listening to her songs all night, and slipped away at dawn.

The prince lived. That is one reason homes light lamps and bring in gold or metal on this day.

ZappyIs that why Ajji lights one lamp facing south?

Prof. BoltYes, the Yama Deepam. South is Yama's direction, so lamps never face south all year. On Dhanteras evening, one does, as a prayer against untimely death.

EP 3

Why a broom? Why steel and brass pots?

The humble broom

In tradition, Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, enters a clean home. A new broom on Dhanteras means sweeping out the old year and making room for the new.

Some families also say: never lend your broom on Dhanteras. You'd be lending your luck!

The pots and vessels

The day is also Dhanvantari Jayanti. In the churning of the ocean, Dhanvantari, the god of Ayurveda, rose holding a pot of amrit, the nectar of life.

A popular explanation: that is why families buy a new vessel, in brass, copper, silver or steel.

WAIT, REALLY?

From 2016, India marked National Ayurveda Day on Dhanteras. Because the date kept moving, a government notice of March 2025 fixed it to 23 September every year.

EP 4

Karnataka does it with water

ZappyIn our house nobody says "Dhanteras". Amma just scrubs every pot and fills water!

Prof. BoltThat is Neeru Tumbuva Habba, the water-filling festival. Many Karnataka homes keep it on the very same day.

What families do

  • Bathrooms, wash areas and water vessels are cleaned.
  • The big copper or brass pot, the hande, is decorated and filled with fresh water.
  • It warms through the night for the early-morning oil bath on Naraka Chaturdashi.

In the Malnad hills

Families fetch water from rivers and wells. Some Halakki homes fill a new clay pot with water and healing tree barks, honouring a goddess of medicinal herbs.

Same idea as the broom and Dhanvantari: clean, fresh and healthy before the lights.

PART 2: AT THE COUNTER7 myths that quietly cost you money

EP 5

Myth 1: "₹10,000 still buys a nice coin"

I've saved ₹10,000. That gets me a decent gold coin, right?

On 3 Nov 2026, 24-carat gold was ₹1,39,640 per 10 grams. ₹10,000 now buys about 0.7 gram of it.

2016: 3.5 g→2020: 2.1 g→2026: 0.7 g

What ₹10,000 bought, using yearly average prices, before GST. Our arithmetic.

VERDICT: MYTH

Your ₹10,000 buys one-fifth of what it did in 2016. That is why coins of 0.5 g and even 0.1 g now sell.

EP 6

Myth 2: "The board price is what I pay"

The board says ₹12,800 a gram for 22 carat. So 10 grams = ₹1,28,000.

VERDICT: MYTH

The board price is only the gold. Here the bill is about ₹20,000 more. Always ask for making charges in writing before you choose.

EP 7

Myth 3: "A hallmark is just a stamp"

Anyone can punch a stamp. A hallmark proves nothing.

Since 1 April 2023, hallmarked gold jewellery cannot be sold without a 6-character HUID code. Each code is unique to one piece, so it can be traced.

Look for 3 marks: the BIS triangle, the purity (916 = 22K, 750 = 18K) and the HUID.

VERDICT: MYTH

Check it yourself in 30 seconds: free BIS Care app → "Verify HUID" → type the code. It should match the shop, purity and type of item on your bill.

EP 8

Myth 4: "A bank coin is safest; the bank will buy it back"

I'll buy my coin from the bank. Purest gold, and they'll take it back if I need cash.

Banks can sell gold coins, but RBI rules don't let them buy them back. Union Bank's own FAQ says it plainly: "No."

ZappySo who buys it later?

Prof. BoltA jeweller, at his rate. And bank coins usually cost more than a jeweller's to begin with.

VERDICT: HALF MYTH

The purity is real. The "easy buy-back" is not. You can pay more going in and get less coming out.

EP 9

Myth 5: "Digital gold apps are regulated"

₹10 of gold on my payment app is as safe as a mutual fund. Surely someone regulates it.

On 8 Nov 2025, SEBI, the market regulator, warned that "digital gold" products are outside its rules. They are neither securities nor commodity contracts, so none of its investor protection applies.

VERDICT: MYTH

If you want gold on a screen, SEBI points to regulated options: gold ETFs (gold funds traded on the stock exchange) and electronic gold receipts.

EP 10

Myth 6: "Gold in 10 minutes must be fake"

Gold delivered with my onions in 10 minutes? Can't be real.

Since Dhanteras 2024, Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart and BigBasket deliver branded coins from big jewellers, some as small as 0.1 gram.

ZOOM!

It caught on fast: on Akshaya Tritiya 2024, Instamart said coin sales jumped 500% over its Dhanteras sales.

VERDICT: MOSTLY MYTH

The coins come from known brands. Still: compare the price per gram with a jeweller's, keep the bill, and check the hallmark.

EP 11

Myth 7: "Jewellery sells back at what I paid"

Gold never loses. If I sell my chain tomorrow, I'll get my ₹1.48 lakh back.

When you sell, you are paid for the gold only. Making charges and GST are gone. Some shops also cut a few per cent for melting; others, in exchange offers, cut nothing.

Our chain from Myth 2, sold the next day with a 2% cut: about ₹1,25,440. That's roughly ₹22,500 less than you paid.

VERDICT: MYTH

Jewellery is for wearing and gifting. The gold price must rise about 18% just for you to break even (our arithmetic). Ask about the buy-back cut before you buy.

From the 2016 average to August 2026, gold grew about 18% a year. Over five years, about 26% a year.

BUT...

The one-line rule

Buying to wear? Jewellery. Buying for the puja? A small hallmarked coin. Buying to save? The cheapest regulated route.

EP 16

Prof. Bolt's verdict (opinion)

KEEP IT!

The stories are worth keeping. A clean home, a new broom, a lamp for the family's health, fresh water in the pot. None of that costs a lakh.

WATCH OUT

The shopping is where money leaks. Making charges, missing HUIDs, bank coins nobody buys back, unregulated apps. The festival doesn't need any of it.

MY TAKE

Buy the meaning, not the weight. Even 0.5 gram of hallmarked gold, or a steel pot, keeps the tradition. Put real savings where costs are low and rules are clear.

EP 17

So what should Zappy do?

Before Friday

  • Fix a budget first. Then decide grams, not the other way round.
  • Install the BIS Care app (free).
  • Ask two shops for making charges and their buy-back cut.

At the counter

  • Check the 3 marks and verify the HUID.
  • Get a GST bill showing weight, purity, making charge and tax separately.
  • Skip bank coins if you might sell; skip "no-bill" discounts always.
