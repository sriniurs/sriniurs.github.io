EP 2

Part 1: the invisible dust (PM2.5)

What it is

PM2.5 means particles smaller than 2.5 thousandths of a millimetre, about 30 times thinner than a hair. They slip past your nose, deep into your lungs and even into your blood.

What "AQI" means

The Air Quality Index turns several pollutants into one score. India's bands: 0–50 good, 201–300 poor, 301–400 very poor, 401+ severe.

The cigarette yardstick (Berkeley Earth)

Breathing 22 µg/m³ of PM2.5 for a day harms your health about as much as smoking one cigarette.

214 µg/m³÷22=~10 cigarettes

Our arithmetic, for one day at the 2 Nov reading. Rough rule of thumb.

EP 3

Part 2: the lid on the pot

Nights get cold→Cool air sits under a warmer layer (an "inversion")→Wind drops below 1 m/s→Smoke can't rise or blow away

Why summer is different

In summer, hot ground air rises and mixes high up, diluting the dust. In winter the "mixing height" shrinks, so the same smoke is packed into a much smaller box of air.

Delhi's geography

Delhi sits in the flat Indo-Gangetic plain, with the Himalayas to the north. Winter winds are weak and the plain acts like a bowl.

Prof. BoltDelhi doesn't make far more smoke in November. It just loses its chimney. Same fire, closed lid.

EP 5

The farm fire puzzle

Why farmers burn

After the rice harvest, farmers have only a couple of weeks to clear the field for wheat. Burning the leftover straw is fast and almost free. Machines that mix straw into the soil cost money and time.

How big is it? (DSS research paper, 2024)

Across the post-monsoon season, fires averaged about 7% of Delhi's PM2.5. On peak days, a single main source (fires or local emissions) can reach 65–69%.

The December surprise (CSE)

In December 2025, farm fires had almost stopped. Yet across the region, average PM2.5 was 28% higher than during the burning period.

If the fires stop and the air gets worse, the fires were never the whole story.

Prof. BoltGRAP is a fire extinguisher, not a fire alarm repair. It reacts after the air is already bad. The real fix happens the other 300 days of the year. (Opinion)

Delhi's yearly average

88.4µg/m³

The 2023 annual PM2.5 level used by the AQLI. That's about 4 cigarettes a day, every day of the year (our arithmetic: 88.4 ÷ 22).

Even India's own bar

Meeting just India's national standard (40 µg/m³) would give Delhi residents back about 3.5 years, since the loss falls from 8.2 to 4.7 years.

Nobody in India, all 1.4 billion of us, lives in air that meets the WHO guideline.

EP 9

Beijing did it. Can Delhi?

Beijing: Beijing Municipal Ecology and Environment Bureau. Delhi 2025: CREA ranks it India's 2nd most polluted city. Different monitors, so compare the direction, not the decimals.

Same starting line

In 2013, Beijing's air was about as dirty as Delhi's is today. Twelve years later it is less than a third.

What it took

China ran a national clean air plan from 2013: cutting coal burning, shifting heating to gas and electricity, and tight targets for whole regions, not one city.

Prof. BoltThe lesson: the air doesn't stop at a city border. Beijing cleaned up with its neighbours. Delhi's smoke comes from five states. (Opinion)

EP 10

The quick fix that wasn't: cloud seeding

The 2025 experiment

Delhi approved ₹3.21 crore for five cloud-seeding trials with IIT Kanpur. A plane released salt and silver iodide flares over the city to try to make rain wash the smog out.

The result

3 trials, ~₹1 crore→Cloud moisture only 15–20%→No significant rain

The full bill

IIT Kanpur estimated a whole winter of seeding at ₹25–30 crore, for perhaps a day or two of relief each time. Delhi's winter is usually too dry for it to work.

Even when rain comes, scientists say pollution returns almost as soon as it stops.

The south isn't clean either (Greenpeace India)

In Bengaluru, PM2.5 was 5–6 times the WHO guideline in every month studied. Bengaluru, Mysuru and Puducherry also showed coarser dust (PM10) at 4–5 times the WHO level.

The national picture (CREA, satellite data)

1,787 of 4,041 Indian towns broke India's yearly PM2.5 standard in every year studied. The national clean air programme covers only 130 cities.

Better winds on the plateau buy us time, not immunity.

Prof. BoltMy bet is the usual ending. The real story is the next ten years: either India treats air like Beijing did, as a whole-region job, or we keep buying air purifiers and calling it a solution.

EP 14

So what should Zappy do?

Before and during the trip

  • Check the AQI each morning (CPCB's Sameer app or any AQI app).
  • Pack well-fitting N95 masks; cloth masks don't stop PM2.5.
  • Skip early-morning walks and outdoor exercise on bad days; smog is worst at night and dawn.

For family at home

  • Keep windows shut on "very poor" days; an air purifier in one bedroom helps most.
  • Children, older people and anyone with asthma or heart disease need extra care; see a doctor for breathlessness.
  • Celebrate with diyas and lights; go easy on crackers.

THE END

Fridge-door cheat sheet

The machine

  • Dust: PM2.5, 30× thinner than a hair.
  • Lid: cold nights + no wind trap it.
  • Smoke: vehicles, NCR towns, industry, dust; farm fires and crackers add spikes.
  • 22 µg/m³ for a day ≈ 1 cigarette.

Deep dive

  • Delhi: 8.2 years of life lost (AQLI).
  • Beijing cut PM2.5 by 70% since 2013.
  • Cloud seeding: ~₹1 crore, no real rain.
  • Cost to India: $95 bn a year.
  • Bengaluru: more than twice the WHO limit.

Your move

  • Check AQI daily in winter.
  • N95, not cloth.
  • No dawn walks on smog days.
  • Purifier in the bedroom.
  • Fewer crackers.
