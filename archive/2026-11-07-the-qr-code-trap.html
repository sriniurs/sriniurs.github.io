PART 1: THE BAITA tempting offer

EP 1

The sofa, the buyer and the twist

A stranger offers ₹8,000 for Zappy’s second-hand sofa. Then comes a QR code with the instruction: “Scan it to receive your money.”

ZappyWhy should I scan his code if he is paying me?
Prof. BoltThat is the right question. Someone sending you money should not need you to authorise an outgoing payment.
EP 2

The direction of money

A UPI payment QR normally identifies the person or business who will receive the money. Scanning it in a UPI app usually prepares a payment TO that account.

ZappySo scanning is not the same as collecting?
Prof. BoltExactly. Look at the payment screen before doing anything else.
EP 3

The PIN is the trapdoor

The stranger says: “Enter your PIN to accept the ₹8,000.” But the app may actually be asking Zappy to authorise paying ₹8,000.

ZappyDoes scanning alone take money?
Prof. BoltNormally, no. A UPI QR scan by itself does not debit you. The risk is approving a payment—or opening a malicious link from an unknown QR.
WAIT! READ THE SCREEN!
EP 4

How a real buyer pays

A genuine buyer can use the seller’s own UPI ID, linked number or receiving QR. The seller checks the incoming credit independently.

ZappySo I do not enter my PIN to receive?
Prof. BoltCorrect. Receiving an ordinary UPI transfer does not require your UPI PIN.
Read the actual action in your payment app before approving.

PART 2: THE TRICKSWhen money moves the wrong way

EP 5

The collect-request costume

A collect request may be described by a caller as a refund or a payment waiting for acceptance. But approving it means you are agreeing to PAY.

ZappyThe label in a message can be false?
Prof. BoltYes. Trust the action shown inside your official payment app, not the stranger’s narration.
A stranger’s promise does not prove a payment is safe.
EP 6

The ₹1 confidence trick

The buyer first sends a genuine ₹1. Now he seems trustworthy, and asks you to scan his QR to receive the remaining amount.

ZappyBut the ₹1 was real!
Prof. BoltA real small credit proves only that ₹1 arrived. It does not make the next instruction safe.
EP 7

A sticker on a shop counter

A fake QR sticker pasted over a merchant’s genuine code can divert a payment to a different account.

ZappyBut the QR works!
Prof. BoltA working QR proves nothing about who receives the money. Confirm the payee name with the shop.
EP 8

Five seconds of protection

Before authorising any transfer: pause, read the recipient, check the amount and confirm the exact action.

ZappyThat is my whole security plan?
Prof. BoltIt is an excellent habit. Add one more: refuse pressure from anyone asking you to hurry.
Read the actual action in your payment app before approving.

PART 3: THE DEFENCEA few habits that matter

EP 9

The fake screenshot

A buyer shows a “payment successful” screenshot and asks for the item immediately. Screenshots can be altered.

ZappyHow do I check?
Prof. BoltLook in your OWN bank or UPI app for the incoming credit. Do not rely on someone else’s screen.
EP 10

Not every QR is a payment QR

A QR can open a webpage, form or download. Unknown codes can lead to fake bank pages or dangerous app-installation prompts.

ZappySo I should not blindly follow QR links?
Prof. BoltCorrect. Never enter banking secrets into an unsolicited link or install an app at a caller’s request.
A stranger’s promise does not prove a payment is safe.

DEEP DIVEWhat official guidance says

EP 11

DEEP DIVE: RBI’s warning

The Reserve Bank of India’s BE(A)WARE booklet and QR-safety campaign warn against scanning a QR code to receive money and say a PIN or OTP is not needed for incoming funds.

ZappySo this is official advice?
Prof. BoltYes. It is established fraud-prevention guidance, not a new viral claim.
EP 12

DEEP DIVE: NPCI’s guidance

NPCI says scanning a QR and entering a UPI PIN is meant for making payments, not receiving them. Its FAQ also warns never to share a UPI PIN.

ZappyDoes any PIN prompt mean money is leaving?
Prof. BoltNot always. Balance enquiries and PIN management may also use a PIN. Read what action you are authorising.
Read the actual action in your payment app before approving.
EP 13

DEEP DIVE: If money has gone

Contact your bank and UPI app promptly. Save transaction IDs and screenshots. Report financial cybercrime via helpline 1930 or the official cybercrime portal.

ZappyCan they guarantee a refund?
Prof. BoltNo. Fast reporting can help, but recovery is not guaranteed. Beware anyone charging an upfront 'recovery fee'.
EP 14

DEEP DIVE: Why it works

The scam combines a familiar action—QR payment—with a false story about refunds, sales or customer care. Urgency makes people stop reading their screens.

ZappySo it is psychology as much as technology?
Prof. BoltPrecisely. A confident voice cannot change what the payment button actually does.

THE VERDICTProf. Bolt’s OPINION

EP 15

OPINION: Prof. Bolt’s rule

UPI is useful and usually convenient. The safest habit is to check every transaction, especially when a stranger gives instructions.

ZappyWhat sentence should every family remember?
Prof. BoltOPINION · To RECEIVE money, never scan someone else’s payment QR or enter your UPI PIN. If someone insists, stop and verify independently.
Read the actual action in your payment app before approving.

ACTION

The five-second UPI checklist

Receiving? Share your own UPI ID or receiving QR. Verify credit in your own app.

Paying? Check recipient, amount and action before approval.

Under pressure? Cancel and verify independently.

Already paid a fraudster? Contact your bank and app, report promptly to 1930 or the official cybercrime portal.

Never disclose a UPI PIN or OTP to anyone.

RECEIVING

No PIN. Share your own receiving details.

PAYING

Read recipient, amount and action.

FRAUD?

Bank + app + 1930. Report promptly.
