THE PASTFrom a king's looms to a government factory

EP 1

A king goes shopping in Europe

ZappyWhy is it called Mysore silk? Didn't silk come from China?

Prof. BoltIt did, long ago. Tipu Sultan revived silk-making here in the late 1700s. But the saree you know started in 1912.

1912

32 looms

Maharaja Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV imported 32 power looms from Switzerland and opened the Mysore Silk Weaving Factory. At first it wove only for the royal family and fabrics for the army.

CLACK-CLACK!

EP 2

From palace to public

How the factory changed hands

1912: royal factory→After 1947: State Sericulture Dept→1980: KSIC takes over→2005: GI tag

KSIC is the Karnataka Silk Industries Corporation, a state government company. Within five years of 1980 it was "chronically sick". It only showed a small profit of ₹1.73 crore in 2005-06.

The GI tag (Geographical Indication) means only KSIC may sell a saree as "Mysore Silk". Like "Darjeeling Tea", the name is protected.

THE PRESENTSold out before lunch

EP 3

The 4 AM queue

Jayanagar, Bengaluru, 2026

Buyers line up by 4 am for a token. Some come at 9 pm the night before and sleep in their cars. One woman said getting a token took nearly six hours.

TOKEN No. 97!

The rules inside

  • Entry in batches of 10, with 10 minutes each to choose.
  • One saree per customer.
  • Fresh stock on Thursdays and Saturdays, about 100 sarees a batch.
  • Price: about ₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh.

ZappySix hours for one saree? Is it made of gold?

Prof. BoltA little bit, actually. We'll get to that. First, the real problem: there just aren't enough.

EP 4

The 600-saree gap

Extra wanted
~600 more

KSIC's managing director said demand runs about 600 sarees a day above production.

In the Assembly in March 2026, the sericulture minister said: "Whatever is produced gets sold within a day."

KSIC even paused online sales so its 12 showrooms get stock first.

Where there's a queue, there are touts. One MLA said he gets calls asking if a saree can be "arranged for money".

EP 5

The Rashmika effect

In March 2026, actor Rashmika Mandanna wore a red "Mysore silk" saree at her wedding reception. Searches and queues jumped again.

But the designer called it "inspired by Mysore Silk". It was pure crepe silk, not a KSIC saree.

ZappySo it wasn't real Mysore silk?

Prof. BoltIt was beautiful silk. But legally, only KSIC's sarees can carry the name. That's the GI tag at work.

EP 6

A stadium on the silk land?

February 2026

A plan to use 5 of the 12 acres at KSIC's T. Narasipura reeling unit for a ₹6 crore stadium alarmed workers. That unit turns cocoons into silk thread for every saree.

About 1,100 employees stopped work. All three units halted, and cocoon buying stopped for a week.

STOP!

How it ended

In March, the minister announced the land plan was dropped. Production restarted.

A one-week stoppage at one reeling unit can empty showrooms across the state. That's how thin the supply line is.

THE DEEP DIVEThe research behind the headlines

EP 7

From worm to wardrobe

The supply line

Farmers rear silkworms on mulberry leaves→Cocoons sold at Ramanagara and other markets→KSIC buys ~2,000 kg a day→T. Narasipura reels 250–300 kg of thread→Mysuru and Channapatna looms weave sarees

The maths

7 kg → 1 kg

About 7 kg of cocoons give 1 kg of silk thread. At about ₹700 a kg of cocoons, that's roughly ₹4,900 of cocoons for every kilo of thread, before anyone weaves a single border.

KSIC figures, March 2026; the ₹4,900 is our arithmetic.

Ramanagara

Asia's largest cocoon market trades about 40–50 tonnes of cocoons a day. It opens 363 days a year, closing only on 26 January and 15 August.

EP 8

What's really inside the saree

The "gold" border

65% silver

The zari (metal thread) is about 65% pure silver and only 0.65% gold. That's why prices move with silver and gold rates.

The crepe secret

The yarn is twisted about 2,200 times per metre. That high twist gives Mysore silk its soft, light, slightly grainy crepe feel.

Each saree carries a unique code, hologram and barcode woven in, to stop fakes.

EP 9

Why not just make more?

Looms in 5 years

Before
189 looms
2026
319 looms

Mysuru unit 159 → 229; Channapatna 30 → 70, with 20 more coming (KSIC, March 2026).

The back-of-envelope sum

319 looms make 300–400 sarees a day: about one saree per loom per day.

To make 600 more a day, KSIC would need roughly 500–600 more looms. That's nearly double today.

Our arithmetic from KSIC's figures.

And the people

1,092 workers against 1,200 sanctioned posts. Only about 150 are permanent; the rest are outsourced.

More looms also need more good cocoons, and traders from Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra compete to buy them.

EP 11

Karnataka: India's silk state

~40%

Karnataka produces about 40% of India's raw silk, the most of any state.

41,121 t

India's record raw silk output in 2024-25, says the Central Silk Board. India is the world's second-largest producer, after China.

So the problem isn't silk. Karnataka has plenty. The bottleneck is KSIC's looms, workers and top-grade thread.

THE FUTUREProf. Bolt's verdict, and Zappy's plan

EP 12

Prof. Bolt's verdict (opinion)

GROW!

Best case: KSIC fills its posts, keeps adding looms and protects its land. Queues shrink within two or three years.

HMM.

Likely case: slow growth. Demand stays ahead, the queue stays, and "inspired" crepe sarees fill the gap.

FAKE!

Risk: long waits push buyers to touts and fakes, hurting a 114-year-old name.

Prof. BoltMy view: the scarcity is part of the magic, but KSIC is leaving money and goodwill on the table. A profitable company should be hiring permanent weavers, not running on outsourced staff.

EP 13

So what should Zappy do?

Before you buy

  • Buy only at a KSIC showroom or KSIC's own online store.
  • Check the woven code and the hologram.
  • Ask for a KSIC bill.
  • Never pay a middleman to "arrange" one.

Smart moves

  • Go on fresh-stock days, early.
  • If time is short, a good crepe silk is fine. Just don't pay a Mysore Silk price for it.
  • Plan wedding sarees months ahead, not weeks.

ZappySo I set an alarm for 3:30 and check the hologram?

Prof. BoltAnd carry a flask of filter coffee. Royal things are worth a little wait.
