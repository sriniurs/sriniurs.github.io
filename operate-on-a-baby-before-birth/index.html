Can Doctors Operate
on a Baby Before
It Is Born?

AIIMS Delhi has just reported India’s first open fetal repair of spina bifida. It sounds like science fiction. It is real medicine — dramatic, delicate, and useful only in very selected pregnancies. Here is what happened, why surgeons do it, and what this first actually means.

AIIMS DelhiSpina bifidaFetal surgery

ABOUT 8 MINUTES

01

The astonishing headline

This week, AIIMS Delhi said it had performed India’s first open fetal surgery to repair a serious spinal defect in an unborn baby.

The operation took place on 12 November 2026, at 25 weeks of pregnancy, lasted about six hours, and involved a multidisciplinary team of more than 30 clinicians and nurses. The condition was a lumbosacral myelomeningocele — the most severe form of spina bifida, in which the spinal cord and its coverings protrude through an opening in the back. [1] [2]

Most people are used to hearing that babies with such defects are operated on after birth. Here the repair happened while the baby was still inside the uterus. That is why the headline feels almost impossible — and why it matters. [3]

ZappyWait. Surgery on a baby who has not even been born yet?

Prof. BoltYes — but think of it less as “operating on a tiny person floating freely” and more as a carefully planned operation on a pregnancy, where surgeons protect both mother and fetus at every step.

02

What exactly is spina bifida?

Spina bifida is a neural tube defect. Very early in pregnancy, the neural tube is the structure that will become the brain and spinal cord. If it does not form or close properly, the spine may remain open and the spinal cord may be damaged. [4]

The mild forms can be surprisingly subtle. The severe end is myelomeningocele, where part of the spinal cord and its coverings bulge through an opening in the back. The higher and larger the defect, the greater the risk of weakness or paralysis in the legs, bowel and bladder problems, orthopedic deformity, and fluid build-up in the brain called hydrocephalus. [4] [5]

That is why this diagnosis is so frightening for families. It is not just “a hole in the back”. It can shape how a child walks, urinates, learns, and lives. Many children need ongoing neurosurgical, orthopedic, urologic and rehabilitation care. [5]

The surgery is not fixing a cosmetic opening. It is trying to protect exposed nerves that control lifelong function.
03

Why operate before birth at all?

The key idea is this: in myelomeningocele, damage does not happen only on day one. The nerves are already malformed early on, but they also remain exposed to the uterine environment and mechanical trauma for months. Surgeons call this the “two-hit” idea: an early developmental problem, followed by ongoing injury during pregnancy. [3] [6]

If the back is repaired before birth, the hope is not to create a normal spinal cord. The hope is to prevent further damage. In other words, prenatal surgery cannot rewind the clock, but it may stop some of the clock’s later damage. [3] [6]

Neural tube does not close properly→Spinal cord is exposed→Months of ongoing injury→Repair before birth may reduce later harm

This is the logic behind fetal repair. It is protective surgery, not magic. [3]

ZappySo the point is to save whatever function can still be saved?

Prof. BoltExactly. Good fetal surgery is an argument against further damage, not a promise of perfection.

04

What AIIMS Delhi actually did

According to AIIMS, the case involved more than five weeks of planning: detailed fetal imaging, counseling, anesthesia preparation and operative simulation. On the day of surgery, specialists from maternal-fetal medicine, obstetrics, neurosurgery, anesthesia, plastic surgery, radiodiagnosis and neonatology worked together. High-end microscopy and continuous ultrasound guidance were used. [1] [2]

In open fetal surgery, the uterus is opened, but the fetus remains inside it. Surgeons expose the baby’s back just enough to repair the defect, then close the womb and continue the pregnancy. Both the mother and fetus require very close monitoring afterwards. AIIMS emphasized that this is a treatment for selected pregnancies, not a substitute for prevention of neural tube defects. It also said the care was provided free under the Government of India’s Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram. [1]

This is what makes the story bigger than a technical milestone. It shows that highly complex fetal therapy can, at least in some cases, be delivered in a public institution in India.

THE DEEP DIVE

Does operating before birth actually help children later on? Here the answer is better than “we hope so”, but more careful than “problem solved”.

05

What the evidence says

The landmark study here is the MOMS trial — a randomized trial comparing prenatal repair with standard postnatal repair. It was stopped early because prenatal surgery was showing important benefits. Babies in the prenatal-surgery group were less likely to need a cerebrospinal-fluid shunt by 12 months, had less hindbrain herniation, and had better motor outcomes at 30 months. Actual shunt placement occurred in 40% of the prenatal group versus 82% of the postnatal group. [3]

Longer follow-up adds nuance rather than disappointment. At school age, children repaired prenatally were more likely to walk without assistive devices and had fewer shunts and shunt revisions, but there was no clear improvement in overall adaptive behavior. In plain English: mobility and some neurosurgical outcomes improved, but prenatal repair is not a guarantee of normal cognition or a normal childhood. [7]

This operation offers a better starting line, not a different species of baby. The condition still remains serious.
06

The catch: this is major surgery for the mother too

There is a reason this surgery is offered only at experienced centers. The benefits come with serious trade-offs. ACOG and the Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine stress that open maternal-fetal surgery is a major procedure for the woman and the fetus, with risks during the operation, during the rest of the pregnancy, and in future pregnancies. It should be offered only to carefully selected patients at properly resourced centers. [6]

The MOMS trial also showed the costs: higher rates of prematurity and membrane rupture, plus the fact that delivery by cesarean is generally required in that pregnancy and future pregnancies because of the uterine scar. [3] [6]

WHY IT HELPS

Can reduce later neurological injury, lower shunt need, and improve average mobility.

WHY IT IS LIMITED

It carries maternal risk, can lead to earlier delivery, and is suitable only for selected cases before about 26 weeks.

That is why counseling matters so much. The ethical standard is not “push surgery”. It is nondirective counseling — giving families the facts and options, including standard postnatal repair. [6]

07

The quieter lesson: some cases are preventable

Whenever a dramatic operation makes headlines, prevention can look boring. It is not. Both the CDC and WHO recommend that women who may become pregnant get 400 micrograms of folic acid daily in the preconception period and early pregnancy, because folic acid helps prevent neural tube defects. [8] [9]

This advice matters because neural tube defects arise very early, often before a woman even knows she is pregnant. That is why “I will start later” can miss the window. Of course, not every case is preventable and not every family had a realistic chance to prepare. But prevention can avert some defects that even the most sophisticated surgery cannot fully correct. [8] [9]

Spina bifida is also often detected on prenatal ultrasound, which is what makes case selection for fetal surgery possible in the first place. Detection, counseling, and referral are just as much a part of this story as the knife and microscope.

ZappyThe flashy headline is fetal surgery. The quietly powerful headline is folic acid.

Prof. BoltExactly. The best surgery in the world is still second prize to prevention.

08

So what does this first mean for India?

It means India now has proof that this level of multidisciplinary fetal care can be organized here. That matters for training, protocols, ethics, neonatal support and public confidence. It may eventually widen access for carefully chosen families who otherwise would have had only postnatal repair as an option.

It does not mean every fetus with spina bifida should be operated on. It does not mean the condition is cured. It does not remove the need for long-term rehabilitation, urology, orthopedics, neurosurgery and family support. And it does not reduce the importance of folic acid and early antenatal care.

Still, it is a landmark worth pausing over. Medicine occasionally produces a sentence that sounds impossible and true at the same time: “The surgeons repaired the baby’s spine before the baby was born.” India now has one such sentence.

The significance is twofold: a new treatment option for selected pregnancies, and a reminder that public hospitals can still surprise us with world-class medicine.
◎

Prof. Bolt’s verdict

THE BIG IDEA

Open fetal repair is real, evidence-based, and aimed at limiting additional nerve damage in severe spina bifida before birth.

THE LIMIT

It is not a cure, and it carries serious risks to the mother and pregnancy. It belongs only in experienced centers and selected cases.

THE QUIET HERO

Prevention still matters enormously: folic acid before conception and in early pregnancy remains one of the most powerful tools in this whole story.

Fridge-door cheat sheet

What happened?

AIIMS Delhi reported India’s first open fetal surgery to repair a lumbosacral myelomeningocele at 25 weeks.

Why before birth?

Because exposed spinal nerves may suffer progressive damage during pregnancy. Earlier repair may reduce some later harm.

What are the proven benefits?

Lower shunt rates, less hindbrain herniation and better average mobility than standard postnatal repair in the MOMS trial.

What are the risks?

Major maternal surgery, prematurity risk, and the need for cesarean delivery in this and subsequent pregnancies.

Who is it for?

Only carefully selected pregnancies, typically before about 26 weeks, at centers with the right team and support.

What should every family remember?

Folic acid before conception and in early pregnancy helps prevent some neural tube defects.

If an antenatal scan suggests spina bifida or another fetal abnormality, discuss the scan with your treating obstetrician and a fetal-medicine specialist. This article is educational, not a guide to choosing surgery; eligibility and risks require individualized counselling.
